## 2. Business

**Created:** members pay $65/$130 a year for access to ~4,000 SKUs sold at
a gross margin Costco deliberately caps — 12.88% TTM company-wide (probe),
against ~30%+ for conventional grocers. The customer surplus is large and
legible: Kirkland Signature priced 15–20% under national-brand equivalents,
gas priced below market ("first to lower prices, last to raise" — CEO
Vachris, Q3 FY26 call, primary-transcribed). In August, gas was up low 30s
and pharmacy and hearing aids led ancillary at high-20s growth (Aug release
recording) — the ancillary businesses are member-acquisition tools priced
as such.

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (1) **Membership fees** — $5.323B in FY25
(10-K), 51% of $10.383B operating income; renewal 92.2% US/Canada (Q3 call),
fee raised roughly every 5–6 years (last Sep 1, 2024), executive tier
growing 9.6% against 4.1% total members (Q3 call). (2) **Merchandise spread
at scale** — 3.82% TTM operating margin on $293.6B revenue; fat in dollars,
thin by doctrine. (3) **Emerging monetization** — retail media (Google
Commerce Media partnership, Q3 call), pharmacy, e-commerce (+17.9% August,
+19.8% Q4 ex-FX), and the Medicare Advantage rollout with SCAN in two
states (WSJ via MT Newswires 2026-08-18, low-confidence market chatter; not
confirmed by an 8-K) — management states 80–90% of retail-media value is
reinvested in price, so this feeds the flywheel rather than the margin.

**Protected:** the fee-funded cost structure is the mechanism. Costco can
run a 12.9% gross margin because $5.3B of fee income arrives before any
goods are sold, and it fills warehouses at $317M revenue per box, 13.45×
inventory turns and $861K revenue per employee (probe). A competitor cannot
copy the price points without the member volume and cannot get the volume
without the price points; the evidence the loop holds is the renewal rate
and the executive-tier mix, not a label. The observable weakness is the
loop's exposure to the affluent US household — renewal is a decision made
once a year by a member whose wallet is wealth-effect sensitive.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder — insiders 0.17%, institutions 73.8% (probe; FY25 10-K cover). The
null answer: nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist path except the
$404B size.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive**, slow and deliberate.

|---|---|---|---|
| FY21 | $195.9B | $6.79B | 3.47% |
| FY22 | $227.0B | $7.91B | 3.49% |
| FY23 | $242.3B | $8.51B | 3.51% |
| FY24 | $254.5B | $9.29B | 3.65% |
| FY25 | $275.2B | $10.38B | 3.77% |
| TTM (Q3 FY26) | $293.6B | $11.23B | 3.82% |

Revenue multiplied 1.5× while operating income multiplied 1.65× (income
statement route). Not loss-making; not levered (debt $8.2B against $20.0B
cash; interest coverage 76.9×). Phase 0 does not kill it.
