## 2. Business

**Created:** F5 sits in the data path in front of mission-critical
applications — load balancing, traffic management, WAF/API security — across
on-prem, cloud, and edge (BIG-IP systems/software, NGINX, Distributed Cloud
SaaS). Customers get uptime, throughput, and a single policy/security layer
across hybrid environments; one large energy customer runs >1,000
mission-critical apps on F5 (Q3 FY26 call). The Q3 print: revenue $865M +11%,
product +19% (8th straight double-digit quarter), systems +32%, software +7%,
services +3%; 69% of revenue recurring; deferred revenue $2.19B +12% (8-K
ex-99.1).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not one — (a) high-margin
hardware systems (rSeries/VELOS; 32% growth at cycle peak, higher-performance
units carry above-average gross margin per CFO), (b) software subscriptions
(90% of software revenue; multi-year FCP agreements with ~2/3 recognized
upfront, rest deferred), (c) SaaS/managed services (Distributed Cloud).
Blended: 81.9% TTM gross margin, 24.5% GAAP operating margin, 29% ROIC
(stockanalysis.com statistics).

**Protected:** the mechanism is switching cost on the data path — ripping
out the traffic layer in front of a thousand production apps risks downtime on
everything at once — plus three decades of operational trust and iRules/policy
configuration accumulated in the installed base. Evidence the moat is real and
not a label: a nation-state actor stole BIG-IP source code (disclosed
2025-10-15 8-K; CISA emergency directive followed) and customers *kept
buying* — the eight double-digit product quarters span the breach, and US
federal (7% of product bookings) still grew. Weakness honestly stated: in the
cloud-first era FY19–FY23 this moat did not produce growth (FY24 revenue grew
0.1% over FY23); the moat protects the installed base, it does not by itself
create expansion.
