## 2. Business

**Created:** ECC gives retail investors exchange-traded access to CLO equity,
which institutional desks otherwise hold in private funds with $5M+
minimums. That is the residual, first-loss tranche of securitized pools of
~1,838 broadly-syndicated corporate loans (look-through, 6/30). The customer
gets a monthly distribution and the 3–10-year arbitrage between loan spreads
(3.15% weighted average, 6/30) and the CLO's AAA–BB liability cost. Since
2025 the book has added non-CLO credit (38% at 6/30): infrastructure credit,
regulatory capital relief, ABS, private credit sourced from the Eagle Point
platform (Q2 call).

**Captured:** There are three distinct flows, and the shareholder gets the
residual of each after the manager and the senior capital.
(a) CLO equity cash distributions: $62.4M "recurring cash" in Q2. Much of
that is return of principal on a wasting asset; GAAP recognizes only the
effective yield (9.0% on amortized cost, 6/30, down from 11.5% a year
earlier). (b) Platform economics: a perpetual revenue-share on future CLO
issuance by Muzinich, plus stakes in collateral-manager platforms (Q2 call).
Q3's NII beat is attributed "principally" to this (8-K, 2026-10-14).
(c) Leverage spread: $512M of fixed-rate notes and preferred at a 6.8%
weighted-average cost (N-CSRS 6/30). That is ahead of the 9.0% effective
yield, but only ~220bp ahead, before fees. The manager takes a 1.75% base
fee (424B filings; the base definition was not obtained) and an incentive
fee on income. The incentive fee payable was $9.21M at 6/30/2026, while
per-share NAV had fallen 42% from its 2024 year-end.

**Protected:** Nothing protects the common holder's economics. Any CLO
manager, private fund or rival listed vehicle (OXLC, EIC, CCIF) buys the
same tranches. ECC's claimed edge is manager selection and its
collateral-manager partnerships, and it cannot be assessed from
disclosures. The Q4-2025 call described the competitive pressure itself:
captive CLO equity funds are return-insensitive buyers that "pushed loan
spreads tighter" and compressed the arbitrage. The closed-end structure
protects the manager (permanent capital, no redemptions), not the
shareholder.

**Control:** One class of voting common, no controlling holder disclosed.
It is externally managed by Eagle Point Credit Management LLC; insiders hold
0.32% (stocks.db). Advisor team members bought "more than 167,000 shares" in
Q1 2026 (Q1 call), immaterial at 0.13% of shares out. Preferred holders
carry statutory class-vote rights, including the right to elect a board
majority if two years of preferred dividends go unpaid (1940 Act §18). The
external-management contract plus a board that sets distribution and
buyback policy forecloses an activist path short of a proxy fight to
terminate the advisory agreement.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): not meaningful — reported as NAV
trajectory.** A fund has no revenue/operating-income split; the
income-statement equivalent is NII versus GAAP net change in net assets:

**Inputs.** A reverse DCF on a closed-end fund measures a narrow thing:
what the distributable-income stream yields against the price. Phase 0's

and the probe CLI prints schema only. That would be the wrong flow for a
fund anyway, because investment purchases and sales sit in operating cash
flow. The base flow is **NII available to common**: Q2 NII $0.17 − $0.05
temporary-equity distributions = $0.12/qtr × 4 × 133.01M shares = **$63.8M**.
It is paired with **market cap $379.7M**. Senior securities are already
served in the flow, so net debt is 0 by the pairing rule. SBC is nil
(externally managed). There is no minority interest.

**Hurdle:** rf 5.21% (DGS10, 2026-10-13) + beta 0.8 × ERP 4.20% (Damodaran
implied ERP, 2026-10-01) = **8.57%**. The regression beta of 0.38 sits
below the 0.8–1.2 band and is clamped to 0.8. It is not a thin-float case
(float 126.7M / 133.0M = 95%; insiders 0.32%). The clamp is for a 48%-levered
first-loss credit fund whose marked NAV fell 27% in one quarter. Sensitivity
at beta 1.2: hurdle 10.25%. 10-year breakeven: 2.35% (T10YIE, 2026-10-14),
the terminal-growth ceiling.

last four quarters, NII less realized losses summed to about −$0.45/share:
