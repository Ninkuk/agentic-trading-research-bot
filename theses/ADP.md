## 2. Business

**Created:** an employer with 40 or 40,000 people has a legal obligation to
pay each of them the right amount, withhold the right tax in the right
jurisdiction, remit it to the right authority on the right day, and prove it
later. Getting this wrong is not a productivity loss, it is a penalty and an
audit. ADP absorbs that obligation. What the customer buys is not payroll
software; it is the transfer of a compliance liability to a counterparty
with 77 years of doing it, coverage across every US state and 140-plus
countries, and the operational scale to have already seen the edge case in
front of them. The surplus is largest for the customer least equipped to
price the risk — the small business that does not know what it does not know
— which is why the downmarket RUN franchise carries the strongest economics
in the book.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one.

- *Per-employee-per-month subscription* on the HCM platforms — RUN
  (downmarket), Workforce Now (mid-market), Lyric HCM and Workforce Suite
  (enterprise), ADP Global Payroll (international). This is Employer
  Services: **$14,831.4M** of FY2026 revenue, +6.8%.
- *Outsourced service labour*, sold as a margin on people rather than
  software: the PEO (co-employment — ADP becomes employer of record and
  resells health insurance and workers' comp) and ES HRO. PEO Services was
  **$7,128.2M**, +6.5%, but roughly a third is zero-margin pass-through of
  insurance premiums and PEO ex-pass-through grew 5%. PEO margins
  *contracted* 110bp in FY2026 and are guided to contract further.
- *Float* — the most underweighted line in the business. ADP holds client
  payroll funds between debit and disbursement: FY2026 average client funds
  balance **$40,410.7M** (+7.4%) at an average rate of **3.4%**, generating
  **$1,354.8M** of interest on funds held for clients. That is 6.2% of
  revenue but **23.3% of the $5,825.3M operating income** (23.6% of pre-tax
  income), and it is near-pure margin. It is a laddered bond portfolio
  wearing a software multiple. Mechanics, from the 10-K: ADP "extend[s] the
  maturities of our investment portfolio … and employ[s] short-term financing
  arrangements," laddering "out to five years (in the case of the extended
  portfolio) and out to ten years (in the case of the long portfolio)" in
  order to "average our way through an interest rate cycle." The short leg
  is funded with commercial paper (FY2026 average daily borrowings $4.2B at
  4.0%) and reverse repos (average $3.6B at 4.1%) — so ADP borrows ~$7.8B
  short at ~4% against a portfolio yielding 3.4%, which is why net client-
  funds strategy income ($1.31B) sits below gross interest ($1.35B). Net
  unrealised pre-tax losses on the AFS book were **−$459.2M** at 2026-06-30.
- *Ancillary attach* — Retirement Services crossed $1B of annual revenue
  across 210,000 clients in FY2026; Insurance Services; ADP Marketplace and
  API Central, priced on consumption.

**Protected:** the moat is the cost of being wrong, not the cost of switching
software. Three mechanisms, descending in strength. (i) *Banking and tax
rails*: $3.5T/yr moved in the US, filed to "millions" of tax authorities and
employees (CFO 2026-09-09); a challenger must reconstruct settlement,
security and remittance infrastructure before it can compete on the
interface, and that work is invisible in a product demo. (ii) *Regulatory
fragmentation as a compounding barrier*: every new state, country or
AI-governance rule adds fixed cost ADP amortises across 1.1M clients and a
rival amortises across far fewer. (iii) *Data*: 77 years of payroll records,
now mined at task level with the Stanford Digital Economy Lab — the weakest
of the three, because it is product differentiation rather than a barrier and
nothing stops a rival buying comparable labour-market data. What is *not* a
moat: the software itself. Management describes Lyric as the newest
enterprise HCM offer on the market, which is an admission that the previous
generation had aged.

**Control:** one class of common stock ($0.10 par, voting), one share one
vote, no controller, no dual class, no supervoting shares — confirmed on the
FY2026 10-K cover page (Section 12(g) securities: None) and in the DEF 14A
filed 2025-09-25. Annual election of directors (no classified board),
majority voting, proxy access (20 holders / 3% / 3 years), **no poison
pill**, independent non-executive chair, and stockholder rights both to call
special meetings and to act by written consent. Largest holders are index
funds — Vanguard 10.07%, BlackRock 8.10%; all 27 directors and executive
officers together hold 794,706 shares, under 1%. Insiders 0.097% of shares
out, institutions 91.03%, float 396.31M of 397.26M (99.76%). This structure
forecloses nothing: a $106B company with no pill, written consent and
special-meeting rights, trading 8.25% below where it was a year ago, is
structurally open to both an unsolicited approach and an activist. The
change-of-control plan triggers at 35% beneficial ownership.

**Thread — the commitments footnote breaks the asset-light read.** This is
the run's sharpest finding and it became the closest attack. FY2026 capex was

asset-light profile. Note 13 of the FY2026 10-K says otherwise: purchase
commitments of **$2,850.5M**, versus **$1,498.9M** in the FY2025 10-K, a 90%
increase in one year, with the FY2026 language adding a category the FY2025

itemise the note — no vendor, no minimum-volume or take-or-pay language, no
separate compute line — so how much of the increase is AI compute cannot be
established, and the prior-year Chubb premium is NOT OBTAINED, which blocks a
clean like-for-like on the near-year tranche. What can be said is that
committed software and services spend is growing far faster than revenue,

**Thread — the float is more durable than the "assumes hikes" framing.**
Entered as a bear thread and came out partly reversed, which is why it is
recorded at length. The CFO's guide "contemplates the market's current
expectations for between 25 and 50 basis points of Fed funds rate hikes"
(2026-07-29), which reads as a directional rate bet inside 23.3% of operating
income. The 10-K's Item 7A quantification refutes the near-term version of
that worry: a hypothetical 25bp move in **both** short- and intermediate-term
rates is worth only **~$21.0M** to pre-tax earnings over the twelve months to
2027-06-30, and a 25bp move in short rates alone only **~$10.0M** — both
figures net of the related short-term borrowings and across the whole
portfolio. The ladder is doing exactly what it is designed to do. Two caveats
carried forward: ADP discloses **no 100bp sensitivity**, so the 25bp figure
must not be scaled linearly as though it were the filing's number; and the
insensitivity cuts both ways over a DCF horizon, because a multi-year decline
in rates rolls through the ladder eventually and is a terminal-value question
rather than a next-twelve-months one. §4's S4 is therefore relabelled as a
3–5 year scenario, not a FY2027 one. The 10-K also names a *non*-rate route
to the same damage: a regulatory change "decreasing the amount of taxes to be

  products and solutions,"* (b) employment-level erosion against contracts
  that *"blend base fees and per-employee fees,"* (c) a regulatory change
  "decreasing the amount of taxes to be withheld or allowing less time to
  remit taxes" shrinking the float balances, and (d) a custody or data breach
  destroying the trust the whole franchise rests on. A terminal rate of 2.36%
  survives all four because it is the 10-year breakeven — nominal inflation,
  zero real growth. It asks only that ADP holds its real position in
  perpetuity, not that it grows. Nothing above the breakeven would be
  defensible against a company that names AI obsolescence as its own leading
  technology risk, and the default read for the terminal period — excess
  returns fading, not persisting, since only ~29% of firms out-earn their
  cost of capital — is what the breakeven encodes.
- *Distribution clamp.* The US median cost of capital is ~7.8% and 80% of
  firms sit within roughly 5–10% (Damodaran, as-of dates in
  `references/damodaran-anchors.md`). The implied returns of 7.4–8.5% sit
  inside that band near the median: this is a **fairly-priced** name, not a
  strong pass on the clamp. Damodaran's absolute companion for a mature
  company — cost of capital ≈ rf + 4.5% = **9.45%** — is 106bp above the
  beta-derived 8.39% hurdle, and against that stricter bar even S3 misses by
  97bp. Beta 0.83 is doing real work in this write-up's favour and the reader
  should know it.

The object of the attack is §1's bull case — the claim that owning ADP at
$268.34 beats SPY — not the pass's own reasoning. Four conditions, which is a
moderate surface area: fewer legs than a typical turnaround, more than the
one-legged "good business at a fair price" that would be safest.

**Internal-consistency check, run first because it is free.** The bull case
asserts growth (≥4.5% organic), margin expansion, and near-zero reinvestment
(capex 0.9% of revenue) simultaneously — the classic strained triple. Here
the arithmetic actually holds: g = reinvestment rate × return on capital, and
at a 60.5% ROIC a 4.5% growth rate needs only ~7.4% of earnings reinvested.
The attack fails on its own terms. What does not fail is the *disclosure*
version of the same attack — see condition 2.

**Per-condition adjudication.**

1. **Ex-float organic ≥4.5%/yr for five years — SURVIVED (at *probable*,
   narrowly).** Attacked three ways. (a) Management's own FY2027 guide brackets
   rather than clears the threshold: 4.3–5.5% ex-float, so year one can miss
   on the company's own numbers. (b) The trend decelerates — FY2026 ES grew
   5% organic constant-currency for the full year against ~1pp of FX tailwind
   that reverses in FY2027 and ~30bp of WorkForce Software contribution that
   anniversaried; the CFO did not rebut the sell-side arithmetic of 4–5% ES
   ex-float for FY2027. (c) ~130bp of the growth is price, which the CFO tied
   explicitly to the macro — "some of it is the macro environment and where
   inflation sits … that rising tide has lifted all boats" — against a ~50bp
   pre-pandemic run-rate, so roughly 80bp of the path is an inflation rental,
   not a franchise property. Against all three: ADP compounded revenue at a
   7.4% CAGR over FY2022–26 and bookings grew 6% to $2.2B. The evidence does
   not stand against the condition. It survives, and the deceleration is the
   runner-up attack.
2. **Adjusted EBIT margin +70–90bp/yr with a durable operational share —
   NOT OBTAINED.** This is where the pass lands. Note 13 of the FY2026 10-K
   (2026-08-05) shows purchase commitments of $2,850.5M against $1,498.9M in
   the FY2025 10-K — +90% in one year — with a category the prior year's

   rather than a saving. The attack could not be completed: ADP does not

   credits rule this is NOT OBTAINED, not SURVIVED: the delivered 80bp is

**Standing checks.**

- *Base rate.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of capital
  (Damodaran's EVA dataset), so "the excess return persists" is a 3-in-10

  terminal growth is pinned at the 2.36% breakeven, which encodes the fade.
  For the five-year organic path the relevant base rate is ADP's own — a 7.4%
  revenue CAGR over FY2022–26 — which is favourable, though it spans an
  exceptional wage-inflation and rate cycle that will not repeat.
