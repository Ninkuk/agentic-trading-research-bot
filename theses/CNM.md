## 2. Business

**Created:** A contractor or municipality installing a water main, a storm
drainage system or a fire sprinkler riser needs several hundred specified
items — pipe, valves, fittings, hydrants, meters, geotextiles — delivered to a
job site on a date, in a jurisdiction whose product specifications were
written locally. Core & Main holds that inventory in 370+ branches, knows
which valve the county engineer's spec sheet actually permits, and can get it
to the trench. The customer is buying availability plus local specification
knowledge, not price: they get a project that does not stall waiting on a part
that was ordered wrong. On treatment-plant and data-center work the same
branch adds technical and project support across multi-year cycles, which is
why management describes those categories as "sweet spot meets sweet spot."

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms, not one.
(i) *Distribution spread* — buy from 5,000 suppliers, sell to 60,000
customers at a 27% gross margin, on spot terms with no long-term contracts.
The top ten suppliers are 45% of purchases, and supplier rebate programs
"effectively reduce the costs of our products," so a slice of the margin is
volume-rebate capture, not markup.
(ii) *Private label and direct sourcing* — CNM's own brands sourced through an
internal master distribution network, which management names as the primary
driver of the +30 to +60bp annual gross-margin expansion it reported through
fiscal 2025 and Q1 FY2026. This is margin taken from the supplier, not the
customer.
(iii) *Product-line mix shift* — fire protection (steel pipe and sprinkler
components, +13.5% in Q2) and smart utility (meters and the software around
them) carry different economics from core pipes/valves/fittings, and
management is deliberately shifting toward "higher-value specialty products"
on treatment-plant projects to raise content per job.

**Protected:** The mechanism is local, and it is modest. A municipality's
product specification is written by its own engineers and referenced against
what the local distributor stocks; a national competitor cannot serve a county
it has no branch in, and a local competitor cannot serve a contractor working
across six states. That two-sided constraint is why exactly two national
distributors exist across a $44B market and the remaining ~83% is "hundreds of
regional, local and specialty niche distributors." The barrier is real but it
is a distribution barrier, worth 27% gross and 9.6% operating margin, not a
franchise. It does not defend against the endgame the 10-K itself names:
customer consolidation leading to vertical integration, or suppliers building
out direct sales forces. The honest statement is that CNM has a durable
position in a business that structurally cannot earn very much on it.

**Control:** Class A common (184,192,708 shares, one vote, NYSE-listed) and
Class B common (6,301,342 shares, voting, no economic rights — paired with
Partnership Interests in Core & Main Holdings, LP held through Management
Feeder). Class B is 3.3% of votes. **CD&R, the 2017 sponsor, exited entirely
— as of January 25, 2024 the CD&R Investors held no Class A and no Class B
shares.** There is therefore no controlling holder and nothing forecloses an
unsolicited takeover or an activist campaign. This is effectively the null
answer with one residual: the Up-C plumbing that survives the sponsor's exit
is the Tax Receivable Agreement (§3), which is a cash claim, not a control
right.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative**, with a recent inflection worth
stating.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal-ROE warning, answered.* The uncorrected run fires
  the tool's "growth without reinvestment" warning, because headline FCF
  ($628M) exceeds net income ($459M). Two causes, and both are answered rather
  than assumed away. First, the earnings base is understated by acquired-
  intangible amortization: D&A is $193M against $55M of capex, and the GAAP-to-
  adjusted EPS bridge ($0.77 → $0.94 in Q2) is dominated by intangible
  amortization, implying roughly $130M/yr — so cash earnings are about
  $459M + $130M × (1 − 0.236) ≈ **$558M**, which is the `--base-earnings` used
  throughout. Second, the residual gap was the un-charged TRA. Once both
  corrections land, scenario B's terminal reinvestment rate is a positive
  8.4% and the warning does not fire.
- *Terminal ROE, read two-sided.* Scenario B implies a terminal ROE of
  **28.0%** — more than five points above the 8.72% hurdle, which the method
  calls "tough to do in perpetuity," and above CNM's own realized ROE of
  23.56% and far above its 12.67% ROIC. This does not rescue the pass; it
  makes it stronger. The +44bp in scenario B is generous, not conservative,
  because the arithmetic already embeds a terminal return on retained capital
  the company has never earned. Scenario C's implied terminal ROE of 5.2% is
  the mirror error — below the hurdle, i.e. value destruction in perpetuity —
  and neither bracket is defensible, which is the honest reading: the terminal
  value is not where this decision is made.

  income versus a 23.6% effective rate. This is not a windfall to normalize
  away and it is not a windfall to keep: it is the Up-C basis step-up, and 85%

  TRA payment (rather than grossing cash taxes up to book) is the
  non-double-counting correction, and it leaves roughly $56M/yr of genuinely
  retained benefit. That residual ends when the step-up amortization is
  exhausted — but the TRA payments end at the same moment, so the two
  substantially offset for a shareholder and no further adjustment is made.
  H1 cash taxes paid were $40M versus $69M in the prior-year H1, so the
  direction is currently widening, not closing.
- *Serial-acquirer charge, answered.* Handled explicitly by scenario, above.
  Scenario A is the un-charged version and is reported only as the error.
- *SBC, answered.* $14M deducted; `sbcByRevenue` of 0.18% means this is
  immaterial here, unlike a software name. No large option or RSU overhang
  hides in the share count — the diluted and basic Q2 EPS are both $0.77.
- *Market-share sentence.* Scenario B's 2.5% for five years puts revenue at
  **$8.71B**; against the 10-K's $44B fiscal-2025 addressable market growing
  at roughly nominal GDP (~$48.6B in five years), that is **~17.9% share
  versus ~17% today** — a modest, credible ask. Scenario C's 6% path implies

  above the breakeven would have to argue that a distributor keeps taking

- *Distribution clamp.* The implied returns span 7.08% to 10.89%, all inside

  below the ~7.8% US median cost of capital. Nothing here is an outlier in
  either direction — which is itself the finding: this is a fairly priced
  business, not a dislocation.
