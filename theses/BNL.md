## 2. Business

**Created:** BNL buys or builds single-tenant industrial, retail, and
restaurant buildings and leases them back on long triple-net terms. The
weighted lease term is 9.6 years, and new BTS leases run 13.7 years. The
tenant gets the building without owning it: capital stays freed for its
operations, and in build-to-suit it gets a purpose-built facility (a
bakery, cold storage, a 100 MW powered shell) that BNL finances and
develops. The surplus to the tenant is cheaper-than-equity capital plus
execution. BNL's surplus is a bond-like rent stream with escalators: 80.2%
of ABR escalates, weighted 2.1%/yr (FY2025 10-K).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (a) The *spread*: the acquisition
or development cap rate (BTS 7.9% cash / 9.9% straight-line, Q2 call) minus
the blended cost of debt (4.12% with swaps) and equity (the AFFO yield). (b)
*Escalators* on in-place leases, giving 2.2% same-store growth and 3.3% in
industrial (Q2 call). (c) *Capital recycling*: selling non-core assets at
6.2–6.4% cap rates and redeploying into ~8% BTS (Q2 call). Tenants pay
taxes, insurance, and maintenance, so 94.9% of revenue is gross margin
(stocks.db). The 62 employees (10-K) internally manage $5.9B of assets.

**Protected:** weakly. Net lease is a capital-cost business. Anyone with
cheaper capital (Realty Income, Blackstone NNN funds, private credit) can
outbid on regular-way deals, and management says that market is "heavily
competitive" (Q2 call). The one partial mechanism is the build-to-suit
developer relationships. BNL takes construction-period and entitlement risk
that pure acquirers avoid and earns ~150 bp more for it. That edge is a
skill, not a barrier. A 62-person team can be out-hired, and the BTS
returns depend on BNL's equity cost staying below its development yields.
At $18.25 the AFFO yield (8.5%) roughly equals the 7.9–8.5% initial BTS cash
yields, so new equity-funded growth is no longer accretive on a cash basis.
The August forward ($20.50) locks in the current tranche. **No durable moat:
the protection is the lease contracts themselves, not the business.**

**Control:** one class of common stock (NYSE: BNL), no controlling holder
(FY2025 10-K cover page). There are 8.3M OP units (4.1% of the OP, 10-Q).
Insiders own 1.16% and institutions ~100% (stocks.db). It is a Maryland
REIT. The standard 9.8% ownership limit and any MUTA opt-in were NOT
OBTAINED from the 10-K extract (§6). The REIT ownership cap, if present as
usual, forecloses an unsolicited control bid without board consent.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): flat.** TTM revenue is $476.2M (+7.7% YoY;
3-yr CAGR 2.4%, 5-yr 7.6%). TTM operating income is $238.5M, a 50.1%
margin. Operating income moves one-for-one with rent because nearly all
cost is depreciation ($171.3M TTM) and ~$30M of G&A. Growth comes from
buying rent, not from leverage over fixed cost. GAAP net income ($144.5M)
is depressed by real-estate depreciation. FFO ($304.5M TTM) and AFFO
($296.3M FY2025, $1.49/sh) are the economic lines. AFFO/share grew 4.2% in
2025 and is guided +~4.7% for 2026 ($1.55–1.57). Not loss-making: 42
consecutive profitable quarters (stocks.db). Leverage: net debt $2.65B =
42% of EV, 6.5× EBITDA (5.9× pro forma per management), interest coverage
2.3×. That is moderate for a REIT, below the 50%-of-EV option-lens gate.

**Integrity checks:**

  ($144.5M) the tool warns that FCF ≥ earnings leaves nothing reinvested.
  GAAP is understated by $171M of real-estate depreciation, so I reran on

  reinvestment, plus growth bought with *external* capital, which is not
  free. So: (a) terminal growth is cut to 2.0% (base) and 1.5% (bear),

  five years takes ABR from ~$460M to ~$560M, a rounding error in a

  limited by cost of capital, not by addressable market.

- **Default fade.** Net-lease spreads are the excess return, and they fade
