MakeMyTrip is a good business in a market with a long runway. Bookings grew
20.9% in constant currency through a quarter in which India's airlines flew
7% fewer domestic departures, because hotels (+18.5% room nights), buses
(+24.7% tickets) and ancillaries picked up the demand that high fares pushed
off planes. That resilience is real and it is the bull's best fact. The pass
is about what the owner receives for it in dollars. Reported revenue grew
2.5%, adjusted operating profit 4.5%, and the margin on bookings fell, because
inducements rose 22% and payment-gateway costs scale with fares that revenue
does not. Management says margin expansion resumes "as fares and volume
normalize" — a condition outside its control, with the market still
shrinking. Meanwhile the convert accretes $29.6M a quarter in non-cash
interest toward a $1.43B put in July 2028, and the India IPO that could
re-rate the equity has no date, size or price. At ~$5.0B of enterprise value
the market already assumes the recovery; the arithmetic needs more than a
recovery.

## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/MMYT-2026-09-16.md` §2 in mechanism; the deltas
from the Q2 print are below.

- **Created:** one app for Indian travel spend — flights, 105,000+ domestic
  accommodation options across 2,080 cities, holiday packages, redBus
  (50,000+ bus schedules a day), intercity cabs (growing 40%+ y/y on a
  small base), trains, forex, visas, and corporate travel (myBiz 84,600
  active corporate customers vs 75,500 a year ago; Quest2Travel 568 large
  accounts; myPartner 26,000+ transacting agents). The Q2 story is
  substitution: high fares pushed leisure demand to drive-down, pilgrimage
  and short-haul trips, and the platform captured it through hotels, buses
  and cabs. Myra (the AI assistant) handles 90,000 conversations and
  ~3,000 assisted bookings a day, 55% from Tier II/III cities (Q2 call and
  release).
- **Captured:** three mechanisms with different economics, and the mix is
  moving. **Air** (58% of GBV, 36.8% of business mix): revenue $56.7M on
  $1,577.4M GBV (3.6%); adjusted margin $108.6M before $52.0M of inducements
  (6.9% of GBV, down from 7.2%); margins are "more flattish than completely
  variable," so a 25–30% fare rise lifted GBV without lifting revenue per
  ticket, while payment-gateway costs scale with GBV — the margin squeeze
  management named. **Hotels & packages**: revenue $107.1M on $640.4M GBV
  (16.7%), adjusted margin $116.8M after $50.7M inducements, margin % 18.2%
  — the profit pool. **Bus and others**: bus revenue $39.1M on $435.3M GBV
  (9.0%), adjusted margin % flat at 10.4%; others $32.3M revenue. Total
  inducements $108.9M (+22% y/y, 4.0% of GBV) plus marketing $38.9M; the
  pair is 5.4% of GBV (5.2% a year ago). Adjusted operating profit $46.2M =
  **1.7% of GBV**.
- **Protected:** supply aggregation of fragmented hotel and bus inventory,
  built "partner by partner" and not on the open web — management's own
  framing of why a horizontal AI agent cannot easily replicate it (Q2 call).
  In air there is no structural moat: IndiGo holds 65.4% of domestic
  passengers (DGCA September, low-confidence press) and the 20-F names
  airline direct-booking and loyalty initiatives, non-exclusive supplier
  agreements terminable on 30–90 days' notice, and the domestic "duopoly" as
  risks (20-F FY26 Item 3.D). Share there rests on brand, habit and the
  inducement budget. Terminal read: excess returns fading.
- **Control:** no controlling holder. Trip.com holds 100% of the Class B
  shares and 12.14% of ordinary shares, **17.08% of voting rights** as of
  2026-03-31 (20-F FY26 Item 3), with board nominees and a non-compete on
  Indian travel investments while it holds ≥10%. The 2028 and 2030 notes
  carry fundamental-change puts and make-whole conversion adjustments that
  the 20-F itself says "could make it more difficult or more expensive for a
