## 2. Business

**Created:** Tetra Tech sells the front end of water, environmental and
infrastructure projects — the science, planning, permitting, treatment
design and increasingly digital automation and cybersecurity of the
systems — to ~500 US municipal water utilities, US federal defense and
civilian agencies (USACE, EPA, FAA, DoD), UK/Irish/Dutch water companies in
regulated investment cycles, Canadian hydro and infrastructure owners,
Australian water and mining clients, and US commercial energy, mining and
data-center developers. The customer gets a bespoke answer to a
site-specific problem (a PFAS treatment train for Dayton, sediment
remediation, a hydropower dam modernization) from a firm with the
regulatory and hydrological record to be trusted with it. "Leading with
Science" is the positioning: high-end applied science rather than
downstream commodity design (CEO, Q3 call). 25,000 employees, 100,000+
projects in 100+ countries (10-K).

**Captured:** three mechanisms, all labour-based. (i) Time-and-materials
billing at negotiated hourly rates — 42.6% of FY25 revenue. (ii)
Fixed-price contracts — 43.5% and rising toward a stated 60% (RBC note,
low-confidence): the firm keeps the productivity gain from its own tools.
(iii) Cost-plus — 13.9%, shrinking to 8.8% of 9M FY26 revenue with USAID
gone. Subcontractor pass-through is ~15% of gross revenue and earns nothing
(gross $5.44B vs net $4.62B in FY25); every margin in this document is on
net revenue, which is also what stockanalysis reports as "revenue". Segment
margins on net revenue: GSG 16.8% (9M FY26, +160bp), CIG ~13.5%.
Acquisitions add capabilities and clients at ~1× revenue (Halvik: 600
employees, federal data analytics/cyber; Providence: Australian advisory;
RPS $854M in FY23).

**Protected:** thinly, and the 10-K says so — "the technical and
professional aspects of some of our services generally do not require
large upfront capital expenditures and provide limited barriers against
new competitors." What protects the revenue is incumbency and record, not
structure: multi-year, multiple-award federal contract vehicles (USACE
districts "where we have worked for decades"), framework contracts in UK
water, a 500-utility municipal base whose regulators and engineers already
know the firm, and the reputational cost to a client of switching designer
mid-programme. DSO of 56 days and 21 years of OCF > NI are the evidence
that clients pay on time and come back. The fragmented market (many
regional and specialist competitors, a few larger — Jacobs, AECOM, Stantec,
WSP) means share is won project by project. This is a people business with
a good record, and its moat is the record.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, 750M
authorized, 256.0M outstanding, no controlling holder. Vanguard 10.1%,
BlackRock 9.7% (proxy, 2026-01-09); Pictet Asset Management 4.9% on a
Schedule 13D/A (2025-10-17) framed as stewardship engagement with no
control intent. Insiders 0.51%. Founder-era CEO Dan Batrack became
Executive Chairman 2026-02-19 (minimum two years); Roger Argus, 30-year
company veteran, is CEO. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist; the
$575M convertible has a fundamental-change put at par.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Net revenue / adjusted
operating income ($M, stockanalysis; FY25 excludes the $92.4M goodwill
impairment and $115M legal charge that GAAP carries): FY21 2,552 / 275
(10.8%) · FY22 2,836 / 341 (12.0%) · FY23 3,751 / 420 (11.2%) · FY24 4,322
/ 512 (11.9%) · FY25 4,617 / 606 (13.1%) · TTM 4,359 / 597 (13.7%). Net
revenue +71% FY21→TTM, operating income +117%; the TTM revenue decline
(−5.6%, USAID) came with a margin increase, so the direction holds through
the shock.
