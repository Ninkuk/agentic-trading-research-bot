## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $53.69** (official close, 2026-08-25). kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** —
conditions=5, refuted=0, unknown=1.

EuroDry owns eleven second-hand drybulk carriers averaging 13.8 years old,
manages none of them itself (Pittas-family affiliates do, for a fee that
scales with fleet size), and sells a commodity it has no influence over the
price of. The trailing numbers are the best in four years because charter
rates are, not because anything about the business changed. Since the Aug 6
Q2 print the stock has risen roughly 80% on no company-specific news
whatsoever, closing tonight at $53.69 — above the consensus analyst target
of $45.33, above the $40 target Alliance Global *raised to* on Aug 18, and
within 0.4% of the 52-week high. At that price the market implies a 10–11%
annual return only if today's rates persist or improve; on a mid-cycle
charter rate taken from management's own slide, it implies 6%, which is
173bp *below* an 8% hurdle that is itself generous. Four newbuildings are on
order whose remaining installments are not disclosed anywhere public and
which triangulate to roughly $125–140M against a $154M market cap.

**Closest attack:** the cheap-cyclical inversion — shipping looks expensive
on trough earnings and cheap on peak earnings, so a "the multiple is on peak
earnings" pass is the classic way to miss the whole up-leg. It forced a real
correction: this is **not** the 2021 peak. FY2021 operating margin was 60.4%
on $64.44M of revenue; TTM is 26.7% on $62.27M. And on the NAV frame the
stock trades at **0.88× management's own $60.81/share estimate** — a
discount, not a premium. The pass rests on the DCF frame and on the
undisclosed capex, not on the NAV frame, which does not confirm it.

Load-bearing conditions for the pass — **five (three probable, two
plausible)**:

1. *probable* — **The trailing multiple is struck on above-mid-cycle

3. *plausible* — **The newbuild commitment is large and externally

4. *plausible* — **The NAV anchor is struck on peak asset values.**

## 2. Business

**Created:** EuroDry moves bulk commodities — iron ore, coal, grain, bauxite
— from where they are mined or grown to where they are consumed, in eleven
hulls totalling 766,420 dwt. What the charterer gets is a certified,
vetted, insured ship at a berth on a date. There is nothing else. A
charterer choosing between EuroDry's 2015-built Ultramax and any other
2015-built Ultramax is choosing on price, position and vetting record. The
customer surplus that usually signals a good business is absent by
construction: the product is fungible steel and the price is set on an
exchange.

**Captured:** three mechanisms, none proprietary. (i) **Fixed time
charters** — a daily hire rate for one to three months, currently $12,500 to
$30,000/day across the fleet; the owner keeps hire less operating cost and
the charterer pays fuel and port charges. (ii) **Index-linked charters** —
four vessels (Yannis Pittas, Maria, Good Heart at 115% of the Baltic
Supramax S10TC index, Molyvos Luck at 101%) earn a *multiple of the index*,
which is a levered bet on the index rather than a hedge against it. (iii)
**Forward freight agreements** — two 90-day Kamsarmax contracts sold for
Q3'26 at $17,250 and $17,100/day, which cap upside on the covered days;
these cost $436,570 of unrealized and $91,500 of realized losses in H1'26 as
rates rose. Value flows *out* through a fourth mechanism: €875/vessel/day to
Eurobulk plus 1.25% of chartering revenue to Eurochart, both Pittas-family
entities.

**Protected:** nothing protects it. There is no moat and no mechanism is
claimed. Anyone with $35M can buy the same ship from the same yard; anyone
with a phone can fix it through the same brokers. The only operating
differentiator visible is cost — $6,608/vessel/day of opex including
management fees in Q2'26 — and that is ordinary for the class, not
advantaged. Scale does not help: EuroDry's 766,420 dwt is about **0.07% of
the ~1.1 billion dwt world drybulk fleet**. The silence here is the finding,
and it is why every condition above is about price and capital allocation
rather than about the business.

**Control:** one class of common stock, par $0.01, 200,000,000 authorized,
2,866,591 outstanding at 6/30/26; no preferred outstanding (the preferred
that paid dividends in FY2020 is gone from the balance sheet). Voting power
is nonetheless concentrated: three affiliated entities — Friends Dry
Investment Company Inc., Family United Navigation Co. and Ergina Shipping
Ltd. — held approximately **46.9% of voting power** as of 2026-03-31.
Aristides J. Pittas chairs both EuroDry and its manager Eurobulk, and
Eurochart is owned by members of the same family. That structure forecloses
an unsolicited takeover and any activist path, and it means the
related-party fee schedule is not renegotiable by minority holders. EuroDry
is also a Marshall Islands foreign private issuer filing 20-F/6-K, so it is
exempt from Section 16 — there are **no Forms 3/4/144** for anyone to read.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative over the full window, positive on
the current up-leg.** Both readings matter and neither substitutes for the
other:

| $M | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 64.44 | 70.18 | 47.59 | 61.08 | 52.26 | 62.27 |
| Operating income | 38.89 | 31.26 | 1.09 | −3.50 | 0.18 | 16.60 |
| Operating margin | 60.4% | 44.5% | 2.3% | −5.7% | 0.3% | 26.7% |

Over the full window revenue is **down 3.4%** (64.44 → 62.27) while
operating income is **down 57%** (38.89 → 16.60): the same revenue now
carries a materially larger cost base, because $103.1M of capex in 2022–23
bought more ships that are earning lower rates. That is negative operating
leverage and it is the more informative direction. Within the current
up-leg the sign flips hard — TTM revenue is $10.01M above FY25 and
operating income $16.42M above it, a 164% incremental margin — which is
exactly the property that makes the down-leg symmetric.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The two post-print re-fixtures came in ~20% lower.** The Aug 6 press
release fleet table and the company's live fleet-employment page agree on
nine of eleven vessels and disagree on exactly two — the 2005-built Santa
Cruz and the 2004-built Starlight — where the live page shows *later*
expiries at *lower* rates: Santa Cruz $19,400 to Sep-26 → **$15,750 to
Oct-26** (−19%), Starlight $19,000 to Aug-26 → **$15,000 to Oct-26** (−21%).
Read as two new fixtures done after the print, that is the first hard
evidence bearing on Condition 2, and it points the wrong way for an owner:
both landed ~17% below the Aug-21 Panamax index of $18,790/day. Caveat, and
it is a real one: the fleet page carries no as-of stamp, so the direction of
the update is inferred from the extended expiries, not stated.

**Management's own 2027 slide does not support management's own 2027
word.** The call describes "a balanced but more uncertain market
environment" for 2027 while presenting scheduled newbuilding deliveries of
**4.5% of the fleet** against Braemar ton-mile growth of **1.8%**. Balance
requires roughly 2.7% of the fleet to be scrapped. 11.8% of the fleet is
over 20 years old so the capacity to scrap exists — but scrapping is
rate-elastic and does not happen while rates are good, which is the trap:
the condition that clears 2027 is the same condition that would already have
hurt 2027. The orderbook at 14.4% is genuinely low against 66% in 2008 and
24% in 2014, and that is the strongest thing in the bull case; it does not
change the 2027 arithmetic.

**Newbuilds are being ordered at prices management calls a peak.** On the
call management explained the four newbuildings as "a deliberate decision to
pursue investments in newbuild vessels rather than acquire secondhand
tonnage at market peak levels" — while in the same call marking the fleet's
NAV at $240M on secondhand values it described as 56% above the historical
median, and citing third-party newbuild costs that market sources put at
~$33.5–36M for a 63,500 dwt Ultramax and ~$36–40M for an 82,000 dwt
Kamsarmax. If secondhand is at peak levels then so is the NAV being quoted
as upside, and so are the newbuild contracts. The three claims cannot all
be load-bearing at once.

**The fee structure rewards the fleet, not the return.** The manager is paid
per vessel per day. Taking the fleet from 11 to 15 hulls raises management
fees ~36% whether or not those hulls clear their cost of capital — and
EuroDry's ROIC at the best earnings in four years is **5.09%**, below any
defensible hurdle. Any thesis that the newbuild program creates per-share
value is quietly assuming management acts against its own fee incentive,
with 46.9% of the vote in the same hands.

**Capital destruction is measurable, not alleged.** Cumulative net income
FY23–FY25 was **−$19.78M** (−2.91, −12.61, −4.26) across three years in
which the company also spent $81.4M of capex (FY23 $65.3M, FY24 $8.73M,
FY25 $7.36M) and sold M/V Tasos for demolition. Damodaran's EVA dataset puts
the base rate for earning above cost of capital at ~29% of firms; a
price-taking commodity shipper with 5.09% ROIC at cycle-good earnings is a
textbook member of the other 71%.

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs and pairing.** Levered TTM free cash flow against **market cap**,
net debt 0 by the pairing rule. Consolidated TTM `fcf` is $17,285,360
(`ncfo` $24,860,149 + `capex` −$7,574,789). Two haircuts before pairing:
**minority interest** — EuroDry owns 61% of the entities holding M/V
Christos K and M/V Maria, with NRP Project Finance AS holding 39%; TTM
minority interest was $1,325,687 of $10,679,329 pretax, so **12.4%** comes
off — and **SBC** of $780,207, which `ncfo` adds back but the owner does not
keep. Owner FCF = 17,285,360 × 0.876 − 780,207 = **$14,361,768** against a
market cap of **$153,931,000** (2,866,591 shares × $53.69).

The mid-cycle base is derived, not asserted, from management's own disclosed
breakevens: EBITDA = (TCE − $8,458) × 11 vessels × 365 days, which at H1'26's
$17,452/day predicts $36.1M against H1-annualized adjusted EBITDA of $33.2M,
so the formula runs ~9% hot and takes a 9% haircut. At management's quoted
historical median one-year Panamax rate of **$13,450/day** that gives $18.3M
of EBITDA, less $5.63M of cash interest ($98.1M at the disclosed 5.74%
all-in cost) and ~$0.5M of vessel improvements = $12.2M, less the same 12.4%
NCI and $0.78M of SBC = **$9.9M**.

**Hurdle:** rf 4.74% + beta 0.80 × ERP 4.28% = **8.16%** (Damodaran, as of
2026-08-01). The reported 5-year beta is **0.64**; it is floored into the
0.8–1.2 stable band and the floor is stated, because a 0.64 beta on a
leveraged microcap drybulk owner is an artifact of thin-float, low-
correlation trading, not a statement that this is less risky than the
market. The absolute companion (a mature company's cost of capital ≈ rf +
4.5% = 9.24%) says 8.16% is if anything **too low** for this name — so every
scenario below that fails the hurdle fails a realistic hurdle by more.

| scenario | base FCF | growth ×5y | terminal | implied return | vs hurdle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Management's 2026 EBITDA scenario ($38.4M) → owner FCF | $26.20M | 0% | 0% | 17% | +886bp |
| TTM + newbuild fleet growth (capex **not** charged) | $14.36M | 6/6/6/3/3% | 0% | 11% | +323bp |

| **Mid-cycle TCE $13,450/day** | $9.90M | 0% | 0% | **6%** | **−173bp** |

Returns are quoted to the nearest whole percent. The IV precision rule keys
off an ATM implied vol this name does not have (no listed options), but its
realized vol plainly clears the 50% line — a 52-week range of $10.52 to
$53.93 — so the same discipline applies: these are ranges, not readings.

**Integrity checks.**

- **The reinvestment warning is answered by cutting terminal growth to
  zero, not by ignoring it.** The tool flagged `growth without reinvestment`
  on every run where terminal growth was positive, and it is right to: TTM
  depreciation of $11,779,055 exceeds TTM capex of $7,574,789, so this FCF
  base is being earned by under-replacing a fleet that averages 13.8 years.
  Ships wear out. Real terminal growth for a shipowner requires vessel
  replacement, and vessel replacement is precisely the spend sitting off
  this cash-flow statement in the newbuild commitment. The zero-terminal-
  growth rows are the honest ones; `implied_terminal_roe` is 0 there by
  construction and carries no information.
- **Fleet growth is not free, and one row charges nothing for it.** The
  6/6/6/3/3% path assumes the four newbuildings arrive and earn, while the
  base FCF pays none of their ~$130M of remaining installments — the same
  error as crediting a serial acquirer's M&A growth without charging the
  M&A. That row is shown to bound the bull case, not to support it. Charging
  the commitment honestly is impossible without the installment schedule,
  which is UNKNOWN (§6.1).
- **Market-share sentence.** There is no market-share constraint and that is
  the point. 766,420 dwt is ~0.07% of the ~1.1bn dwt world fleet;
  compounding the fastest growth path to 2031 takes revenue to ~$79M and the
  fleet to 15 vessels, still under 0.1% of a world fleet growing 3.3%/yr.
  Nothing here is "bigger than the market" — the binding constraint is
  price, and EuroDry has exactly zero influence over it.
- **Base-year cash tax rate.** Effectively 0%, and structurally so: EuroDry
  is a Marshall Islands entity and its TTM income statement carries no tax
  line ($10,679,329 pretax flows to $9,353,642 after minority interest
  alone). This is a permanent feature of the shipping tax regime, **not** an
  NOL or a deferral that unwinds, so no haircut is applied and none is
  needed. Flagging it as a red flag would be wrong.
- **Distribution clamp.** The mid-cycle and replacement-adjusted rows (6%,
  8%) sit at or below the US median cost of capital of 7.79% and inside the
  5.26–9.88% 80% band — i.e. the market is pricing this cyclical,
  externally-managed, leveraged microcap as though it were an average-risk
  firm. That is the clamp failing in the direction that matters.
- **Altman Z of 1.06 is not treated as distress, and the leverage gate does
  not trip.** Net debt of $71.8M is 32% of a $225.7M enterprise value —
  below the ~50% line — book equity is positive at $100.09M common, and
  there is no going-concern language. The Z-score reads distressed because
  its asset-turnover term (revenue/assets = 0.29) is structurally low for
  any shipowner, which is a known false positive for the asset class. The
  DCF frame therefore governs §1's ownership call; the equity-as-option lens
  is omitted, as the template directs at normal leverage. Piotroski F of 8
  is noted and is a cyclical-recovery artifact, not evidence of quality.
- **The NAV frame, stated because it is the co-equal frame here and it does
  not agree.** At $53.69 against management's ">$60.81" estimate the stock
  trades at **0.88× NAV** — a 12% discount. Reconstructed: common equity
  $100.09M + the $80M excess of management's ~$240M fleet valuation over
  $160.19M of book = $180.1M, or ~$62.8/share. This frame does not support
  the pass; it is neutral-to-supportive of ownership. What it *is* sensitive
  to: a 20% haircut to vessel values takes NAV to ~$46/share (14% below
  spot); a reversion to management's own $19.5M historical median for a
  10-year-old Panamax (−36%) takes it to roughly **$33/share**. The pass
  rests on the DCF frame and on §6.1, not on this one.

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy):**

- **Shift —** Q4'26 and 2027 charter cover gets fixed at or above the Q2'26
  TCE of $20,398/day. That would convert Condition 1 from "above mid-cycle"
  to "the new run-rate is contracted," and the mid-cycle DCF row stops being
  the governing one.
- **Shift —** the company discloses the newbuild contract prices and
  installment schedule and they land materially below the ~$145M triangulated
  here, or the yards' terms are delivery-weighted with committed bank
  financing at the current ~5.7%. §6.1 closes and Condition 3 dissolves.
- **Shift —** a price retracement toward the reconstructed NAV floor. At
  ~$33–46/share the vessel-value haircuts above are already paid for and the
  DCF's mid-cycle row clears the hurdle.
- **Break (for the pass) —** the buyback restarts at these prices with size.
  Management repurchased 358,130 shares for $5.8M since Aug 2022, an average
  of ~$16.20; buying at $53.69 would be an informed insider marking the
  asset far above where I do.

**For an owner (sell):**

- **Break —** an equity issuance to fund the newbuildings. A 2.87M-share
  company cannot fund ~$130M from $25.6M of unrestricted cash and $14.4M of
  annual owner FCF while also paying $22.0M of debt amortization over the
  next twelve months. Dilution at any price below NAV ends the per-share
  story.
- **Break —** TCE fixtures at or below the all-in cash-flow breakeven of
  $12,872/day. Below that line the company funds debt service from the
  balance sheet, and $21.7M of the debt is current.
- **Shift —** further re-fixtures at the Santa Cruz/Starlight discount
  (~17% below index). Two is an observation; four is a trend in how this
  fleet's age profile prices.
- **Shift —** a vessel-value print (an S&P transaction, a broker valuation
  in the next 6-K) materially below the $240M fleet mark. NAV is ~100%
  levered to this input.
- **Shift —** the 2027 orderbook/ton-mile gap widening, or scrapping running
  below the ~2.7% of fleet that 2027 balance requires.

## Kill-thesis record

**Ledger:** UNPROVEN — conditions=5, refuted=0, unknown=1.

**Per-condition adjudication.**

1. *Above-mid-cycle earnings* — **SURVIVED, corrected.** The attack was the
   cheap-cyclical inversion, and it forced a genuine correction: the draft
   said "peak," which the evidence does not support. FY2021 delivered a
   60.4% operating margin on $64.44M of revenue against TTM's 26.7% on
   $62.27M, so this is well below the last peak. The condition survives in
   its corrected form — H1'26 TCE $17,452/day vs the $13,450/day historical
   median management quoted — but "peak" was refuted and had to be rewritten.
2. *Immediate re-chartering exposure* — **SURVIVED.** Primary and
   unambiguous: Q4'26 ~6% fixed, every charter but one expiring Aug–Nov 2026,
   four vessels already floating on the index. Corroborated by the two
   post-print Panamax re-fixtures ~20% below their expiring rates.
3. *Large, externally-funded newbuild commitment* — **UNKNOWN.** Existence
   and scale are primary (four hulls, 291,000 dwt, $14,395,854 of advances,
   management's statement that disclosed debt excludes newbuild financing).
   The dollar figure is not disclosed anywhere reachable and the estimate is
   a triangulation. This is the single condition that could not be attacked
   because the evidence does not exist, and it is what makes the verdict
   UNPROVEN rather than SOUND.
4. *NAV struck on peak asset values* — **SURVIVED, strained.** Management's
   own $30.5M vs $19.5M median is evidence for it. But it does not carry the
   pass: at 0.88× NAV the frame is a discount, and a 20% value haircut still
   only puts NAV 14% below spot. Conceded in §4 rather than argued away.
5. *Fee structure takes a standing cut and scales with fleet size* —
   **SURVIVED, and partly neutralised.** The arithmetic stands (H1'26 fees
   $2.08M vs $6.84M owner net income; fee raised 2.9% on 1 Jan 2026; 46.9%
   voting bloc). But the strongest counter landed: the fees are already
   inside every cash-flow figure used in §4, so counting them again as an
   incremental negative would be double-counting. They matter to the NAV
   frame (an externally-managed vehicle deserves a discount) and to the
   incentive read on the newbuild program, not to the DCF.

**Closest attack:** the cheap-cyclical inversion — that a pass grounded in
