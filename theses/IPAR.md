## 2. Business

**Created.** Prestige fragrances under rented fashion-house names (Jimmy Choo,
Coach, Montblanc, GUESS, Lacoste, DKNY, Ferragamo — 77% of 2025 sales) sold in
120+ countries. The customer buys an affordable entry point into a luxury
brand (~$100 vs a $2,000 handbag); the *licensor* gets a competent fragrance
operator without building one — IPAR's actual customer is as much the brand
owner as the consumer. Fragrance was again the fastest-growing US beauty
category in H1'26 (Q2'26 call).

**Captured.** Wholesale margin on juice it designs and outsources: 58.9% gross
margin, then royalties (8.2% of sales, creeping up from 7.9% in 2023) and A&P
(19.8% of sales, guided toward a 21% long-term target) out of SG&A, leaving a
17-19% operating margin. Two segments: 72%-owned, Paris-listed Interparfums SA
(Europe-based ops, 68% of 2025 sales) and wholly owned US ops (32%). Capex
~0.7% of sales — the balance sheet is inventory and receivables, not plants.

**Protected.** Contracts, not physics: the license *terms* are the moat, and
the current book is long — Coach 6/2031, Montblanc 12/2030, Jimmy Choo
12/2031, Ferragamo 2031+5, DKNY 2032+5, Van Cleef 2033, Lacoste 2038,
Longchamp 2036, GUESS 2048, Nautica 2049, Beckham 2047 (FY25 10-K license
table). The renewal track record is the real evidence: Coach renewed 5 years
early (Mar 2025), GUESS extended 15 years eight years before expiry (Dec
2025), Van Cleef +9 years. Licensors keep choosing IPAR because a fragrance
business is cheap for IPAR to run and expensive for a fashion house to build.
What the moat does NOT stop: a licensor with scale taking the brand in-house
at expiry (Burberry did exactly this to IPAR in 2012). Structural mitigation
underway: owned brands (Rochas, Lanvin, Off-White, Goutal, Solférino) — but
they are small today.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Op margin 19.2% FY24 → 17.3% TTM
while guided 2026 EPS declines to $4.85 from $5.24 (see §1 and §3 for the
margin-bridge detail).

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The 2030–31 renewal wall (the crux).** Montblanc (15% of sales) expires
  12/2030; Coach (15%), Jimmy Choo (17%), Ferragamo (4%) in 2031. ~51% of 2025
  revenue renews inside 18 months of itself. History says renewals land, but
  royalty expense as % of sales has crept 7.9% → 8.1% → 8.2% (2023-25, 10-K
  Note 11) and the CFO attributes SG&A pressure to "royalty costs growing
  ahead of sales" — renewal pricing power sits with the licensor. Not a 2026
  event, but it is exactly what a 2% terminal growth assumption has to
  survive.
- **Q2'26 print and the downgrade cluster.** Q2 EPS $0.95 vs $1.01 consensus
  (miss); H1 op margin 17.9% vs 20.0% prior year; FY26 guide affirmed at
  ~$1.48B / $4.85 EPS — a down-EPS year, and the guide leans on $17.6M of
  one-time IEEPA tariff refunds (~110 bps of the guided ~150 bps FY
  gross-margin improvement). Since the print: Goldman Buy→Neutral (8/10), TD
  Cowen Buy→Hold (8/10), Berenberg Buy→Hold (8/16). Stock −11% from the 7/28
  high ($129.29), still +36% YTD. The downgrades follow the run, not new
  facts.
- **Is the margin compression spend or decay?** H1 organic +1% (ex-Middle East
  +4% Q2); the compression is concentrated in A&P (+ royalties + logistics),
  and management frames A&P at ~21% of sales as reinvestment of the tariff
  refunds ahead of 2027 launches. The distinction matters: A&P is
  discretionary; royalty creep is contractual. Both are real, only one comes
  back.
- **2027 blockbuster cycle.** All four $100M+ brands (Montblanc, Coach, GUESS,
  Jimmy Choo) get full new pillars in 2027 — management says "quite unusual,"
  expects high-single to low-double-digit growth in such years, continuing
  2028, plus first Longchamp and Off-White launches (call, CEO). This is the
  bull case, and at 21.9× trailing / 23.4× forward it is already in the price.
- **Working-capital release is flattering TTM FCF.** TTM NCFO $256M vs FY25
  $215M, FY24 $188M, FY23 $106M; inventory −12% YoY (−34 days to 269), AR −3%.
  TTM FCF $245.9M vs net income to common $167.8M. Management calls the
  inventory efficiency structural; even so, a WC release is one-time in level
  terms. Valuation runs on both bases (§4).
- **Minority interest is not decoration.** Interparfums SA is 72% owned; NCI
  income is $37.4M of $205.1M consolidated TTM (18.2%). Consolidated FCF
  paired with IPAR's market cap overstates the yield; both DCF bases in §4
  haircut for it.
- **Capital allocation change.** First-ever share repurchase authorization
  (either IPAR or Interparfums SA shares) plus a new $250M credit line,
  announced with Q2 (10-Q/call). Board signaling the stock matters; measured
  framing, no committed amount. Dividend $3.20/yr (2.8% yield, 61% payout),
  25 straight years of payment.
- **Insider filings.** No Form 4 activity since 2026-04-03 (EDGAR) — silence
  around the print, neither confirming nor alarming. Insiders hold 43.6%
  (float 18.0M of 32.0M shares).
- **Options read (mandatory):** path 2 (Robinhood stopgap — IPAR not in the
  CBOE catalog, no options.db history); see §4's implied-move table and
  liquidity-gate verdict.
- **Dead ends.** Macy's concentration (10% of sales) — real but shrinking with
  channel shift to Amazon/TikTok; no current stress. Tariffs — Section 301
  rates "largely in line" with what they already operated under (CEO), $8.2M
  net H1 expense against refunds received; not thesis-moving. Earnings-pattern
  check (Robinhood, 8 quarters): beats in 6, the two misses both June quarters
