## 2. Business

**Created:** trucking freight runs on 30–60-day broker payment terms that
small carriers can't finance. Factoring (Triumph Business Capital) buys the
carrier's invoice and pays same-day. TriumphPay sits between brokers and
carriers/factors: it audits, pays and reconciles invoices. Brokers use it
to outsource payables and get fraud control; carriers and factors get
faster, verified payment. LoadPay is a free carrier wallet/bank account
that keeps the carrier's cash inside the network. A small bank (TBK Bank)
supplies deposits and a balance sheet (Q2 2026 letter, 8-K).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (a) Factoring discount: 1.29% of
purchased volume ($4.1B in Q2) — spread income that scales directly with
invoice price. (b) Payments fees ($11.2M in Q2) plus net interest on
average float ($560.9M) — the float income falls when rates are cut.
(c) LoadPay interchange/fees and deposits ($195.1M funding). (d) Bank net
interest margin on ordinary loans and deposits. Intelligence (Greenscreens,
Isometric) is a $2.4M/quarter data product at ~85% gross margin and is
immaterial so far.

**Protected:** the network. $13.5B in Q2 payments ($49.4B annualized in
June) against a brokered freight market management puts at $135B. Brokers
integrate TriumphPay into their payables workflow, and factors join
because it verifies invoices across brokers. That two-sided reach is a
mechanism, not just a label — a rival would need both brokers and factors
to switch at once. Evidence it binds: network engagement at 65.6% (Q2
letter). Evidence it doesn't fully bind: engagement slipped 0.5pt QoQ. On
the Q2 call management deflected the question about Amazon Freight's
expansion. And factoring itself is a commodity service with no moat beyond
scale and underwriting data.

**Control:** one class of common (NYSE: TFIN) plus non-voting 7.125%
Series C preferred depositary shares (10-K cover). No controlling holder;
insiders 5.0%, institutions 92.6% (stocks.db). As a bank holding company,
any acquirer of a control stake needs Federal Reserve approval. That slows
an unsolicited takeover but does not foreclose one. The activist path is
open.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative across the cycle, turning
positive since 2024.** Revenue here is net interest income plus
noninterest income; pretax income stands in for operating income (a bank
reports no operating-income line). EDGAR XBRL, FY:

| 2019 | 287.5 | 75.4 |
| 2020 | 345.1 | 84.7 |
| 2021 | 423.6 | 145.0 |
| 2022 | 484.6 | 137.0 |
| 2023 | 418.3 | 52.8 |
| 2024 | 415.9 | 20.5 |
| 2025 | 439.0 | 33.0 |
| H1 2026 | 226.0 | 24.0 |

Revenue +53% from 2019 to 2025 while pretax income fell 56%. That reflects
the TriumphPay/LoadPay build-out plus the 2022–25 freight recession.
H1 2026 annualizes to ~$452M revenue and ~$48M pretax — improving, but at
a third of 2021's level.
