## 2. Business

**Created:** Keystone moves Alberta heavy crude from Hardisty to Wood River
and Patoka (IL), Cushing (OK) and the US Gulf Coast. Q2 2026 throughput was
596 kbbl/d, and 800 kbbl/d on the Gulf Coast segment. Oil-sands producers
need a reliable, long-lived export path to US heavy-crude refiners, and a
buried 36-inch pipe is the cheapest one there is. Rail is the alternative.
Shippers get assured egress, which is worth a lot when pipeline capacity is
short and wide differentials cut producer netbacks.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms.
- (a) Fixed take-or-pay toll on 585 kbbl/d committed capacity, recovering
  the initial capital over the contract.
- (b) A variable toll passing operating, maintenance and admin cost through
  to shippers.
- (c) Uncommitted/spot capacity (6% of the system reserved) priced off the
  Hardisty–Cushing–Gulf differential. This is where H1 2026 upside came
  from, and it fades as Cushing inventories fall: Q3 guided ~10% below Q2's
  $280M.
- (d) Marketing (buys and sells crude to use capacity; $2M of Q2 EBITDA) and
  Intra-Alberta laterals such as Grand Rapids and Blackrod ($19M).

Keystone segment EBITDA was $259M of $280M in Q2 (Q2 2026 release).

**Protected:** permits and physical incumbency. Keystone XL's 2021 permit
revocation is the proof that a new cross-border crude line is close to
unbuildable. Management calls "permit durability" the key condition before
committing to Prairie Connector. Protection is real while the asset has
customers. It weakens as initial-capital recovery completes: at renewal a
shipper negotiates against a depreciated asset, and the AIF says renewal
rates are "determined during renegotiation". TMX and Enbridge are named
competitors for egress (AIF).

**Control:** one class of common shares, one vote each. No controlling
holder: TC Energy distributed all shares pro rata at the spin. Insiders
0.19%, institutions 64.1% (stockanalysis). A shareholder rights plan
triggers at 20% and must be reconfirmed at the 2027 AGM. It forecloses a
creeping or unsolicited takeover without board negotiation. It does not
foreclose an activist.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): flat.** Revenue is flat and operating
margin creeps up (stockanalysis income statement; revenue includes Marketing
pass-through):

| year | revenue | op income | op margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $2,005M | $700M | 34.9% |
| 2024 | $2,120M | $774M | 36.5% |
| 2025 | $1,986M | $724M | 36.5% |
| TTM Jun-2026 | $2,001M | $750M | 37.5% |

Phase 0 gates: profitable (NI TTM $460M). Leverage is heavy but not a kill:
net debt/normalized EBITDA 4.4x (hybrids at 50% equity), gross debt/EBITDA
5.67x, interest coverage 2.24x, Altman Z 0.91. The equity-option gate does
not fire: net debt is 41% of EV, book equity is positive ($2.67B), and there
is no going-concern language.
