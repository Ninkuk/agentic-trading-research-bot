## 2. Business

**Created:** a hot pizza at a known price in about 30 minutes, ordered in
under a minute on a phone. The customer gets speed and price certainty
("$6.99 Mix & Match", "Best Deal Ever") plus a delivery network the
independent down the street cannot fund. In carryout the value is pure price
and proximity — 7,231 U.S. stores, the densest pizza footprint in the
country, and management says a new store's carryout is ~80% incremental.
The surplus is real at the low end: in Q2'26 QSR order counts were flat and
Domino's grew orders in both delivery and carryout (management, Q2'26 call).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, from the Q2'26 revenue table
($1,194M):

- *Royalties and fees* — U.S. $164M + international $82M (21% of revenue,
  near-100% margin): a percentage of franchisee retail sales, so Domino's
  earns on menu-price inflation without reinvesting.
- *Supply chain* — $732M (61% of revenue, 12.0% gross margin): the company
  makes the dough and sells the food basket to every U.S. and Canadian
  store at a price it sets (+2.2% YoY in Q2'26). The profit-sharing
  arrangement with franchisees ties them to it; scale in purchasing is the
  cost advantage management names first.
- *Advertising fund* — $135M pass-through, but it is the mechanism: the
  biggest ad budget in pizza is franchisee money aggregated by Domino's.
- *Company-owned stores* — $82M, shrinking by design (77 stores
  refranchised in Q2'26; 186 remain of 22,531).

Digital is 85%+ of U.S. sales; aggregators (Uber, DoorDash) are priced at a
premium so the franchisee is profit-neutral, with management claiming ~50%
incrementality.

**Protected:** scale in three places that compound — purchasing (lowest
market-basket cost), advertising (largest budget), and store density (30-min
delivery radius, carryout proximity). Each is a cost the next entrant must
match before it earns a dollar, and the ad budget grows with the sales it
drives. The honest limit: the moat is against *chains*, not against the
independent pizzeria offering dine-in, and it does not extend to the
aggregator channel, where Domino's is one tile among many and the
platform owns the customer relationship. DPC Dash's H1'26 (own-channel sales
−11.8%, aggregator sales +81%) is what that looks like when the platform
wins.

**Control:** one class of common stock, 33.1M shares, no controlling holder.
Largest holders per the 2026 proxy: Vanguard 3.88M (11.7%), BlackRock 2.27M
(6.8%). Insiders 0.5%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist. A
securitization structure that pledges all royalty income and U.S.
supply-chain income to noteholders is the practical constraint on any
acquirer — the debt travels with the cash flows.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue FY21 $4.36B → TTM $5.03B
(+15%); operating income $780M → $969M (+24%); operating margin 17.9% →
19.3%. One down year (FY22, op income $747M on food-cost inflation), then
four up. Net income $510M → $597M (+17%, slower than op income because
interest is fixed at ~$198M on a shrinking base of pre-tax income growth).

## 5. Falsifiers

For the pass (flip toward buy):

- **Shift —** Q3'26 (2026-10-13) U.S. same-store sales ≥ +2.0% with
  management stating order counts still positive and the FY26 operating-
  income guide (mid-to-high single digits ex FX) held: condition 3 moves
  from plausible to probable and scenario B is the base case at a price

- **Shift —** a 2027 refinancing announced at or below 5.0% all-in on the
  $1.32B wall: condition 4 becomes probable and scenario C collapses into B.

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** two consecutive quarters of negative U.S. order counts
  (management's own headline metric) or U.S. net store growth turning
  negative — the flywheel claim is over and the supply-chain margin base
  shrinks with it.
- **Break —** Holdco leverage above 5.0× on Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA
  (principal amortization begins on the 2017–2021 series; a ~15% EBITDA
  decline from $1.10B) or a missed anticipated repayment date.
- **Shift —** aggregator incrementality disclosed below ~50%, or U.S.
  first-party digital sales share falling while aggregator share rises
  faster than total — the China pattern arriving; revalue the supply-chain
  margin and the terminal rate.
- **Shift —** DPE portfolio sale or master-franchise restructuring in its
  12 markets — royalty follows the store; re-underwrite the 800/yr guide.
- **Shift —** T10YIE or DGS10 moving more than 50bp — the terminal ceiling
  and hurdle both move; re-run §4.

## Kill-thesis record

**UNPROVEN** — conditions=6 (3 probable, 3 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=2.
Six legs is a lot of surface for a "fairly priced compounder" story; the
count is said out loud because the pass in §1 rests on the *plausible*
three, and two of them cannot be attacked with anything management has
disclosed.

Per-condition adjudication:

1. *Franchisor/supply-chain economics hold* — **SURVIVED.** Attack:
   internal-consistency — 62% ROIC, near-zero reinvestment, and a moat claim
   made at once. It holds only because the reinvestment is the
   franchisees', and the proxy shows why the franchisor is insulated even
   when they suffer: AIP pays on Incentive Adjusted EBITDA (100% of target
   in 2025) and PSUs on 70% EBITDA growth / 30% global retail sales ex-FX —
   nothing on comps, nothing on franchisee profit. Food-basket pricing
   +2.2% in a flat-comp quarter is EBITDA for Domino's and margin
   compression for the store. The mechanism survives; the incentive tilt
   is the seed of the attack on condition 5.
2. *Chain closures convert to Domino's share* — **SURVIVED, narrowly.**
   Disconfirming search: Technomic has Domino's at 30% of fast-food pizza
   in 2024, up from 26% in 2019, so the share series is not a management
   artifact (low-confidence trade press citing Technomic). But the same
   source reports monthly delivery ordering fell from 61% (2022) to 55%
   (2025) and 61% of pizza chains lost sales in 2024 — the pie Domino's
   wins is shrinking as a channel while independents win dine-in.
   Share of a shrinking channel is not refuted growth, but it is the
   closest attack (below).
3. *U.S. comps return to +2–3% by H1'27* — **UNKNOWN.** The condition is a
   management sentence about a ticket lap. Order counts, the metric it
   rests on, are not disclosed in any filing; the only observable is the
   Q3 print. Not refuted; unverifiable until 2026-10-13.
4. *2027/2028 walls refinance at ~5.2% and are absorbed* — **SURVIVED.**
   Attack: the arithmetic. $1.32B (2027) + $827M (2028) rolling from ~3.9%
   and 2.66% to ~5.2% is ~$41M; at 5.0× Holdco leverage the older series
   start amortizing, but that needs a ~15% EBITDA decline from $1.10B
   against an EBITDA that grew every year but FY22. The 2025 deal priced
   inside 5.3% in a similar rate environment. Survives as plausible, not
   probable, because the coupon is not yet a fact.
5. *International ~800/yr with DPE stabilizing* — **UNKNOWN on the U.S.
   half, SURVIVED on the international half.** International Q2 +183 net
   and DPC Dash +235 stores H1'26 carry the number without DPE. The U.S.
   pipeline leans on franchisee store EBITDA, which is disclosed only in
   an investor deck this session could not fetch; the ~$166K 2025 figure
   is an analyst's citation. Absence does not kill a pass; it blocks a buy.
6. *CEO handover is continuity* — **SURVIVED.** Attack: the CTO left within
   a month of the succession announcement and sold ~$4.0M of stock; the
   lead independent director and two board seats changed in the same
   8-K. Read together they are a board refresh around an internal
   successor, not a rupture; no disagreement disclosed; Weiner stays. The
   base rate for internal succession at a franchisor with a stable
   algorithm is continuity.

Standing checks:

- *Base rate.* ~29% of firms earn above their cost of capital (Damodaran
  EVA dataset); Domino's has for two decades at 60%+ ROIC, and the
  question is persistence, not attainment. The relevant base rate is
  mature-category leaders holding excess returns — high for franchisors
  with scale advantages, which is why the terminal does not fade them. The
  pass does not rest on the base rate; the price already pays for it.
- *Short case (strongest form).* A 4.3×-levered royalty stream on a
  category growing 1–2% whose delivery channel is shrinking (Technomic),
  whose U.S. comps are ~0%, whose franchisees are being squeezed between
  flat sales and +2.2% food-basket inflation while the franchisor's
  incentives pay on EBITDA, facing $2.1B of refinancing at +150bp, a
  first-time CEO, and an aggregator channel that in China cut the
  franchisee's own-channel sales by 12% in six months — at 17× FCF and
  27× EV/earnings. Everything in it is true; what it lacks is a
  catalyst beyond the print, and the option lens says the equity is not
  time premium.
- *Management incentives.* Stated above: EBITDA (70%) and retail sales
  (30%). The pass thesis quietly assumed management would protect
  franchisee profitability at the expense of its own supply-chain
  margin if needed; nothing in the plan pays them to.
- *Disconfirming search.* Ran on the category (independents/dine-in) and
  on franchisee economics (cheese/food basket). Both returned evidence
  against — the channel erosion and the royalty-stack insulation — and
  neither reached refutation.
- *Moat: checkbox or mechanism?* Mechanism — purchasing scale, ad-fund
  scale, store density — but bounded to the delivery/carryout chain
