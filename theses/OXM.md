## 2. Business

**Created:** Customers buy into a resort / "island" lifestyle (Tommy
Bahama), a Palm Beach prints aesthetic (Lilly Pulitzer), boho embroidered
womenswear (Johnny Was), and preppy menswear (Southern Tide, Duck Head).
What they pay for is identity and status, not function. Gross margin is
60.7% TTM, so customers pay well above make-cost for the brand.

**Captured:** Through four mechanisms. (a) Full-price DTC, both stores
and e-commerce, which is the bulk of sales and margin. (b) Tommy Bahama
restaurants and "Marlin Bars" (food & beverage sales +11% in Q2). These
are a marketing expense dressed as a revenue line, and they need
capex-heavy locations. (c) Wholesale to department and specialty stores,
down 14% in Q2 as off-price clearance shrank. (d) Licensing royalties,
which are small. The DTC model carries $575.5M of undiscounted lease
payments (10-Q), so most of the "debt" is store rent.

**Protected:** Brand equity only. That moat is real but perishable and
fashion-dependent. The Lilly episode shows how it fails: a wrong-price-point
season goes straight to comps, and no switching cost holds the customer.
Store footprint and a distribution center are not barriers. No network or
scale mechanism stops a competitor; the protection is taste, re-earned each
season.

**Control:** One share, one vote; no controlling holder. Insiders hold ~4.5%
(`stocks.db`) and institutions ~105% of the float (a short-inflated count).
Nothing forecloses a takeover or activist path; at $366M market cap with
24% short interest, an activist or strategic buyer is a live possibility.
That is option value, not a condition.
