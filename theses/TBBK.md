## 2. Business

**Created:** The Bancorp rents a bank charter, a deposit-insurance wrapper
and a payments back office to companies that want to look like banks without
being one. A neobank, a payroll card issuer or a brokerage needs FDIC-insured
deposit accounts, card issuance on a BIN, ACH and card-network settlement,
BSA/AML and compliance oversight, and regulatory examination cover. Building
that takes a charter application, years, and — in the CEO's own framing on the
Q2 2026 call — "well north of $100 million... hundreds of millions of dollars"
and "multiple years to build a platform that's robust enough." What the
customer gets is speed to market and someone else's regulatory exposure.

**Captured:** four distinct ways, not one.
(i) **Program sponsorship fees** — $106mm TTM, charged on the programs
themselves. (ii) **Payment services** — $23mm TTM, transaction-linked.
(iii) **Sponsored lending fees** — $21mm TTM, from originating consumer credit
for fintech partners who supply credit enhancement. (iv) **The float** — the
largest and least visible: $8.1B of partner deposits, 96% of all bank
deposits, funded at 1.63% and lent at 5.7–8.0% depending on the book (real
estate bridge 7.9%, fleet leasing 8.0%, small business 7.3%, institutional
5.7%), producing a 3.85% net interest margin. Fee income is 29.7% of revenue;
the other 70% is spread earned on money the fintech partners bring. That
asymmetry is the whole risk: a partner leaving takes both the fee and the
funding.

**Protected:** weakly, and the mechanism was tested and failed six weeks ago.
Management's stated moat is switching cost and replication cost — the middle
office "can't be replicated by these programs." The rebuttal is that a partner
does not have to replicate it; it can *buy* it. Chime paid $590M for Stride
Bank, a $5.4B-asset national bank in Enid, Oklahoma, and expects more than
$100M of annual net synergies from lower sponsor-bank fees, broader lending
products and a lower cost of funds. LendingClub bought Radius Bank; SoFi
bought Golden Pacific. The observed pattern is that the moat holds exactly
until a partner is large enough that the sponsorship fee exceeds the cost of a
charter — which makes it not a moat but a graduated tax on customers you are
helping grow out of you. The 40+-partner "diversification" is the wrong
reassurance: it is the big ones that leave.

**Control:** single class of common stock, one share one vote, no controlling
holder — the null answer. Insiders hold 6.12%, institutions 101.92% (above
100% reflecting share-lending double-count against an 8.06% short float).
Nothing is foreclosed: The Bancorp is structurally takeover-able, though bank
M&A requires regulatory approval and a fintech-concentrated deposit base is a
hard asset to underwrite. IPO 2004-02-03.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** TTM revenue $531.902M, up 3.10%;
operating income $312.448M at a 58.74% operating margin; net income $231.944M
at a 43.61% profit margin; diluted EPS $5.306, up 14.14%. Operating income and
EPS are growing several times faster than the top line. But read the
composition before crediting it: revenue growth has collapsed from an 11.74%
five-year CAGR and an 8.56% three-year CAGR to 3.10% trailing, and the
earnings growth above it comes from falling credit provisions (criticized
loans down sharply year over year) plus a buyback retiring ~10% of shares
annually — not from operating cost leverage on a growing revenue base. The
direction is positive; the engine is not the one the phrase usually implies.
I could not print the multi-year revenue/operating-income table: the
statistics probe prints schema rather than values under this session's
sandbox, and the FY2025 10-K exceeded the fetch size limit (§6).

## 4. Valuation

**A bank, valued as a distributing perpetuity.** A reverse DCF on a bank is
usually a category error, because "free cash flow" is not free — regulatory
capital must fund asset growth. The Bancorp is the case where it is honest:
management's stated policy is to return ~100% of net income, and it has done
so, retiring 33% of shares and returning $885mm between 2022 and YTD 2026
while the balance sheet stayed roughly flat. When payout is ~100% and the
balance sheet does not grow, net income *is* the owner's cash flow.

**Inputs.** Levered flow paired with market cap; net debt 0 by the pairing
rule (deposits are the funding of a bank's operating asset, not a claim to
net out, and enterprise value is meaningless for a depository). Base flow =
TTM net income **$231.944M** (stockanalysis; cross-checked against the
composite `sa_fcf_yield` of 11.43%, which implies $233M — the two agree, so
the NCFO-based and earnings-based flows do not diverge materially here).
Market cap **$2,048,721,416** (40,786,809 shares × $50.23). No SBC haircut is
applied and none is owed: the base is net income, in which the $19.89M TTM
share-based comp (3.74% of revenue) is already expensed — haircutting again
would double-count. No minority interests, no pension or litigation haircut
identified. Effective tax rate 25.8% ((312.448 − 231.944)/312.448), *above*
the 21% federal marginal rate, so the base is not flattered by NOLs or
deferrals.

**Hurdle:** rf 4.96% (DGS10, 2026-09-11) + beta 1.18349 × ERP 4.14%
(Damodaran implied ERP, as of 2026-09-01, against his T-bond rate of 4.75%)
= **9.86%**. Beta sits inside the 0.8–1.2 band; no clamp applied.
**Terminal growth 2.37%** = the 10-year breakeven (`T10YIE`, 2026-09-14),
used as the ceiling in every scenario.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal growth.* The solver prints its "growth without
  reinvestment" warning in every run, because base FCF equals base earnings
  by construction at a 100% payout. The warning is answered, not dodged:
  terminal growth is set exactly at the 10-year breakeven, i.e. **zero real
  growth**, which is pure nominal repricing and needs no reinvestment. This
  is also the only internally consistent assumption for a bank distributing
  all of its earnings — a 100%-payout bank cannot grow its balance sheet in
  real terms. `implied_terminal_roe` is not printed because the terminal
  reinvestment rate is 0.
- *Terminal risk from Item 1A.* The FY2025 10-K exceeded the fetch limit and
  its risk factors were not read (§6). The dominant structural terminal risk
  needs no filing to name and has now been realized: fintech partners
  obtaining charters and disintermediating the sponsor bank. The 2.37%
  terminal rate survives it only in the weak sense that it assumes no real
  growth in perpetuity — I am not claiming the franchise compounds forever,
  only that it reprices with inflation.
- *Market-share sentence.* Scenario A compounds net income to ~$375M in five
  years; at the current 43.61% profit margin that implies revenue near $861M
  against $531.9M today, roughly a doubling of GDV from $196B toward ~$390B
  on an unchanged take rate. That is consistent with the 22.5% YoY GDV growth
  just printed, sustained for about 3.5 years — *and* with Chime being fully
  replaced. I did not verify a US debit/prepaid volume denominator this run,
  so I cannot state the end-state as a market share; the doubling itself is
  the check, and scenario A is the one I trust least.
- *Serial acquirer / high SBC / cash-heavy.* None apply. Growth is organic
  (no acquisition spend to charge against the base); SBC is already expensed
  in the net-income base; the cash-heavy bias is not applicable to a bank.
- *Distribution clamp.* The 9.86% hurdle sits at the top edge of Damodaran's
  80% US cost-of-capital band (5.26–9.88%, median 7.79%, Data Update 5,
  2026) — high, but inside it. Implied returns of 9–18% are at or above the
  band's top, so the clamp's strong-pass rule (implied return below the 10th
  percentile) does not fire. The PASS here is not a valuation call.
- *Excess-return base rate.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of
  capital (Damodaran's EVA dataset). The Bancorp's 29.63% ROE is a tail
  observation, and every scenario above except D assumes it does not fully
  revert. The default read — excess returns fade — is the one the price is
  arguing about.
- *The leverage gate does not fire.* Liabilities/equity is 12.07x and
  equity/assets 7.65%, which looks like the Phase 4 gate's territory but is
  not: the liabilities are insured customer deposits funding the operating
  asset of a solvent, profitable, well-capitalized depository, not debt with
  a strike. There is no going-concern language and book equity is positive
  ($705.4M). `equity_option` was deliberately not run; the DCF frame governs
  §1's ownership call.

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy)**

- **Break — Chime is disclosed small.** The Bancorp quantifies Chime's share
  of deposits and of fee income at the Q3 print and it is modest (say deposits
  under ~15% of the $8.1B and program-sponsorship fees under ~25% of $106mm).
  Condition 1 becomes probable and the thesis reverses.
- **Shift — FY2027 is re-based and still clears.** Management publishes a
  post-Chime FY2027 guide at or above ~$6.60 EPS (roughly flat net income
  after buyback), with the Chime run-off explicitly modelled.
- **Shift — the price does the work.** At or below ~$42 even the
  franchise-halves scenario is adequately paid (+45bp over hurdle), and the
  unbounded unknown stops mattering.
- **Shift — a replacement of scale lands.** The Cash App program, or one of
  the "two new sponsored lending programs expected within six months,"
  ramps to a disclosed size that visibly offsets Chime.

**For an owner (sell)**

- **Break — routing starts before the deal closes.** Any disclosure that
  Chime is directing new account originations to Stride ahead of close. The
  MSA permits it, and it converts a 2028 problem into a 2027 one.
- **Break — a second large partner announces a charter path.** The thesis
  that this is a Chime-specific event dies the moment it is a category event.
  LendingClub and SoFi are already precedent; Chime makes three.
- **Break — the FY2027 guide is withdrawn rather than re-based.** Withdrawal
  without a replacement number says management cannot yet size its own
  exposure.
- **Shift — the buyback is cut.** Equity/assets is 7.65%; any deposit-driven
  balance-sheet stress, regulatory objection, or return to consent-order
  status that pauses the $50mm/quarter repurchase removes condition 3, which
  is currently doing most of the per-share compounding.
- **Shift — fintech credit deteriorates.** Fintech net charge-offs were
  $55.0mm Q2-YTD against a 0.04% ex-fintech ratio. A step-up here, or any
  restatement touching the credit-enhancement account flagged in the 2025
  material weakness, reopens the accounting question.
