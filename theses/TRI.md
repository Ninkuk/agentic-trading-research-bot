## 2. Business

**Created:** the reference layer for legal, tax, and compliance work —
Westlaw's citator/headnote graph, Practical Law's maintained know-how,
Checkpoint, ONESOURCE — sold as subscriptions professionals bet their
malpractice exposure on. "Fiduciary-grade" is the value claim: answers a
lawyer can cite, not drafts a lawyer must verify. 92% retention, 81–82%
recurring revenue (FY25 annual report).

**Captured:** per-seat subscriptions with annual price escalation across
three segments that are 83% of revenue and growing 10% organic (Q2'26:
Legal $772M at 48.1% adj-EBITDA margin; Corporates $537M/37.2%; Tax & Audit
$311M/38.7%), plus Reuters News (anchored by the LSEG contract — $398M/yr
minimum to **2048**) and Global Print, 51% of which is being sold to KKR
(~$500M gross, discontinued-ops from Q3). GenAI products (CoCounsel,
Westlaw Advantage) are sold as add-ons and bundles — **32% of annualized
contract value at Q2'26, up from 30% at Q1** (call coverage) — i.e. the AI
threat is currently being *monetized*, not suffered.

**Protected:** proprietary content and its editorial pipeline (the thing an
LLM cannot scrape legally — though the 40-F flags genuine copyright-law
uncertainty here); workflow lock-in and citation standards; a multi-model
AI position (OpenAI custom models, CoCounsel rebuilt on Anthropic's agent
SDK, an MCP connector into Claude, and a ~$40M proprietary "Thomson" legal
LLM) — the strategy is to be the trusted layer *on top of* whichever
frontier model wins. The 40-F states the counter-case bluntly: AI "has
reduced barriers to entry" and customers "may explore internal
development." Both claims are live; only one shows up in the numbers so
far.

**Assumptions and their defense:**
- **The `growth without reinvestment` warning fired on every positive-g
  run, and the answer is structural, stated here:** TRI's FCF exceeds even
  cash earnings because (i) the reinvestment that funds its ~8% organic
  growth is **already charged inside FCF** — $593M/yr of capitalized
  software development sits in the capex line (accrued capex 8% of
  revenue, guided), so terminal growth does not draw on retained earnings
  the way the ratio implies; and (ii) subscription deferred-revenue float
  adds cash ahead of earnings. The earnings-understatement half (BR-class
  acquisition amortization) was already applied and is not sufficient
  alone — both halves are needed, and both are documented company figures.
- **Terminal 2.5% ≤ risk-free (tool-enforced), and it must survive the
  40-F's lead risk**: "deep domain expertise and content-driven technology
  focus being undermined by competitors... reduced barriers to entry,"
  plus copyright-law uncertainty over content protection. 2.5% survives
  only on the walled-garden outcome (proprietary content licensed/monetized
  through whichever model wins — the multi-model posture and the 2048 LSEG
  contract are evidence this is management's actual plan); scenario B is
  the honest alternative, priced at 0% terminal with outright decline, and
  it is what a −334bp spread looks like.
- **Market-share sentence:** path C puts revenue at ~$11.5B by 2031 —
  against a legal/tax/compliance information-and-software market well into
  the tens of billions and growing, no impossible share is required; the
  contested variable is *retention against agentic substitutes*, not TAM.
- **Commitments check:** $1,962M unconditional purchase obligations
  (multi-year cloud/tech, ~$400–520M/yr) sit inside the guided capex/opex
  run-rate; no take-or-pay traps. Net debt 0.6× adjusted EBITDA vs a 2.5×
