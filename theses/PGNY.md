## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $26.56.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=4 (3 probable,
1 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=2. Progyny is a genuinely good, net-cash,
founder-run-quality business whose competitive position is strengthening, but
at today's price the equity implies roughly a **7–9% annual return once stock
compensation is charged as the real expense it is** — below the 9.1% hurdle
on conservative *and* optimistic growth paths. The August selloff (−15% in
three sessions) removed the froth from a +90% run since April, not more.

Two sentences: the market has already re-priced Progyny from despair
(post-Amazon, $13s in 2025) to fair (implied return ≈ US-median cost of
capital), and the remaining upside case leans on an SBC fade and
TAM-expansion options (Select, health plans) that have no revenue evidence

## 2. Business

**Created:** Progyny manages fertility / family-building / women's health
benefits as a carve-out for self-insured employers (604 clients, ~7.2M avg
covered lives at Q2'26). The employer gets managed unit costs and a hard-ROI
story against 10%+ medical trend — bundled "Smart Cycle" benefit design, an
actively managed specialist network, concierge member support, and integrated
pharmacy (Progyny Rx) — versus the carrier default: an unmanaged
dollar-maximum rider with "no network management of any kind" (CEO, Canaccord
conference 2026-08-11). The member gets outcomes: the pitch is fewer failed
cycles and fewer multiples. The carrier is structurally indifferent (ASO PEPM
economics — they earn the same either way), which is why the default
competitor under-invests.

**Captured:** two revenue lines billed per utilization: fertility benefits
services (Q2'26 $230.2M, +7.6% y/y) and pharmacy benefit services (Q2'26
$120.3M, +1.2%). Revenue = lives × utilization × revenue/cycle. Gross margin
25.5% (Q2'26, +180bp y/y) — a pass-through-heavy claims model, so margins are
structurally thin; value capture scales with lives. Modest CPI-linked price
escalators on the fertility side; pharmacy absorbs some cost inflation to
keep clients whole (CFO, Q2'26 call).

**Protected:** a decade of client-auditable outcomes data (the sales weapon
in brownfield takeaways), the largest specialist network, broker
relationships, and now carrier distribution (Cigna partnership live since
Sept 2025, first full selling season now; more health-plan partnerships in
pipeline). Switching is an annual benefits decision, so the moat is earned
yearly — but observed churn is low ("vast majority of retention risk
removed" for 2027, earlier than usual) and brownfield wins from both
VC-backed competitors (Carrot/Maven/Kindbody cohort) and carrier solutions
are accelerating: "we win the majority of our deals against all competitors
collectively every year" (CEO). The moat is real but annuity-shaped, not
structural: it must be re-proven every selling season.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue 2022→TTM:
$786.9M→$1,310.8M (+67%); GAAP operating income $23.3M→$112.1M (op margin
3.0%→8.6%) (EDGAR companyfacts). Part of the recent margin lift is the SBC
decline, so leverage is flattered but real: adj EBITDA margin has held ~17.2%
TTM while absorbing platform investment.

  print (Nov 5).
- **Amazon-loss aftermath (the 2024 crash).** Resolved: FY25 had **no client
  >10% of revenue** (10-K), vs 12% in FY2024. Revenue grew ~11% ex-transition
  through the loss of its largest client — strong evidence the value
  proposition survives its worst-case churn event.
- **SBC — the valuation crux.** FY25 SBC $131.9M (10.2% of revenue); FY26
  guided to ~$80M (~5.8%); H1'26 actual $40.2M vs $64.9M H1'25 (−38%).
  Meanwhile 10.8M shares repurchased since Nov 2025 (−12.5% of shares out;
  77.4M at Q2'26). Adjusted EPS ($2.04–2.10 guide) excludes SBC; GAAP EPS
  guide is $1.26–1.32. The valuation below charges SBC in full.
- **Growth engine decomposition.** Growth is lives-driven: 604 clients
  (+62 y/y), FY26E avg members 6.9M vs 6.42M FY25 (comparable basis, +7.5%);
  target +1M new lives/yr, early 2027 commitments "meaningfully ahead."
  Utilization assumptions sit at the 5-year low band (female utilization
  1.04–1.05% vs 1.03–1.09% range since 2021); ART cycles per utilizer has
  drifted 0.99 (2023) → 0.93–0.94 (guide) — mix shift, not deterioration of
  the model, but it means guidance already assumes no utilization recovery.
- **TAM expansion options (unpriced-in, unevidenced).** Progyny Select
  (fully insured market; adds ~50M lives to a 105M-life TAM) is explicitly
  not material for 2027 — first year is channel-building with general
  agents/brokers. Cigna partnership in first full selling season; 2.7M of

  none of it carries current revenue evidence.
- **Benefits-budget squeeze / GLP-1 crowd-out.** Cuts both ways: employers
  under 10%+ trend are exactly the brownfield buyers switching to Progyny for
  cost control — but a hard benefits recession could hit adoption. Currently
  the cost pressure is a demonstrated tailwind (more RFPs, earlier
  commitments), not a headwind.
- **Commitments footnote (asset-light check):** no material purchase
  obligations, no take-or-pay, no litigation flagged as material (10-K note
  15). Capex is genuinely small (FY25 $18.4M incl. capitalized software,
  elevated by a platform build guided to taper from 2027). The asset-light

- **Dead ends:** Bad debt / receivables — H1'26 bad debt expense $10.2M
