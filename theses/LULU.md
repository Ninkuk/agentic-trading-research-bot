## 2. Business

**Created:** technical athletic apparel — proprietary fabrics (the Align/Nulu,
Wunder Train, Scuba, Define, ABC franchises), fit, and finishing — sold at full
price through the company's own stores and sites, wrapped in a community and
ambassador program (SeaWheeze, summer series, US Open and marathon
activations). The guest surplus is fit and durability plus the status of the
logo; "the wellness trend is strong" and the company "remain[s] the market
leader" in leggings even while that category fell ~20% (Q2 call). The surplus
is what the challengers are now contesting with lower prices and "dupes"
(Q2 10-Q Item 1A).

**Captured:** a vertical retailer, so the whole retail margin, not a wholesale
cut. (a) *Company-operated stores* — 825 at Q2, 483 Americas / 174 China
Mainland / 168 Rest of World; FY2024 store revenue $5.01B (47% of revenue);
(b) *e-commerce* — $0.9B in Q2, 39% of revenue, FY2024 $4.57B; (c) *other
channels* — outlets (58, mostly Americas — the clearance valve), Like New
re-commerce, wholesale to studios/campuses, 45 licensed third-party locations
(Middle East), lululemon Studio — FY2024 $1.01B. By category FY2025: women's
$7.00B (63%), men's $2.66B (24%), accessories and other $1.44B (13%). By
geography FY2025: US $6.33B (57%), Canada $1.42B (13%), Mexico $0.10B, PRC
$1.95B (18%, of which China Mainland $1.75B), other $1.30B (12%). Gross margin
55–60% and operating margin 20–24% in FY2021–FY2024 came from full-price
selling at a store fleet that grew into demand; both are now reversing
(10-K FY2025 Note 3; Q2 10-Q; stockanalysis `metrics/`).

**Protected:** weakly, and the filing says so. "We hold limited patents and
exclusive intellectual property rights in the technology, fabrics or processes
underlying our products, [so] our current and future competitors are able to
manufacture and sell products with performance characteristics, fabrication
techniques, and styling similar to ours"; "our brand presence and visibility
in certain markets may encourage some guests to try or migrate to emerging
competitors" (Q2 10-Q Item 1A). The mechanism that protected value was brand
heat — a customer who would not consider an alternative at any price — plus
the density of the store fleet in affluent trade areas. Brand heat is exactly
what management now says is under pressure, and nothing structural stops Alo
or Vuori from opening across the street; both are doing so. The honest answer
is that the moat is a reputation, currently being tested and currently losing.

**Control:** one class of common stock (105,594,064 shares at 2026-08-28) plus
5,115,961 shares of special voting stock paired one-for-one with exchangeable
shares of Lulu Canadian Holding — voting parity, no economic rights, no
super-vote. No controlling holder: founder Chip Wilson's group holds 9.57M
shares (8.6%), 5.12M of them the exchangeables. The board was classified; a
declassification proposal passed 73.1M–0.3M at the 2026-06-25 AGM and the
company must put full annual election to the 2027 meeting, effective 2028.
Under the 2026-05-26 Cooperation Agreement two Wilson nominees (Laura Gentile,
Marc Maurer) joined the board, a third apparel-brand director must be seated by
2026-10-01 with Wilson's approval, and one more incumbent will not stand in
2027; Wilson is under standstill and non-disparagement until ~30 days before
the 2028 nomination deadline. The structure forecloses nothing — an unsolicited
bid is possible and the activist path has already been exercised (10-Q cover;
13D/A 2026-09-03; 8-Ks 2026-05-27, 2026-06-26).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Positive through FY2024, then
sharply negative — revenue is flat while operating income has fallen 26% from
its peak.
