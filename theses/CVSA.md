## 2. Business

**Created:** A working adult who wants to become — or move up as — a
clinician gets a credential they can actually finish while employed, at a
school whose graduates pass licensure and get hired. Chamberlain runs
pre-licensure BSN on physical campuses with clinical placements plus online
post-licensure programs; Walden runs online graduate degrees weighted to
nursing and the mental-health professions (counseling, social work,
psychology) with a competency-based option; the three Caribbean schools
(AUC, Ross Medical, Ross Veterinary) admit students who did not clear the US
medical-school bottleneck. The preference being satisfied is not price — it
is **access on the student's schedule to a licensed profession with a
shortage**, and the surplus is real: the credential is the gate to a wage
step that persists for a career.

**Captured:** Not "tuition" — four distinct mechanisms. (1) Per-credit-hour
tuition at Walden, where the FY2026 lever was a 2.6% average rate increase
*plus* a rise in average credit hours per student — volume and price on the
same student. (2) Per-session tuition at Chamberlain, up ~3.4% in FY2026,
against a largely fixed campus cost base, which is why 450–500-student
campuses at $9–12M of leased fit-out reach EBITDA breakeven in 24 months and
then run accretive. (3) Semester tuition and administrative fees at the
medical and veterinary schools, up 4.5%/4.6%/3.0% for AUC, RUSM and RUSVM —
the highest-priced, lowest-volume, most policy-exposed slice. (4) Employer
contracts (SSM Health, and Advocate Health announced 2026-06-29, first cohort
September 2026), where a health system funds tuition and supplies clinical
placement in exchange for a hiring pipeline — this lowers customer
acquisition cost and raises persistence, and it is the only mechanism that
changes the *unit economics* rather than the price.

**Protected:** The mechanism is regulatory and operational, not brand.
To compete you need, simultaneously: institutional accreditation recognized
by ED, programmatic nursing accreditation, state authorization in every state
you enroll in, a clinical-placement network with hospitals that already have
their pick of schools, and a licensure pass-rate history long enough for
those states and hospitals to trust. The CEO's closing framing on the Q4 call
is the correct one and it is checkable: these "can't be assembled quickly."
Two things bound the moat. It is a **regulatory** moat, which means the same
regulator that raised the barrier can lower the value behind it — which is
exactly what OBBBA is doing. And it does not protect against *substitution*:
a state university system that decides to expand its own nursing seats faces
no such barrier. Against the third threat — a for-profit entrant — the moat
is strong. Against the second it is weak, and Chamberlain's FY2026 enrollment
stall is what that looks like.

**Control:** One class of common stock, $0.01 par, 200,000,000 authorized and
34,033,000 outstanding at 2026-06-30; one share, one vote; no controlling
holder. Insiders hold 2.59% and the float is 97.4% of shares outstanding.
Delaware incorporation. Nothing in the structure forecloses an unsolicited
takeover or an activist campaign — this is the null answer, and it is written
here deliberately: a $4.3B company at 10.8× FCF with a wide-open register is
structurally acquirable, which is a mild positive not counted in §4.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Chamberlain, the crown jewel, quietly stopped growing — and the
  segment-level profit went backwards.** This is the thread the CEO's script
  buries under "12 consecutive quarters of total enrollment growth *across
  the company*." Chamberlain's own session series went from +12.1%/+11.7%/
  +11.5% in FY2025 to −1.0%/−0.7%/+0.5%/+1.6% across FY2026; revenue grew
  3.4% and was mostly the 3.4% tuition increase. Segment adjusted EBITDA
  **fell 3.1%** to $185.5M and the margin compressed 170bp to 24.7%. The
  cause is disclosed: post-licensure (RN-to-BSN) enrollment declined all
  year while pre-licensure grew. Chamberlain is 38.4% of revenue and, on the
  Investor Day plan, is supposed to go 40,000 → ~50,000 students by FY2029 on
  10–15 new campuses. A segment that shrank its profit in the year before
  that ramp is the single biggest execution risk in the plan. Two details
  sharpen it. First, **the moat does not cover the part that broke**: the
  protection mechanism in §2 — programmatic nursing accreditation, clinical
  placement capacity, licensure history — guards pre-licensure BSN, which
  grew for a 16th straight quarter. Post-licensure RN-to-BSN needs almost
  none of it, and that is precisely where enrollment declined all year. The
  moat is real and it is not load-bearing for the segment's growth problem.
  Second, **the metric that would settle it stopped being disclosed**: asked
  directly at Q4 for the application-growth number management had supplied in
  each of the two prior quarters, the CEO answered "we don't guide to
  enrollment growth by institution or program" and did not provide it.
  Disclosure cadence is itself a signal, and a leading indicator that goes
  dark at the moment it becomes decisive is not a neutral omission. For
  context the enterprise-level series from the Q4 deck runs Q1 97.4k (+8.0%),
  Q2 97.0k (+6.3%), Q3 100.6k (+6.8%), Q4 99.5k (+8.4%) — the company total
  is fine; it is the mix underneath that has shifted.

  OBBBA.** Walden was 41.2% of FY2026 revenue but **51.4% of total adjusted
  EBITDA** ($268.0M of $521.7M), with segment margin up 350bp to 33.3% and
  revenue +16.1%. It is also, by the company's own description at the
  Investor Day, "graduate-focused" with "over 80% of enrollments" in clinical
  healthcare roles — where the 80% explicitly includes "social workers,
  counselors, therapists, psychologists," not just nursing. OBBBA's Do No
  Harm test asks whether a *graduate* program's completers out-earn a
  *bachelor's-degree holder*. Mental-health and social-work master's degrees
  are precisely the graduate credentials where that comparison is closest.
  The offsetting facts are real and dated: the test is effective 2027-07-01,
  requires failure in two of three years, nursing was reclassified as a
  professional program (raising its borrowing cap), and Walden's *fastest*-
  growing slice — undergraduate, doubled to ~12,000 since the prior Investor
  Day, +20% in Q4 — is tested against the far easier high-school-graduate
