## 2. Business

**Created:** G-III designs, sources and delivers branded apparel (dresses,
outerwear, sportswear, swim, handbags, footwear) to North American
department stores and off-price chains, and to its own stores and sites.
The retailer gets a vendor that can fill a whole classification floor with
recognizable labels on time at wholesale economics — forty years of vendor
relationships, ~72% of product from Vietnam, China and Bangladesh (two
Vietnamese vendors alone 42.6% of purchases), no owned factories (10-K).
The consumer gets accessible-premium fashion (DKNY, Donna Karan, Karl
Lagerfeld Paris, Vilebrequin, now Marc Jacobs) and licensed names (Levi's,
Champion, Converse, NFL/NBA) at department-store price points. Owned brands
were 57% of FY2026 sales (47% in FY2024); licensed 43% (10-K).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (i) Wholesale margin on owned-brand
product — the highest-margin stream because no royalty is paid; wholesale
segment gross margin 43.3% in Q2 FY2027 (call). (ii) Wholesale margin on
licensed product, net of royalties and minimum advertising — minimum royalty
commitments were $182.0M at 2026-01-31, $78.4M due within a year (10-K
contractual obligations). (iii) Licensing royalties on owned brands
(DKNY/Donna Karan fragrance via Be Delicious, Karl Lagerfeld categories,
hospitality) — management called it "on track to earn $75 million in royalty
income" in December 2023 (transcript, primary-transcribed; not re-stated
since as a number). (iv) Retail/DTC through DKNY and Karl Lagerfeld stores
and sites, plus now ~100 Marc Jacobs stores; the segment lost $5.0M in
FY2026 after $14.0M and $30.5M (10-K). Marc Jacobs adds a fifth: a 50% share
of IPCo's licensing income (fragrance with Coty, beauty, eyewear, kids), net
of the royalty G-III's own opco pays IPCo under a license running to
December 2041 with ten five-year renewals (8-K 2026-09-02, Item 1.01).

**Protected:** the wholesale franchise is protected by scale and service —
"supplier of choice" for classification floors, an order book ~90% complete
for the year (CFO) — but PVH just demonstrated the limit of that protection:
a licensor can take the brands back, and G-III is suing PVH over one
category (10-K, litigation filed 2025-06-13). The owned brands are protected
by brand equity (DKNY/Donna Karan ~$2.7B of annual retail sales, per the
CEO) that must be re-earned each season with marketing spend (advertising
$139.0M in FY2026, 10-K). Nothing stops Macy's from moving floor space to
another vendor, TJX from buying elsewhere, or PVH/Nike/Levi's from
in-housing a category. Honest answer: a service moat on the wholesale side
that the customer and the licensor both control, and a brand moat on the
owned side that is real but rented from the consumer season by season.

**Control:** one class, one vote per share, no controlling holder (DEF 14A
2026-05-05). Morris Goldfarb (Chairman/CEO, 75, with the company since 1974)
beneficially owns 11%; directors and officers as a group 15%; BlackRock
14.0%, Vanguard 11.3%, Dimensional 7.7%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an
activist, but the founder's 11% plus a $39.6M change-in-control formula
(2.99× five-year average pay) and 2.0× severance make him the gate. Say-on-
pay passed with 83% for in June 2026 (5.9M against), down from 89%; FY2026
CEO pay $12.5M, pay ratio 470:1 (8-K 2026-06-12; DEF 14A). On 2026-08-18 a
Form 4 recorded 840,000 shares acquired by the CEO (code A — the FY2024
performance-retention award) with 464,520 withheld for tax at $33.72; no
open-market insider purchase appears in the 2026 Form 4 list.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue is 3% above FY2022 and
operating income is 61% below it; the margin went from 11.5% to 4.4%
(stockanalysis income statement, `opinc`; impairments sit below that line on
