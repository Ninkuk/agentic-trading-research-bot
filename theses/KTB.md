## 2. Business

**Created:** a Wrangler buyer gets a durable, fit-consistent jean at
$20–$60 at Walmart, Target, Boot Barn, Tractor Supply or wrangler.com — the
brand is the default in Western and workwear (cowboy cut, Riggs) where
authenticity is the purchase driver and the retailer's private label cannot
credibly copy it. A Helly Hansen buyer gets technical sailing/ski/workwear
with a Norwegian-heritage safety proposition — "high-performance gear and
protection" is what consumers credit it for (Q2 call). Lee, until the
close, is the Asian-channel and department-store denim brand.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (1) US wholesale — $398M of Q2's
$584M continuing revenue — sold on purchase orders (no long-term
contracts) to mass merchants and specialty; Wrangler's segment margin is
23% because Kontoor still owns Mexican manufacturing (USMCA-exempt from
the new Section 301 tariffs) and runs a short-lead replenishment model.
(2) International wholesale ($112M in Q2, 60% of it Helly) — order-book
driven, bought to the book, no speculation. (3) Direct-to-consumer ($74M in
Q2, +51%) — e-commerce and a nascent full-price store fleet (Fort Worth;
two more Texas leases for early 2027). Licensing and the unconsolidated
China Helly JV with Youngor add a small fourth stream. Helly's margin
lever is the platform: shared sourcing, logistics, planning and tax
structure lifting a brand that ran at ~1% operating margin standalone.

**Protected:** Wrangler's protection is a mechanism, not a label — 80 years
of Western authenticity that the mass retailer's private label cannot
replicate, plus owned manufacturing that lets it hold price and service
replenishment while import-dependent rivals eat tariff volatility. It is
not a wide moat: the 10-K says outright that "many of our largest customers
have already developed significant private label brands" and that Walmart
is 30% of revenue on purchase orders. Helly's protection is brand heritage
in a narrow technical niche; outside sailing and ski it is competing with
Arc'teryx, Patagonia and Columbia on their ground, with ~30% US aided
awareness (Q2 call). Honest reading: one real, narrow moat (Wrangler
Western/work) and one brand with heritage but no structural protection
(Helly).
