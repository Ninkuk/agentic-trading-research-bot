## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $44.24.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=5 (2 probable,
3 plausible), refuted=1, unknown=2.

Steve Madden is a well-run, trend-chasing fashion footwear and handbag house
that is genuinely regaining brand heat (US comps +17%, branded wholesale +20%
in Q2), but the price already needs that recovery to run for years. The
6.9% FCF yield that put it on the candidates list is an artefact: trailing
cash flow carries a $92.1M one-time IEEPA tariff refund (28% of the whole
market cap's FCF yield) and, scrubbed, the yield is ~4.1%. On a normalized
base — management's own 2026 adjusted-EPS guide converted to cash — the price
implies 8.0% a year against a 10.1% cost of equity; even a 12%-a-year FCF
compounding path for five years lands 61bp short. Operating leverage has run
negative for five years (revenue +47% since 2021, operating income down on
every honest cut), the moat is a designer's eye plus speed-to-market, and the
new Section 301 tariff regime replaces the one the Supreme Court just struck
down.

**Closest attack:** "you are scrubbing the refund out of FCF but leaving the
tariffs it refunded inside the base — the IEEPA tariffs the company paid in
Q3'25–Q1'26 are also inside TTM, so the true no-tariff run-rate is *higher*
than $132M, and 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.10 is a trough number that recovers
toward the $2.35–2.77 the business earned in 2022–24 as pricing laps the
tariffs and Kurt Geiger scales." It is the best case for the name, and it is
why scenario D exists — and D still comes in below the hurdle.

## 2. Business

**Created:** trend-right footwear, handbags and apparel at accessible price
points, delivered fast. The customer gets this season's look (split toes,
jellies, hidden wedges, ballet flats, needle heels — the CEO's own Q2 list)
weeks after it appears on runways and social feeds, at a Steve Madden price
of roughly $60–150 rather than a designer's $400+. The surplus is currency
and speed, not durability: "we're still in the business of trend, and the
trend cycles move faster than ever" (CFO, Q2 call). Kurt Geiger (acquired
May 2025) adds a UK-premium handbag/footwear brand with a personalization
hook (one-of-a-kind Kensington bags drove 17% of handbag sales where offered).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (i) Wholesale footwear — 40.9% of
2025 revenue — branded sell-in to department stores, off-price and mass
(top ten: Nordstrom, Macy's, Dillard's, DSW, TJX, Ross, Burlington, Amazon,
Walmart, Target; none >10%), plus a shrinking private-label business for
the mass channel. (ii) Wholesale accessories/apparel — 25.3% — the same
customers, handbags and apparel. (iii) Direct-to-consumer — 33.4% — 382
company stores (92 outlets), 8 e-commerce sites, 164 international
concessions; gross margin 64% vs 35% wholesale. (iv) Licensing — 0.5% —
royalties on the brand names. Geography: 66% US, 34% international (KG made
international $856M in 2025 vs $424M in 2024). Receivables are factored
(factor AR $307M) — normal for the channel, not a red flag.

**Protected:** thin, and honest to say so. There is no contractual moat, no
switching cost, no network. What exists is (a) a design engine led by the
founder — the 10-K's own key-person risk names the loss of Steve Madden as
potentially "severely disrupt[ive]" — with a speed-to-market sourcing
apparatus in China (56.1% of 2025 purchases) and Cambodia (24.6%); (b) shelf
position at the ten wholesalers above, earned by sell-through; (c) brand
recognition that lets it price ~2× fast-fashion private label. Zara, Aldo,
Sam Edelman (Caleres), Dolce Vita's own peers and every DTC start-up can copy
a jelly sandal in a season; what they cannot easily copy is the hit rate of
the design team. That is a talent moat, and talent moats leave with the
talent.

**Control:** one class of common stock (73.0M shares at 3/27/26), one share
one vote, no controlling holder. BlackRock 14.8%, Vanguard 11.3%, FMR 11.0%;
all directors and officers 2.21%, CEO/Chair Rosenfeld 1.45%. Steve Madden
himself is neither a director nor a named executive officer in the 2026
proxy and holds no disclosed ≥5% stake. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an
activist path; the $250M buyback authorization (2004 program, no expiry) is
the board's own lever.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue up 47% from 2021 to the
TTM; operating income down on GAAP, on the IEEPA-scrubbed line, and on
management's adjusted line.

## 5. Falsifiers

  Q2 price increases lap (i.e. Q4'26 and Q1'27) under the Section 301/15%
  regime, with wholesale branded volume still growing — condition 2 moves
  to probable and scenario D's margin path becomes evidenced.
- **Shift —** A disclosed Kurt Geiger P&L (segment, or an investor-day

  leaves UNKNOWN.
- **Shift —** Buyback resumes at ≥ $25M/quarter with the term loan below
  $100M — the 2023–24 return-of-capital math re-enters the base case.
- **Shift —** Price below ~$33 on an unchanged normalized base (scenario C
  at $33 ≈ 10.1%) — the hurdle clears without any story change.

- **Break —** Steve Madden leaves or his agreement is not renewed — the

- **Break —** Gross margin falls below ~42% for two quarters absent a named
  tariff shock — pricing power is gone.
- **Shift —** A third Section 301 action (excess capacity or Vietnam IP)
  takes the blended import tariff above the 15% assumed — reset the base.
- **Shift —** Private label declines >20% for the year and branded
  wholesale slows to single digits — the mass-channel thread was worse than
  sized.
- **Shift —** Goodwill/intangible impairment on Kurt Geiger — the deal
  did not earn its price.
