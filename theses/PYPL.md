whose highest-margin product, branded checkout, grows ~2% a year while
device, platform and now agentic wallets take the marginal transaction. At
8.1× SBC-adjusted trailing FCF the price discounts a business shrinking

stabilisation, a ≥$6.0B adjusted-FCF guide, an ~8% buyback yield, and a
cost programme now visibly executing (a 20% workforce reduction under way
in September) make that decline the pessimistic case, not the central one.
This remains a "priced for decline" thesis, not a turnaround thesis — but
the margin is thinner than on 8/31: the 10-year Treasury rose 50bp to
5.24% and the breakeven decline rate tightened from −4%/yr to −3%/yr.

12bp *under* the hurdle. A second year like 2026 makes the breakeven row

## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/PYPL-2026-08-31.md` §2 in substance (no filing
since); restated briefly with deltas.

**Created:** 439M active accounts (228M monthly active) and ~$1.9T trailing
TPV. Consumers join for a stored wallet with buyer protection, BNPL and
one-touch checkout; merchants for the consumers, conversion lift, risk
absorption and one integration across ~200 markets. Venmo is the US P2P
habit ($351B TTM TPV, +14%); Braintree is the unbranded enterprise
processor. Delta: an agentic channel — Muse checkout via PayPal across
every PayPal merchant (company post, 2026-09-22; low-confidence colour for
the details). (FY25 10-K; Q2'26 release and call.)

**Captured:** seven mechanisms. (1) Branded checkout per-transaction fee —
total take rate 1.78%, transaction take rate 1.61% (−7bp YoY). (2)
Braintree/PSP processing at thin margin (transaction expense 0.90% of TPV).
(3) Credit — BNPL, PayPal Credit, working capital: "close to 20%" of TM$,
BNPL originated held-for-sale and sold forward-flow to March 2028. (4)
Interest on customer balances, $281M in Q2'26 (~7% of TM$). (5) Venmo
monetisation, $1.7B revenue in 2025 (+20%): debit interchange, Pay with
Venmo, instant transfer. (6) FX spreads and instant-transfer fees. (7)
OVAS partnerships, referral and gateway fees. TM$ $15.5B (2025), guided
~$15.6B (2026).

**Protected:** a two-sided habit — stored funding instruments and history
in the wallet, the button on most e-commerce checkouts. Real, and eroding
at the margin: branded +2% vs TPV +9%, take rate −7 to −9bp/yr, active
accounts flat at 439M, management's own "execution has not been what it
needs to be" (Q4'25 call) and a withdrawn 2027 outlook. What still defends
it: Venmo's US P2P graph, the risk/credit stack (loss rate 0.08% of TPV),
the merchant integration footprint, and the fact that no rival wallet is
two-sided across ~200 markets — the reason an agent builder like Meta
plugs into it rather than around it. A moat that holds the base, not the
growth.

**Control:** one class of common stock, ~855M shares outstanding, no
controlling holder — Vanguard 10.05%, BlackRock 8.06% (13G/A, Jan 2026),
insiders 0.10% (stocks.db `sharesInsiders`), institutions 78.3%. Special
meetings need 20%. Nothing structural forecloses a takeover or activist;
the Stripe/Advent $60.50 approach was declined by board judgment. Rights
plan: not verified (§6).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive over 2021–2025, inflecting
