## 1. Verdict and thesis

**BUY at $52.67.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (3 probable,
2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

PayPal is a scaled, cash-generative two-sided checkout-and-wallet network
whose highest-margin product, branded checkout, now grows ~2% a year while
device and platform wallets take the marginal transaction. At 6.8× trailing
GAAP FCF (≈8.8× after deducting stock compensation and normalising a
one-off low cash-tax year) the price already discounts a business shrinking
about 4% a year for five years and standing still after. Two quarters of stabilisation (transaction-margin
dollars ex-interest +3%, branded checkout +2%, transactions per active
account ex-PSP +7%), a ≥$6.0B adjusted-FCF guide, an 8% buyback yield from
a $10.9B authorisation, and a board that declined $60.50 make the priced-in
decline the pessimistic case rather than the central one. This is a
"priced for decline" thesis, not a turnaround thesis: nothing below needs
the transformation to work, only for the decline to run slower than −4%/yr.

**Closest attack:** the FY2026 guide itself implies adjusted FCF down ~6%
(≥$6.0B vs $6.4B in 2025) and non-GAAP operating income down ~5–6% (EPS
+1% on ~8% fewer shares). If that is the run-rate rather than a
reinvestment year, the price implies ~8.8% against a 10.2% hurdle and the
buy fails. Whether FY2027 turns is not knowable from any filing today.

Load-bearing conditions (5):

1. *probable* — Transaction-margin dollars excluding interest on customer
   balances hold ≥0% growth through FY2027: branded checkout stays ≥0% FXN
   and Venmo, credit and Braintree keep offsetting the take-rate/mix drag.

   FY26 guide raised to ~$15.6B TM$ / ~$14.5B ex-interest (Q2 release, call).

## 2. Business

**Created:** 439M active accounts (228M monthly active) and ~$1.9T of
trailing TPV on a network that a consumer joins because a stored wallet with
buyer protection, BNPL and one-touch checkout removes card entry and fraud
risk from the purchase, and a merchant joins because the button is where
the consumers are — 60% of merchants on the new payment pages, conversion
lift, risk absorption, and one integration for ~200 markets. Venmo is the
US P2P habit (TPV $351B TTM, +14%); Braintree is the unbranded enterprise
processor behind large marketplaces and platforms. (10-K FY25; Q2'26
release and call.)

**Captured:** seven distinct mechanisms, ranked by transaction-margin weight.
(1) Branded checkout: a per-transaction fee on TPV — transaction revenue
$30.7B TTM, total take rate 1.78% (1.61% transaction take rate, −7bp YoY).
(2) Unbranded processing (Braintree/PSP): low take rate, transaction expense
0.90% of TPV, "profitable for nine consecutive quarters", mid-teens growth.
(3) Credit — BNPL, PayPal Credit, working-capital loans: "close to 20%" of
TM$ and growing double digits (Q2 call); BNPL receivables are originated as
held-for-sale and sold under a forward-flow agreement with a third-party
investment firm (extended in 2025 to March 2028; HFS balance $1.9B at
June 30, 2026), so the balance sheet keeps only $5.5B of consumer loans and
$6.8B total loans net. (4) Interest on customer balances: $281M in Q2'26
(~7% of TM$), reported in OVAS revenue and stripped from management's
preferred TM$ metric because rates set it. (5) Venmo monetisation: $1.7B
revenue in 2025 (+20%) from debit-card interchange, Pay with Venmo (+44%
volume) and instant-transfer fees; debit + Pay-with-Venmo users generate
>9× the ARPA of P2P-only users. (6) Cross-border FX spreads and instant
transfer fees inside transaction revenue. (7) OVAS partnerships, referral,
subscription and gateway fees ($850M/qtr). Transaction-margin dollars —
revenue less transaction expense less transaction and credit losses — were
$15.5B in 2025 and are guided to ~$15.6B for 2026.

**Protected:** the mechanism is the two-sided habit — a consumer's stored
funding instruments, balance and purchase history live in the PayPal wallet,
and the button's presence on most e-commerce checkouts makes that wallet
worth keeping; each side justifies the other. The mechanism is real and it
is eroding at the margin, measurably: device and platform wallets (Apple
Pay, Shop Pay, Google Pay — named by management in 18 and 16 of 93
transcribed calls) built the consumer side on-device, branded checkout
volume grows 2% while TPV grows 9%, the take rate falls ~7–9bp a year, the
active-account base has stopped growing (439M flat, −0.2M sequentially),
and management's own Q4'25 framing was "our execution has not been what it
needs to be" before withdrawing the 2027 Investor Day outlook. What still
defends it: Venmo's P2P graph in the US, the credit and risk stack (loss
rate 0.08% of TPV), the merchant integration footprint, and the fact that
no rival wallet is two-sided across ~200 markets. Honest summary: a moat
that holds the base and no longer holds the growth.

**Control:** one class of common stock ($0.0001 par, 4,000M authorised,
862M outstanding at June 30, 2026), preferred authorised but unissued, no
controlling holder — Vanguard 10.05% and BlackRock 8.06% (13G/A, Jan 2026),
insiders 0.10%. Special meetings need 20% (a proposal to cut it to 10%
failed at the May 2026 AGM). Nothing structural forecloses a takeover or an
activist path; the $60.50 Stripe/Advent approach was declined by board
judgment, not by structure. Whether a rights plan exists was not verified
(UNKNOWN).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive over 2021–2025, inflecting
negative in 2026.** Revenue / GAAP operating income, $M (stockanalysis
