## 2. Business

**Created:** A mid-career working adult gets an accredited degree from a
physically real university (campus, NCAA Division I athletics) delivered
online around a job, at a tuition GCU has held flat for years and that sits
below the federal graduate borrowing cap. The preference served is credential
legitimacy at a payable price. The hybrid arm gives partner universities that
cannot physically expand nursing/health programs a turnkey off-campus site
(47 sites, 20 partners): GCE supplies the building, equipment and back office;
the partner supplies the degree.

**Captured:** Three mechanisms.
1. **GCU master services agreement — 89.4% of service revenue.** 60% of GCU's
   tuition and academic fees. Since 2026-07-01 the base excludes ancillary
   revenue (housing, food, athletics, arena, hotel, golf, apparel), about
   −$20M/yr of revenue. GCE bears its own delivery cost, so incremental
   students are near-pure contribution; quarterly operating margin swings
   from ~22% (summer) to a guided ~37% (Q4).
2. **University Partners — ~10.6% of revenue.** Per-site revenue share,
   5,829 students at "slightly above 60% capacity," ~20% site margins at
   scale (CFO, Q2 call).
3. **Cost relief inside the amended fee.** GCE no longer reimburses GCU for
   certain academic costs ($3M in Q3, $5M in Q4 2026), which is why the
   $20M revenue cut nets to ≤$1M/quarter of operating income.

**Protected:** GCE *is* GCU's operating infrastructure: the 5,830-employee
counselling, marketing, technology and financial-aid organisation that GCU
has never had in-house, built as one company until the 2018 separation.
Replacing it mid-flight with ~131,800 students enrolled is a real barrier.
It is a barrier with a date on it (2041), whose price has been renegotiated
in GCU's favour twice since 2018. Outside GCU, the partner contracts are
ordinary competitive business with no moat.

**Control:** One share class, one vote, no controlling holder. Insiders
2.26%, institutions 106.5% (securities-lending double count), float 25.09M
of 25.73M shares (`stocks.db`). Nothing in the charter forecloses a takeover
or an activist. The structural constraint is commercial: an acquirer buys a
contract with a nonprofit it cannot also buy. The CEO is also President of
GCU (10-K Item 1A names this a risk).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive** from the FY2022 base, negative
measured from FY2021.
