## 2. Business

**Created:** A working adult — median GCU online student is a mid-career
professional, not an 18-year-old — gets an accredited degree from a
recognised, physically-real university at a tuition GCU has held flat for
years and which sits below the federal graduate borrowing cap, delivered
online around a job. The preference being satisfied is *credential legitimacy
at a payable price*: a campus, an NCAA Division I athletics programme and a
72,000-seat-brand exist behind the online transcript, which is exactly what a
purely-online provider cannot offer. The hybrid arm satisfies a different
preference: a partner university with a nursing or health-sciences programme
it cannot physically expand gets a turnkey off-campus classroom-and-lab site
— 47 of them — where GCE supplies the building, equipment and back office and
the partner supplies the degree.

**Captured:** Not "a services fee." Three distinct mechanisms:

1. **The GCU master services agreement — 89.4% of service revenue.** A fixed
   60% of GCU's tuition and academic-related fees. GCE bears its own cost of
   delivery, so every incremental enrolled student is close to pure
   contribution; this is why operating margin swings from 22.0% in the summer
   quarter to a guided 36.9–37.4% in Q4. As of 2026-07-01 the base excludes
   ancillary revenue (housing, food service, athletics, arena, hotel, golf,
   apparel), which now accrues to GCU alone.
2. **University Partners — ~10.6% of revenue.** Per-site revenue share with
   20 partner universities across 47 off-campus sites, 5,829 students, running
   "slightly above 60% capacity." CFO stated ~20% site-level margins at scale.
3. **Cost relief inside the fee.** The amendment eliminated GCE's obligation
   to reimburse GCU for certain academic/faculty costs — worth $3M in Q3 and
   $5M in Q4 2026 — which is why a $20M revenue cut costs "not more than $1
   million per quarter" of operating income.

**Protected:** The mechanism, stated without the label: GCE and GCU were a
single company until the 2018 separation, so GCE *is* GCU's operating
infrastructure — 5,830 employees running the counselling, marketing,
technology and financial-aid processing that GCU has never had in-house.
Displacing GCE means GCU rebuilding that organisation from nothing while
131,800 students are enrolled. That is a real barrier, not a checkbox. But it
is a barrier with an expiry date on it: it runs to 2041, and the price has
already been renegotiated once in GCU's favour. Nothing in the structure
prevents the base narrowing again at the next renewal. Outside GCU, there is
no moat at all — the 20 partner contracts are ordinary competitive business.

**Control:** One share class, one vote, no controlling holder. Insiders hold
2.26% of shares; institutions 106.25% (above 100% on securities-lending
double-count); float 97.5%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist —
the structural constraint is commercial, not charter-based: an acquirer buys a
contract with a nonprofit it cannot also buy.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The amended master services agreement — the thread that reframes the name.**
Executed 2026-07-29, effective 2026-07-01. Term reset to 15 years running to
2041-06-30 (an 8-year extension from the prior 2033 expiry), auto-renewing for
up to three additional 5-year terms absent 18 months' notice. What GCE got:
those 8 years; the removal of GCU's right to terminate for convenience; and
relief from reimbursing certain academic costs. What GCE gave up: ~$20M/yr of
ancillary revenue share; the early-termination fee; and — the part the bull
framing omits — the non-renewal payment, replaced by an obligation to keep
providing services for 18 months after expiry. The honest scoring is not
"de-risking mispriced as a revenue cut." GCU's convenience-termination right
was priced at 100% of trailing-twelve-month fees, roughly $1.05B at the
current run rate; no rational counterparty exercises that, so cancelling it
bought less than it sounds. The real consideration was 8 years of term
against GCE's terminal protection. GCU's stated motive, per the 2026-06-02
8-K, was that the fee structure was "an existing barrier to GCU's ability to
issue tax-exempt financing" — GCE conceded value to make its only customer
cheaper to finance. Symbiotic, and defensible, but GCE is the party that
conceded, for the second time since 2018.

**The litigation the call did not mention.** Q3 2025 (reported 2025-11-05)
carried a $35M legal-settlement charge: pretax income fell from ~$59.1M to
$21.66M and diluted EPS came in at $0.58 against a $1.78 estimate. Operating
income that quarter actually *rose* year-over-year, $48.18M → $55.43M — the
charge sat below the operating line, which is why an operating-margin screen
would have seen nothing. That reserve resolved *United States ex rel.
Mackillop*, a 2018 qui tam under the False Claims Act alleging GCU and GCE
paid recruiters based on enrolment in violation of the federal incentive
compensation ban; $28M was paid, $19.9M to the Treasury, no admission of
liability. Three matters remain live in the 10-Q's contingencies note, all
over the same doctoral-programme cost disclosures: *Smith and Wang v. GCE*
(D. Ariz., filed June 2024, RICO and state consumer protection; one RICO count
dismissed, the other and the consumer claims proceeding; fact discovery closed
2026-05-12, **class certification pending, no trial date**); *Ogdon v. GCE*
(D. Ariz., filed May 2020, RICO and New York law claims proceeding); and
*Valerio et al.* (Maricopa County Superior Court, filed 2024-12-24, ~300
individual plaintiffs, motion to dismiss denied 2025-09-17). The company
"cannot estimate a range of loss" and "has not accrued any liability." Civil
RICO carries treble damages and fee-shifting.
