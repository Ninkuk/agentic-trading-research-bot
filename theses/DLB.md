## 2. Business

**Created:** a device maker gets a certified, interoperable implementation of
the audio and video formats the content already carries — Dolby Digital Plus
and AC-4 for broadcast and streaming sound, Dolby Atmos for spatial audio,
Dolby Vision for HDR video, HEVC/VVC codec patents through pools — plus the
trademark that tells a shopper the TV "does Dolby." The content side (studios,
streamers, broadcasters, game publishers, now automakers) gets a mastering and
delivery chain that plays back the way the creative intended on every screen
that carries the logo. The surplus is real on both ends: a TV OEM pays cents
to dollars per unit for something it could not build, certify and brand alone,
and a streamer pays nothing for the device-side decode because the OEM
already did (10-K FY2025, Business; Q3 FY2026 call).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one royalty. (i) Per-unit
branded-technology royalties from ~1,000 device OEMs, often under annual or
multi-year minimum-volume commitments whose value is recognised up front —
which is why quarterly revenue is lumpy and why "recoveries" (back royalties
from under-reporting OEMs) recur every quarter (10-K, Licensing). (ii) Patent
pool royalties through Access Advance, Via LA (majority-owned, co-owned with
Philips and Mitsubishi), Sisvel and Vectis — "the vast majority" of patent
licensing revenue, plus Via LA administration fees. (iii) Content-side
revenue, new since FY2026: the Video Distribution Program licenses imaging
patents to streamers on usage, and Dolby OptiView (low-latency streaming, ad
insertion) sits in services; the stated target is 10% of revenue from content
platforms by the end of FY2028. (iv) Dolby Cinema, a share of box office at
premium-format sites, plus cinema hardware and services (Products & Services
$101M in FY2025, 7.5% of revenue). Licensing was $1,248M of $1,349M FY2025
revenue, split Broadcast 34%, Mobile 22%, CE 12%, PC 12%, Other (auto,
gaming, cinema) 20% (10-K FY2025).

**Protected:** three mechanisms, each named. The standards position: Dolby
formats are written into broadcast specifications (ATSC 3.0 for AC-4) and into
the mastering workflows of every major studio and streamer, so a device maker
that drops Dolby loses playback of content that already exists — a switching
cost that sits with the content library, not the chip. The ecosystem
position: Dolby works both sides, and a format only matters when content
carries it and devices decode it; a competitor must win both simultaneously.
The patent estate: ~6,100 pending applications and issued patents expiring
2025–2047, refreshed so that "licensing revenue from technologies covered by
expiring patents" is replaced by newer claims (10-K FY2025, Risk Factors).
The honest limit: the foundational codecs are the standards, and their
patents are the ones expiring — what remains after expiry is the trademark,
the certification and the implementation, whose pricing power against a free
open-source decoder is exactly the unknown in §6.1.

**Control:** dual class. 59,393,554 Class A shares (one vote) and 34,251,901
Class B shares (ten votes) at 2026-06-26; the Dolby family and affiliates
held 99.8% of Class B, which is 85.0% of combined voting power (10-K FY2025).
That forecloses an unsolicited takeover and any activist path; the family's
economic stake is ~36.6%. The Dagmar Dolby Trust converted and gifted 300,000
Class B shares on 2026-08-28 (0.9% of the class), a charitable transfer, not
a sale. One share, one vote does not apply here.

FY2022 trough.**
