## 2. Business

**Created:** Canadian Natural converts bitumen, heavy and light oil and gas
in Western Canada into ~1.25 million bbl/d of liquids and ~2.6 Bcf/d of gas,
two-thirds of the liquids being synthetic crude, light oil and NGLs that
sell at or above WTI. The customer (a refiner) gets reliable, high-volume,
diesel-rich SCO and heavy feedstock from a jurisdiction with no
expropriation risk; the surplus the customer captures is the refining
margin on a feedstock priced at a persistent discount (WCS) to lighter
global crudes. The company's own value creation is cost: a mine and
upgrader complex that runs at 106% of nameplate with cash costs of
C$22.19/bbl of SCO (Q2-2026), thermal SAGD/CSS pads that produce above
nameplate (Jackfish 136 kbbl/d on a 120 kbbl/d plant, Pike 1 pads at an SOR
of 1.8), and multilateral heavy-oil wells whose payout is measured in
quarters.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (i) Commodity netback — realized
SCO C$125.78/bbl and E&P liquids C$105.11/bbl in Q2-2026 against opex in
the C$10–22/bbl range; a US$1/bbl move in WTI is C$409M of annualized
operating cash flow (40-F sensitivity table, Q4-2025 volumes). (ii)
By-products and integration — sulphur (30% of Canada's supply, C$450M net
revenue in H1-2026), 50% of the North West Redwater refinery (92,748 bbl/d
of ULSD from bitumen), and a 15-year JKM-linked gas supply deal with
Cheniere from 2030. (iii) Zero-decline mining barrels acquired rather than
drilled — the Chevron oil-sands and Duvernay assets (2024, C$9.2B) and the
Shell AOSP swap (2025-11, +31,000 bbl/d of zero-decline bitumen for 10% of
the Scotford upgrader) — bought when they were cheap and now producing at
Q2-2026 prices.

**Protected:** a resource-and-cost mechanism, not a label. The moat is the
reserve life and its decline profile: 15.9 billion BOE proved (about 26
years at current rates) of which the mining and thermal barrels decline at
zero to low single digits, so sustaining capital is ~C$6B on ~C$19B of
funds flow at TTM prices and ~C$15–16B at mid-cycle. A competitor cannot
build Horizon or Albian for a decade and tens of billions of dollars, and
provincial approval for a new mine is currently unobtainable; the Oil Sands
Alliance's five members hold the asset class. What the moat does not
protect is price: the company is a price-taker on WTI, on the WCS
differential (16–20% of WTI in 2026) and on Canadian carbon policy, and
those three set the cash flow, not the moat.

**Control:** one class of common shares, one vote per share, unlimited
authorized; unlimited preferred authorized, none outstanding (40-F, share
capital). No 10% holder except Capital World Investors at 10.3% (passive,
per its 62-103F3 of 2026-02-02, cited in the circular). Directors and
officers hold ~2% (47 million shares; Executive Chair N. Murray Edwards is
the bulk of it). Nothing in the charter forecloses a takeover or an
activist; 11 of 13 directors are independent. Say-on-pay is advisory only.
