Sprouts is still a good small-box specialty grocer with a clean balance
sheet (zero revolver, ex-lease net cash), a self-funded ~8–10% unit-growth
runway whose 2026 vintage is beating plan, and a brand penetration (26% of
sales) that conventional grocers do not have. But the core fleet is losing
transactions to value channels, management has conceded the pressure "was
not cyclical," and the fix is a deliberate margin reset — price investment
broad enough to show up as ~90bp of Q4 gross-margin deleverage before the
national January roll-out — under a CEO who takes the chair in nine weeks.
The price now sits near fair value for the trough, not below it: the
implied return straddles the hurdle depending on which margin lands, and
the first evidence that traffic responds is dated. A turnaround priced at
fair value is a pass; it becomes interesting if transactions inflect or the
price falls to where the floor scenario clears the hurdle (~$40).

## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/SFM-2026-07-27.md` §2, with these deltas.

**Created:** 504 small-box (~23–30k sq ft) health-and-wellness grocery
stores in 25 states, produce at the centre, >70% of products attribute-led
(organic, gluten-free, grass-fed, non-GMO), >7,000 new items a year via a
"foraging" team that gets emerging brands onto the shelf early (10-K, Ex.
99.3 deck). E-commerce 16% of sales, +12% in Q3 (Q3 call). The customer
"skews higher income, slightly higher educated" and "is willing to make an
additional trip" (deck) — the problem in 2026 is that the less-engaged half
of them stopped making it, and the core customer has begun to soften too
(Konat, Q3 Q&A).

**Captured:** gross margin 38.2% in Q3 (38.8% FY25, 36.7% FY22) — far above
conventional grocery — from attribute mix, Sprouts Brand at 26% of sales
($2.3B in FY25, +33%), and self-distribution of produce and now meat/seafood
(95% of stores by end-2026). Operating margin 7.8% FY25 → 7.3% TTM → ~6.6%
FY26 guide → ">5%" 2027 floor. The capture is now being deliberately handed
back: "the lion's share of the P&L impact will come through gross margin and
that affordability investment" (CFO).

**Protected:** honestly narrow, and the Q3 call confirms it. Speed to shelf
for innovative brands, the foraging/merchandising cadence, a differentiated
store experience and a young loyalty dataset (~55% of sales identified by
end-2025). Nothing stops Kroger, Walmart or Aldi stocking organic produce
and grass-fed beef at a lower price — which is what management says is
happening ("competition across a number of channels continue to chase the
health and wellness customer"). The moat is identity and assortment speed,
not lock-in; when the price gap widens, the less-engaged customer leaves.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder (10-K cover: common stock $0.001 par, Nasdaq; undesignated preferred
authorised, none issued). Insiders 0.7%, institutions >100% of float
(stockanalysis statistics). Non-affiliate float $15.8B at June 2025 (10-K
cover). Nothing forecloses an activist or an unsolicited bid; the 10-K
itself lists "actions by activist stockholders" as a risk factor.
Leadership turnover is the live governance fact: CEO Jack Sinclair →
executive chairman and Nick Konat (COO since 2022) → CEO effective
2027-01-04 (8-K 2026-09-01); CTO James Bahrenburg stepped down 2026-10-12
(8-K 2026-10-14, "not the result of any disagreement"); a new Chief
Merchandising Officer and Chief Customer Officer both arrived in 2026 (deck).
