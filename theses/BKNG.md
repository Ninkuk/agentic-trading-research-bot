## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $199.61.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=6 (4 probable,
2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=2.

Booking is the largest accommodation marketplace in the world: 1.24B room
nights and $186B of gross bookings in 2025, converted at a ~14.5% revenue
take into a 35% operating margin on $320M of capex, with essentially all of
the cash handed back through buybacks that shrink the share count ~4% a
year. At $199.61 the price implies an 8.5–9.7% annual return on a base-case
path (7% fading to 5%, 2.5% terminal) — the range depends entirely on whether
the merchant-payments float that lifts reported FCF above earnings is
counted as owner cash. Against a 9.29% cost of equity that is fair, not
cheap, and the one structural question that decides the terminal value —
whether AI agents book *through* Booking or *around* it — has no evidence
yet in either direction. A fair price for an excellent business carrying an
unpriced open question is a pass; the flip is a lower price or evidence on
the agentic channel.

**Closest attack:** the float. TTM free cash flow ($9.54B) exceeds net
income ($7.21B) because deferred merchant bookings — travellers' money held
until check-in — grew with the merchant mix (73% of gross bookings, +4 pts
YoY). Count that growth as owner cash and the base case clears the hurdle by
+37bp; strip it and the same path prints −76bp. The 6.4% FCF yield that put
the name on the screen rests on the float.

## 2. Business

**Created:** a traveller gets the widest single inventory of places to stay
— 4.7M properties on Booking.com at June 30, 2026, of which 4.1M are
alternative accommodations and 500k hotels (Q2 10-Q) — with verified
reviews, one checkout, one customer-service desk, and increasingly flights,
cars and attractions on the same itinerary ("Connected Trip", a low-double-
digit share of Booking.com transactions and growing at twice the
transaction rate). The surplus is search cost and trust: an independent
hotel in Portugal is findable by a Korean traveller because Booking already
did the translation, payment, and dispute handling. A property gets demand
it cannot buy itself in 40 languages, paid for only on a completed stay.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not "a commission" (Q2 2026 10-Q
income statement, 10-K Item 1):

- *Agency revenue* — a commission on stays where the hotel collects the
  money; $1.90B in Q2, shrinking (−7% YoY) as merchant grows.
- *Merchant revenue* — Booking collects the traveller's payment, holds it
  as "deferred merchant bookings" ($10.1B at June 30) until check-in, and
  books the commission plus payment-related fees and FX spread; $5.13B in
  Q2, +15% YoY; 73% of gross bookings. This is where the float comes from.
- *Advertising and other* — Kayak/OpenTable and meta-search placements;
  $0.32B in Q2.

Aggregate take: $26.9B revenue on $186.1B gross bookings in 2025 (14.5%),
of which $8.2B was re-spent on performance and brand marketing (4.4% of
gross bookings), mostly at Google.

**Protected:** the mechanism is a two-sided liquidity lead that is
expensive to buy against. Booking spends $8B a year on marketing and still
runs a mid-50s direct mix; a challenger must buy the same clicks without
the repeat base (Genius levels 2–3 are >30% of active customers and a high-
50s share of room nights), the 4.7M-property supply, or the payments and
customer-service stack. That lead is real but it is not a lock: the 10-K's
own Item 1A (FY2025) says barriers to entry are low and that Google "links
travel search to its dominant search engine" and has integrated travel into
Gemini, while "AI agents may further evolve into full-service booking
platforms." The honest protection statement is: the supply, payments and
service layer is hard to replicate; the *discovery* layer is rented, and
whoever owns the front end can re-price the rent.

**Control:** a single class of common stock (Q2 10-Q balance sheet: one
"Common stock, $0.008 par value" line), no controlling holder; insiders own
0.2%, institutions 98% (stockanalysis); Vanguard and BlackRock are the
listed >5% holders (2026 DEF 14A). One share, one vote, no controller — the
null answer, which forecloses nothing.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue and operating income

## 5. Falsifiers

For the pass (flip toward buy):

- **Shift —** price at or below ~$170 (scenario E clears the US hurdle at
  $175 and by +48bp at $165) with no change in the Q3 read.
- **Shift —** Q3 room nights at or above 5% with direct mix up and
  marketing at or below 4.7% of gross bookings — the ex-conflict run rate
  reasserting itself.
- **Shift —** a disclosed, rising agentic/LLM referral share (Google AI
  Mode or OpenAI CPC) with stable take rate — evidence that the front end
  moves and Booking is still the fulfilment layer.

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** marketing as a percentage of gross bookings rising above
  ~5.5% while direct mix falls — the discovery rent repricing.
- **Break —** a quantified hotel-parity class judgment or settlement above
  ~$3B (2% of market cap), or a DMA/competition remedy that bans ranking
  by commission across the EU.
- **Shift —** room nights below 3% for two consecutive quarters outside a
  named exogenous shock.
- **Shift —** merchant mix stalling below 75% with contribution margin
  flat — the float stops growing and the raw-FCF base reverts to the
  ex-float one.

1. Room nights hold mid-single digits — **SURVIVED.** Attack: the series

   Evidence: adjusted EBITDA margin +40bp, sales & other 1.9% of gross
