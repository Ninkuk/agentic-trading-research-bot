## 2. Business

**Created:** a four-year, profession-oriented degree (medicine/health 
sciences, engineering/IT, business ≈ 75% of enrollments) from a nationally 
recognized brand, at a price the local middle class can pay out of pocket, in 
countries where public universities are capacity-constrained and 
underfunded. Participation rates are 35% in Mexico and 57% in Peru against 
developed-market levels well above that (Q3 slides, year-end 2024 data), so 
the surplus the student buys is access plus employability. For working adults 
the fully-online product adds flexibility at a lower price point.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (i) Face-to-face tuition from 18–24 
year olds, priced at local inflation each intake, collected privately — 
"essentially all of our revenues were generated from private pay sources as 
there are no material government-sponsored loan programs in Mexico or Peru" 
(10-Q Note 3). (ii) Fully-online tuition from working adults in Peru 
(>40,000 students) and Mexico, lower price per student, near-zero capex 
("an ROIC that is very superior because there is no CapEx in online", Q2-26 
call), diluting average revenue per student while lifting margin. (iii) New 
campuses in new cities (Puebla, Ate, Monterrey opened since 2025; Southern 
Lima and Mérida in 2027; land secured for 2028) that take 4–5 years to fill, 
~6,000 seats in a secondary city vs 9,000–11,000 in a capital, "very similar 
IRR profile" (Q3-26 call).

**Protected:** brand and scale, as mechanisms rather than labels. In a 
fragmented secondary city a nationally ranked brand (UVM top-5 in Mexico; 
UPC) wins "a very commanding consumer preference" (CEO) because a degree is a 
credence good bought once; a 500,000-student back office spreads marketing, 
curriculum and AI tooling over a base no local rival has; and medical-school 
and national licenses are hard to obtain. What it does not protect: the 
fully-online segment, where the CEO said plainly "our success is being copied 
by our competitors, just as we would've expected." Public universities are 
free and the 10-K names them as the primary competitor; the moat is in 
quality-adjusted access, not in price.

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controller. Class A and B were retired 
(10-K exhibit 3.4). Largest holders at 2026-03-24: FMR 14.0%, Vanguard 10.1%, 
Wengen Alberta 8.9% (the 2007 LBO vehicle; CPV/Snow Phipps direct its vote 
and together with CPV's direct stake reach 13.0%), BlackRock 7.0%; all 
directors and officers 1.5% (proxy). Wengen has registration and demand 
rights (ten requests) and a proxy over pre-IPO management shares; nothing 
forecloses a takeover or an activist path, and the sponsor stake is the 
supply to watch. Incentives: the 2025 AIP weighted Adjusted Financing EBITDA, 
revenue, new enrollments and unlevered FCF 25% each; PSUs on Adjusted EBITDA 
margin (proxy).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue 2021 → TTM $1.087B → 
$1.915B (×1.76); operating income $67.8M → $494.5M (×7.3); operating margin 
6.2% → 25.8%. By year (stockanalysis `financials/income-statement/`, raw): 
2021 1,086.7 / 67.8; 2022 1,242.3 / 270.2; 2023 1,484.3 / 341.9; 2024 
1,566.6 / 374.0; 2025 1,701.9 / 431.1; TTM 1,914.9 / 494.5. Part of the 
2021→2022 step is the divestiture-era corporate cost coming out; the 
2022→TTM run (revenue ×1.54, operating income ×1.83) is the operating 
business, still positive. Stock compensation is small ($14.2M TTM, 0.74% of 
revenue) and sits inside direct costs/G&A, so the income statement and the 
cash flow describe the same business. No persistent losses, no heavy 
leverage: gross debt $204.2M incl. finance leases vs $189.9M cash (release); 
stockanalysis `debt` 782.6M includes $579.9M of operating lease liabilities.

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** a Peru main-intake (Feb/Mar 2027, read on the Q1-27 call) with 
  new enrollments flat or down in fully online, or management guiding revenue 
  per student down more than mid-single digits: the online lever is a price 
  war, condition 3 fails.

  universities differently, or re-restricting virtual credits (the 2024 
  amendment reversed): the regulatory scenario input becomes a condition 
  failure.
- **Shift —** Adjusted EBITDA margin flat or down for a full year ex-new-
  campus drag, or capex running above ~8% of revenue for two years without 
  enrollment acceleration: condition 2 weakens, conversion falls below 50%.
- **Shift —** a Wengen secondary or 13D changing control dynamics: supply 
  overhang, revalue not sell.
