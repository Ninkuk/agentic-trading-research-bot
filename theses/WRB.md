## 2. Business

**Created:** underwrites specialty and excess & surplus commercial risks the
standard market prices badly, through ~60 decentralized operating units each
with its own P&L and underwriting authority. Niche expertise — liquor
liability, habitational, excess comp, private client, A&H — prices risk a
generalist cannot. The insured gets coverage a standard carrier will not
write; the broker gets an answer from a specialist rather than a committee.

**Captured:** two engines, plus a small third. (1) Underwriting profit: TTM
net premiums earned ~$12.6B at a Q2'26 combined ratio of 90.0%, roughly $318M
of quarterly pretax underwriting income. (2) Float: ~$23.2B of gross reserves
funds a $34.2B investment portfolio (75.8% fixed maturities, AA- average
quality, 3.2-year duration) earning a record $418.7M of quarterly net
investment income — now the larger engine at ~57% of pretax. (3) Non-insurance
businesses and fee income, ~$134M of quarterly revenue.

**Protected:** a mechanism, not a label — decentralized underwriting authority
paired with a demonstrated willingness to shrink. The unit that can say no is
the unit whose own P&L bears the loss; the Reinsurance & Monoline Excess
segment cut NPW to $306M (−9% YoY) into soft casualty ceding commissions while
printing a 79.3% CR, and commercial-auto exposure is being reduced while rate
is taken. Insiders own the outcome. What this does **not** protect against is
industry-wide soft pricing compressing everybody's margin at once — that is
cycle, not competition, and the company's answer (60 years of cycle
management, ROE never below 8.7% since 2010) is a track record, not a
structural barrier. §4 prices exactly this distinction.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share. The Berkley family
holds ~24.6% (13D/A, event 2026-07-17, 95.56M shares across LLCs, a 2011 GST
trust, a grantor trust and the founder's estate); Mitsui Sumitomo Insurance
holds 15.1% (58.78M shares, 13D/A event 2026-06-03) with one board seat under
a Framework Agreement. Two anchors at ~40% foreclose an unsolicited takeover
and any activist path, and leave a 243.5M-share float (62.7% of shares out).
Rob Berkley Jr. has been CEO since 2015; the founder died in July 2026.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal ROE.* Run A trips the tool's
  growth-without-reinvestment warning (base FCF = base earnings leaves nothing
  retained) and is quoted only as the ceiling of the earnings-based reads.
  Runs B/D/E each charge retention = g/ROE and are the answer to it. Their
  implied terminal ROEs are 9.44%, 8.02% and 7.15% against the 8.26% hurdle —
  B assumes a modest surviving excess return, D assumes excess returns fade to
  roughly zero, E assumes mild terminal value destruction. Damodaran's EVA
  base rate (~29% of firms earn above their cost of capital) makes D's fade
  the default, not the pessimistic case.
- *The excess-return cross-check, which is the real finding.* Justified P/B =
  (ROE − g)/(r − g). Inverting at the market price: required perpetual ROE =
  2.36% + 2.7593 × (8.26% − 2.36%) = **18.65%**. Against that bar: trailing
  ROE 20.2%, five-year 19.5%, but the 2010–2020 mean was ~12% (range
  8.7%–15.0%). The bull's correct rebuttal is that 2010–2020 was ZIRP, so
  price the tailwind: $34.2B of net invested assets on $9.84B of equity is
  3.47x investment leverage, and a book yield of 4.8% today against roughly
  3.25% in the ZIRP era is +1.55pt × $34.2B = $530M pretax = $425M after tax =
  **+4.3pts of ROE**, i.e. a structurally-uplifted mid-cycle ROE near
  **16.3%** — 2.3 points short of what the price requires. The 3.25% ZIRP-era
  book yield is an estimate (§6 #2), so test it: at 3.75% the mid-cycle
  estimate is 14.9%, at 2.75% it is 17.7%. The shortfall survives the whole
  plausible range.
- *Why the bull's own arithmetic is not admissible.* At a 20% ROE and the
  company's actual ~70% payout, sustainable g is 6%, and justified P/B =
  (20% − 6%)/(8.26% − 6%) = 6.2x. That number is not evidence of anything: it
  is the Gordon formula detonating as g approaches r. Terminal growth is
  capped at the 10-year breakeven precisely to stop this, which is why the
  two-stage runs above are the governing frame.
- *Base-year integrity.* Cash tax rate 19.77% against management's stated ~23%

  haircut. Cat load in H1'26 was benign (2.0 points in Q2); a normal cat year
  worsens the base, and Runs D/E subsume it.
- *Market share.* 5% a year for five years takes revenue from $14.90B to

  $450–500B (order-of-magnitude, not pulled this run), so WRB goes from ~3% to

  forecast fails, if it fails, on margin, not on size.
- *Terminal risk (10-K Item 1A, FY2025, filed 2026-02-27).* The disclosed
  structural risks that bear on year-10 cash flows are the P&C pricing cycle,

- *Distribution clamp.* Runs D and E imply 7.36% and 6.51%, inside Damodaran's

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy):**

- **Shift —** the beta convention. If the 0.8 floor is rejected on argued
  grounds — and it is the single most load-bearing convention in §4 — the
  hurdle falls to 6.11%, the required perpetual ROE to ~15.5%, and the ~16.3%
  mid-cycle estimate clears it. This is a disagreement about method, available
  today, not a fact awaiting disclosure.
- **Shift —** price. Below roughly $60 (P/B ~2.37 on the market's share basis)
  the required perpetual ROE falls to ~16.4% and Run D crosses the hurdle;
  below ~$55 Run E does. The 52-week low is $62.87.
- **Shift —** proof the ROE plateau is real: two or three consecutive quarters
  of combined ratio in the low 90s with written rate *re*-accelerating and
  pretax margin back above 16.5%, i.e. FY2025's flat year shown to be the cat
  year it looks like rather than the first step down.
- **Shift —** a durable move higher in the 10-year that lifts the book yield
  faster than it lifts the hurdle (the book yield is a 3.2-year average
  repricing into spot; the hurdle repriced instantly). The asymmetry is
  temporary and it runs in the owner's favour.

**For an owner (sell):**

- **Break —** the reserve story fails: material multi-quarter adverse casualty
  development — other liability, commercial auto or assumed reinsurance
  strengthening at $100M+ scale, not the current $7–14M. The discipline claim
  would be false, and the book value the P/B is struck on would be overstated.
- **Break —** underwriting discipline fails: accident-year ex-cat combined
  ratio sustained above 95% while net premiums written keep growing — growing
  into a soft market, the industry's standard way of destroying a decade.
- **Shift —** pricing: rate ex-comp goes negative, or earned rate falls clearly
  below stated loss trend for two or more quarters. Revalue on Run D or worse.
- **Shift —** control: the estate or family bloc begins large open-market
  sales, or Mitsui Sumitomo moves off 15% in either direction. Revalue
  governance and float — and note that a float expansion would also raise the
  measured beta toward the floor this document already applies.
