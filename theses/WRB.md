leverage structurally lifts mid-cycle ROE, and (b) the reverse DCF still
clears the hurdle at a 15%-ROE normalized base (see §4).

## 2. Business

**Created:** underwrites specialty and excess & surplus commercial risks the
standard market prices badly — through ~60 decentralized operating units,
each with its own P&L and underwriting authority, so niche expertise (liquor
liability, habitational, excess comp, private-client, A&H) prices risk the
generalist cannot. The customer gets coverage a standard carrier won't write;
the distribution partner gets an answer from a specialist.

**Captured:** two engines. (1) Underwriting profit: TTM net premiums earned
~$12.6B at a 90.0% Q2 combined ratio → ~$318M quarterly pretax underwriting
income. (2) Float: $23.2B gross reserves fund a $34.2B investment portfolio
(75.8% fixed maturities, AA- average, duration 3.2y) earning a record $418.7M
quarterly NII — now the larger earnings engine (~57% of pretax).
Non-insurance businesses and fee income are a small third stream (~$134M
quarterly revenue).

**Protected:** the moat is a mechanism, not a label — decentralized
underwriting authority plus demonstrated willingness to shrink. The
Reinsurance & Monoline Excess segment's NPW fell to $306M (−9% YoY) into soft
casualty-reinsurance ceding commissions while its CR printed 79.3%;
commercial-auto exposure is being cut while taking rate. Insiders own the
outcome (family 24.6%). What it does NOT protect against: industry-wide soft
pricing compressing everyone's margin — that is cycle, not competition, and
the company's answer (60 years of cycle management, ROE never below 8.7%
since 2010, including COVID) is a track record, not a structural barrier.
