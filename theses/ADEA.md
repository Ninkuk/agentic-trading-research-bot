## 1. Verdict and thesis

> **PASS at $26.28.** kill-thesis: **SOUND** — conditions=3 (3 probable,
> 0 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0.

Adeia is a genuinely excellent little machine — 150 employees, 100% gross
margin, 21.7% ROIC, a Piotroski 7 and an Altman Z of 4.01 — that rents out
14,250 patent assets to media and semiconductor companies who would rather pay
than litigate. The semiconductor half is real and accelerating: AMD, sued in
November 2025, took a multi-year portfolio license four months later, and
hybrid bonding is on a genuine adoption ramp. The problem is the price. At
$26.28 the stock has run 126% off its November 2025 low of $11.61, and on a
clean post-separation owner-cash-flow base it implies about an **8% annual
return against an 8.89% hurdle** — 107bp *short*, before you charge anything
for a moat with a statutory expiry date, a CEO who is leaving, and a company
guiding its own current-year revenue *down* while raising its five-year target.
This is a good business at a price that already pays you nothing for the risk.

**Closest attack:** the growth path. If the semiconductor ramp compounds at 12%
rather than management's own 7.7%, the base case moves from 7.81% to 9.02% and
the call flips. The attack survives only because it needs the growth *and* the
higher base: on the clean $124.9M base, even the 12% path clears the hurdle by
just **+13bp**. The bull must be right about growth *and* wrong about the base
to make this stock work at $26.28.

1. **The owner-cash-flow base is ~$125M, not the $188.6M the TTM prints.**
   *probable* — clean post-separation years only (2023–2025 FCF averages
   $171.95M) less the current SBC run rate ($37M) less average patent purchases
   ($10M) gives $124.9M. An independent route (TTM less both haircuts) gives
   $135.0M. If instead the true base is the headline $188.6M, the implied
   return is 11% and this call is wrong.
2. **Management's own 7.7% five-year path is generous, not conservative.**
   *probable* — FY26 revenue guidance of $395–435M sits *below* FY2025's
   $443.4M, forcing an H2'26 decline of 13–28% against H2'25. The clean
   semiconductor run rate is Q2'26's $14.8M, i.e. **$59M annualized**; the $200M
   target needs 3.4× on top of that. Refuting this condition — evidence that
   growth beats management's own target — is the live path to a buy.
3. **The 8.89% hurdle is right for this risk, and if anything too low.**

## 2. Business

**Created:** nothing, in the ordinary sense. Adeia's customer does not receive
a product, a service, or a surplus — it receives *freedom to operate*. Charter,
Comcast, Google (YouTube TV), Hulu + Live TV, Disney, SanDisk, Kioxia, AMD and
now L'Oréal and ten RPX members pay Adeia because litigating against 14,250
patent assets costs more, and risks more, than the license does. That is an
honest description and it should be stated plainly: this is a toll on other
people's products, not a value-creating service. The consumer-surplus test the
skill asks for does not apply — the counterparty is paying to make a risk go
away, and would pay zero if the risk did.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not one.
(a) *Per-subscriber recurring royalties* from MVPDs and virtual MVPDs — a
monthly fee per subscriber, which decays with the traditional pay-TV base and
grows with YouTube TV and Hulu + Live TV. (b) *Fixed-fee multi-year portfolio
licenses* to OTT, consumer-electronics, e-commerce and semiconductor
counterparties — signed in lumps, recognized largely at signing under ASC 606,
collected over years. This is why revenue is violently lumpy and why the
balance sheet carries **$177.7M of unbilled contracts receivable** against only
$14.3M of ordinary accounts receivable. (c) *Litigation-driven settlements* —
past damages plus a go-forward license, as with AMD. Management calls itself
"a small volume, high dollar shop"; the income statement agrees.

**Protected:** by statute, with a clock. The moat is the patent grant itself —
a legal monopoly that expires. Two mechanisms stack on top of it and are worth
naming separately. First, *portfolio-wide licensing*: a customer facing 14,250
assets cannot practically clear them one by one, so individual expiries do not
end the relationship (>90% renewal rate). Second, *credible enforcement* —
Adeia has demonstrated, repeatedly and fast, that it will sue and that
defendants settle: Disney settled across Germany, the Netherlands and Brazil in
December 2025; AMD settled four months after suit, with an ITC exclusion order
pending against its 3D V-Cache products. Against that, the 10-K's own language:
"our patents will expire in the future... Our current U.S. issued patents
expire at various times through the next two decades." The last currently
issued patent expires March 2045; the weighted-average remaining life is not
disclosed (§6.1).

**Control:** one class of common stock, no controlling holder, no dual-class
structure. Insiders hold 2.28%; institutions 105.47% of shares outstanding
(over 100% alongside 7.10% short float — securities lending, not an error).
Nothing forecloses an unsolicited takeover or an activist campaign. That is the
null answer, and it earns its place here because on July 22, 2026 the
compensation committee enhanced severance terms — including 12-month equity
vesting acceleration on a qualifying termination — for the CFO, CRO and CLO
while the CEO search runs (§3).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue grew 20.4% from 2021 to
TTM; operating income grew 76.2%.

The direction is positive, but the mechanism is not productivity — it is a

22.6%, H2'25 59.4%, H1'26 30.0%. The TTM 46.5% is a deal-timing artifact, not
a run rate, and no valuation should be built on it.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The TTM is a peak and management says so.** FY2026 revenue guidance of
  $395–435M is *below* FY2025's $443.4M and 12% below the $470.9M TTM. H1'26
  revenue was $200.9M, so the guide implies H2'26 of $194–234M against H2'25's
  $270M — a 13–28% decline. GAAP net income is guided to $57.2–80.4M against
  TTM GAAP net income of $122.7M, a 44% fall at the midpoint. The CFO guided
  full-year cash from operations to "about $150 million" against TTM CFO of
  $191.0M. Every headline multiple on this name — the 24.2× trailing P/E, the
  6.5% FCF yield that put it on the `candidates` screen — is computed on a base
  its issuer is guiding away from. On the FY26 GAAP net income midpoint of
  $68.8M the market cap is 42×. This is the single most load-bearing finding in
  the document.

- **Two of the five years in a naive FCF average are contaminated by Xperi.**
  Adeia spun off the Xperi product business on October 3, 2022. The income
  statement restates for discontinued operations; the cash flow statement does
  not cleanly. The tell is depreciation and amortization in the cash flow
  statement: $227.2M (2021), $114.2M (2022), $95.3M (2023), $72.8M (2024),
  $58.6M (2025) — against continuing-operations intangible amortization on the
  income statement of $98.1M in 2021 and $97.1M in 2022. A $129M gap in 2021
  and $17M in 2022 is the product business's cash flow still sitting in
  operating activities. So 2021's $220.8M and 2022's $170.4M FCF are not
  comparable to today's business. Restricting to clean years, 2023–2025 FCF
  averages **$171.95M**, and that is the number the base case normalizes on.
  This correction moves the implied return *against* the stock, from 8.11% to
  7.81%.

- **The semiconductor number contains a settlement.** CFO Keith Jones gave semi
  revenue as $14.8M in Q2'26 and ~$48M year-to-date, against $26M for all of
  FY2025. Q1'26 semi was therefore ~$33.2M — more than double the Q2 figure, in
  the quarter the AMD license was signed (March 9, 2026, resolving the WDTX and
  ITC actions filed the prior November). A patent settlement carries past
  damages recognized on signing. The clean run rate is Q2's $14.8M, or **$59M
  annualized** — still a 2.3× step-up on FY2025 and genuinely encouraging, but
  the $200M long-term target needs 3.4× on top of that. Sell-side colour
  (low-confidence) estimates AMD's ongoing royalty starts near $2M/quarter and
  ramps to $5–10M; if that is right it corroborates rather than contradicts the
  reading that Q1's step-up was catch-up, not run rate.

- **Management is paid on revenue, not on cash.** The 2026 proxy sets the 2025
  annual cash incentive at **50% revenue / 30% non-GAAP operating expenses
  excluding litigation / 20% non-financial**, identically for all five named
  executives. PSUs vest on **annual EBITDA (excluding litigation)** targets set
  at the start of each of three separate one-year periods, modified by a
  three-year relative TSR. Three consequences worth stating. First, no metric
  anywhere in the plan is a cash-flow or per-share measure — which is precisely
  the incentive that produces a $177.7M unbilled receivable balance and a
  diluted share count up 2.0% year over year. Second, both financial metrics
  exclude litigation expense, i.e. management is paid on a number that treats
  the cost of enforcing its only asset ($23.0M TTM) as if it were not a cost.
  Third, the revenue goal is measured against "the midpoint of our revenue
  guidance" — management sets the guidance and is then paid for hitting its own
  midpoint. The three-year relative TSR modifier is the single genuinely
  long-term alignment in the structure, and it is a modifier, not a gate.

- **DIRECTV is a live hole, not a resolved one.** DIRECTV filed a declaratory
  judgment action in NDCA on December 29, 2025 seeking non-infringement of
  seven Adeia patents and invalidity of three; Adeia countersued in SDNY on
  January 12, 2026 for breach of contract and misappropriation of trade
  secrets, and moved for a preliminary injunction three days later. As of the
  FY2025 10-K no dates were set in either case. A licensee that files a DJ
  action has stopped paying. Adeia then sued Fubo on July 1, 2026 (four
  patents). Management's own framing — "the timing of resolution to any
  litigation is difficult to predict and thus could impact us in the short
  term" — is the honest version. Note the asymmetry the bulls correctly cite:
  these disputes are also the path to the *top* of the guidance range, because
  a settlement lands as lumpy catch-up revenue.

- **The cash-tax gap is structural, not a burning fuse.** TTM cash taxes paid
  were $12.95M against pretax income of $166.4M and book tax expense of
  $43.71M — a 7.8% cash rate against a 26.3% effective rate. My first reading
  was that this is a shield running out: federal NOLs are indeed effectively
  exhausted at **$2.1M**. The deferred tax note refutes that reading. Net
  deferred tax assets *grew* from $31.0M to $52.1M during 2025, and the
  research-credit DTA grew from $48.9M to **$67.1M** — Adeia is generating R&D
  credits faster than it consumes them, which it will keep doing as long as
  R&D spend ($71.7M TTM and rising) continues, which the innovation engine
  requires anyway. Foreign tax credits jumped from $0.3M to $13.2M (Korean
  withholding on the memory licensees, creditable). There is also a $110.8M
  valuation allowance against state and foreign attributes that could partly
  release. The honest conclusion is that the low cash tax rate is more durable
  than it first looks. This does not help or hurt the valuation, because the
  base case is built from actual historical cash flow that already embeds the
  low rate — but it removes a downside I had drafted as a condition.

- **Revenue runs ahead of cash, cumulatively.** Unbilled contracts receivable
  is $132.9M current plus $44.8M long-term = **$177.7M**, against deferred
  revenue of $92.5M — a net $85.2M of revenue recognized ahead of collection.
  On the record in both directions: it is $177.7M of cash already earned and
  not yet banked (an asset the market cap does not obviously credit), *and* it
  means GAAP revenue in a signing year systematically overstates cash
  economics. Deferred revenue more than doubled in H1'26 ($19.7M → $43.9M
  current), a genuinely good near-term cash signal.

- **The CEO is leaving, and the retention paperwork followed.** Paul Davis
  notified the board on May 4, 2026 that he intends to step down "to focus on
  his health and personal pursuits"; the 8-K states it is not the result of any
  disagreement, and a successor is targeted by Q4 2026. The stock topped at
  $34.34 that same day and fell 17% the next session. Then on July 22, 2026 the
  compensation committee enhanced severance agreements for the three remaining
  senior executives. The benign read is ordinary retention through a
  transition; the other read is preparation for a change of control. I cannot
  distinguish them from the filing and will not pretend otherwise — noted, not
  scored.

- **Buybacks are not shrinking the share count.** Diluted shares were 114.4M in
  Q2'26 against 112.2M in Q2'25 — **up 2.0%** — despite $10.0M of repurchases
  in the quarter and $65.7M over the TTM. SBC of $36.9M TTM outruns the
  buyback; reported buyback yield is −0.55%. FY2025 repurchases averaged
  $13.55/share and Q2'26's averaged $28.33/share: the program is
  price-insensitive, which is a mild negative on capital allocation, though
  buying at $13.55 was plainly right.

- **Asset-light survives the balance-sheet test.** Trailing capex is $2.4M and
  management guides ~$2M for FY26 — the kind of number that usually deserves
  suspicion. The 10-K commitments footnote clears it: total unconditional
  purchase obligations are **$7.5M** ($3.2M due 2026, $2.4M 2027, $1.9M
  thereafter), plus operating-expense reimbursements capped at $7.5M/year under
  the Xperi cross-business agreement, "not material" to date. There is no
  hidden capacity obligation. The real reinvestment is R&D ($71.7M TTM, already
  in opex) and patent purchases ($16.6M TTM, charged against the base in §4).

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs and pairing.** Levered TTM free cash flow of **$188,571,000**
(`fcf` = `ncfo` $190,952,000 + `capex` −$2,381,000, post-interest under US
GAAP) paired against **market cap $2,898,162,342** (110,280,150 shares at
$26.28). Net debt is set to **zero by the pairing rule** — the $258.2M of net
debt is already served inside `ncfo`, and pairing this flow against the
$3,156,343,342 enterprise value would double-count it. There are no minority
interests, no pension underfunding, and no probable litigation payout to
haircut (Adeia is plaintiff in every material matter). Two haircuts apply and
both are charged: **stock compensation of $36,943,000 TTM** (added back inside
`ncfo`, but it is compensation, not cash the owner keeps — the SBC-adjusted
$151.628M matches `stocks.db`'s own `adjustedFCF`), and **patent purchases of
$16,591,000 TTM** (`salePurchaseIntangibles`, an investing outflow `fcf`
excludes; for a licensor whose asset legally expires, replenishment is
maintenance, and the growth path below charges for growth separately). Beta
0.969, inside the 0.8–1.2 stable band, used unclamped.

**Hurdle.** rf 4.74% + beta 0.969 × ERP 4.28% = **8.89%** (Damodaran implied
ERP and 10-year Treasury as of August 1, 2026). Cross-check: a mature company's
cost of capital ≈ rf + 4.5% = 9.24%, and stockanalysis independently computes
ADEA's WACC at 9.24% — consistent. One caveat the beta hides: realized
volatility is 71–78% annualized (options table below) against a 0.97 beta, so
almost all of ADEA's risk is idiosyncratic. The hurdle is the right number for
a diversified holder and understates the risk to a concentrated one.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal-ROE warning, answered.* Scenario A trips the
  `growth without reinvestment` warning: base FCF of $188.6M exceeds even cash
  earnings of $183.1M (GAAP net income $122.7M + acquired-intangible
  amortization $60.4M), giving a *negative* terminal reinvestment rate of
  −3.0% while claiming 2% perpetual growth. Answered by cutting terminal growth
  to zero (A0), which drops the implied return from 11% to 9% and the spread
  from +176bp to +55bp — the headline case's entire margin over the hurdle is
  terminal-growth arithmetic the earnings base cannot fund. The cash-earnings
  add-back is also *less* defensible here than for a typical acquirer: for a
  patent licensor, amortization of acquired patents tracks the real consumption
  of a legally expiring asset, so on GAAP earnings the warning is worse, not
  better.
- *Market share.* Management's $600M target decomposes as $400M media + $200M
  semiconductor; compounding 7.7% for five years from $415M gives $602M.
  Against a semiconductor market management itself sizes at >$1.5 trillion,
  $200M of royalties is **0.013%**; against global pay-TV plus OTT subscription
  revenue, $400M is well under half a percent. There is no "bigger than the
  market" failure — a licensor's binding constraint is enforceability and
  negotiating leverage, not addressable market.
- *Terminal growth vs the Item 1A terminal risk.* The dominant structural risk
  in the risk factors is patent expiry: "our patents will expire in the future,
  the timing of which varies from jurisdiction to jurisdiction. Our current
  U.S. issued patents expire at various times through the next two decades.
  Consequently, to maintain and grow our licensable portfolio of IP rights we
  need to develop or acquire successful innovations." A 2.0% terminal rate asks
  the innovation engine to replace expiring claims forever and add real growth
  on top; management's own accounting disagrees, amortizing *acquired* patents
  over **3 to 10 year** useful lives. I have kept 2.0% as the generous
  assumption and shown at F what 1.0% does. Nothing in this document depends on
  the terminal rate: the base case already fails the hurdle at the generous
  rate, and cutting it only widens the failure.
- *Base-year cash tax rate.* The 7.8% cash rate against a 26.3% book rate looks
  like a fuse, and the deferred tax note says it is not (§3). Nothing is
  charged for tax normalization, and on the evidence nothing should be — the
  base is historical cash flow that already embeds the low rate.
- *Serial-acquirer check.* Adeia buys patent portfolios (six tuck-ins for
  $9.5M in Q2'26 alone) and `fcf` excludes that spend entirely. Charged against
  the base rather than growing the organic base only — stated, so a reader who
  disagrees can undo it. Note the internal consistency requirement: the base
  case assumes 7.7% growth *and* charges the acquisition spend that partly buys
  it. Removing the charge without cutting the growth path would be
  double-counting in the bull's favour.
- *Cash-heavy check.* Not applicable: $137.1M of cash against $392.6M of debt,
  i.e. net debt. No excess-cash adjustment.
- *Distribution clamp.* The US median cost of capital is ~7.79% and 80% of US
  firms sit between 5.26% and 9.88% (Damodaran Data Update 5, 2026). The base
  case's 7.81% sits almost exactly at the median and well inside the band, so
  the clamp does not itself kill the name — it says the market is pricing ADEA
  as an average-risk US company. Given a moat with a statutory expiry, a 37-point
  swing in half-year operating margin and 71–78% realized volatility, that is
  the mispricing: not the level, the risk it ignores.
- *Excess-return fade.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of capital
  (Damodaran EVA dataset). ADEA's 21.7% ROIC is genuinely above its 9.2% WACC
  today; the terminal story needs that spread defended against expiry, and the
  fact that would settle it is not disclosed (§6.1).
