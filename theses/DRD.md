## 2. Business

**Created:** DRDGOLD does not mine rock. It hydraulically re-mines the
tailings storage facilities (TSFs) that a century of Witwatersrand gold
mining left across Johannesburg — ~25Mt a year at Ergo (East Rand, 3.5t of
gold) and FWGR (West Rand, 1.3t) — pumps the slurry up to 135km through
pipelines to two carbon-in-leach plants, recovers ~0.19 g/t, and
redeposits the residue on lined, cladded, vegetated modern facilities.
Three parties get value: the shareholder gets an unhedged, debt-free
gold-price exposure with no underground safety, seismic or geological
grade risk; the state and the landowner get a legacy pollution source
removed and land freed for redevelopment (51ha in FY2026); the neighbours
get dust exceedances down to 0.5% of samples and 900ML/yr less potable
water drawn. The customer for the gold is Rand Refinery (DRDGOLD owns 11%);
gold is a commodity and the buyer is irrelevant to the economics.

**Captured:** one mechanism — the spread between the ZAR gold price and the
all-in cost of moving and treating a tonne of sand — and it is captured
with almost no working capital. FY2026 (results booklet): 4,839kg
produced, average price received R2,289,250/kg (+40%), cash operating cost
R967,523/kg (+7%; R188/t), AISC R1,078,068/kg (+8%), AISC margin 53% (FY2025
38.8%). Ergo (R1.12M/kg cash cost, 76% of revenue) is the lower-grade,
higher-cost engine; FWGR (R561k/kg cash cost, 76% operating margin) is the
growth leg Vision 2028 doubles. Two second-order captures: the 2024 solar
farm plus BESS (R2.9B) now supplies 146GWh, cutting Eskom draw per tonne
from 13.6 to 8.6kWh and saving R13.50–14.50/t at Ergo (call); and the
mining-tax formula lets unredeemed capex shelter income, so FY2026 cash
tax was R489M against R1,627M booked — a shield that fades as Vision 2028
capex is redeemed (deferred tax liability R2.9B, the largest liability).
Cash returned: 65% of FCF as dividends in FY2026 (interim 50c + final 120c
per ordinary share; ~$1.05/ADS gross), policy "return excess cash over a
predetermined buffer and cash reserved for capital projects" (20-F Item 8).

**Protected:** the protection is a resource position, not a franchise.
DRDGOLD owns or holds the rights to 5.85Moz of reserves (June 2025, +67Mt
Kloof 2 since) spread over the largest tailings footprint in South Africa,
with the TSF deposition capacity (Daggafontein 120Mt, RTSF 800Mt, Withok
310Mt pending) that a competitor would need years of licensing to
replicate — deposition space, not gold, is the binding constraint, which
is why Vision 2028 is mostly dams and pipes. Sibanye-Stillwater, the only
party holding comparable Witwatersrand dumps, is the controlling
shareholder rather than a rival. There is no pricing power (gold is gold),
no customer lock-in, and the operating know-how (blend management, 0.193
vs 0.183 g/t is R60M) is real but not proprietary. The moat protects
volume and life, never margin.

**Control:** one class of ordinary shares, one vote each, no golden share.
Sibanye Gold Proprietary Limited (wholly owned by Sibanye-Stillwater)
holds 50.1% (50.16% at 2025-09-30) and "has the ability to control our
board of directors ... and all matters requiring shareholder approval"
(20-F FY2025 Item 3D). Special resolutions need 75%, so minorities hold a
blocking stake on constitutional changes only. Insiders hold 0.145%; float
is 43.2M of 86.7M ADS-equivalents (49.8%). This forecloses any unsolicited
takeover or activist path, and makes a Sibanye buy-in or vend-in the only
plausible control event; the 20-F names "any sale or expectation of sale"
of Sibanye's block as a share-price risk. Board: two executives (CEO Niël
Pretorius, 19 years presenting; CFO Henriette Hooijer since 2025-10), eight
non-executives including Sibanye's Richard Brady, six independent.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive — but price leverage, not volume
leverage, and symmetric.** Revenue / operating income (R million,
stockanalysis income statement, FY June): FY2022 5,119 / 1,171 · FY2023
5,496 / 1,366 · FY2024 6,240 / 1,556 · FY2025 7,878 / 2,858 · FY2026 11,159
/ 5,742. Revenue 2.2× and operating income 4.9× over four years while gold
produced stayed in a 4.8–5.0t band; operating margin 22.9% → 51.5%. Every
point of it came from the ZAR gold price, and the lever runs the same way
down: at FY2024's R/kg the FY2026 cost base would earn a ~25% margin.
