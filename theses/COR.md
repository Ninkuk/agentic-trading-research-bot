## 2. Business

**Created:** A pharmacy, hospital or oncology practice gets next-day
delivery of any of tens of thousands of drugs from one truck, one invoice
and one credit line, instead of contracting with hundreds of manufacturers;
a manufacturer gets national reach, returns handling, chargeback
administration and credit risk taken off its hands. For specialty drugs —
cold-chain, high-cost, physician-administered — the service widens to GPO
contracting, reimbursement support, 3PL and clinical-trial logistics (World
Courier). Community oncologists and retina specialists get a management
platform that lets them stay independent of hospital systems.

**Captured:** Five distinct mechanisms. (1) Brand distribution: a
fee-for-service percentage of list price paid by the manufacturer, plus
prompt-pay discounts — low margin, scales with list price, which is why list
price cuts are the structural worry. (2) Generics: a sourcing spread, bought
through the WBAD joint purchasing arrangement with Walgreens and sold at a
markup — the highest-margin pills in the truck. (3) Specialty distribution
and GPO administrative fees to physician practices and health systems — the
Part B buy-and-bill channel, the fastest-growing profit pool. (4) MSO
ownership: since January 2025 (RCA, 85%) and February 2026 (OneOncology,
~92%) Cencora consolidates practice-management earnings directly; gross
margin rose 61bp to 4.16% in Q3 largely from this. (5) International
(Alliance Healthcare European wholesale, World Courier, 3PL) — $7.7B
revenue and $166M operating income in Q3. Consolidated margins are thin by
design: TTM gross 3.96%, operating 1.35% on $332.8B revenue.

**Protected:** Scale and density in a three-firm oligopoly (with McKesson
and Cardinal): a new entrant would need a national DC network, DEA and
state licences, manufacturer authorisations and the balance sheet to carry
$21B of inventory, to compete for a 1.35% operating margin. Payables of
$54.7B exceed receivables plus inventory of $46.5B — suppliers fund the
working capital. In specialty the protection is relationship depth:
three decades of GPO and distribution ties to community oncology, now
locked in by ownership. What is *not* protected: the customer side. Ten
customers are 66% of revenue, the largest is private-equity owned, and
customers insource freely (Express Scripts/CuraScript took biosimilar
fulfilment in-house in FY26; a large oncology customer left in July 2025).
The moat holds against entrants, not against the buyers.

**Control:** One share, one vote; no controlling holder. The FY25 10-K
carries no related-party note for Walgreens, formerly a large holder (its
current holding, if any, was not verified in this run); insiders hold
0.39%, institutions 97.6% (stockanalysis statistics). Nothing forecloses an
activist or a bid beyond antitrust — a three-firm industry cannot
consolidate further.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue FY21→TTM $214.0B →
$332.8B (+56%); GAAP operating income $2.68B → $4.49B (+68%); operating
margin 1.25% → 0.93% (FY23 trough) → 1.35% TTM.
