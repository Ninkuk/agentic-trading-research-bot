## 1. Verdict and thesis

> **PASS at $9.88.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (3 probable,
> 2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=2.

NerdWallet is a trusted consumer-finance comparison brand whose original
engine — free Google traffic monetized by referral fees — is being melted by
AI answer engines, and whose replacement engine — bought traffic plus
"owned audiences" (in-house insurance agents, loan brokering, registrations)
— is real but young. The price is cheap against current cash flow: a fully
normalized, zero-growth owner FCF of ~$70M implies ~12%/yr against a 10.0%
hurdle, and management is retiring ~9% of the share count per year. But the
zero-growth case is the *mid* case, not the conservative one: with credit
cards −24% in FY2025 and SMB still shrinking, a persistent ~−5%/yr decline
puts the implied return below hurdle. The single most load-bearing condition
— that consumer-vertical offsets keep outrunning search decay — is merely
plausible today, and its first real test is the Q3 print (Nov 5), a guide
that leans on one-time student-loan and acquisition tailwinds. Interesting,
not proven; the price does not yet pay for the uncertainty the transition
carries.

**Closest attack:** the revenue "growth" that makes the flat-forever base
look conservative is bought, cyclical, and concentrated — 59% of Q2 revenue
was re-spent on performance marketing, the FY25 insurance surge (+47%) was
one carrier cohort's ad budgets and the largest carrier has already wobbled,
and personal-loans growth is near/below-prime lender budgets that vanish in
a credit downturn. Strip those and the "flat" base is a declining one.

Load-bearing conditions (5 — 3 probable, 2 plausible):

1. *plausible* — Total revenue holds roughly flat or better through the AI
   transition: consumer-vertical offsets (loans, insurance, banking) keep
   outrunning organic-search decay. Evidence for: TTM revenue +11.5%, Q3'26
   guide +17% at midpoint. Evidence against: credit cards −24% FY25, SMB −11%
   in Q2'26, consumer growth decelerating 24%→6% in five quarters, and the
   Q3 guide leans on student-loan regulatory changes plus the College
   Finance acquisition — partly one-time.
2. *probable* — Normalized owner FCF is ≥ ~$70M once the federal tax holiday
   ends (management: normal cash taxes return Q2/Q3 2027). FY26 GAAP
   operating income guide $65–80M; arithmetic in §4.
3. *probable* — The buyback keeps retiring shares well below intrinsic

   remaining (letter, 6/30/26).
4. *plausible* — Vertical-integration cohorts (NerdWallet Insurance Experts,

   Policies sold nearly doubled QoQ (letter); the 5× step-up in

5. *probable* — Founder control (Tim Chen holds all Class B, ~89% of votes,

## 2. Business

**Created:** free, editorially-credible comparison and guidance for the
highest-stakes consumer money decisions — credit cards, personal/SMB loans,
insurance, banking, mortgages — plus tools (credit tracking, calculators)
and, increasingly, humans (licensed insurance agents, mortgage brokers,
advisory). The consumer pays nothing and gets a trusted shortlist; the
surplus is real, which is why the brand ranks and converts.

**Captured:** per-action referral fees from ~450 financial-institution
partners, and the mechanism differs by vertical: per-approval bounties
(credit cards), per-funded-loan fees (personal and SMB loans), per-policy
commissions — now increasingly in-house agency commissions with renewal
streams (auto insurance), per-funded-account fees (banking deposits),
mortgage brokerage gain-on-sale (Next Door Lending, acquired Oct 2024), and
advisory fees. That is at least four distinct businesses wearing one brand;
the newer ones (agency commissions, brokering) carry recurring renewal
economics the classic referral click never had.

**Protected:** the honest answer is that the old moat — dominant organic
rankings earned by a decade of editorial investment — is the thing being
eroded; AI answer engines compress exactly that surface. What remains is
brand trust (still the reason LLM-referred users convert at high intent,
per management) and a registered-user base whose size the company no longer
discloses. The replacement moat — owned audiences with recurring
relationships — is under construction, not standing. Written down as such.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive** over the five-year window,
negative year-over-year in the transition:

| FY | revenue | operating income | op margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $379.6M | −$20.9M | −5.5% |
| 2022 | $538.9M | −$8.8M | −1.6% |
| 2023 | $599.4M | $5.0M | 0.8% |
| 2024 | $687.6M | $18.4M | 2.7% |
| 2025 | $836.6M | $65.2M | 7.8% |
| TTM (Q2'26) | $860.0M | $89.1M | 10.4% |

But Q2'26 alone: revenue +6% YoY while GAAP operating income fell 35% (to
$6.9M) and NGOI fell 41% (to $12.2M) — the incremental IRR-based marketing
spend is being charged now against paybacks later.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **AI / search disintermediation (the core thread).** The FY2025 10-K Item
  1A names the dependency plainly: reliance on Google for traffic; algorithm
  changes have already caused declines; "the introduction and acceptance of
  AI-assisted technologies could further impact search engine relevance."
  The P&L confirms it is not hypothetical: credit cards −$43.0M (−24%) in
  FY2025, SMB products −9% FY2025 and −11% in Q2'26, both attributed in the
  filing to organic-search pressure. Management (Q2'26 call) says
  LLM-referred traffic converts with very high intent but "continues to be a
  pretty small part of our business."
- **Revenue quality is shifting from earned to bought.** Q2'26 S&M was
  $145.4M — 74% of revenue — of which $116M (59% of revenue) was performance
  marketing (letter). The 93.5% gross margin is optical; the traffic that
  organic rankings once delivered free is increasingly repurchased from ad
  platforms. Brand marketing was cut to fund it. This is the mechanism by
  which "revenue grew 6%" and "operating income fell 35%" are the same fact.
- **The tax holiday inflates headline FCF.** TTM adjusted FCF of $141.2M
  (letter reconciliation) was earned while paying no federal cash taxes and
  collecting $9M of OBBBA refunds; deferred taxes added +$8.4M in H1'26
  cash flow; the remaining DTA is $20.9M (6/30/26 balance sheet) and
  management says normal cash taxes return Q2–Q3 2027. Run-rate owner FCF is
  materially below the headline — quantified in §4.
- **Insurance concentration.** FY2025 insurance revenue +$89.2M (+47%) was
  carrier-ad-budget-driven; the Q2'26 call concedes the largest auto carrier
  "has stabilized but not yet returned to levels seen earlier in the year."
  One buyer's budget swing moves the largest growth vertical.
- **Near/below-prime credit exposure.** The personal-loans growth (+$12M YoY
  in Q2) explicitly targets near-prime and below-prime borrowers (letter,
  call). Lender acquisition budgets for that segment are the first thing cut
  in a credit downturn — the growth offset is credit-cycle sensitive.
- **Buyback.** 3M Class A shares for $23M in Q2; $160M TTM (≈25% of today's
  market cap); weighted diluted count −14% YoY; $67M authorization remains
