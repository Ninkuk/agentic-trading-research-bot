## 2. Business

**Created:** A police agency buys one integrated stack — TASER 10 CEDs, body
and in-car cameras, fixed ALPR (Outpost/Lightpost), real-time crime center
(Fusus), drones and counter-drone (Dedrone, Skydio partnership), 911 call
handling (Prepared/Carbyne) — all landing evidence in one cloud (Axon
Evidence) with AI on top (Draft One report writing, real-time translation).
The customer's surplus is officer time and chain-of-custody defensibility; the
June 2026 customer webinar's own phrasing was efficiency and community trust,
with interoperability as the vendor-selection test.

**Captured:** (a) multi-year subscription bundles (5-year "plans") that wrap
hardware, cloud storage and premium software into one per-officer fee —
Software & Services $398M/qtr (+36%), ARR $1.64B; (b) hardware sold on those
plans with refresh cadence built in (TASER 10 bookings already exceed TASER
7's lifetime); (c) Platform Solutions — Dedrone/counter-UAS, now $150M/qtr
(+123%), with a DHS $1.5B counter-UAS program seat; (d) enterprise and
international (bookings tripled YoY). Future contracted bookings $15.1B, 20–25%
fulfilled in 12 months, remainder over ten years.

**Protected:** The mechanism is evidence-data gravity plus procurement inertia:
years of body-cam video, CED logs and case files live in Axon Evidence, every
new sensor lands there, and a risk-averse customer (management's own words)
faces re-training, re-integration and open-bid friction to leave — NRR 126%
with "de minimis attrition". The July 2025 decision to cut Flock's API access
is the moat being used as a weapon: the ALPR feed either lands in Axon
Evidence natively or not at all. This is a real, mechanistic lock-in at the
agency level; it does not extend to fixed ALPR itself, where the 10-K lists
Flock, Genetec, Jenoptik, Motorola, Neology, Rekor and others as competitors.

**Control:** One share, one vote, no controlling holder. Directors and officers
own 4.2% (DEF 14A, April 2026); founder-CEO Rick Smith ~3.1M shares (~3.9%).
Annual elections, majority voting, shareholders may call special meetings and
act by written consent. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist — the
null answer, but written.
