## 1. Verdict and thesis

**BUY at $229.51.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (3 probable,
2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

Cardinal is one of three US pharmaceutical wholesalers that between them move
nearly every brand and generic pill from manufacturer to pharmacy, paid a
per-unit service fee plus a generics sourcing spread, on a balance sheet where
suppliers fund the working capital ($38.3B of payables against $31.1B of
receivables and inventory). Fiscal 2026 was the best year in its history —
operating earnings +30%, all five segments up double digits — and fiscal 2027
is guided to +13–15% EPS on a business that has beaten its own guide seven
quarters of the last eight. Against a normalized levered FCF of ~$3.35B
(management's guide midpoint less the ~$0.4B/yr opioid settlement drag), the
price implies a 10.2% annual return, 207bp over an 8.16% cost of equity. The
one thing the price cannot tell you is whether CVS Health — 28% of revenue —
renews the distribution agreement that expires June 30, 2027; nothing filed
says it has, and the CEO steered around the question on the Q4 call.

**Closest attack:** the unrenewed CVS contract (condition 2). The FY25 cash
flow statement shows what losing a large customer does to this model — NCFO
fell to $2.4B as the negative working capital tied to the OptumRx contracts
unwound — so a CVS loss is a profit hit *and* a multi-billion cash outflow.
The attack does not land because nothing refutes renewal either; it withholds
SOUND.

Load-bearing conditions:

   base, not a peak. Three-year average GAAP FCF FY24–26 is $3.21B *after*

## 2. Business

**Created:** a pharmacy or hospital gets every drug it dispenses delivered
daily from one dock, with credit, returns, controlled-substance compliance,
and generics purchasing it could not run itself; a manufacturer gets ~one
invoice for the whole US channel instead of 60,000 customer accounts. The
customer surplus is logistics and float — CAH extends trade credit and
carries $17.3B of inventory on the customer's behalf.

**Captured:** four mechanisms, not one. (1) Brand distribution: fee-for-
service agreements with manufacturers paid per unit / on WAC, plus prompt-pay
discounts; low single-digit basis points on $235B of Pharma revenue. (2)
Generics: buy-side spread through Red Oak Sourcing, the CVS joint venture
that negotiates generic supply for both companies (term through June 2029;
CAH pays CVS quarterly for it). (3) Specialty: higher-margin distribution of
oncology/GI/urology drugs plus practice management fees through the MSO
platforms bought for $7.2B over FY25–26 (GIA, ION, Solaris, ADS). (4)
Products and services: Cardinal-brand medical products (GMPD, $258M profit on
$12.7B revenue), at-Home Solutions, Nuclear & Precision Health (theranostics
/ PET), OptiFreight Logistics — "Other," $707M profit, guided +15–18%.

**Protected:** scale economics on a national three-node network, plus
switching cost. The Big Three share ~90% of US drug distribution and a
customer moving wholesalers re-plumbs every store's ordering, credit, and
controlled-substance reporting — which is why contracts run multi-year and
renew more often than not. The mechanism has a known failure mode: it does
not bind the largest customers, who can and do move (OptumRx left CAH at
June 2024; 17% of FY24 revenue). The moat is against the small customer and
the new entrant, not against CVS.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $181.3B (FY22) → $205.0B
→ $226.8B → $222.6B → $254.2B (FY26); GAAP operating earnings $1,243M (FY24)
→ $2,275M → $2,613M (10-K); stockanalysis's operating-income series (which
excludes some unusual items) runs $1,648M (FY22) → $3,288M (FY26). Revenue
+40% over four years, operating income roughly doubled; GAAP operating
margin 0.55% (FY24) → 1.03% (FY26).
