## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/CAH-2026-08-23.md` §2. No new 10-K or 10-Q has
been filed since. The substance, restated:

**Created:** a pharmacy or hospital gets every drug it dispenses delivered
daily from one dock. It also gets credit, returns, controlled-substance
compliance, and generics purchasing it could not run itself. A manufacturer
gets roughly one invoice for the whole US channel instead of 60,000 customer
accounts. CAH carries $17.3B of inventory and extends trade credit on the
customer's behalf.

**Captured:** four mechanisms:
- Brand distribution: fee-for-service paid per unit or on WAC, plus
  prompt-pay discounts; basis points on $235B of Pharma revenue.
- Generics: a buy-side spread through Red Oak Sourcing, the CVS joint venture
  (term through June 2029).
- Specialty distribution plus MSO practice-management economics (GIA, ION,
  Solaris, ADS; $7.2B bought over FY25–26).
- Products and services: GMPD medical products ($258M profit on $12.7B), and
  "Other" (at-Home, Nuclear & Precision Health, OptiFreight: $707M profit,
  guided +15–18%).

**Protected:** scale economics on a national three-player network, plus
switching costs. Moving a pharmacy chain to another wholesaler re-plumbs
ordering, credit, and controlled-substance reporting. The failure mode is
known: this does not bind the largest customers, who can and do move
(OptumRx left CAH in June 2024, 17% of FY24 revenue). The moat protects
against small customers and new entrants, not against CVS.

**Control:** one share, one vote. No controlling holder: insiders hold
0.12%, institutions 90.98% (statistics probe). The 2022 Elliott cooperation
agreement is concluded. Nothing forecloses a takeover or activist path
beyond ordinary Ohio-law defences. The 2026 annual meeting is virtual on
November 12, 2026 (DEF 14A, 2026-09-21).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.**

## 5. Falsifiers

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** CVS does not renew the distribution agreement, or renews with
  materially smaller scope, by June 30, 2027. The fee-on-volume franchise
  story ends and a working-capital unwind follows.
- **Break —** a regulatory or channel change that moves brand compensation
  off the WAC base, or diverts material volume to direct-to-patient channels
  (MFN/TrumpRx), with management unable to show a fee re-negotiation. The
  terminal-growth assumption fails.
- **Shift —** FY27 adjusted FCF tracking below $3.5B at the Q2 FY27 print
  (Feb 2027) with no working-capital explanation: the base was a peak.
- **Shift —** Pharma segment profit growth below 8% for two consecutive
  quarters, excluding one-time items: the growth path is too generous.
- **Shift —** material new MSO or at-Home M&A (> ~$1B) in FY27 without
  disclosed returns: the growth is being bought, and the §4 rows are
  overstated.
- **Shift —** the IRS NOPAs resolve against the company at or near the full
  ~$2B: revalue with the claim as debt.
- **Shift —** open-window selling above ~30% of holdings by the CEO or CFO
  before the CVS renewal is announced.

For the buy (what would have made this a pass):

- **Shift —** price above ~$292. The base case meets the 8.49% hurdle at a
  $67.5B market cap (verified by re-run).
- **Shift —** DGS10 sustained above ~7.2%. At the floored beta that lifts the
  hurdle to the base row's 10.47% (7.16% + 0.8 × 4.14%), holding ERP
  constant.
