## 2. Business

**Created:** A community bank or credit union cannot build its own ledger,
card processing, digital banking app, fraud monitoring and regulatory
reporting, and cannot run without them. Jack Henry supplies all of it as an
integrated, hosted stack (SilverLake/Symitar cores, Banno digital, card and
bill-pay processing, Financial Crimes Defender, treasury) with service that
clients rate above the two larger rivals. The customer gets a compliant
system of record and a product roadmap it could never fund alone; the
average core client uses 40–50 Jack Henry products.

**Captured:** Four distinct mechanisms. (1) *Cloud/hosting and support
fees* on the core — 32% of revenue is private/public cloud, priced on
assets/accounts, on ~6-year contracts; 79% of core clients are now hosted.
(2) *Processing* — 44% of revenue, per-transaction fees on debit/credit
cards, bill pay, Zelle/RTP/FedNow, remote deposit; rides US consumer
spending volumes. (3) *Complementary products* sold into the core base and
increasingly outside it (Banno, treasury, fraud) — per-user and per-module
subscriptions. (4) *Deconversion fees* — contractual termination payments
when a client is acquired away ($48M of FY26 non-GAAP adjustments, guided
$23M for FY27); lumpy, excluded from non-GAAP, real cash. Segment revenue
FY26: Core $768M, Payments $936M, Complementary $752M, Corporate $88M.

**Protected:** Switching cost, as a mechanism: a core conversion is a
15–24 month, board-level, examiner-watched project that touches every
account and retrains every employee, and the 40–50 attached products come
out with it. Hence 99%+ retention ex-M&A. The second layer is a three-vendor
market (Fiserv, FIS, Jack Henry; Corelation and Finastra at the edges) in
which the two larger players run many legacy cores and are distracted;
Jack Henry is taking share from all of them. What does *not* protect it: it
has no pricing power over a shrinking count of institutions beyond
CPI-linked escalators, and client losses to M&A are outside its control.

**Control:** One class of common stock, one vote per share; 500,000
preferred authorized, none issued. No controlling holder — insiders 0.59%,
institutions ~100%. Nothing in the share structure forecloses a takeover or
an activist; board classification and other charter defenses were not read
(NOT OBTAINED, §6).
