## 2. Business

**Created:** A 9,500-square-foot store where a child with $5 can buy
something, and a parent can say yes without thinking. Roughly 80% of the
assortment sits at whole-dollar prices of $1–$5 (simplified in 2025 from
the prior $5.55/$5.95 fractional ladder); "Five Beyond" items up to ~$25 are
now merchandised in line rather than walled off at the back. The customer
is explicit — Gen Alpha, Gen Z, and the millennial parent — and the
merchandise is trend: candy, tech accessories, room decor, toys, games,
collectibles, craft, seasonal. Value is created by speed (a trend spotted
on social is in the store in weeks, then amplified back into social), by
newness on a rolling cadence ("curtain-up" moments six times a year), and by
a treasure-hunt format that converts a visit into a basket of many
low-priced units. Management's own framing is "a business of desire, not of
need" (CEO, 2026-09-15). Category mix FY25: Leisure 44.5%, Fashion & Home
30.9%, Snack & Seasonal 24.6% (10-K).

**Captured:** Three mechanisms. (i) Merchandise margin on goods sourced
~60% domestically and ~40% imported (China the largest origin; no vendor
above 5% of purchases), gross margin 35.6% adjusted in Q2 and 37.6% TTM as
stated on the statistics page; (ii) fixed-cost leverage — occupancy and
store labor are largely fixed per box, so a 14% comp dropped 360bp to the
adjusted operating margin in Q2; (iii) new-unit returns — a ~$0.4M box
doing ~$2M in year one pays back in about a year, so unit growth is the
main use of cash and earns above the hurdle when the box is placed well.
There is no membership, no credit, no meaningful e-commerce (BOPIS and
third-party delivery only), and no dividend.

**Protected:** Not a structural moat; an execution moat, and the record
shows it can lapse. What stops a competitor tomorrow is the combination of
a kid-specific assortment at a price-point discipline mass merchants and
dollar stores do not replicate (Dollar Tree's multi-price is need-based;
Walmart and Target sell to the parent), speed to trend that a
2,000-store fleet can now amplify with its own social marketing, and a
"one-of-one" positioning management repeats at every conference. The
counter-evidence is FY24: comps −2.7%, the CEO departed, and a securities
class action covers statements made March–July 2024 (class certified
2026-08-03). Temu was answered for eight straight quarters until the de
minimis loophole closed; management said on 2025-08-27 it "never played a
huge role", which is a claim the corpus cannot test.

**Control:** One class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder; BlackRock 9.0% is the only 5%+ holder in the 2026 proxy; all
executive officers, directors and nominees as a group own 0.96%; annual
election of all directors (nine at the proxy, ten after Scott Settersten
joined the audit committee 2026-09-21). A shareholder proposal to remove
supermajority provisions was on the 2026 ballot (board neutral-to-opposed
per the proxy text). Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist; insider
skin in the game is thin (2.2% per stockanalysis).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive now, after three negative years.**
Fiscal years end late January/early February; stockanalysis labels the year
ended 2026-01-31 "FY2026", the company calls it fiscal 2025.
