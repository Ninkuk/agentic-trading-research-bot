## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $219.70.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=6 (2 probable,
3 plausible, 1 possible), refuted=1, unknown=2.

Take-Two is three publishers under one ticker: Rockstar (Grand Theft Auto and
Red Dead — decade-long live-service annuities), 2K (NBA 2K plus Borderlands,
Civilization, WWE, Mafia) and Zynga (casual mobile). Grand Theft Auto VI ships
2026-11-19 and turns FY2027 into an $8.0–8.2B net-bookings year (+20%). The
buy case is that the launch resets the company's scale to ~$9B and that cash
margins finally show through once ~$2.15B of capitalized, unreleased-title
development stops being funded. The price does not leave room for that to
merely happen: at the 8.94% hurdle, $41.1B needs ~$2.8B of owner FCF (after
~$0.45B SBC) from FY2028 onward — a ~36% cash margin on $9B of bookings, or
~30% on $11B. The Damodaran industry operating-margin anchor is 33.9%; TTWO's
best OCF/revenue in the five years on record is 9.4%, the FY2027 guide floor
is ~12%, and adding back the capitalized development spend gets to ~22%.
Consensus FY2028 revenue growth is ~9%, not 35%. The launch can go perfectly
and the stock can still be fairly priced at best.

**Closest attack:** on the pass — the "no precedent" margin argument leans on
a five-year window that GTA VI's own development spend depressed; a
pro-forma add-back (~$750M/yr of capitalized development) narrows the gap to
~22% vs 36%, and a launch year with front-loaded $80–100 units could print a
one-off number near the anchor. It narrows the gap; it does not close it, and
one year is not a terminal margin.

## 2. Business

**Created:** Rockstar sells the interactive medium's biggest cultural
products: GTA has sold-in 465M units lifetime, GTA V 230M since 2013, and
GTA Online still sets engagement records 13 years in (10-K; Q1 FY2027 call).
The customer gets a world people keep living in for a decade — the surplus is
obvious from the price/hours ratio, which is why management says "our goal is
not to maximize price." 2K sells the licensed NBA simulation (NBA 2K26: 12M+
units, +9% y/y; MyCAREER DAU +25%) and a slate of owned IP. Zynga sells
nine-minute sessions five to seven times a day (management's own description)
in Toon Blast, Empires & Puzzles, Match Factory!, Words With Friends, Color
Block Jam and Zynga Poker.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not "games". (1) Premium unit sales —
$70–100 at launch, net of a platform take on digital and distributor margin on
physical; GTA VI is $79.99 standard / $99.99 Ultimate. (2) Recurrent consumer
spending — 84% of Q1 FY2027 net bookings, guided to 64% for the launch year:
virtual currency (GTA$, NBA 2K VC), add-on content, GTA+ subscriptions,
season passes; NBA 2K RCS +7%, GTA series RCS +3% in Q1. (3) Mobile IAP plus
advertising (ads monetize the >80% of players who never pay) plus a
direct-to-consumer web channel that bypasses the app-store take — "a material
positive effect on our margins in the mobile business". (4) Licensing and
merchandise inside the net-bookings definition. Distribution is concentrated:
the five largest customers were 80.6% of FY2026 net revenue, with Apple, Sony,
Google and Microsoft each above 10% (10-K).

**Protected:** the mechanism is owned IP with sunk player investment — a
GTA Online character, inventory and friend graph built over years is what
stops a competitor tomorrow; the brand is the label on top. That protection
is franchise-specific: it covers GTA/RDR and, through the NBA licence and
annual cadence, NBA 2K (licence renewal is the exposure — a lapsed sports
licence is how EA lost FIFA). Zynga has no moat: casual mobile is a
user-acquisition auction, and management says competitors "vastly
overspending in UA ... can crowd us out of the market." The 10-K's own
framing: five franchises were 54.3% of FY2026 net revenue and "a relatively
small number of hit titles account for a large portion of total revenue in
our industry."

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.01 par (10-K cover); no
controlling holder; insiders hold ~0.35% (stockanalysis). Nothing in the
structure forecloses a takeover or an activist. The non-null fact is that
management is outsourced: ZMC Advisors, L.P. (Zelnick, Slatoff) runs the
company under a management agreement through 2029-03-31 — $3.3M annual fee,
up to $13.2M annual bonus, plus restricted units; FY2026 expense $16.6M
consulting + $62.9M stock compensation (10-K note 3). The CEO's economics run
through ZMC's grants, not through a personal stake; Zelnick sold 40,000 shares
at ~$250–255 on 2026-08-10 (Form 4) and retains ~162K.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue has nearly doubled while
operating income went from +$545M to a loss:

- **Break —** launch-window sell-through below GTA V's 2013 pace or a

  in two consecutive prints (Zynga is 34% of bookings).
- **Shift —** an equity issuance, or an acquisition announced at or near
  the FY2028 cash peak (condition 6).
