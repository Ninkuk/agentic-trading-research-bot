## 2. Business

**Created:** a bundled premium club — pools, courts, spa, cafe, kids academy,
group training, personal training — sold to affluent families (member median
household income $161k, 1.4× the trade area; ~60% couples or families;
deck, 2025-12-31 data) at $245/month average dues. The customer surplus is
the bundle: one membership replaces a swim club, a Pilates studio, a tennis
club, childcare and a gym, each of which would cost more à la carte
(management's own framing on the Q2 call). Engagement is the evidence that
the surplus is real: 151 visits per membership per year, up ~41% since 2019;
new clubs open with waitlists and are contribution-positive in month 2–3
(Akradi, Q2 call, primary-transcribed). Clubs average 97,000 sq ft indoor;
18M indoor and 7M outdoor sq ft across 195 clubs in 32 states and one
Canadian province (10-K; deck as of 2026-06-30).

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (i) Dues and enrollment fees — 72%+ of
center revenue, month-to-month, repriced on legacy members and at higher
rack rates on new joins (price contributed 2.9 pts of comps; new clubs are
designed for 3,000–4,000 memberships at "much higher dues"). (ii) In-center
businesses — Dynamic Personal Training, LifeSpa, LifeCafe, kids programs —
~28% of center revenue, growing double digits in DPT and spa and adding 2.9
pts to comps. (iii) Real estate arbitrage — build at cost, sell to a REIT or
private buyer, lease back at a rent per square foot management says is
"significantly below" market (Q2 call), recycling $2.8B since 2015 into new
clubs. Mix management is a fourth lever: qualified medical memberships
(restricted-hours, insurer-paid, low dues) are being run off (−18.9% YoY, now
3.3% of dues revenue) to free peak capacity for full-dues members.

**Protected:** partly a mechanism, partly not. The mechanism is scale plus
real estate: a 95,000 sq ft ground-up club costs ~$60–65M gross ($885–910M
FY26 growth capex for 14 clubs), takes 3–4 years to ramp, and needs a
development team that has opened 78 sale-leaseback properties — a competitor
cannot replicate the footprint in a market quickly, and developers court
Life Time as an anchor tenant (Fitt/PR colour; management's own claim). What
does not protect it: memberships are month-to-month with no switching cost;
Equinox, Bay Club, Invited and local country clubs sell adjacent premium
bundles (10-K competition list); and the premium consumer is cyclical —
the 10-K's own claim that its members are "less susceptible" to downturns is
an assertion, not a measured base rate. Verdict on the moat: real but local
and capital-based, not structural — it earns time, not permanence.

**Control:** one share class, one vote each, no controller. The pre-IPO
"Voting Group" (LGP, TPG, founder Akradi, others under a stockholders
agreement) held 62.7% at YE2024, 38.3% at YE2025, and after LGP's August
sales (5.1M shares 8/10 at $43.16; 2.9M 8/26 at $43.80; 2.6% remaining per
the 8/12 13D/A) and TPG's Form 144s on the same dates, no sponsor holds 5%.
Akradi held 7.3% (proxy, Feb 2026). Controlled-company status ended June 5,
2025; two LGP directors resigned 2026-08-26 (8-K); the board is 12 with 10
independent, classified into three staggered classes — the classified board
is the one provision that slows an unsolicited approach. Nothing forecloses
an activist.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue 2.4× since 2021 while
operating income swung from −$491M to +$532M; incremental operating margin
2023→TTM is 29%.

  rent per square foot (the "below market" claim failing).
- **Shift —** founder-CEO succession; Akradi is the development engine and
  the 10-K names no successor.
