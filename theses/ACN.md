## 2. Business

**Created:** an enterprise that wants to change how it runs — a new ERP, a
cloud migration, a cybersecurity program, an AI deployment, or an outsourced
finance/IT function — buys a single accountable counterparty that already
knows its industry, the software vendor's product, and how to staff 500
people in India and the Philippines by Monday. The customer buys certainty
of delivery and transfer of execution risk, not hours. ~9,000 clients, 195 of
the top 200 for 10+ years (10-K FY25; Q3 FY26 call).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (i) *Consulting* — project fees,
>60% fixed-price, $37.6B FY25, ~1-year contracts; margin comes from staffing
pyramid and utilisation. (ii) *Managed services* — multi-year application,
infrastructure and business-process outsourcing, $34.6B FY25, revenue
recognised over time, priced on volume/outcomes and increasingly non-FTE.
(iii) *Ecosystem resale and IP* — partner-sourced work (SAP, Microsoft/
Avanade, Salesforce, AWS, Google, the AI labs) where Accenture is the
largest systems integrator, plus a growing software layer (Ookla, Dragos/
runZero/NetRise at ~$208M ARR). Industry split FY25: Products 30%, H&PS 21%,
Financial Services 18%, CMT 17%, Resources 14%; geography Q3 FY26: Americas
49%, EMEA 37%, Asia Pacific 14% (8-K exhibit 2026-06-18).

**Protected:** the mechanism is *scale of trusted delivery capacity*: 798k
people, 52 countries, certified deepest on every major enterprise platform,
and a decade-long relationship with almost every large enterprise, so the
switching cost is the incumbent's knowledge of the client's systems, not a
contract. That is a real but *labour-based* moat: it protects share against
Deloitte/IBM/TCS/Infosys, and it is exactly what a client with a capable
agentic-AI stack can partially insource. The 10-K names the threat in its
own risk factor (Item 1A: clients and ecosystem partners "may develop their
own internal AI-related capabilities, as well as new AI-native companies").
Management's answer is to move to non-FTE models and buy software; that
answer is unproven at scale.

**Control:** Irish plc, one class of listed equity (Class A ordinary, one
vote per share); Class X ordinary shares (no economic rights, 14 holders of
record) are a legacy of the 2001 partnership conversion. No controlling
holder; insiders 0.03%, institutions 81%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or
an activist beyond the Irish Takeover Rules and the company's $115B size.
Null answer: no controller.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive, mild.** Revenue FY21→FY25 +38%,
operating income +42%; operating margin 15.1% → 15.6% → 15.8% TTM. Stock
compensation is charged in that line and has grown faster than revenue
(FY21 $1.34B → TTM $2.08B, 2.6% → 2.9% of revenue).
