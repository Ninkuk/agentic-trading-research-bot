## 2. Business

**Created:** a small or mid-sized employer (median client well under 100
employees; "largely an under 100 play" for PEO) outsources the thing it
can least afford to get wrong — paying people and remitting their taxes
correctly and on time — plus, increasingly, the HR, benefits and
compliance work it has no department for. The customer buys accuracy and
indemnity (Paychex files the returns, guarantees the tax remittance, and
its WISE engine now intercepts ~90% of payroll errors before they post,
per the CEO), access to health plans and a 401(k) it could not buy alone
(PEO co-employment pools 2.6M worksite employees; 130,000 retirement
plans holding $66B), and a human adviser (650 HR professionals). Over 60%
of revenue is now something other than payroll processing (10-K).

**Captured:** four mechanisms, reported in three lines. (a) Management
Solutions ($4.9B FY26; $1,213M in Q1, +4%): a fixed fee per pay run plus a
fee per employee or per module — payroll, tax filing, ASO HR outsourcing,
retirement recordkeeping, time and attendance, the Perks marketplace —
growing through ~3% annual price realization and attach, on a client base
that is not growing. (b) PEO and Insurance Solutions ($1.4B FY26; $368M
in Q1, +12%): a per-worksite-employee administrative fee on co-employed
workers, reported net of pass-through wages and most health premiums
except the Florida minimum-premium plan, where premiums run through
revenue and claims through cost; plus workers' compensation and benefits
brokerage commissions. An ASO client that upgrades to PEO is worth ~3.5×
the revenue (CEO). (c) Interest on funds held for clients ($211M FY26;
$50M in Q1): the float between collecting payroll taxes and remitting
them, averaging $5.4B in Q1 at 3.7%, invested in a 3.1-year-duration AFS
book. (d) Corporate investment income sits below the line. The 2019
Series B notes and the $4.2B of April 2025 bonds that bought Paycor cost
$267M a year in cash interest against all of it.

**Protected:** a switching-cost-and-trust mechanism, not a label. The
client's tax history, year-end forms, direct-deposit rails and — for PEO
— its employees' health plans live inside Paychex; leaving mid-year means
re-filing and re-enrolling, which is why the CFO calls PEO "very
disruptive for them to leave" and payroll retention holds at 82–83% every
year. More than half of new payroll clients arrive through CPAs, banks
and benefit brokers (10-K), a referral channel that took decades to build
(three national broker partnerships signed in six months; referrals up
43% y/y, per the call). Scale gives the float, the carrier negotiating
position and now the data (50 trillion proprietary workforce data points
behind WISE). The limits are equally concrete: the 17–18% of payroll
clients that leave every year must be re-sold, the corpus of 27 calls
never names Gusto, Rippling or Deel and names ADP once (2025-12-10), the
10-K's own risk factor says customers "may use AI technologies from other
providers instead of using our services," and the last growth step of
size (Paycor, $4.1B) was bought, not won, and is growing high-single
digits against the double digits it grew at purchase. Honest read: a
durable, high-return franchise in the sub-100-employee market whose unit
base is flat and whose growth is price, attach and mix.

**Control:** one class of common, one vote per share, 356.0M shares out;
ten directors elected annually by majority vote; no dual class, no
classified board, no rights plan or supermajority provision disclosed
(DEF 14A 2026-09-04). Founder B. Thomas Golisano holds 35,970,209 shares
(10.1%) and left the board on 2025-07-09; Capital International 8.6%,
