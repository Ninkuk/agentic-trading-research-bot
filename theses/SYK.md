## 2. Business

**Created:** a surgeon gets a system, not a part — the Mako robot, the
Triathlon knee or Insignia hip it plans and cuts for, the instruments, and a
Stryker rep in the operating room, with 2.5M procedures of evidence behind
it; a trauma surgeon gets the Pangea plating system; an interventionalist
gets Inari's FlowTriever/ClotTriever for pulmonary embolism and DVT where the
competitor is "conservative therapy." The hospital gets one vendor across the
OR, emergency department and ward: ProCuity beds, LIFEPAK 35 defibrillators,
Neptune waste management, OR lights and booms, endoscopy towers, Vocera
communications. The surplus is outcomes and workflow for the surgeon, capital
productivity for the hospital, and for both a supplier whose products serve
"high acuity, medically necessary and emergent care" — demand that does not
wait for a better economy (10-K Item 1; Q2 call).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (1) Implant and disposable
pull-through on an installed base: a Mako is placed (capital sale, lease or
per-procedure arrangement) and the knees, hips and shoulders that follow
carry a ~66% gross margin — the razor-and-blade of Orthopaedics ($9.5B, 38%
of 2025 sales). (2) Capital equipment sold outright with service — beds,
stretchers, defibrillators, OR infrastructure, endoscopy — order-book and
backlog driven, lumpy by quarter, the part of the business the cyber outage
hit hardest. (3) Per-procedure disposables at hospital scale: Sage skin prep,
Neptune, SurgiCount, Inari catheters (MedSurg and Neurotechnology $15.6B, 62%).
Pricing contributed +0.1% in MedSurg and nothing in Orthopaedics in Q2, and
+0.4% for 2025 — growth is unit volume and mix, not price. The US is 76% of
sales (10-K Note 14; Q2 8-K sales analysis).

**Protected:** a surgeon trained on a robot-and-implant system does not switch
for a discount — the training, the planning software and the instrument sets
are hours of the surgeon's own time, which is why Mako RPS launches are aimed
"much more on the competitive front" than at existing users. The installed
base then compounds: every placed robot pulls implants for a decade. Behind
that sit ~5,600 US patents, the OR sales presence, and the scale to run
1,200-patient trials (PEERLESS II) that reset a market's standard of care.
None of it protects price: reimbursement policy sets the ceiling, and Zimmer
Biomet, DePuy Synthes and Smith+Nephew all have robots — the gap is
execution (US knees +6.2% vs Zimmer's US knees +1.4% in the same quarter),
not structure (10-K Item 1 and 1A; Q2 call; ZBH Q2 release via web).

**Control:** one class, one vote per share; 382,984,253 shares at the record
date. Vanguard 8.1%, BlackRock 6.5%, John W. Brown (former CEO) 5.2%; Ronda
Stryker, the founder's granddaughter and a director since 1984, held ~2.6M
shares in the reporting trust after selling 350,000 in August, with 13.1M
more held jointly per the proxy; insiders 9.1% in total. Annual elections,
majority voting, a lead independent director, no controller. Nothing in the
structure forecloses a takeover or an activist; the $116B size does the
former (DEF 14A 2026-03-23; Form 4 2026-08-20; statistics page).
