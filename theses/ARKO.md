## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $4.41.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=5, refuted=1,

ARKO is no longer a convenience-store operator; since 13 Feb 2026 it is a
**holding company** whose largest asset is 35.0M Class B shares of
separately-listed ARKO Petroleum (APC), worth **$641.9M against ARKO's entire
$494.7M market cap**. That reads as a free option on the retail chain, but it
is not: parent net debt (~$258M, derived) and ~$100M of preferred sit ahead
of the common, and netting them leaves ~$211M of implied enterprise value for
a c-store business carrying **$1,659.7M of lease obligations** with
same-store fuel gallons −5.7% and merchandise −1.7%. I pass because the two
inputs that decide whether the discount is real — the parent-vs-APC debt
split and the operating-vs-finance lease split — are exactly the two I could
not verify, and the business underneath is shrinking on purpose.

## 2. Business

**Created:** ~1,200 company-operated c-stores (fas mart, Scotchman, Jetz and
~two dozen other regional banners) selling fuel plus high-margin inside items
in small towns and along secondary roads. The customer buys proximity and
speed — a 3-minute stop, not a destination. Real preference satisfied, but a
weak one: the next exit has the same offer.

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms, not one: (a) retail fuel at 48.7 cpg
on a declining gallon base; (b) merchandise at 34.7% gross margin (+110bp
YoY) where cigarettes are the traffic driver and food service the margin
driver; (c) since 2024, **dealerization** — 471 company stores converted to
dealer sites, moving them out of retail and into APC's wholesale segment at
~7.6 cpg but with near-zero operating cost. Management targets "a little over
500."

**Protected:** *No good answer, and that silence is the answer.* There is no
mechanism stopping a competitor: real estate is leased not owned, fuel is a
commodity, the banners carry no pricing power, and the loyalty program (2×
spend for enrolled members) is a discount scheme, not a switching cost. The
only genuine asset is the wholesale/terminal position — and that now sits
inside APC, 26.4% of which belongs to someone else.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue 2021 $7,417M → 2025
$7,643M (+3.0%) while operating income $142.1M → $102.3M (**−28%**) and net
income $53.5M → $17.0M (**−68%**). Revenue is a noisy denominator here (it
tracks the fuel price), which is why the operating-income line matters more:
flat volume, falling profit.

## 3. Threads pulled

  consolidates. At APC's $18.34 close the stake is $641.9M — 130% of ARKO's
  own market cap. The apparent free lunch dissolves once the claims *between*
  the stake and the common are netted (§4). Not a mispricing; a
  capital-structure illusion.
- **APC's dividend is the load-bearing prop under the stake — and it is not
  covered.** APC pays $2.00/yr on 47.57M shares = **$95.1M**, against APC TTM
  operating cash flow of $58.4M less $26.0M capex = **$32.4M of free cash

  interest and ~$26M capex the payout is not funded from cash generation, and
  APC is about to spend $205M cash on U.S. Petroleum Partners (closing
  leverage 3.0–3.5×). The $30M of EBITDA USPP adds roughly closes the gap by
  2027 — so this is tight, not yet broken. ARKO's own $70.0M/yr of APC
  dividend receipts covers parent interest and its own $19.2M of
  common+preferred dividends, which is the actual reason the holdco works.
- **Economics are migrating from the wholly-owned entity to the 73.6%-owned
  one.** Every dealer conversion moves profit from 100%-owned retail into

## 4. Valuation

**Sum-of-the-parts** (prices as of the 2026-08-11 close):

| line | $M |
|---|---|
| ARKO market cap (112.19M × $4.41) | 494.7 |
| + parent net debt (consol. $674.5M debt − APC $184.7M; less parent cash ~$231M) | ~258 |
| + preferred (derived: $5.75M dividend ÷ 5.75%) | ~100 |
| = parent enterprise value | ~853 |
| − APC stake at market (35.0M × $18.34) | −641.9 |
| **= implied retail EV, before leases** | **~211** |

Retail adjusted EBITDA ≈ $99M (ARKO's $245–265M FY26 guide less APC's ~$156M;
triangulated by Q2: consolidated $72.0M − APC $39.8M = $32.2M retail). So the
market prices retail at **~2.1× EBITDA** — which looks absurdly cheap until
the **$1,659.7M of lease liabilities** are added, at which point it is an
ordinary ~6–7× EBITDAR for a c-store chain. The sale-leasebacks are the
reason the stake looks free.

**Reverse DCF** (levered FCF ↔ market cap; `fcf = ncfo + capex` is
post-interest, so market cap is the correct denominator and `--net-debt`
stays at zero). Hurdle = 4.74% + 0.96 × 4.28% = **8.85%** (Damodaran
risk-free and implied ERP **as of 1 Aug 2026**; beta 0.96 is inside the
0.8–1.2 band, used as-is). ATM IV is 55.8%, above the 50% line, so **implied
returns are quoted to the nearest whole percent and the range is wide.**

| base FCF | rationale | growth | implied return | vs hurdle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $36.7M | TTM as reported | 3%/3yr, term 2% | ~10% | +94bp |
| $28.2M | less APC minorities' 26.4% of APC's $32.4M FCF | 3%/3yr, term 2% | ~8% | −87bp |
| $9.9M | less $18.3M SBC as well ("owner FCF") | 3%/3yr, term 2% | ~4% | **−474bp** |
| $50.2M | 4-yr avg FY FCF $77.2M, same two haircuts | 0%, term 0% | ~10% | +130bp |
| $50.2M | as above | 2%/3yr, term 1.5% | ~12% | +309bp |

**The spread is the finding, and here the spread changes sign with the base
period** — that is the refutation in §1. On trailing-twelve-months the stock
is 474bp below its hurdle and below the 5.26% tenth percentile of US costs of
capital; on the four-year average FCF ($110.7M, $24.9M, $107.9M, $65.3M for
FY22–25) it clears by 130–309bp. I cannot adjudicate without segment cash
flows. Both haircuts are mandatory and both are real: SBC of $18.3M is
compensation, not owner cash, and 26.4% of APC's cash belongs to APC's
minority holders.

The "growth without reinvestment" warning fired on every run with terminal
growth > 0 (base FCF exceeds base earnings of ~$9.0M). Answering it rather
than ignoring it: ARKO's D&A is overwhelmingly store PP&E, **not**
acquired-intangible amortisation, so the earnings base is not understated —
the company genuinely earns almost nothing. Hence the zero-terminal-growth
rows, which are the ones I weight. **Terminal risk**: this is a gasoline
retailer whose same-store gallons fell 5.7% this quarter; 2% perpetual growth
does not survive that, and the 0% rows are the honest read. A market-share
sentence is not needed — 3% growth on $9.4B of revenue is trivial against a
>$800B US c-store market.

## 5. Falsifiers

- **Shift —** ARKO announces a tax-efficient distribution or spin of the APC
  stake — the single event that would most change the answer; it converts a
  stranded $641.9M into something shareholders can price directly.
- **Shift —** two consecutive quarters of same-store fuel gallons at or above
  0% — the terminal-decline assumption behind the 0%-growth rows would be
  wrong.
- **Break —** APC cuts its dividend — for the stake mark: $18.34 is a 10.7%
  yield on a payout TTM free cash flow does not cover, and the stake is 130%
  of ARKO's market cap.
- **Break —** parent-only leverage disclosed above ~4× retail EBITDA, or the
  common dividend suspended — the equity is a thin residual behind $675M of
  debt, $1.66B of leases and $100M of preferred.
- **Shift —** price near ~$2.53 (the stake less parent net debt and
  preferred, i.e. retail valued at zero) — at that level the retail business
  is free rather than the stake.
