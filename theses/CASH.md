## 2. Business

**Created:** Fintechs, prepaid-card program managers, and tax preparers
need a chartered bank to hold deposits, issue cards, and originate
refund-advance loans; Pathward rents them the charter, compliance stack,
and network membership. Small and mid-sized businesses get asset-based,
working-capital, equipment, SBA and structured (largely renewable-energy)
loans from a lender that underwrites niche collateral.

**Captured:** (a) **Deposit spread** — partner programs bring ~$6.0B of
deposits; ~68% of balances carry a variable card-processing expense paid to
partners and tied to effective fed funds, so the funding is cheap only on
the remaining third plus the rate-insensitive slice (deck). Lent into
commercial finance at 6.8–10.8% (structured 6.82%, working capital 10.81%),
warehouse 8.47%, consumer 17.0%. (b) **Card and deposit fees** — interchange
and program fees inside $76.7M quarterly noninterest income. (c) **Tax
season** — refund-advance originations $1.87B (9M FY26), $107.7M revenue,
$77.1M pre-tax. (d) **Custodial servicing fees** — $575M of off-balance-sheet
custodial deposits earn EFFR-linked fees ($7.5M Q3). (e) **Renewable tax
benefits** — solar equipment held as rental/lease assets (XBRL
`SolarPanelsandPeripheralEquipmentMember`) depresses the effective tax rate
to ~16% vs 21% statutory.

**Protected:** The mechanism is regulatory scarcity plus switching cost: a
national bank charter with a 20-year BSA/AML and card-network record is
slow to obtain, and moving a card program's deposits and cardholders to a
new sponsor bank is a months-long migration. Post-Synapse (2024) regulators
made sponsor banking harder, which raises the moat *and* the compliance
burden. The commercial-finance book has no moat beyond underwriting skill,
which the renewable NPLs now put in question. Partner concentration (e.g.
the large tax-preparer relationship) is NOT OBTAINED this run (§6).

**Control:** One share, one vote; no controlling holder (insiders 1.42%,
institutions ~103% per stocks.db). Nothing forecloses a takeover or activist
path; Delaware corporation, but bank-holding-company acquisition rules
(Fed approval for ≥10–25% control stakes) slow any hostile bid.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue grew faster than
