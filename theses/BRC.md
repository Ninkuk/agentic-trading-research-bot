## 1. Verdict and thesis

> **PASS at $90.13.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (1 probable,
> 2 plausible, 2 unknown), refuted=0, unknown=2.

Brady is the same high-quality identification and safety business the prior
run described — 51.8% gross margin, ROIC ~19%, FY26 organic growth 5.3%
accelerating to 8.4% in Q4, printer units +25% in the quarter feeding the
consumables annuity, 41 consecutive dividend increases — and it has now put a
formal number on the transformed company: $6.25–6.75 adjusted EPS for FY27.
At $90.13 the guide-based base case clears the cost of equity by a dozen
basis points. That is the market pricing the deal as roughly fair, and the
base case is built on management's adjusted figure before the one-time
integration cash costs it excludes and before any audited view of what PSS
converts to cash. The conservative build is 176bp underwater; the optimistic
one 162bp over. Symmetric around the hurdle is the definition of a fair
price, and a fair price for an integration whose target still grows a third
as fast as its main rival is a pass. It becomes a buy at ~$84 (base +56bp)
or when the first IPS segment print (Q1 FY27, 2026-11-16) shows the
low-double-digit segment margin and the cash costs the guide hides.

**Closest attack:** IPS's relative share loss did not end with the sale.
Zebra's directly competing segment grew 7.5% organically in the July quarter
and raised guidance; Honeywell's Industrial Automation products (where PSS
sat) grew 1%; Brady guides IPS to low single digits *including* memory-driven
price increases, which management conceded means "we are expecting volumes
to go up" only modestly. A business that grows 2% nominal in a market
growing high single digits on price is stable, not fixed — and the $0.80
accretion assumes it stays at least stable.
