## 2. Business

**Created:** a working adult — median age over 30, 63% over 30, 68% in
bachelor's programs — gets an accredited degree (associate through
doctoral) in business, IT, nursing, criminal justice or psychology that
they can complete around a job, on a mobile-first platform (97% of students
opted into the app) with an adaptive learning engine (intellipath) and
session-based calendars. 91% of students are fully online (FY2025 10-K).
What they are buying is convenience and credential, not selectivity: CTU
and AIUS are open-enrollment. USAHS is the opposite product — a selective,
campus-based graduate health-science school (DPT, OTD, SLP, nursing) whose
students are buying a licensure pathway into a credentialed profession.

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (i) Tuition and fees, 99.4% of revenue,
billed per term and ~76% funded by Title IV grants and loans drawn down by
the institution on the student's behalf (deferred revenue jumps from $38M
to $80M between December and June on Title IV timing). (ii) Corporate
student programs — employer-paid tuition for partner companies' employees,
growing, and non-federal money that also helps the 90/10 ratio. (iii)
Military/VA benefits (Tuition Assistance, GI Bill), which count as federal
money under the 2023 90/10 rule. Margin structure: gross margin 79.9%;
"educational services and facilities" 24% of revenue; general and
administrative 46% — that is where marketing and admissions live, so the
reinvestment for growth runs through opex, not capex (capex ~1% of
revenue). Operating margin 24.6% TTM, 25.7% in Q2 2026.

**Protected:** not a moat in the pricing-power sense — the online adult
market is fragmented and competitors (Grand Canyon, Strategic, Adtalem,
SNHU, WGU) sell a comparable product — but three real frictions. First,
regulatory licence: Title IV certification, state authorization in every
state and institutional accreditation take years, and the Department's
change-of-ownership rules re-certify an institution provisionally for up to
three years on any control change — an entrant cannot buy its way in fast,
and the 2010s closures (Corinthian, ITT, EDMC, Dream Center) removed
capacity rather than adding it. Second, the shared-services spine
(financial aid processing, compliance, marketing, course development,
registrar) is the acquisition capability the company sells to itself: USAHS
went from $3.2M FY2025 operating income to a ~$43M adjusted run-rate in six
quarters under it. Third, retention data: eleven straight quarters of CTU
enrollment growth on retention near multi-year highs is evidence the
platform holds students once enrolled. The honest silence: nothing stops a
rival buying the same Google/LLM keywords, and management says search is
already shifting toward LLM-based discovery — its answer is that most
inquiries come from channels not affected, which is a claim, not a moat.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder; insiders own 1.04% (CEO Nelson 301,782 shares after his 2026-09-15
sale, of which 297,899 are unvested RSUs). Largest holders: BlackRock
14.3%, Vanguard 7.9%, Dimensional 6.6%, Renaissance 5.8%, First Trust 5.3%
(DEF 14A, as of 2026-03-27). The null answer applies to the charter; the
effective anti-takeover provision is regulatory — a change of control
triggers Department, state and accreditor re-approval, which the 10-K says
"may influence future decisions by us and our stockholders regarding the
sale" of the company. Compensation: the annual bonus's sole financial
metric (80% weight) is adjusted operating income; the 2025 target was
$208.0M against a $247.1M actual, a 200% payout on a target set ~8% above
the prior year's result.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $693M (2021) → $695M
→ $710M → $681M → $846M (2025) → $859M TTM; operating income $149M →
$133M → $165M → $179M → $196M → $211M. Revenue +24% over the span (the 2025
step is the USAHS acquisition, closed 2024-12-02), operating income +41%;
excluding the acquisition, 2021–2024 revenue was flat (−2%) while operating
income rose 20%, so the leverage is cost discipline plus mix, not volume.
Q1 2026 revenue $221.7M / operating income $63.1M; Q2 $213.4M / $54.9M.
