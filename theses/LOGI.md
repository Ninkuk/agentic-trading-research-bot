Logitech is a very good business at a fair price, and fair is not enough.
It sells the physical layer between people and their computers — mice (a
record 60% global share), keyboards, gaming gear, conference-room cameras
and headsets — at a 45% gross margin and a 19% non-GAAP operating margin
with 1.2% of sales in capex, and it returns nearly all of the cash: $768M
in FY26 against $976M of free cash flow, $406M in the September quarter
alone. The stock fell 12% on 2026-10-28 because the holiday-quarter guide
(non-GAAP operating income $220–255M against $312M a year ago, gross margin
41–42% against 43.5%) said the margin expansion of the past two years is
reversing on component inflation, freight, promotions and a deliberate
10% step-up in opex while sales grow 2–4%. That is a shift, not a break,
but at $95 the shares are priced for the shift not to happen: the
conservative path misses the hurdle, and only crediting the $1.4B cash
pile or a 4% owner-cash growth rate gets to par. Below roughly $87 the
same business clears on the conservative path.

## 2. Business

**Created:** a mouse, keyboard, webcam or headset that works the moment it
is plugged in, lasts years, and is noticeably better to hold and use than
the one that came in the box (MX Master, MX Keys, PRO X SUPERSTRIKE). The
customer gets ergonomics, precision and cross-device software (Options+,
G HUB) for $30–$200 — small money against the $1,500 machine it attaches
to, which is why the surplus is large and the purchase is rarely
price-shopped. For a business buyer, Logitech's Rally and MeetUp room
systems are the certified hardware layer for Teams/Zoom/Meet rooms,
managed at fleet scale through Sync. 71% of the Fortune 500 buy Personal
Workspace products (CEO, transcribed).

**Captured:** five mechanisms, not one. (i) Premium-priced consumer
peripherals through retail and e-tail — Amazon 18%, Ingram Micro 14%, TD
Synnex 12% of FY26 gross sales, no long-term commitments (10-K). (ii)
Gaming hardware under the Logitech G brand (28–29% of sales, +7% cc),
where esports sponsorships and title partnerships (Call of Duty) set the
spec. (iii) Video Collaboration (15% of sales, +12% cc) sold to IT
departments on a 5–7-year refresh cycle, with Q2 growth 60% price / 40%
volume (CFO). (iv) Service attach — Sync management, Streamlabs Ultra
subscriptions — "recurring, high-margin" per the letter but not disclosed
as a figure. (v) Manufacturing economics: ~35% of production by value in
the owned Suzhou plant (2,300 of 7,300 employees), the rest at ODMs/CMs in
China, Vietnam, Thailand, Mexico, Malaysia and Taiwan, so capex runs 1.2%
of sales and ROIC prints above 100% (statistics probe).

**Protected:** a mechanism with a soft edge. Distribution shelf and
category captaincy at the three channel giants; a brand that lets a $99
mouse sit beside a $15 house brand (Amazon Basics is both customer and
competitor, per the 10-K); 45 years of input-device engineering (the
haptic trigger system in SUPERSTRIKE); and in Video Collaboration the
platform certifications and installed-base management that make a
competitor's room system a procurement project rather than a swap. What
it does not have: switching costs for a consumer, patents management
itself calls "of less competitive significance than innovation, ease of
use and design" (10-K), or a platform owner's blessing — Microsoft, Apple,
Google and Amazon can prefer their own peripherals (Item 1A). The 60%
mouse share has compounded at roughly 50bp a year for a decade (CEO),
which is what a real but slow moat looks like; Chinese challengers
(Rapoo, Keychron, Mchose, Eweadn) are named in the 10-K competitor list
for the first time as "less-established" pricing pressure.

**Control:** one class of registered shares, CHF 0.25 par, one vote each,
no voting cap, no preferential voting rights, no non-voting equity (10-K
Exhibit 4.1). No controller: UBS Asset Management 7.6%, BlackRock 6.8%
(proxy, 2026-06-30 basis); directors and officers as a group 0.15%; the
CEO holds 14,815 shares (~$1.4M) after nearly three years. Shareholders
holding 0.5% may add agenda items and 5% may call a meeting; Swiss
takeover law governs an unsolicited bid (the exhibit records no opting-out
clause). Nothing in the structure forecloses an activist or a bidder; the
27.6% say-on-pay dissent at the 2026-09-08 AGM shows the owners vote.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive on the current leg, after a
negative leg; revenue is still 10% below its FY22 peak.**
