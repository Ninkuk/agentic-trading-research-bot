## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $6.65.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (3 probable,
2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

Icahn Enterprises is a listed holding company that publishes its own
indicative net asset value every quarter. At 6/30/2026 that figure was
$2.602B against 710.9M units — **$3.66 per unit** — while the unit closed
that day at $7.21. Marking the one large exchange-observable leg (CVR
Energy) to today and applying the company's own July fund figure puts NAV
near **$4.08 per unit**, against a $6.65 price: you are paying about $1.63
for $1.00 of the company's own stated value. The 30% distribution yield is
not income — the default election is additional units, unit count has grown
from 293M to 711M since 2021 (~21%/yr), and stockanalysis puts the buyback
yield at −21.15% against the 30.08% dividend yield, i.e. a ~9% shareholder
yield before any NAV decline. The distribution's own arithmetic then
requires the underlying assets to compound at roughly **38%/yr** merely to
hold NAV per unit flat; the investment funds returned **−10.9%** last
quarter.

**Closest attack:** the premium is not new and has never converged — it was
~43% at 12/31/2024 and ~49% at 12/31/2025, so a pass that waited for
convergence would have waited five years. The answer is that this thesis
does not need convergence: the unit fell 88% over five years *with the
premium intact*, because NAV per unit fell faster. The pass rests on NAV
decay, not on re-rating.

**Load-bearing conditions (5):**

1. *plausible* — Indicative NAV (~$3.66/unit disclosed, ~$4.08 marked to
   today) is a fair measure of the assets behind a unit. Seven of eight
   segment marks are either exchange-observable or small; the $1,417M real
   estate mark (54% of net NAV) is management's own and I could not reach
   its basis (§6.1).
2. *probable* — NAV per unit is compounding down: $6.31 (12/31/24) → $5.02
   (12/31/25) → $3.66 (6/30/26), −42% in 18 months.
3. *probable* — The distribution is a return of capital, not income. Units
   +142% since FY2021; buyback yield −21.15%; the rate has been cut from
   $2.00/qtr to $1.00 to $0.50 since 2023.
4. *probable* — Nothing forces the premium to close. Units are not
   redeemable, Icahn holds ~86%, and IEP's own 10-K says the indicative NAV
   "does not represent the market price."
5. *plausible* — The Pep Boys sale does not change the trajectory for
   unitholders: $700M (~27% of NAV) is earmarked for the 2027 notes, i.e.
   it moves value from the automotive mark to debt reduction inside a NAV
   that is already net of holdco debt.

## 2. Business

**Created:** almost nothing, at the holdco level. IEP is a wrapper. What a
unitholder gets is a claim on eight marks — investment funds $1,978M, CVR
Energy $1,961M, real estate $1,417M, Icahn Automotive $765M, Vivus $153M,
WestPoint Home $148M, Viskase $96M, CVR Partners $30M ($6,548M gross) — less
$4,426M of holdco debt plus $381M of holdco cash, netting to the disclosed
$2,602M at 6/30/2026. The operating subsidiaries create value in the
ordinary way (CVR refines crude and makes nitrogen fertilizer at >98% crude
utilization; Viskase makes casings; WestPoint makes home textiles). The
holdco's own contribution is capital allocation and activist campaigns.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, and only one of them is a business.
(a) Consolidated subsidiary profits — $102M of energy-segment adjusted
EBITDA in Q2 2026 vs $40M a year earlier, the only segment working.
(b) Investment-fund returns on ~$2.0B of invested capital plus $741M cash —
this quarter −7.7% ex-refining-hedges, −10.9% including them, with long
positions +3.9% and shorts −15.5% in attribution. (c) **Unit issuance.**
Because the distribution's default election is additional units priced at a
five-day VWAP, IEP funds most of a $355M quarterly distribution without
cash: only ~$71M of a comparable prior-quarter distribution went out in
cash. That is not value capture from a customer; it is a transfer from
unit-electing holders to cash-electing ones.

**Protected:** at the operating level, CVR's refining assets have real
replacement-cost and permitting barriers, and the fertilizer business is
cost-advantaged on cheap gas. At the holdco level there is no moat and the
question is the wrong one — what protects the *unit price* is the premium
to NAV, and the honest answer is that nothing protects it. IEP's own risk
factors name short-selling attacks and Icahn's pledged units as risks to
the unit price. Two of the three legs (funds, holdco structure) are
capital, not franchise.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The premium, measured at three dated points.** Pairing each disclosed NAV
with the unit count and price of the same date: 12/31/2024 — $3.3B /
522.74M = $6.31 vs ~$9.00, **+43%**; 12/31/2025 — $3.2B / 637.21M = $5.02
vs ~$7.50, **+49%**; 6/30/2026 — $2.602B / 710.9M = $3.66 vs $7.21,
**+97%**. The premium widened while the price fell, because NAV fell
faster. (Caveat: the 710,915,093 count is the 10-Q cover-date figure, a few
weeks after 6/30, so the 6/30 NAV/unit is slightly understated and the +97%
slightly overstated; using the Q2 weighted-average 669M it would be +85%.)

**Marking the CVR leg independently — the one mark I could verify.** IEP
carries CVR Energy at $1,961M at 6/30/2026. CVI closed near $28.0 that
week, implying ~70.0M shares, or ~70% of CVI's 100.53M shares outstanding —
consistent with IEP's known ~71% stake. **The mark is the exchange price,
not a private markup**, which materially strengthens condition 1 for 75% of
gross NAV. CVI closed $36.70 on 2026-08-19, so the same 70.0M shares are
worth ~$2,569M today, **+$608M** over the 6/30 mark. That corroborates the
company's stated "+$575M from CVI through July." Applying it and the
company's stated −$312M fund decline gives an estimated current NAV of
~$2,898M, or **$4.08/unit** — the number §4 uses.

**The distribution mechanics, and who pays.** From the company's own
disclosure that a comparable quarterly distribution sent out $71M of cash,
the cash-election fraction is ~14%. Cross-checking against observed unit
growth (637.21M at FY2025 → 710.92M, ~5.0%/quarter at an average price near
$7.90) implies ~20%. Either way, ~80% of the distribution is paid in new
units. Per quarter, NAV/unit goes to `(n − c·0.50) / (1 + (1−c)·0.50/P)`;
at n=$4.08, P=$6.65, c=0.20 that is −8.0%/quarter, **−28%/yr with zero
investment return** — and that rate *accelerates*, because at a constant
premium the price falls with NAV, so each fixed $0.50 buys progressively
more units. Note the perverse corner in the same equation: a *higher*
premium reduces the dilution rate. The premium is load-bearing for the
distribution's own sustainability, which is why a de-rating and a
distribution cut arrive together rather than as independent events.

## 4. Valuation

**`reverse_dcf` refuses this input, correctly.** TTM levered FCF is
−$244M (operating cash flow $107M + capex −$351M):

```
$ uv run python -m tools.valuation.reverse_dcf --market-cap 4728000000 \
    --base-fcf -244000000 --growth 0.02 0.02 0.02 0.02 0.02 \
    --terminal-growth 0.02 --risk-free 0.0474 --beta 0.8 --erp 0.0428
refused: base_fcf must be positive, got -244000000.0        # exit 2
```

That is a category refusal, not an error: a holding company's value is its
asset marks less its debt, and IEP publishes exactly that. The honest
substitute follows. **No reinvestment or terminal-ROE warning was printed
because no DCF ran**; the terminal question is answered below in the
distribution arithmetic instead.

**Inputs and pairing.** Indicative NAV $2,602M at 6/30/2026 against
710,915,093 units = **$3.660/unit**. Marked forward for the one
exchange-observable leg (CVI +$608M to the 8/19 close) and the company's own
stated July fund decline (−$312M): ~$2,898M = **$4.08/unit**. No SBC
haircut is needed (this is a NAV, not a cash flow) and **no minority-interest
haircut applies** — indicative NAV is already IEP's own share of each
segment, and it is already net of the $4,426M holdco debt, so pairing it
against market cap is correct and adding `--net-debt` would double-count.
Gearing: $6,548M gross segment value over $2,602M net NAV = **2.52×**, so a
10% decline in gross asset value is a 25% decline in NAV.

**Hurdle.** rf 4.74% + beta 0.80 × ERP 4.28% = **8.16%** (Damodaran, as of
2026-08-01). Beta prints 0.74 on the statistics page — below the 0.8–1.2
stable band, and structurally so, because the funds run a net-short book
and ~86% of units never trade. Floored to 0.80 and stated. Note the absolute
companion check: a mature company's cost of capital ≈ rf + 4.5% = 9.24%, so
8.16% is if anything generous to the name.

**The substituted scenario table.** Not a `reverse_dcf` output. Three-year
paths for a buyer at $6.65 who elects **cash** each quarter (the favourable
election — it extracts NAV from unit-electors), holding the premium multiple
constant at 1.63× and running NAV/unit through
`n' = (n·(1+r) − c·D) / (1 + (1−c)·D/P)` with c=0.20:

| scenario | asset return | distribution path | NAV/unit yr3 | price yr3 | cash 3y | IRR | vs hurdle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — flat assets, distribution held | 0%/yr | $2.00/yr held | $0.43 | $0.70 | $6.00 | +0.4% | −776 bp |
| A′ — flat assets, distribution cut as NAV shrinks | 0%/yr | $2.00 → $1.00 → $0.50 | $1.79 | $2.92 | $3.50 | −1.5% | −966 bp |
| B — good asset year, distribution held | +10%/yr | $2.00/yr held | $1.04 | $1.69 | $6.00 | +6.8% | −136 bp |
| C — what the price needs | **+38%/yr** | $2.00/yr held | $4.08 | $6.65 | $6.00 | +30% | +2,194 bp |

IRRs run on the actual quarterly cash-flow timing (−$6.65 at t0,
distributions as received, the unit sold at year 3), not on a terminal
lump — which flatters the early years and is the fair way to state the
bull case.

**The dilution is self-reinforcing, and that is the finding.** At a constant
1.63× premium a falling NAV per unit drags the price down with it, so each
fixed $0.50 distribution issues progressively more units: the decay runs
−8.0% in the first quarter and −26% by the twelfth. Scenario A therefore
does not settle at a lower level — it consumes itself, ending with NAV/unit
at $0.43. **Holding the $2.00 rate constant is not a scenario, it is an
arithmetic impossibility**, which is why the board has already cut twice
(from $2.00/qtr through May 2023, to $1.00, to $0.50). A′ models the cut
the arithmetic forces and returns **−1.5%/yr** against an 8.16% hurdle,
before any premium compression — and compression is what has actually
accompanied each prior cut. Even B — a genuinely good year for the funds
and CVI, with the distribution somehow held — lands 136 bp *below* the
hurdle. Scenario C states the requirement plainly: **the assets must
compound at ~38%/yr** for today's price to be defensible (algebraically,
the per-quarter gain needed to hold n flat is `(1−c)·D·n/P + c·D` = 8.5% of
NAV). The funds returned −10.9% last quarter.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Distribution clamp.* The scenario IRRs run −1.5% to +6.8%, i.e. at or
  below the 10th percentile of the US cost-of-capital distribution (80% of
  US firms sit between 5.26% and 9.88%; median 7.79%). Only scenario C,
  which needs a 38%/yr asset return, clears it.
- *Excess-return base rate.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of
  capital. IEP's operating income has been at or below zero in four of the
  last six periods (§2) and its funds have a net-short book; assuming
  persistence of an excess return here has no evidence behind it.
- *Market-share sentence.* **Not applicable, and stated rather than
  skipped**: there is no addressable market for a holding company. The
  equivalent Damodaran "bigger than the market" check for this structure is
  scenario C's 38%/yr asset return, which is the implausible number, and it
  is printed above rather than buried in a growth path.
- *Terminal risk vs terminal growth.* No terminal growth rate was used (no
  DCF ran). The dominant structural risk the 10-K names — that the
  indicative NAV "does not represent the market price," units are not
  redeemable, and holders have "no right to obtain the indicative net asset
  value" — is precisely the risk that makes a terminal convergence
  assumption unusable here, which is why none was made. **Source caveat: I
  read that risk-factor language through a web-search rendering of the 10-K,
  not the filing itself (SEC 403, §3), and tier it low-confidence.**
- *Base-year cash tax rate.* Not applicable — a partnership; taxes pass
  through to unitholders on a K-1. This is itself an ownership friction
  (§6.3).

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy):**

- **Shift —** Price falls to or below the disclosed indicative NAV per unit.
  At ~$4.08 today that is a −39% move; at that level you would be buying the
  assets at or under management's own mark and the distribution arithmetic
  reverses in the holder's favour.
- **Shift —** A distribution cut to a rate the NAV can actually fund
  (roughly $0.10–0.15/quarter at current NAV), *accompanied by* the price
  falling to NAV. A cut alone is not the signal; a cut that stops the
  ~28%/yr mechanical NAV bleed while the price also de-rates is.
- **Shift —** The Q3 print (2026-11-04) shows NAV per unit *rising* on
  something other than the CVI mark — i.e. the funds stop losing and the
  Pep Boys proceeds land without a corresponding NAV write-down.
- **Break —** A credible independent appraisal or a third-party sale of the
  real estate portfolio at materially above the $1,417M mark. Total NAV
  would have to reach $4.73B to justify $6.65, which with everything else
  unchanged means real estate at ~$3.54B, 2.5× the mark. If that were shown,
  the whole pass fails at condition 1.

**For an owner (sell):**

- **Break —** A forced sale of Icahn's pledged units (a margin call, an
  estate event, or a loan-covenant trip). ~86% of the float behind one
  90-year-old holder with a pledge history is the tail that ends the name.
- **Break —** The 2027 notes are not addressed by the Pep Boys proceeds and
  IEP has to refinance at a distressed coupon, or issues equity into the
  premium at scale.
- **Shift —** Fund losses continue at the Q2 pace (−10.9%/quarter). Two more
  such quarters puts NAV/unit near $2.50 with the price still above $6.

## Kill-thesis record

**Ledger:** UNPROVEN — conditions=5, refuted=0, unknown=1.

**Per-condition adjudication:**

1. *NAV fairly measures the assets* — **UNKNOWN**. Attack run: try to show
   the marks are conservative, which would collapse the premium. It partly
   failed — the CVR leg ($1,961M, 75% of gross NAV together with the funds)
   reconciles to ~70.0M CVI shares at the 6/30 market price, so it is an
   exchange mark and cannot be understated. The funds ($1,978M) are marked
   to their own book. But the $1,417M real estate mark, 54% of net NAV, could
   not be attacked in either direction: its basis lives in a 10-K note that
   SEC EDGAR would not serve. Marked UNKNOWN rather than credited, per the
   rule that uncertainty never earns SOUND.
2. *NAV per unit is compounding down* — **SURVIVED**. Attack: the decline is
   an artifact of one bad quarter's CVI mark, and CVI has since rallied.
   Partly true — CVI is +$608M since 6/30 — but the series is five
   consecutive dated observations ($6.31 → $5.02 → $3.66 per unit) and the
   mechanical distribution bleed of −28%/yr operates independently of any
   asset move.
3. *The distribution is not income* — **SURVIVED**. Attack: a cash-electing
   holder receives real cash and does not care about unit count. Correct as
   far as it goes, and modelled generously in scenario A. It fails on the
   next step: at a constant premium the price falls with NAV, so each fixed
   $0.50 issues more units than the last, and holding the rate consumes
   NAV/unit down to $0.43 in three years. Scenario A′ — with the cut the
   arithmetic forces — returns −1.5%/yr. Even a +10%/yr asset year with the
   rate held (scenario B, +6.8%) falls short of the 8.16% hurdle.
4. *Nothing forces the premium to close* — **SURVIVED**, and it is the
   company's own disclosed risk factor. Note this condition cuts *against*
   the pass as much as for it: it is why the premium has persisted for years
   and why this is a valuation pass, not a short case.
5. *Pep Boys does not change the trajectory* — **SURVIVED**. Attack: $700M
   is 27% of NAV and materially deleverages. But indicative NAV is already
   net of holdco debt, so paying down debt with sale proceeds is close to
   NAV-neutral by construction; it improves the maturity profile, not the
   per-unit value.

**Standing checks.** *Base rate:* only ~29% of firms earn above their cost
of capital, and closed-end structures trading above stated asset value
persistently underperform their own NAV — IEP is −88% over five years with
the premium intact, which is the base rate in this name's own history.
*Short case (strongest form):* a fixed-dollar distribution funded by issuing
units at a premium is a self-consuming structure — the NAV per unit
mechanically decays ~28%/yr, the fixed rate becomes unpayable, the cut
compresses the premium, and the lower price accelerates the dilution rate on
the next distribution; the short is 18.36% of float at 27.98 days to cover,
so it is crowded but the mechanism is arithmetic, not sentiment. *Management
incentives:* Icahn owns ~86% and takes units, so his interest is squarely in
maintaining the premium at which those units are issued — the thesis does
not require him to act against that incentive; it observes that a minority
buyer is on the other side of it. *Disconfirming search:* I went looking for
reasons the marks understate value (the CVI reconciliation, the real estate
concentration, the Pep Boys retained assets) rather than for confirmation,
and one of those three — CVI — came back against the bear case on that leg.
*Moat as mechanism, not checkbox:* at the holdco level the answer to "what
stops a competitor tomorrow" is nothing, and that silence is written into §2
rather than papered over with "activist track record."

**Statistical checks.** The premium series is 3 dated observations, not a
sample — quoted as measurements, never as a trend with significance. The
NAV/unit series is 5 quarterly points from a single issuer, so effective n
is 1 issuer, and no hit rate or p-value is claimed anywhere in this
document. No repo signal or backtest is relied on (the DBs were unreadable),
so there is no multiple-comparisons or overlapping-window exposure.
