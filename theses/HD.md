## 2. Business

**Created:** a homeowner or contractor gets the widest local assortment of
home-improvement product (roughly 30,000–40,000 SKUs per store, a million
online) available today, at a price the largest buyer in the category can
set, with the store itself as a same-day pickup and delivery node — 65% of
stocked parcel deliveries now arrive same- or next-day and a three-hour
Express tier went nationwide in August. For the Pro, the surplus is time:
one supplier open at 6am with job-lot quantities, trade credit, a sales
desk, and since SRS/GMS a catalog that reaches roofing, drywall, pool and
landscape from the same account. For the DIYer it is confidence — the
store associate, and now the Magic Apron assistant, that turns a project
they were not sure they could do into one they attempt.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, all product margin. (1) Retail
gross margin on $152B of Primary-segment sales (32.8% TTM company-wide) —
a buying-scale spread over smaller rivals, partly reinvested in price.
(2) Distribution margin on $12.7B of SRS/GMS "Other" sales (roofing 53% of
it) — a lower gross margin, higher-turn branch model management says earns
a higher ROIC per branch than a store. (3) Services and credit: $5.7B of
installation and rental services, plus a private-label credit card and
trade credit that management notes Pros "increasingly use" — credit is a
demand enabler here, not a captured spread (the receivables sit with the
bank partner). Advertising (Orange Apron Media) exists but is not
separately disclosed. Roughly half of sales are Pro (Investor Day 2025,
primary-transcribed).

**Protected:** scale purchasing (the largest buyer of most of what it
sells, and a proprietary battery-platform lineup competitors cannot stock)
and store density — 2,364 boxes averaging 104,000 sq ft plus 1,340 SRS
branches, which is the delivery network a pure-play e-commerce entrant
would have to build. The second mechanism is the Pro relationship: a
contractor's account, credit line, and now cross-platform catalog are
switching costs of hours per week, not dollars. Neither mechanism protects
against Lowe's, which has the same shape one size smaller; the gap between
them is execution (HD's US comp beat Lowe's by 1.1 points in Q2) rather
than structure. No moat protects against the housing cycle.

**Control:** one class, one vote per share; 996.0M shares at the 2026 record
date. Largest holders Vanguard 10.0% and BlackRock 7.1% (proxy, Schedule
13G/A basis). Shareholders may call special meetings and act by written
consent; proxy access and majority voting in place; lead independent
director (Brenneman) chairs the board during the CEO's leave. Nothing in
the structure forecloses a takeover or activist path; the $320B size does.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The Q2 beat was a cash refund, not demand.** Reported gross margin rose
  25bp; management's own bridge is +145bp from a $685M IEEPA tariff refund
  booked to COGS, −60bp from "unplanned" fuel, energy and commodity cost,
  and −60bp from SRS/GMS mix. Ex-refund the margin fell ~120bp. Guidance was
  reaffirmed only because the refund "fully offsets" the cost pressure over
  the year; Q3 gross margin will carry the offset and Q4 is guided flat YoY.
  Adjusted EPS +5.1% is therefore a one-time cash event on the P&L (call,
  10-Q). Section 301 tariffs replacing IEEPA are also incremental to the
  plan.
- **Management has called the bottom of housing turnover for two and a half
  years.** Corpus scan (88 of 92 calls fetched, 2012-06→2026-08; the
  company's first 29 years as a filer are uncovered, so silence before 2012
  is unquantified): "housing turnover" appears in 45 management documents.
  The specific claim that turnover is at a floor — "40-year low, don't see
  that going lower" (2024-02), "most folks think that can't get much lower"
  (2024-05), "one could argue we're at the bottom" (2024-11), "I'm not sure
  it could be any lower" (2025-04), "going on four years" (2026-04), "it has
  always bounced up relatively quickly" (2026-08) — has been repeated at
  eleven consecutive earnings seasons while comps stayed 0–2%. Two older

   recovered HD earns 15% or 13.5% operating margin decides whether the
   recovery path is 7.7% or nearer 6%. Only a recovery year discloses it.
   Absence does not kill the pass; it bounds the upside below the hurdle
   either way.

   "increasingly use trade credit" and their ability to pay depends on
   their industry's health; no receivable aging or loss rate is disclosed
   for SRS/GMS trade credit. Would come from a credit-quality note; absent
   one this is a small, unquantified tail on the FCF base.
