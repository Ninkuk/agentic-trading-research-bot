## 2. Business

**Created:** a manufacturer needs someone whose signature a regulator, an
insurer, a retailer and an authority-having-jurisdiction will all accept. UL
Solutions tests a product against a safety standard and, if it passes, lets the
manufacturer put the UL mark on it. What the customer buys is not the test — it
is market access: the electrical inspector who signs off on the installation,
the big-box buyer whose vendor requirements name the mark, the insurer who
prices the risk. The surplus is large and asymmetric, because the alternative to
a $50k certification is not a cheaper certification, it is not selling the
product.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one fee.

- **Certification testing (~28% of revenue)** — project fees to get a new
  product listed. This is the acquisition motion, and it grew 10.9% YTD 2026.
- **Ongoing certification services (~33%)** — the annuity. Once a product
  bears the mark, the manufacturer pays UL annually for factory surveillance
  and follow-up service, per plant, per product family, for as long as the
  product ships.
- **Non-certification testing and other (~30%)** — performance, EMC,
  benchmarking and advisory work sold to the same buyer, off the same lab
  footprint. This is where utilization leverage lives, and where the US
  solar/wind advisory book sits — the CFO named it as the Q2 soft spot.
- **Software (~9%)** — Risk & Compliance Software. On Q2 2026 numbers this
  segment earned $0M of operating income on $52M of revenue. It is a
  cross-sell hook, not a profit centre, and management has been shrinking it
  (EHS divested April 2026; DQS stake sale pending).

**Protected:** a switching cost borne by the customer, not by UL. Moving to
another certifier means re-testing and re-listing every affected product, then
persuading every downstream party that accepts the UL mark by name to accept
the substitute. The mark itself is owned by UL Solutions — the FY2025 10-K
states "We are the owner of the iconic UL-in-a-circle certification mark" —
which is the single most important fact in this section, because the obvious
structural worry (a brand licensed from the nonprofit parent) does not apply.
Standards *development* sits with UL Standards & Engagement, a separate
nonprofit; testing against them sits here. The mark is embedded in US
electrical codes and retailer vendor requirements, which is what makes this a
mechanism rather than a label: a competitor can build an equally good lab
tomorrow and still not be the name written into the code.

The honest limit: this protects the installed base, not the next dollar. In
non-certification testing — 30% of revenue and the fastest-growing slice —
SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek and TÜV compete on capability and price with no
mark advantage at all.

**Control:** two classes. Class A 78,143,173 shares (public float 77.11M);
Class B 123,755,000 shares held by UL Standards & Engagement, whose sole member
is UL Research Institutes. The 10-Q states ULSE "controls the majority of the
voting power." The exact votes-per-share ratio was not disclosed in the
documents read this run, and it does not change the conclusion: at 61% of
shares outstanding ULSE has absolute control at one vote per share. This
forecloses an unsolicited takeover, any activist path, and any shareholder vote
management does not want. It also means the controlling holder is a nonprofit
funding an endowment — a shareholder whose objective function is cash, on its
own schedule, not share-price maximization.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive**, and strongly so.

**Inputs.** Levered TTM free cash flow $436M (operating cash flow $678M less

exactly what prints an artificially low regression beta and would hand the
hurdle a free pass. The unclamped 0.62 would give a 7.51% hurdle; the

**Integrity checks.**

- **Reinvestment / terminal ROE, answered.** The base run implies a terminal
  reinvestment rate of 7.95% and therefore a terminal ROE of **29.33%** —
  more than 20 points above the 8.25% hurdle, which is "tough to do" in
  perpetuity by any standard. It is close to ULS's actual ROE (38.71%) and

- **Break —** a buyback authorization large enough to absorb ULSE's
  distributions, or a disclosed orderly-disposition schedule that bounds them.
  This would convert condition 4 from unknowable into a quantity, which is
  what currently prevents any confidence in the price path.

**For an owner (sell)**

- **Break —** H2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin below the ~27% guide. The entire
  bull case is a margin story; the guide missing is the story ending, not an
  input moving.
- **Break —** the February 2026 class action surviving a motion to dismiss with
  a certified nationwide class, or any settlement large enough to require a
  disclosed accrual. The risk is not the dollars, it is a court record about
  concealment attached to the mark.
- **Break —** ULSE selling a block at a discount to the prevailing price. That
  establishes the clearing level for the remaining ~$7.9B and re-prices the
  float.
- **Shift —** capex guidance rising again above 8.5% of revenue, or FCF margin
  falling below 11% for a second consecutive quarter.
- **Shift —** the Eurofins E&E deal closing materially above €575M, or
  breaking on regulatory grounds after the €34.5M break-fee exposure.
