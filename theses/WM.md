## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $224.92.** kill-thesis: **SOUND** — conditions=5 (4 probable,
1 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0.

WM is the best kind of boring: an irreplaceable disposal network that prices
ahead of cost inflation every year, run by a management team that guides
conservatively and hits. The Stericycle integration is done, leverage is back
inside target, and a three-year sustainability capex cycle is ending — FCF is
guided to $3.75–3.85B, roughly double three years ago. None of that is in
dispute. The price is: even a guide-anchored optimistic reverse DCF implies
7.87%/yr, 29bp short of an 8.16% cost-of-equity hurdle, and the base case
implies 7.43% (−73bp). A wonderful business at a price that pre-pays for the
wonder is a pass, not a buy. The flip price is ~$196 (−13%), where the base
case meets the hurdle.

**Closest attack:** the beta floor. WM's raw regression beta is 0.44; taken
at face value the hurdle drops to 6.62% and the base case clears it by
+81bp, flipping the verdict. The floor to 0.8 survives because a 6.62% cost
of equity — 188bp over the 10-year Treasury, below the US *median firmwide
WACC* of 7.79%, on equity behind $22.8B of net debt at 2.96× EBITDA — is not
a return a rational owner requires; it is the "artificially low beta hands
the hurdle a free pass" case the anchors reference exists to catch.

Load-bearing conditions of the pass:

   return; raw 0.44 fails the mature-firm companion (cost of capital ≈

2. *probable* — Base FCF ($3.573B TTM / $3.8B guide midpoint) is not

## 2. Business

**Created:** somebody has to take the trash, every week, forever. WM sells
non-discretionary removal and disposal of solid, recyclable, and medical
waste to ~commercial/industrial/residential customers across the US and
Canada — reliability and regulatory compliance the customer cannot
practically self-provide. Demand is population-and-GDP-shaped, nearly
recession-proof (beta 0.44), and contractual.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one. (1) Collection fees with
annual price escalation — Q2'26 core price 5.7%, C&D yield 3.6%, against
~4% labor inflation, with collection operating-cost growth held under 1.7%
by routing/automation (Smart Truck: $300M run-rate EBITDA, per management).
(2) Disposal tipping fees at 257 landfills (219 owned) and transfer
stations — the internalization spread: competitors' waste pays WM's gate.
(3) Commodity-linked streams: recycled fiber/plastics sales and renewable
natural gas (RIN credits; 90% of 2026 volumes locked, 1/3 of 2027 pre-sold).
(4) Stericycle/WMHS: regulated medical-waste disposal and secure information
destruction, now cross-sold through the national-accounts machine.

**Protected:** the moat is a permit, not a brand. Per WM's own 10-K, new
disposal permits "have become more difficult and expensive to obtain," take
years of hearings, and face organized public opposition; land near
metropolitan areas is scarce. Nobody gets to build a competing landfill
network — the asset base is grandfathered scarcity, and management talks
about it exactly this way (Q2 call, on the Medley/South Florida land + rail
purchases: "how important that moat is around our business, which is
disposal"). Route density in collection is a second, weaker mechanism —
contestable at the edges (residential churn, national-account price
losses), which is exactly where WM is currently shedding volume on purpose.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $17.93B (2021) →
$25.67B TTM (+43%); operating income $2.96B → $4.84B (+64%); margin 16.5% →
18.9%, with a one-year dip in FY2025 (18.6%) as lower-margin Stericycle
revenue folded in. (FY figures: /financials/income-statement/, TTM row
excluded from FY indexing.)

  exactly at $78.2B market cap; verified by re-run).
- **Shift —** an FY2027 guide (Q4'26 call, ~late Jan 2027) putting FCF at
  ≥$4.3B with capex flat-to-down — evidence the step-up exceeds the 8/8/7
  path already credited.

- **Break —** price-to-cost spread inverts: core price at or below cost
  inflation for two consecutive quarters (the moat's cash register).
- **Break —** a structural RFS/D3 RIN policy reversal that strands the RNG
  buildout's returns.
- **Shift —** C&D volume declines steepen past ~−2% FY ex-events, or
  national-account losses spread from commercial into disposal
  internalization.
- **Shift —** leverage above 3.0× without a named acquisition — capital
  discipline break.
