## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $12.95.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=5 (1 probable,
2 plausible, 1 possible, 1 refuted), refuted=1, unknown=1.

American is the largest airline in the world by passengers and the most
levered of the US big three, and in 2026 it is running an unhedged fuel
book into a Middle East supply shock: Q2 fuel cost $4.05/gal against $2.29
a year earlier, the full-year headwind is guided near $6B, and Brent sat at
$92–95 on September 1 after the US–Iran ceasefire lapsed. Revenue is at a
record (+16% YoY in Q2, premium and corporate leading) and liquidity is
$11.3B with 2027 refinanced, so this is not a solvency call for the next
eighteen months. It is a pass because the equity is a call option on the
firm (net debt is 76% of EV, book equity is −$4.0B), because the case for
owning that option rests on a fuel path nobody can evidence, and because
the one thing that would make the option worth more than the market pays —
margin expansion when fuel normalises — is contradicted by the most recent
normal-fuel year: 2025 fuel fell 8% and operating income halved. Trailing
levered free cash flow of $285M against an $8.57B market cap implies a
5.55% return, 478 bp under a 10.33% cost of equity, and the next three
years of cash flow are spoken for by a $4.0–4.5B/yr fleet capex programme
with $24.3B of aircraft commitments behind it.

**Closest attack:** on the pass. Price the equity as the option it is —
market EV $35.94B against $35.85B of debt face including cumulated coupons,
four years' duration, Damodaran's 37.77% air-transport firm-value vol — and
the call is worth $13.1B, 1.53× the $8.57B the market pays; even at 30% vol
it is 1.31×. Management's own pre-shock plan (FY26 pre-tax ~$1.5B, "four
times 2025") run as a normalisation path from the trailing base clears the
hurdle by 368 bp. The attack fails because the option value is time
premium on a 45% risk-neutral chance the firm covers its debt at maturity,
not a claim on cash flow; and because the normalisation path needs the
margin expansion that condition 5 shows has not arrived in three
normal-fuel years. A cheaper option on the same unevidenced path is still
the same bet.

Load-bearing conditions for an ownership case (tested, not endorsed):

1. *possible* — Jet fuel returns toward the 2025 average ($2.39/gal, 10-K)
   within roughly 18 months. Geopolitical; no disclosure can evidence it.
   AAL carries no hedges and $50M of annual expense per cent per gallon
   (10-K, Q2 10-Q). Marked UNKNOWN in the kill pass.
2. *plausible* — The 2026 revenue gains are structural (premium +19%,
   managed corporate +26%, AAdvantage enrolments +32%, Citi exclusive from
   January 2026 — Q2 call, 10-K) rather than industry fare inflation on
   4–5% capacity cuts every carrier made at once.
3. *probable* — Liquidity bridges to 2028: $11.3B available liquidity at
   June 30, the 2027 Series K and January-2027 term loans refinanced into
   2033 paper, $1.3B of incremental Q2 financings (10-Q Note 5, Q2 call).
4. *plausible* — Fleet capex does not consume the whole operating cash
   flow: ~$4.0B capex in 2026 and ~$4.5B in 2027 against management's
   guide of positive FY26 FCF and lower year-end net debt at the midpoint
   (Q2 call). FY2025 printed −$680M on $3.8B capex.
5. *refuted* — Margins expand when fuel normalises, taking net debt/EBITDA
   inside 3× and the rating to BB (CFO, Q2 call). The trajectory runs the
   other way: operating income $4.01B (2023) → $3.37B (2024) → $1.69B
   (2025) → $1.14B (TTM) on revenue up 11%, and 2025 was a *cheaper*-fuel
   year than 2024 ($2.39 vs $2.60/gal) in which operating income halved.

## 2. Business

**Created:** a network. American sells the ability to get from more of
North America to more of the world on one ticket than any other carrier —
ten hubs, more than 6,000 daily flights to 350+ destinations, joint
businesses with British Airways (Heathrow), JAL (Tokyo) and Qantas
(Sydney). The customer who values it most is the premium and corporate
flyer who wants a lie-flat seat, a lounge, and a schedule that connects;
nearly 60% of revenue comes from households above $150k (Q2 call). The
mass customer gets a bargain — real fares are below 2019 (CEO, Q2 call) —
which is the surplus and also the problem: the marginal seat is priced at
cost.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one. (i) Passenger travel
$45.6B in 2025 — the ticket, yield-managed by cabin; premium is ~half of
ticketed revenue on ~30% of seats (10-K revenue note, Q2 call).
(ii) Loyalty — travel $4.0B recognised inside passenger revenue on mile
redemptions, plus $3.5B of "marketing services" in other revenue: Citi (US
exclusive from 2026, a 10-year agreement) and Mastercard pay for miles and
brand access; cash from co-brand and other partners was $6.2B in 2025
(10-K). This is the highest-margin dollar in the company and the collateral
behind the AAdvantage term loan. (iii) Cargo $0.8B. (iv) Other $0.6B —
bags, seats, change fees, the Basic-to-Main upsell that rose 5 points in Q2.
The cost side is what decides whether any of it is kept: fuel was 20% of
opex in 2025 at $2.39/gal and is the single unhedged line; labor is at
"market-based, top-of-industry" rates already (CEO, Q2 call), with the
pilot contract amendable August 2027.

**Protected:** slots, gates and the hub-and-spoke fixed cost base — no
entrant can replicate DFW, Charlotte or Miami — plus the loyalty programme's
switching cost for the elite-tier flyer and the co-brand card's annuity.
Against the other two network carriers there is no moat: same aircraft,
same fuel, same labor market, same premium-cabin strategy (Delta and
United configure comparable widebody LOPAs — JPMorgan's premise on the
call, not disputed). American's stated edge is cost — "industry-leading
unit cost" with labor certainty while rivals' new contracts have yet to
land (CEO) — and the stated gap is revenue: it earns a lower margin than
either rival and management calls closing that gap "the upside". Against
fuel there is no protection at all, by policy (10-K, 10-Q: "we will
continue to be fully exposed").

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder (10-K cover, Item 5); insiders hold 1.0%, institutions 78.4%
(stockanalysis). Two structural caps: non-US citizens may not hold more
than 24.9% of votes or 49.0% of equity (Aviation Act, charter), and the Tax
Benefit Preservation Plan dilutes any holder crossing 4.9% (protects $11.9B
of federal NOLs). The second forecloses an activist accumulation or an
unsolicited bid; a merger would have to be board-negotiated.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue has grown every year
since 2021 while operating income has fallen since 2023.

| 2021 | $29.9B | −$5.51B | −$1.99B | $0.50B | $0.21B |

| 2023 | $52.8B | $4.01B | $0.82B | $1.21B | $2.60B |
| 2024 | $54.2B | $3.37B | $0.85B | $1.30B | $2.68B |

Interest expense ran $1.66–2.15B a year across the period (stockanalysis

operating income is down 72%.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Fuel — the whole 2026 story, and it is unhedged.** Q2 fuel expense
  rose $2.2B (+83%) to $4.05/gal; management recovered "nearly half" with
  revenue (Q2 call). The Q3 guide assumed $3.75/gal off the July 21 curve
  and a $1.7B YoY increase; the FY26 assumption is ~$4.00 (Q2 call; 8-K
  exhibit 99.1). H1 spot ranged $1.86–$4.78 (10-Q). Since the call: the
  US–Iran ceasefire expired August 17, Brent was $92–95 on September 1
  after strikes near Hormuz, and A4A's CEO says carriers are "eating the
  cost" (news feed, web — low-confidence on the price level). Whether Q3
  fuel is running above the $3.75 assumption is UNKNOWN (§6.1); every
  cent is $50M a year. Policy is not to hedge (10-K, 10-Q, verbatim).
- **Is the +16% revenue American's, or the industry's?** Domestic unit
  revenue +11%, Atlantic +9%, Pacific +15%, Latin +7% (Q2 call). In the
  same quarter Delta guided capacity flat and United cut ~5 points of
  planned growth on fuel (web, low-confidence); an industry that cuts 4–5%
  of seats while demand holds reprices every carrier. American's own
  claim of "pickup vs OA" (other airlines) is asserted on the call and not
  disclosed as a number; the 10-Q reports PRASM, not relative PRASM. The
  Q4 capacity guide was subsequently trimmed again (domestic +10.1%,
  Atlantic +1.8%, Pacific −2.9% — web, low-confidence). Condition 2 stays
  *plausible*, not probable: the structural pieces (premium mix, Citi,

about $900M of sustained levered FCF, three times the trailing figure.

- **Reinvestment / terminal ROE.** Scenario B's warning is answered by not
  using B. In A and C the base FCF is below earnings power and terminal
  growth of 2% is at inflation — repricing existing assets, no real growth
  claimed. There is no `implied_terminal_roe` print on a negative-equity
  name; the equity ROE is undefined, which is the finding.
- **Market share.** Scenario C's path puts FCF, not revenue, on a steep
  slope; revenue is held at the ~3–5% capacity growth the fleet plan
  allows (CFO), which keeps American's US share roughly where it is. No
  scenario needs more of the market.
- **Terminal growth vs Item 1A.** The dominant disclosed structural risk is
  the pairing of fuel dependence with the debt load ("our substantial

  non-survival states are priced, which is why the DCF rate is read only
  inside the option frame.
- **Distribution clamp.** 5.55% (A) sits at the bottom of the US
  cost-of-capital band (Damodaran 2026: median 7.79%, 80% between 5.26%
  and 9.88%) — a strong pass regardless of story. C at 14.01% sits above
  the 90th percentile — interesting *if* the path is real.
- **Cash taxes.** TTM cash taxes are nil and will stay so under $17.9B of

## 5. Falsifiers

For the pass (flip toward buy):

- **Shift —** Q3 or Q4 2026 shows pre-tax margin expansion *at* elevated
  fuel: a Q4 adjusted pre-tax guide above zero at a fuel assumption of
  $3.50+/gal, or TRASM growth exceeding CASM-plus-fuel growth for two
  consecutive quarters. That would be condition 5 un-refuting itself.
- **Shift —** FY26 FCF positive and year-end net debt below the 2025
  year-end figure *as printed*, not guided, with 2027 capex held at or
  below $4.5B (Q4 print, February 2027 10-K).
- **Shift —** Brent sustained at or below $75 for a quarter, with the
  Hormuz disruption resolved — condition 1 becoming *probable*. A price at
  or below $9 on its own does not flip the pass; a lower price inside a
  fuel spiral is the option repricing its underlying, not a discount.
- **Break (for the pass) —** a 2028 refinancing done early at investment-
  grade-adjacent spreads, or the Citi/loyalty cash flow monetised in a way
  that retires the 2028 wall — the option's time premium would then be a
  real claim.

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** liquidity below $8B with the 2028 wall unaddressed, or any
  going-concern or covenant language in a 10-Q; the credit-card processing
  holdback provisions the 10-K warns of being triggered.
- **Break —** a pilot-contract outcome in 2027 that lifts CASM-ex above
  mid-single digits at low-single-digit growth.
- **Shift —** Q4 domestic capacity guide cut again below +8% while peers
  hold — the low-margin producer conceding share to protect cash.
