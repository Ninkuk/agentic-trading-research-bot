## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $209.86.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=5 (1 probable,
4 plausible), refuted=1, unknown=1.

Morningstar is still a good business and this is not a sell call — it is a
"would I add here" call, and the answer is no, for one reason only: at this
price there is no margin of safety in either direction. Charge the $67.0M
stock-compensation run-rate against free cash flow, grow slightly below the
demonstrated trailing rate, and terminal at inflation, and the implied
return comes out at **8.76% against an 8.81% cost of equity — five basis
points short of exactly fair**. The market has priced this correctly. Around
that centre the answer swings roughly ±100 bp on assumption choices that are
each defensible, and the hurdle itself carries ±70 bp of estimation error, so
the *sign* of the excess return is not reliably determined. A name with no
determinable edge, whose two largest segments' margins are compressing, and
whose one falsifying disclosure management has flagged as possibly becoming
"less meaningful," is one to hold and watch rather than add to.

The Phase 5 pass refuted two of this write-up's own supporting
constructions — see the Kill-thesis record. Neither reverses the call, but
both made the §4 table look more tilted than the evidence supports, and §4
has been corrected accordingly.

**Closest attack:** the falsifiability attack. The prior thesis's own #1
falsifier was the annual renewal-rate table, and in the August 25 Q&A
management pre-announced that "some of these metrics may become less
meaningful as pricing models change and as we move toward more firmwide,
value-based pricing structures." Put beside 2025's same-year methodology
revision and Advisor Workstation's renewal disclosure already going dark,
the measuring instrument is degrading in the same direction as the risk it
measures. That does not refute the business; it means a thesis resting on
it should demand *more* margin of safety, and there is now less.

## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/MORN-2026-07-30.md` §2 — created, captured, and
protected as described there (curated investment data across public funds,
private markets, credit and indexes; capture through per-seat and enterprise
licences, compulsory ratings licensing, issuance-linked ratings fees,
AUM-linked basis points, and index licensing; protection through
regulatory licensure at DBRS, an analyst-built non-scrapeable private-markets
dataset, and prohibitive index switching costs). Deltas this run:

**Captured — the pricing model is being rebuilt, on the record.** The
August 25 Q&A is the most explicit statement yet that seat-based pricing is
being replaced rather than supplemented. Management distinguishes value-based
pricing ("an annual licence fee that reflects business impact… without
considering the number of seats") from consumption-based pricing (fees
"inherently vary from period to period"), and says the impetus is that
"customers adopt tools and technology to execute more work with the same (or
smaller) workforce." That is management conceding the seat count is a
shrinking base — and it is why Q&A #14 answers a question about revenue per
licence with "we would not recommend revenue per licence as a proxy for
price."

**Protected — the connector layer is currently given away.** Q&A #23: paying
PitchBook subscribers access PitchBook data through premium connectors "in
platforms like Claude and ChatGPT **at no additional charge today**." The
defensive design is real — proprietary IP (VC Exit Predictor, Manager
Scores, Valuation Estimates) is deliberately platform-exclusive and
*excluded* from connector access — but the distribution win is a moat
argument, not yet a revenue argument. The 2026-08-25 Google Cloud Gemini
Enterprise for Financial Services launch-partner announcement sits in the
same bucket: distribution reach, no disclosed economics.

**Control:** unchanged. Founder Joseph D. Mansueto retains ~37%; insiders
~48.5% of shares against a 19.27M float on 37.51M shares outstanding. One
share class. This forecloses an unsolicited takeover and any activist path,
and it is also why the 5-year beta of 0.98 should be read with suspicion —
a thin-float, family-controlled name prints an artificially low beta, and
the 1-year beta is 0.75. Two things follow from the same fact and both are
true: the alignment against value destruction is as strong as it gets (a
founder with 37% does not strip the business), *and* a controlling
founder-owner who holds no earnings calls and answers investors only in
writing faces materially less pressure to keep an inconvenient metric alive
than a widely-held peer would. The second reading is what gives §3's
falsifier thread its force; the first is the best argument against it.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive at the consolidated line, negative
at the two largest segments.** That split is the finding, and it is new.

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs.** Levered TTM FCF **$497.5M** (`ncfo` $646,900,000 + `capex`
−$149,400,000, cash-flow-statement route) paired with **market cap
$7,872,604,096** (37,513,600 shares × the $209.86 official close). Net debt
is **0 by the pairing rule** — `fcf` here is post-interest, so the debt has
already been served and enterprise value would double-count it. No minority
interests (`minorityInterest` is null on the income statement), so no NCI
haircut. Cash of $523.8M is 6.7% of market cap — not a large-net-cash name,
no excess-cash adjustment. Two haircuts are modelled as their own scenario
rows rather than baked in: stock compensation $67.0M TTM (2.6% of revenue)
and TTM acquisition spend $359.5M (CRSP).

**Hurdle:** rf 4.75% + beta 0.98 × ERP 4.14% = **8.81%** (Damodaran implied
ERP and T-bond rate as of 2026-09-01, fetched live). Beta 0.98 sits inside
the 0.8–1.2 stable band so no clamp was applied — but see §2's control note:
a 48.5%-insider, 19.27M-float name plausibly prints a beta below its true
risk, and the 1-year beta is 0.75. Damodaran's absolute companion (a mature
company's cost of capital ≈ rf + 4.5% = 9.25%) sits *above* this hurdle, so
every spread below is, if anything, flattered.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (demonstrated growth) | $497.5M | 8/8/7/7/6% | 2.0% | 10.03% | **+122 bp** |
| conservative | $497.5M | 6.5% flat | 2.0% | 9.79% | **+98 bp** |

**The bolded row is the finding.** Charge stock compensation, grow at 6.5%
(below the demonstrated 9.3% trailing revenue growth and the 8.2% organic
rate ex-sunsets), terminal at inflation, and the implied return lands five
basis points under the cost of equity. Everything else in the table is that
number ±120 bp depending on which defensible assumption you vary.

Two corrections the Phase 5 pass forced, recorded here rather than buried:

- **A 5%-flat path is a bear case, not a conservative one**, and the
  original table mislabelled it. TTM revenue grew 9.3%, Q2 organic
  ex-sunsets was 8.2%, and ~1.2pp of sunsetting drag rolls off in Q2 27
  (Q&A #9). 6.5% flat is the honest conservative rate, and it prints
  +98 bp rather than the +51 bp the 5% row showed.
- **The original acquisition-charged row double-counted.** It charged
  $150M/yr of deal spend *and* used an organic-only 5% growth path —
  paying for the M&A while denying the revenue it buys. Phase 4's rule is an
  exclusive or: grow the organic base only, **or** charge the acquisition
  spend. The corrected row charges the spend and allows acquisitive growth,
  giving −116 bp instead of −167 bp. The sign holds either way: $150M/yr is
  a 30% haircut to base FCF that five years of 8% growth cannot recover.
  ($150M/yr is the run-rate reading of MORN's periodic deal cadence — DBRS
  2019, LCD 2022, CRSP 2026 — not the lumpy $359.5M TTM CRSP outlay, which
  would be harsher still.)

ATM IV is 37.5%, below the 50% precision trigger, so two decimals are
admissible — but the spread, not the rate, is the finding, and the whole
table spans less than 320 bp.

**Hurdle sensitivity is the honest limit on all of this.** The 8.81% hurdle
is not known to better than about ±70 bp: MORN's 1-year beta of 0.75 would
give 7.86%, Damodaran's mature-company companion (rf + 4.5%) gives 9.25%,
and `stocks.db`'s WACC of 8.24% is a *cost of capital*, structurally below
the cost of equity this pairing requires and so not a valid substitute. That
band overlaps the implied-return band. The correct summary is that the
market's price and a defensible cost of equity are indistinguishable at
today's precision — not that the stock is expensive.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal-ROE warning: answered both ways.* With trailing
  net income of $421.6M the tool fires its "growth without reinvestment"
  warning, because FCF exceeds earnings. Both of the skill's honest
  responses apply here. First, the earnings base *is* understated: the
  cash-flow statement carries $102.7M of `otherAmortization` TTM, which for
  MORN is acquired-intangible amortisation (M&A-heavy: goodwill $1,745.6M,
  other intangibles $569.2M), giving cash earnings of $524.3M — every row
  above uses that base, and the warning clears. Second, terminal growth is
  held at 2.0% ≈ inflation, which reprices existing assets and needs no
  reinvestment. The implied terminal ROE on the base row is then 39.1% —
  far more than 5 points above the hurdle and not defensible as a perpetual
  incremental return, which is exactly why the **terminal real-growth stress
  row exists**: at 1.0% terminal the implied terminal ROE falls to 19.6%,
  essentially MORN's actual ROIC of 18.2%, and that row is the only one in
  the table whose terminal growth is funded by reinvestment the company
  actually retains at a return it actually earns. It prints **−24 bp**.
- *Market-share sentence.* The base path compounds to 2031 revenue of
  $3.63B, an increment of $1.07B — about 44% of FactSet's entire current
  TTM revenue of $2.44B. Against a competitive set (Bloomberg, LSEG
  Workspace, FactSet, S&P Capital IQ Pro — management's own list in Q&A #15,
  plus the ratings peers) that is materially larger than Morningstar, the
  path does not require an implausible share. This is not a
  bigger-than-the-market forecast.
- *Terminal growth vs the disclosed terminal risk.* The dominant structural
  risk remains AI displacement — the FY2025 10-K Item 1A language on "the
  increasing amount of information and external tools… available online for
  free, or at low cost, that use AI to scrape data — including our own
  content" (quoted from the prior run; Item 1A could not be re-read this
  session, EDGAR 403). Management's own framing of the same risk is Q&A #1,
  the disintermediation question, answered with the licensing-relationship
  argument: "In each case, we maintain the licensing relationship with our
  clients so we believe that we can continue to align price with the value
  that our content delivers." A 2.0% terminal rate survives that only if the
  non-scrapeable mechanisms hold — regulatory ratings licensure,
  analyst-built private data, index licensing, compulsory marketing
  licences. The AI-decay row at 0% terminal is the run that assumes they do
  not, and it prints −188 bp.
- *Base-year cash tax rate.* TTM effective tax rate 25.0% against a US
  federal-plus-state marginal in the mid-20s — no NOL or deferral flattery
  in the base. No haircut needed.
- *Distribution clamp.* Every row lands between 6.93% and 10.40%, inside the
  roughly 5–10% band that holds 80% of US firms; the US median cost of
  capital of ~7.8% sits below the conservative row. So nothing here is a
  strong pass on distribution grounds — it is a thin call on the spread.
- *Buyback is not an independent positive.* Shares are −5.6% YTD, but a
  reverse DCF solves for the return on the whole equity claim; repurchases
  redistribute that return per share, they do not create it. Worth saying
  because it is the most common way a thin spread gets talked up.
