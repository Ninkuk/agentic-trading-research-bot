## 2. Business

**Created:** Victory runs 189 strategies through autonomous "Investment
Franchises" (RS, Sycamore, Integrity, Pioneer, Victory Income Investors, …)
and a Solutions platform (VictoryShares ETFs $23.2B at 34bp, VFLO $7.8B).
The client — a financial advisor, an institution, a USAA-heritage direct
investor — gets an active manager with a long record (71% / 68% / 65% / 81%
of AUM beating benchmark over 1/3/5/10 years; 60% of rated fund AUM at 4–5
stars) inside one operating, compliance and distribution wrapper. Investment
teams keep their process and brand; the platform handles everything else.

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (i) Investment management fees on average
AUM — $362M in Q2 2026 on $331B average AUM, a blended 47.9bp realization
(guide 46–47bp; 49.4bp a year earlier, so mix is drifting down ~1.5bp/yr).
(ii) Fund administration and distribution fees — $73M in Q2, charged to the
proprietary funds. (iii) Episodic annual/fulcrum fees that crystallize in
Q2 and lift the realized rate above the wrap rate (Q2 call, Policarpo).
Costs are two-thirds variable (compensation, distribution, outsourced
back office), which is why adjusted EBITDA margin has held above 49% every
quarter since 2020 and printed 55.8% in Q2 2026 (long-term guide raised to
50%). Post-close a fourth mechanism arrives: CLO management fees and
alternative-credit fees from Napier Park ($41B), with a higher fee rate and
longer capital than the mutual-fund book.

**Protected:** the protection is operational, not franchise-level. Victory's
edge is a scaled, outsourced platform onto which an acquired manager's
expense base collapses by 27–50%; no rival has done it as repeatedly at
this size, and a $571B platform can bid for targets a $150B one cannot. The
underlying strategies have no moat a competitor cannot copy — an active
mid-cap fund is an active mid-cap fund, and two Sycamore funds ($20.6B) are
already closed for capacity (10-K Item 1A). Distribution is partly rented:
Amundi holds the exclusive right to distribute Victory products outside the
US (10-K Item 1A). So the moat is "we integrate better than anyone," which
is a mechanism, and it protects margin, not AUM.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, 61.5M shares
out (statistics probe; the Class A/B split of the 2018 IPO is gone from the
cover page). Amundi Asset Management holds 20.04M Series A non-voting
convertible preferred economically equivalent to common (≈24.6%
as-converted) plus the right to two board seats; it converts only on
transfer to a non-affiliate (10-K Note on Preferred Stock; Shareholder
Agreement). Post-close Genstar adds 14.6% economic / 4.9% voting with two
more seats and a three-year lockup; the board goes to 11 with David Brown as
Chairman and CEO. Insiders hold 11.4%; float is 47.0M of 61.5M (76%). The
charter carries anti-takeover provisions, exclusive Delaware forum, and a
corporate-opportunity waiver for Amundi and legacy Crestview entities (10-K
Item 1A). Net: no single controller, but two strategic holders with ~39% of
the economics and four of eleven seats, and a shareholder vote on the
equity issuance whose "no" outcome hands the seller 8–15% perpetual
preferred instead — the vote is real but the alternative is designed to be
worse.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive over the cycle, negative through
the 2021–2023 outflow years.** Revenue / operating income ($M, stockanalysis
income statement): 2021 890 / 408 · 2022 855 / 370 · 2023 821 / 358 · 2024
893 / 429 · 2025 1,306 / 583 · TTM 1,559 / 720. Revenue −8% and operating
income −12% from 2021 to 2023 (organic outflows, no acquisition); +75% /
+76% from 2021 to TTM (Amundi US closed 2025-04-01). Operating margin
45.9% → 43.3% → 43.6% → 48.0% → 44.6% → 46.2%. The lever runs with
acquisitions and market level, not with organic growth.
