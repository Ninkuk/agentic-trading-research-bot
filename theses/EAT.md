## Kill-thesis record

**Ledger:** conditions=4 (2 probable, 2 plausible), refuted=1, unknown=0,
pending=1, not_obtained=0 → **FLAWED**. The object of the attack is the bull
case — that owning EAT at $210.61 beats SPY over 63 trading days — and one of
its four legs fails on the company's own disclosures, for the second time in

**Internal-consistency check, run first.** The bull needs mid-single-digit
comps (1), 20–40bp of margin a year (2), and ~10% aggregate FCF compounding
(4) at once. On 09-12 the FY27 guide showed those three are not jointly
satisfiable. The FY29 release makes the strain explicit: management's own
algorithm is 4–6% revenue, double-digit EPS *with* a 3–5% buyback inside it,
and a capex plan that adds 30 new restaurants a year on top of 10% of the
fleet reimaged. Aggregate net income at those targets grows ~5–7% a year;
free cash flow grows less than that because capex rises faster than D&A. A
second strain the release introduces on its own: 3–5% of market cap
repurchased ($265–440M a year at today's cap) plus a capex step-up of
$40–140M over FY26 against $480–560M of FCF means the top of the buyback
range is debt-funded — on a revolver, floating at SOFR+1.25%, as the sole
funded instrument. "High growth, low reinvestment, low risk" is the
three-way claim the skill flags; this release asserts growth and
reinvestment and quietly lets the balance sheet carry the third.
