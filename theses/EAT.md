## 2. Business

**Created:** Chili's sells a full-service sit-down meal at a per-person average
of $23.12 including alcohol, positioned deliberately $3–4 below casual-dining
competitors and marketed against *fast food* rather than against other casual
diners. The customer surplus is explicit and quantified: the "3 for Me" platform
gives a drink, an appetizer and an entrée from $10.99, and the Big Crispy
chicken sandwich, the Big Smasher and the Big QP exist to make the comparison to
a drive-through unfavourable to the drive-through. Roughly 21% of guests opt
into 3 for Me and about 40% of those take the $10.99 tier — so four fifths of
the base pays up, and the value platform functions as a traffic door rather than
as the menu. Maggiano's sells a different thing (Italian-American family-style
plus banquet space, $41.36 per guest, $9.5M per unit) to a different occasion.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not one. (a) Company restaurant sales —
1,114 Chili's and 49 Maggiano's owned outright, where Brinker keeps the whole
18% restaurant-level margin on $5.81B of sales; this is essentially all of it.
(b) Franchise royalties on 469 franchised Chili's, of which 370 are
international — immaterial to revenue but, per the CEO, expected to contribute
about 4% of Brinker profits in FY27, which would surpass Maggiano's entire
profit contribution. (c) The operating-leverage capture itself: a restaurant is
a largely fixed box, so every incremental guest through a fleet running at ~80%
of its historical guest-count capacity converts at a far higher rate than the
average. That third mechanism is the whole earnings story of the last four
years, and it is the one with a ceiling.

**Protected:** thinly, and the company does not pretend otherwise. There is no
patent, no switching cost, no network. What Chili's has is (i) 1,583 system
Chili's locations built over 51 years — a physical footprint no entrant
replicates, and the reason a share shift inside casual dining accrues to the
incumbent with the most doors; (ii) a scale advantage in purchasing and
advertising (3% of sales on advertising against a $5.8B base) that a
single-market competitor cannot match at the $10.99 price point; and (iii) a
brand moment — the CEO cites YouGov buzz at the highest level ever recorded for
the brand — which is precisely the kind of asset that is not durable and that no
one should underwrite past the next few years. Menu items are copied within a
season; the Big Crispy is itself Chili's copying the fast-food chicken-sandwich
war. The honest answer is that the moat is distribution and cost scale inside a
segment that is not growing, and the excess return rests on execution that a
different management team could lose.

**Control:** one class, Common Stock $0.10 par, NYSE, Delaware. No controlling
holder, no dual-class structure, no takeover defense disclosed on the cover.
Insiders own 1.235% and the float is 41.23M of 41.77M shares outstanding
(98.7%). One share, one vote — the null answer; nothing is foreclosed, and both
an unsolicited bid and an activist path are fully open.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive**, strongly and unambiguously — and
decelerating.

Beta is 1.256, marginally outside the 0.8–1.2 stable band on the high side. No
clamp is applied: the band's floor exists to catch thin-float names printing
artificially *low* betas, and EAT's float is 98.7% of shares outstanding with
insiders at 1.235% — the opposite case. A beta slightly above 1.2 is what a
high-operating-leverage discretionary consumer name should print.

**Hurdle: rf 4.95% + beta 1.256 × ERP 4.14% = 10.15%.** Risk-free is `DGS10` at
4.95% dated 2026-09-10 from `data/fred.db`; the implied ERP is Damodaran's
4.14% headline trailing-12-month figure as of 2026-09-01 (solved against his own

check (a mature company's cost of capital ≈ rf + 4.5% = 9.45%) puts 10.15% just
above it, which is the right side for a name with this operating leverage.

**Precision.** The path-2 ATM IV is 49.58% on the December expiry and 56.42% on

roughly 10% hurdle, wide," not as four-significant-figure knowledge. The width
cuts both ways — the true return could be higher or lower than the point
estimates — so the finding is that there is **no demonstrable margin of safety**,
not that underperformance is assured.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal ROE.* Scenarios D and E trip the tool's "growth
  without reinvestment" warning: trailing FCF ($525.3M and $557.5M) exceeds
  trailing earnings ($487.0M), so nothing is left to reinvest, yet terminal
  growth of 2.36% is claimed. This is answered rather than ignored — it is
  exactly why those scenarios are bookends and not the base case. Normalizing
  capex to the guided run rate resolves it: scenario B's base of $482.2M sits
  just below earnings and the warning clears. There is no acquired-intangible
  amortization escape hatch here (goodwill is only $194.5M and D&A of $218.7M
  runs *below* capex), so the "cash earnings are understated" defence is not
  available. Scenario A's `implied_terminal_roe` of 28.9% is more than five
  points above the hurdle and therefore "tough to do" in perpetuity — I do not
  defend it, and the correct response is to cut terminal growth, which is what
  B′ does and which makes the return worse, not better. Scenarios B and C print
  a nonsensical 239% terminal ROE purely because base FCF and base earnings are
  within 1% of each other; the FCF-vs-earnings proxy degenerates when capex
  ≈ D&A, and the economically meaningful figure is capex-minus-D&A over
  earnings — roughly 12–19% reinvestment, implying a terminal return near
  12–20%, above the hurdle but not absurdly so for a 25%-ROIC operator.
- *Market share.* Compounding scenario B out: $5,807.4M × 1.05⁵ = $7,412M of
  company revenue in FY31. Company-owned units go from 1,163 (FY27 adds four
  net, with a ramp from FY28) to perhaps 1,250, which implies AUV rising from
  $5.0M to about $5.93M, +19%. Management states restaurants currently run at
  roughly 80% of their historical guest-count capacity, so the required volume
  fits inside disclosed physical headroom. This is not a "bigger than the
  market" forecast — the growth is intensive, not extensive, and the path is
  physically available. It is the *return* on the capital required to unlock it
  that is unproven, not the volume.
- *Base-year cash taxes.* The 16.165% effective rate ($93.9M on $580.9M pretax)
  runs roughly 8–9 points below a marginal US rate. It is not an NOL or a
  deferral: it is the FICA tip credit, structural to a tipped-employee
  restaurant and not expiring. It does dilute as pretax income outgrows tipped
  wages, and FY27 is guided to ~19% — a headwind already inside the EPS guide
  and inside the FCF normalization above. No further haircut.
- *Serial acquisition.* Not applicable — growth is organic; the sole
  transaction is a $27.5M franchise buy-in that the CFO says is flat to EPS.
- *SBC.* $32.2M deducted in A–D. No unusual option or RSU overhang was
  disclosed; share count fell 2.82% year over year on $400M of buybacks, so
  dilution is being more than offset with cash.
- *Cash-heavy bias.* Not applicable — EAT carries net debt, not net cash, so
  the market cap is not inflated by an idle cash pile.
- *Other equity claims.* No defined-benefit pension underfunding and no

## Kill-thesis record

**Ledger:** conditions=4 (2 probable, 2 plausible), refuted=1, unknown=0,
pending=1, not_obtained=0 → **FLAWED**. The object of the attack is the bull
case — the claim that owning EAT at $212.49 beats SPY — and one of its four
legs does not stand. That is what leaves the PASS in §1.

**Internal-consistency check, run first because it costs nothing.** The bull
case asserts mid-single-digit comps (condition 1) and 20–40bp of margin
expansion (condition 2) and ~10%+ aggregate FCF compounding (condition 4)
simultaneously. Those three are not jointly satisfiable on the disclosed
numbers. Grant conditions 1 and 2 exactly as guided and compound them out:
revenue from $5,807.4M at ~5% to about $7,412M in FY31; operating margin from
10.81% at +30bp a year to 12.31%, giving $912M of operating income; less ~$35M
interest and 19% tax, about $710M of net income; add D&A of roughly $290M, SBC
of $45M and the working-capital source a negative-working-capital restaurant
throws off, less capex of roughly $340M at the committed 10%-of-fleet reimage
cadence plus the unit ramp — about $745M of free cash flow, $700M after SBC.
Against the $482.2M normalized base that is **7.7% a year**, not 10.5%.
Conditions 1 and 2 survive precisely because they are pitched at management's
own guided levels, and at those levels condition 4 fails. The surviving legs
are the ones that do not reach the hurdle.

**Per-condition adjudication.**

1. *Mid-single-digit comps with positive traffic through FY27* — **SURVIVED**
   at *probable*. Attack: the stack is punishing (five-year cumulative Chili's
   comp of 71% per the 8-K; Q4's +5.6% rolled a +24%), traffic decelerated from
   +3.6% for FY26 to +1.5% in Q4, and 77% of the Q4 comp was price (4.3% of
   5.6%). If FY27 price runs "just over 3%" as guided and traffic goes to zero,
   comps are 3% — below mid-single. The attack fails on dated counter-evidence:
   the CFO confirmed on 2026-08-12 that the quarter to date runs above Q4's
   +6%, the 8-K states momentum "accelerated in July," the Big Crispy went from
   20 to 55 sandwiches per restaurant per day and "continues to build," and
   this is the 21st consecutive positive quarter. The condition asks only for
   mid-single digits and the business is currently running above it.
2. *20–40bp of restaurant margin expansion in FY27* — **SURVIVED** at
   *probable*. Attack: Q4 delivered only +20bp with food and beverage 80bp
   unfavourable on 4.4% commodity inflation, Q1 commodity inflation is guided
   at 4% against roughly 4% of price (a wash), the CFO conceded labor "was not
   as efficient as we wanted," and advertising, insurance and delivery fees are
   all inflating. It fails because the guide already contains every one of
   those items explicitly — the CFO walked the 4/3/2/1 commodity cadence and
   the 4/3/3/~3 pricing cadence line by line, called the inflation assumptions
   deliberately conservative, and said the FY26 labor overspend is baked into
   the FY27 base. The 8-K's segment table adds a further prop: Chili's Q4
   restaurant margin was 18.6% and rising while Maggiano's fell 300bp to 10.3%,
   so the consolidated figure is being dragged by a segment guided flat off a
   near-zero base. Survives — but note the bar is 20–40bp, which is the lowest
   it has been in the turnaround and does very little work for the bull.
3. *Reimage and unit capital earns above the cost of capital* — **PENDING
   2026-09-17.** Routes run before tagging: the 10-K business-combination note
   covers only the $27.5M franchise buy-in and says nothing about reimages; the
   goodwill balance is $194.5M and its impairment test is uninformative here;
   the Q4 8-K exhibit was fetched and carries no reimage economics; the CFO
   declined the number twice on the call, saying it is "still finalizing" and

   10.15% cost of equity needs about $75k of pre-tax restaurant profit per unit
   per year, which at an 18.6% Chili's restaurant margin is roughly $418k of
   incremental sales — an **8.4% permanent comp lift** on a $5.0M AUV. That is
   a demanding bar for a remodel. It is not enough to refute, because the
   per-unit cost is my inference from a "majority of" statement rather than a
   disclosure, and because an unquantified share of the spend is deferred
   maintenance whose true counterfactual is decay rather than flat. Genuine
   uncertainty, dated disclosure five days out: PENDING, never SURVIVED.
4. *Aggregate FCF compounds at ~10%+ for five years* — **REFUTED.** This is

   them a forecast of mine. (a) The FY27 guide produces roughly **zero**

**Base rate.** Two, both cited. Damodaran's EVA dataset puts roughly **29%** of
firms above their cost of capital, so "the excess return persists" is a
three-in-ten proposition before any evidence; EAT is in that 29% today at 25.1%
ROIC against a ~9.6% computed WACC, and the default terminal assumption is
therefore fade, which is exactly what §4's 2.36% terminal rate encodes. The
turnaround base rate ("turnarounds usually do not work") is the wrong prior
here and was not used — this one demonstrably already worked, across 21
quarters, +600bp of restaurant margin and AUVs from roughly $3M to $5.0M. The
right prior is narrower and worse: casual-dining traffic has fallen in fifteen
of the last sixteen months (low-confidence aggregator figure, §7), so every
point of Chili's traffic gain is share taken from a shrinking pool, and the
company says so itself in Item 1A.

**The short case, strongest version.** 14.55% of the float is short and the
reason is coherent. The comp stack is built on three things that are each

marginal guest with it. Then the operating leverage runs backwards with the

flow-through and removes on the order of a fifth of unit-level profit — into a
multi-year capex commitment sized for the up case, on a stock up 592% in three

It is the reason the highest-ROIC operator in casual dining carries the lowest
multiple in its quality tier, and it is why the pass does not become a short.
