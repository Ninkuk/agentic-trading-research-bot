**Disputed expectation:** the market is pricing roughly 0.5%/yr cash-flow growth
for five years and ~1% thereafter — an implied return of 10.8% against an 11.1%
hurdle on the owner-earnings base — i.e. a business that stops getting worse but

effective fee rate *and* non-equity net inflows exceeding equity net outflows
would force that up; a Q3 fee rate below 37.8bp or net outflows above ~$17.5B

T. Rowe Price is a genuinely good business being paid for a unit of service that
gets cheaper every quarter. Revenue is flat over five years while operating
income fell 32% — through one of the strongest equity bull markets on record.
Average AUM grew 12.6% year-over-year in H1 2026 and investment advisory fees
grew only 8.3%, which is fee-rate erosion of about 3.8% a year, and it is
present in *every* asset class, not just the bleeding active-equity book. The
price (10.5x trailing earnings, a 4.99% dividend, 7.5% FCF yield, $2.78B of net
cash) is not obviously wrong for that; it is roughly right. At 10.8% implied
against an 11.1% hurdle this is a fair-value melting ice cube with a good
dividend, not a mispriced compounder — there is no margin of safety left to pay
for the possibility that the melt accelerates, and management's own H2 guidance
says it will.

**Closest attack:** the moat is real and it protects the wrong variable. The
retirement/target-date franchise ($561.4B, 31.6% of AUM at 2025 year-end) is

stop the *price* falling: TROW's target-date investor-class expense ratios run
0.45–0.64% against Vanguard Target Retirement's 0.08%, and the disclosed 4.2%/yr
multi-asset fee erosion plus management's own "~25% of target-date assets are now

## 2. Business

**Created:** TROW sells the promise that a professional will pick securities
better than an index, and — far more durably — that a plan sponsor can put one
fund in front of an employee who will never make another allocation decision.
About two-thirds of the $1.89T is retirement-related. The customer surplus in
the active-equity products is contestable and contested; the surplus in the
target-date products is real and mostly non-financial: the employee gets a
one-decision, age-appropriate, rebalanced portfolio, and the plan sponsor gets
a defensible fiduciary choice with a ten-year record behind it.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one. (a) An asset-based advisory
fee, 38.1bp annualized ex-performance fees in Q2 2026, charged on daily average
AUM — $3,427.8M of investment advisory fees in H1 2026, 91% of net revenues.
The rate differs sharply by asset class: H1 2026 equity fees $1,986.6M on
$873.9B average (45.5bp), multi-asset $1,038.6M on $656.6B (31.6bp),
alternatives $177.3M on $60.0B (59.1bp), fixed income $225.3M on $216.4B
(20.8bp). (b) Administrative, distribution and servicing fees — $282.6M in H1
2026, largely recordkeeping on the retirement book, a cost-plus business that
buys distribution rather than earning a margin. (c) Performance-based advisory
fees, $14.0M in H1 2026 — trivial. (d) Capital allocation-based income (OHA
carried interest), $40.0M in H1 2026 with $46.5M of accrued carry change — the
only line with real operating leverage, and the smallest.

**Protected:** the defensible mechanism is distribution lock-in in defined
contribution, not investment skill. A 401(k) plan's target-date default sits
inside a recordkeeping and payroll integration that costs real money and real
committee minutes to re-paper; the assets do not walk on a bad quarter. The
undefended flank is price. Nothing in that lock-in stops a sponsor from
negotiating the fee down, moving to a collective investment trust, or taking
TROW's own cheaper blend series — and TROW is actively building the cheaper
alternatives itself (34 ETFs at $30B, 43 SMA strategies at $20B, hybrid/blend at
~25% of target-date assets). In active equity there is no mechanism at all
beyond performance, and the disclosed record is mixed: 44% of funds beat peers
over five years, "over half" over one, three and ten. The risk factor says it
plainly — "investment advisors that emphasize passive products have gained and
may continue to gain market share from active managers like us."

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.20 par, 218,072,901 shares
outstanding at 2026-02-11, one vote per share, no dual-class structure and no
controlling holder; institutions hold 90.19%, insiders 1.80%. Nothing is
foreclosed — TROW is takeable and activist-reachable, which at 10.5x earnings
with $2.78B of net cash is an unpriced option in the bull's favour and belongs
in §6, not in the condition list.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The fee rate is falling in every asset class, not just equities.** This was
  the thread that decided the verdict. H1 2026 versus H1 2025, from the earnings
  release's own average-AUM and advisory-fee tables: equity average AUM +8.5%
  against fees +5.5% (−2.8% rate), fixed income +11.1% against +7.7% (−3.1%),
  multi-asset +19.1% against +14.1% (−4.2%), alternatives +12.6% against +8.4%
  (−3.7%), total +12.6% against +8.3% (−3.8%). A mix-shift story would show the
  erosion concentrated where assets are moving. It is everywhere, including in
  alternatives, the highest-fee and fastest-growing block. That is repricing, not
  mix.

- **Alternatives AUM did not collapse — an extraction artifact did.** A first
  pass read the Q2 2025 alternatives column as $100.3B against $61.7B in Q2 2026,
  which would have been a 38% collapse and the headline of this document. The
  10-Q's average-AUM table contradicted it ($53.9B in Q2 2025), so I pulled the
  earnings-release AUM roll-forward directly: alternatives were $58.5B at
  2025-12-31 and $61.7B at 2026-06-30 with +$4.7B of H1 net inflows before
  −$1.4B of manager-driven distributions. Alternatives is growing. Recorded
  because the near-miss is the finding: the number summed correctly to the stated
  total and was still wrong.

- **Unfunded alternatives commitments are shrinking, slightly.** $21.0B at
  2026-06-30 against $21.6B at 2025-12-31, explicitly outside fee-basis AUM. At
  the 59.1bp alternatives rate that is roughly $124M of latent annual revenue,
  about 1.6% of total revenue — real, and too small to carry condition 3. The
  sequential decline is the more interesting half: the latent pool is being drawn
  down faster than it is being refilled.

- **The de-rating is idiosyncratic, not a sector move.** From the 2026-07-27
  weekly open to the 2026-09-07 weekly close, TROW −11.6% while BEN +2.4%, IVZ

  the weight: n=4 peers over one seven-week window with no null — suggestive of

- **Earnings quality: the beats are real but the GAAP line is flattered.** Six

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs and pairing.** Market cap $22,220,000,000 and the flow figures below are
from the statistics probe route. The pairing rule: stockanalysis `fcf` is
levered (`ncfo + capex`, post-interest), so it pairs with **market cap** and
`--net-debt` stays at zero. TTM levered FCF $1,674M, TTM operating cash flow
$1,930M, TTM capex $258.4M, TTM D&A $431.3M, TTM SBC $209.8M (2.76% of revenue).

Two bases, run in parallel, because for this company they differ by a third and
neither is obviously right:

- **Base A — reported FCF, $1,464M.** $1,674M less the $209.8M SBC run-rate.
  SBC is added back inside NCFO and is compensation, not cash the owner keeps.
- **Base B — owner cash earnings, $2,206M.** TTM adjusted diluted EPS $10.34
  (Q3'25 $2.81 + Q4'25 $2.44 + Q1'26 $2.52 + Q2'26 $2.57) × 213.32M shares. No
  SBC deduction here: TROW expenses stock compensation inside adjusted
  "compensation, benefits and related costs," so deducting again double-counts.

**Why they diverge, and why it is not noise.** Reported FCF sits $493M *below*
net income even though D&A ($431M) exceeds capex ($258M) — arithmetic that
should not happen. The cause is consolidation: H1 2026 operating cash flow of
$1,341.6M is struck after a $(547.4)M "net change in securities held by
consolidated investment products," which is the CIPs' own trading, not TROW's.
Net income attributable to T. Rowe Price Group is already clean of this (the
$49.3M H1 redeemable non-controlling interest is deducted above the line), so
Base B needs no minority haircut while Base A carries an unquantified CIP drag.
Base A is therefore conservative to an unknown degree and Base B is the better
owner measure — but Base B leans on a non-GAAP figure management defines, so I
refuse to pick one and report both.

**Hurdle:** rf 4.94% (DGS10, 2026-09-17, `data/fred.db`) + beta 1.48 × ERP
4.14% (Damodaran implied ERP, 2026-09-01, against his own 4.75% T-bond rate) =
**11.07%**. Beta is above the 0.8–1.2 stable band and is left unclamped: the
band's clamp exists for thin-float distortion, and TROW's float is 98.1% of
shares out with 1.80% insider ownership, so 1.48 is a genuine regression beta
with an economic reason — revenue is a linear function of market levels by
construction. At a clamped 1.2 the hurdle is 9.91%; the mature-company companion
(rf + 4.5%) is 9.44%; stockanalysis's own WACC estimate is 12.15%. Note the
distribution clamp cuts against the hurdle, not the stock: the US median cost of
capital is 7.79% with 80% of firms between 5.26% and 9.88%, so an 11.07% hurdle
sits above the band and is a demanding test. The 9.91% clamped hurdle is the
fairer one, and the verdict does not change under it — the two central scenarios
land at 10.82% and 11.75%, i.e. within ±100bp of either hurdle.

**Terminal growth.** The 10-year breakeven `T10YIE` is **2.33%** (2026-09-18) and
is the default ceiling. It does not survive the disclosed terminal risk. Item 1A's
competition factor is explicit that "investment advisors that emphasize passive
products have gained and may continue to gain market share from active managers
like us" — that bears directly on whether cash flows still grow in year 10, and
the company's own five-year record (revenue −1.0%, operating income −32.3%)
is what that risk looks like when it materializes. So the working terminal is
**1.0%**, with 2.33% shown as the bull bound and 0% as the structural-decline case.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal ROE, answered.* On Base A the terminal reinvestment
  rate is 32.4% and the implied terminal ROE is 7.18% — **389bp below** the
  11.07% hurdle. That is the tool saying the terminal value assumes TROW destroys
  value in perpetuity, and here that is intentional and correct: it is the
  arithmetic form of the Item 1A competition risk, and the default is excess
  returns fading, not persisting (only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of
  capital). On Base B the tool fires its "growth without reinvestment" warning,

## 5. Falsifiers

For the pass (flip toward buy):

- **Shift —** Two consecutive quarters in which the annualized effective fee
  rate excluding performance fees is flat or higher sequentially (Q3 2026 at or
  above 38.1bp, Q4 2026 at or above that). This is the single number that turns
  a melting ice cube into a cheap stock, and it is disclosed every quarter.
- **Shift —** Non-equity net inflows exceed equity net outflows in a quarter —
  H1 2026 was +$17.3B against −$36.1B, a 48% offset. Above 100% the organic
  decay is over.
- **Shift —** Adjusted operating expense growth guided below 3% for 2027 while
  average AUM is flat or up, i.e. management converts the AI and vendor-
  outsourcing programme into actual operating leverage rather than reinvestment.
- **Break (toward buy) —** Price below roughly $88, where B1's consensus path
  clears the 11.07% hurdle by more than 150bp and the dividend yield exceeds 5.9%.

For an owner (sell):

- **Break —** The quarterly dividend is held flat or cut, ending the 40-year
  growth streak. That is the last thing this management will do and therefore the
  clearest signal that the cash-flow outlook has broken.
- **Break —** Net client outflows exceed 5% of average AUM in any full year
  (roughly $90B+), the 2023 rate returning with no large-mandate offset.
- **Shift —** The effective fee rate falls below 37.0bp, which would be an
  acceleration past the current ~3.8%/yr and would move the primary scenario
  decisively below the hurdle.
- **Shift —** The buyback slows materially below the H1 2026 pace ($500.8M, 2.4%
  of shares in six months) while the balance sheet is unchanged — capital being
  retained rather than returned at 10x earnings says management sees a use for it
  that the shareholder cannot.
