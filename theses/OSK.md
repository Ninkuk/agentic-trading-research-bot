## 2. Business

**Created:** three customer sets get purpose-built vehicles they cannot
sensibly build or source elsewhere. Rental fleets (United Rentals, Sunbelt,
Herc) buy JLG boom lifts, scissor lifts and telehandlers that must survive a
decade of rental abuse with parts and service everywhere — uptime is the
product. Municipal fire departments buy Pierce apparatus to a written spec
with 2–3-year lead times and pay in advance; refuse haulers buy McNeilus
bodies; airports buy AeroTech boarding bridges and ground-support gear. The
US Army, Marines and allied militaries buy tactical trucks (FMTV, FHTV, JLTV
derivatives such as ROGUE-Fires); the USPS buys the Next Generation Delivery
Vehicle under a 10-year IDIQ for up to 165,000 units.

**Captured:** (a) unit sales of access equipment priced against a rental
customer's ROI, with mix and price/cost swinging margins between 4% (Q1
2026) and 15% (Q2 2025) inside two years; (b) fire trucks and airport
products sold from a multi-year backlog at prices set at order — customers
fund the build through advances ($2.06B on the balance sheet, on which
Pierce pays interest: $32M in H1 2026), so the segment throws off cash ahead
of revenue; (c) refuse bodies on a shorter, cyclical cycle; (d) cost-plus
and fixed-price-with-EPA government contracts — the NGDV is fixed price with
economic price adjustment and the segment ran 3.7% margins in 2025 as the
JLTV wound down (last domestic unit May 2025); (e) aftermarket parts and
service across all three. US government was 20% of 2025 sales; Transport is
92% US government.

**Protected:** Pierce's position is a real mechanism — a national dealer and
service network, a custom-spec engineering culture and a backlog that itself
deters entry (Rosenbauer and REV are the competitors). JLG's protection is
scale and installed base with a duopoly partner (Genie/Terex); its customers
are three consolidated rental majors with pricing leverage, which the 10-K
names as a risk. Defense protection is program incumbency, and it just lost
its flagship (JLTV recompete). NGDV is a sole-source ten-year contract — a
moat only for as long as the customer keeps ordering. Net: a moat in
Vocational, scale-but-not-pricing-power in Access, and program risk in
Transport.

**Control:** one class of common (300M authorized, 75.1M issued, 61.74M
outstanding), one vote per share, no controlling holder — Vanguard 12.2%,
BlackRock 9.0% (2026 proxy), insiders 0.65%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or
an activist. Null answer.
