## 2. Business

**Created:** PAH and PH-ILD patients get a prostacyclin (treprostinil) they
can inhale from a small dry-powder device. Liquidia's PRINT particle
engineering is built for tolerability at higher doses, and management's
whole pitch is that tolerability drives dose and dose drives outcomes (Q2
2026 call, CEO Jeffs). Doctors voted with scripts: ~5,900 unique patient
prescriptions, >5,000 patients started and >1,100 prescribers by Q2. About
75% of scripts are treatment-naive patients and 25% are switches from other
prostacyclins (Wells Fargo conference, 2026-09-08).

**Captured:** (a) Yutrepia net product sales: $170.4M in Q2 2026, $300.3M in
H1 (10-Q). This is specialty-pharmacy distribution with a gross-to-net
haircut, and gross margin is 92.5% TTM. (b) Promotion/service revenue on
Sandoz's generic treprostinil injection: $1.3M in Q2, shrinking. Nothing
else is material. It is a single-product business.

**Protected:** weakly, and the ruling just showed where the wall is. What
stops UTHR (Tyvaso DPI) from taking the patient back is product preference:
tolerability and dosing. That is a clinical-experience advantage, not a
legal one. UTHR, meanwhile, holds a method-of-use wall around PH-ILD until
2042-02-03, and that wall stood up in court. Liquidia's own patent estate
and Yutrepia's exclusivity term are not quantified in the 10-Q ("duration of
our patent rights" is listed only as a risk). The next tolerability claim,
L606 (licensed from Pharmosa), is in Phase 3. The protection is an
UNASSESSED moat: the clinical differentiation is real on scripts but sits
outside my competence to rank against sotatercept and ralinepag.

**Control:** the 10-Q cover lists one class of common stock (89,508,891
shares at 2026-07-29). Whether there is a controlling or activist holder was
not checked: the proxy was not read this run. Insiders hold 8.54% and
institutions 65.37% (stockanalysis). Nothing seen forecloses a takeover.
