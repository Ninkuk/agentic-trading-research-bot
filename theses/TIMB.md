**Yes — it closed on 2026-09-11 at ~66.627% acceptance, and the control leg

2026-09-11. On 7 September, with adhesion short of the two-thirds target,
Poste's board added €0.30 cash per TI share (cash leg to €1.97, on top of

Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters.

**(a) The timing objection that produced both prior PASSes is gone.** The
07-27 run passed explicitly "on timing rather than quality" — the Q2'26
print was two days out and the control window ran to Sep 11. Both are now
behind. The 07-29 reopen already cleared the print (clean: EBITDA +7.0%,
EBITDA-AL +7.8%, OpFCF +10.1%). Nothing timing-shaped remains.

**(b) TIM S.A. has filed nothing about it.** Between the Jul 29 board
minutes and today, its only SEC filings are two routine CVM-358 monthly
reports (Aug 10, Sep 10) and the Aug 19/20 buyback pair. No material fact
on the parent's change of control, no tag-along notice. Read straight: the
company does not treat Poste's acquisition as a company-level event for its
own shareholders. (Caveat: TIM files to CVM in Portuguese and does not
translate everything into a 6-K, so absence here is evidence, not proof.)

**(c) The asymmetry runs the right way, and this is new.** The 2025 20-F
states two facts the prior runs never joined up: TIM S.A. has been on B3's
**Novo Mercado since 2011**, whose rules it says are "fully implemented,"
including "**100% tag along, with the same conditions for all shareholders
in transfer of control**"; and TIM Brasil S.A. (the TI Group subsidiary)
holds **67.48%** of TIM S.A.'s share capital directly. So the bad scenario
falsifier 5 named — Poste selling TIM Brasil on terms that bypass ADS
holders — is the scenario Novo Mercado is built to prevent: a sale of that
67.48% block is a transfer of control and drags 100% tag-along at the same
price. What Poste just did is the *other* case: it bought a parent that had
**no controlling shareholder** (Poste held 27.32% of TI, BlackRock ~5.1%,
per the 20-F) through a market tender. There was no control block to
alienate, which is my reading of why no Brazilian tag-along obligation
attached and why the press describes tag-along as "possible but not
automatic, subject to CVM analysis." That reading is reasoning, not sourced
law — see §6.1 — but the direction is not in doubt: the structurally
protected case is the one a future Poste exit would fall into.

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**BUY at $18.88.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=8 (3 probable,
5 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0, pending=3, not_obtained=1.

**p(beat SPY, 63 td): 0.53** · kill-thesis: 0.48
**Disputed expectation:** the market prices TIM at ~8.5× tax-normalized
lease-adjusted owner FCF ($1.06B against a $9.02B cap), which solves to
roughly zero real USD growth in perpetuity once a Ba1-country equity risk
premium is charged; the thesis is only that USD owner FCF does not *shrink*
by more than ~1.5% a year, and the Q3'26 print (2026-11-02) — which carries
the two amber operating counters and the first clean read on Brazil's new
15% minimum tax — is what revises it.

The two reasons for the prior PASS were timing, and both expired. What is
left is a cash machine at a price that no longer needs growth: lease-adjusted
owner FCF ≈ R$5.76B ≈ $1.11B on a $9.02B market cap — $1.06B once the base
is charged Brazil's newly enacted 15% minimum tax — of which 6.09% is paid
as dividend and 0.89% bought back, with a fresh R$1B buyback authorized on
2026-08-19 in which the board states in writing that "the current market
price of the Company's shares does not adequately reflect TIM's intrinsic
value." Operating margin is still expanding (25.02% FY2025 → 25.82% TTM) and
EBITDA-AL grew 7.8% in Q2'26. Against a hurdle that already charges Brazil's
full 7.47% country ERP, the tax-normalized price clears on conservative
assumptions (+382bp) and still clears if USD FCF never grows again (+87bp).
The name loses to that hurdle if USD FCF declines about 1.5%/yr forever, or
if cash taxes normalize to the full 20%+ rather than the 15% floor — both
honest bear cases, neither the base case while EBITDA-AL compounds at 7–8%
in BRL.

**Closest attack:** Brazil legislated away the tax rate this base FCF was
built on. The 2025 20-F discloses an FY2025 effective rate of ~5% driven by
"higher volume of interest on equity" and "an increase in tax benefits" —
and, in the same document, that Law No. 15,079 imposed a **Pillar Two 15%
minimum effective rate via a QDMTT surtax on CSLL from 2026-01-01**, while
Complementary Law No. 224 (2025-12-26) began a "systematic and linear
reduction of federal tax incentives and benefits" across IRPJ, CSLL and
PIS/COFINS from the same date. The TTM rate has already doubled to 10.06%.
This attack **landed**: the base FCF in §4 is cut by $46M to the 15% floor,
and the zero-growth spread falls from 138bp to 87bp (to 36bp at a 20% rate).
It fails to refute only because the base case still clears by 382bp. The
standing threat behind it is the lease scissors plus the *price-follower*
admission — the CEO, asked whether TIM would raise Controle front-book
prices: "we have two competitors with a higher postpaid market share. Let's
see what they do, then we will move accordingly." Detail in the Kill-thesis
record.

## 2. Business

Unchanged from `research/TIMB-2026-07-27.md` §2 — mobile connectivity for
~62M Brazilian subscribers under three-player spectrum scarcity, monetized
through postpaid ARPU, prepaid top-ups, B2B/IoT contracts and a C6 Bank
distribution partnership, protected by auction-allocated spectrum and a
R$4.5B/yr build. Deltas since:

**Created (delta).** Four new value propositions launched in H1'26, all
aimed at the softening customer base: TIM Ultracombo (the first genuinely
convergent fiber + mobile + content offer, national footprint, launched
weeks before the Q2 call and "before we even go to the wider
communication"), TIM Play (paid content aggregation, an ARPU lever), TIM
Controle Fit (credit-card-paid Controle plans, aimed at prepaid customers
who fail the credit score), and a PicPay distribution partnership. All from
the Q2'26 call.

**Captured (delta).** Postpaid is now ~70% of mobile service revenue. B2B is
~7% of service revenue and growing double-digit, as is broadband
(Ultrafibra). Mobile itself grew 4.7% in Q2'26 and core client-generated
revenue 3.1% — the mix is doing the work the core is not.

**Protected (delta, and it is a downgrade).** The oligopoly still holds
structurally, but its *pricing* discipline weakened: a competitor pushed a
below-the-line offer through an MVNO agreement at end-2025, the other
matched, and TIM had to answer with TIM Fit and PicPay. Management describes
this as cyclical ("there is a good moment, there is a moment that is not
that good") and says it will move front-book prices only after its two
larger postpaid competitors do. Protection against *entry* is intact;
protection of *price* is currently not.

**Control:** one share class, common voting shares only (Novo Mercado
requires it). TIM Brasil S.A., a Telecom Italia Group subsidiary, holds
**67.48%** of the share capital — a hard controller, so an unsolicited
takeover and any activist path are both foreclosed. Since 2026-09-11 that
controller's own parent is controlled by Poste Italiane (~66.6%). The
compensating protection is Novo Mercado's **100% tag-along in a transfer of
control**, which the 20-F states TIM has fully implemented, plus a mandatory
public offer if TIM ever delists from the segment.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue and operating income,
BRL millions (stockanalysis, TIM's reporting currency):

| | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | TTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| revenue | 18,058 | 21,531 | 23,834 | 25,448 | 26,625 | 27,403 |
| operating income | 3,248 | 3,440 | 4,798 | 5,834 | 6,660 | 7,076 |
| operating margin | 18.0% | 16.0% | 20.1% | 22.9% | 25.0% | 25.8% |

Revenue +52% since FY2021, operating income +118% — five consecutive years
of expansion after the FY2022 Oi Mobile integration dip, and the TTM figure

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs.** Levered TTM FCF paired with **market cap**; net debt 0 by the
pairing rule (the statistics `fcf` is `ncfo + capex`, post-interest). Market

R$27,402.761M TTM revenue / $5,289.291M = **5.181 BRL/USD**.

Base FCF is **lease-adjusted**, because under IFRS-16 TIM's lease principal
*and* lease interest are both paid in financing activities, outside `ncfo` —
the unadjusted $1.786B overstates owner cash by ~60%:

| ncfo | 13,916 |
| capex | (4,664) |
| lease cash (principal + interest) | (3,342) |
| loan interest | (~150) |

Q2'26 R$861M are filed figures and Q2'25 R$791M is backed out of the stated
+8.9% Q2 growth.

**The base is then tax-normalized to $1.06B, and that is what the scenarios
below use.** TTM cash tax was $93.3M on $928.3M pretax — 10.06%, against a
34% Brazilian statutory rate — and Brazil's Law 15,079 QDMTT sets a 15%
minimum effective rate from 2026-01-01 (§3). Charging the 15% floor costs
$46M, so base FCF becomes **$1,064M**. This is the Damodaran base-year cash
tax check answered with a number rather than a caveat: the concession is not
optional, because it is enacted law with a stated effective date, and a base
FCF resting on a 10% rate the legislature has already replaced would inflate
every projected flow.

Haircuts: **SBC negligible** — `shareBasedComp` $4.30M TTM,
0.081% of revenue, so no deduction is worth taking (contrast INTU near 10%);
**no minority interest** — I-Systems went to 100% in May 2026; **not
cash-heavy** — cash $874M against $3.18B debt, net debt $2.30B, so no
excess-cash netting applies.

**Hurdle: rf 4.95% + beta 0.8 × ERP 7.47% = 10.93%.** Risk-free is DGS10 at
2026-09-10 (`fred.db`). The ERP is **Brazil's total ERP, 7.47%** (Damodaran
country premium table, "Last updated: January 5, 2026" vintage; Ba1, default
spread 2.13%, CRP 3.24%) — not the US headline 4.14% of 2026-09-01, because
essentially 100% of operations are Brazilian. **Beta is clamped.** The raw
regression beta is **0.105**; neither thin-float trigger fires (insiders
0.053%, float 99.95% of shares out), so the clamp is on the stable-band rule
alone — 0.105 is far outside 0.8–1.2, it is a low-correlation-to-SPY
artifact rather than a low-risk finding, and unclamped it would produce a
5.73% hurdle, below the 10th percentile (5.26%) of the US cost-of-capital
distribution. Floored to 0.8. At beta 1.0 the hurdle is 12.42% and the
conservative scenario still clears by +286bp.

precisely the "no real growth, no reinvestment" case.
