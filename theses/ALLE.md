## 2. Business

**Created:** a building owner, architect or facilities manager gets a door
opening that meets fire and life-safety code, ADA, and the owner's own
security standard, delivered as a configured set (lock, exit device, closer,
door, credential reader, software) that a single spec covers and a single
channel can install and service. The buyer is rarely price-shopping a lock;
they are buying certainty that the opening passes inspection, keys into the
existing master-key or credential system, and can be serviced for decades.
Residential buyers (Schlage at retail; the residential business is 70%
aftermarket, 30% new build per the call) get a keypad or smart lock that
works with Apple/Google/Amazon platforms. Reference: 10-K FY2025 Item 1;
Q2 2026 call.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (i) *Specified mechanical hardware,
Americas* — Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN sold through specialty distribution
into institutional and commercial projects at a 28–30% segment margin; the
company writes the spec through its own field force and the product is
then pulled through distributors (10-K "Customers"; segment note). (ii)
*Electronics and access control* — electrified locks, readers, credentials,
Zentra/Schlage software, ELATEC readers and Interflex workforce software in
Europe; ~30% of revenue, growing high-single digits, priced above the
mechanical equivalents it replaces (CMD 2025; 10-Q electronics disclosure).
(iii) *Residential retail* — Schlage through home centers and e-commerce,
70% aftermarket, high-single-digit growth in Q2 on electronics (call).
(iv) *Services and doors* — Stanley Access Technologies automatic-entrance
service, Krieger and DCI specialty doors, sold directly to end users
(10-K Item 1; 10-Q Note 3). Ten largest customers were 26% of 2025 revenue,
none above 10%; ~25% of revenue is non-US.

**Protected:** the mechanism is *the specification*. Allegion's spec
writers and end-user demand-generation teams get into the design phase,
write the opening to code with Allegion SKUs, and create end-user standards
(a university, a hospital system, a hyperscaler) that every subsequent
building and retrofit inherits — the installed key system and credential
platform make switching brands an operational project, not a purchase
decision. That is why the Americas earns 28–30% while the acquired
European mechanical businesses earn 9%: the moat is the North American
spec-and-channel franchise, not the lock. The 10-K names the threat
plainly: the market is "highly competitive and fragmented", principal
global rivals are ASSA ABLOY and dormakaba (Fortune Brands in US
residential), and "as we move into more technologically advanced product
categories, we may also compete against new, more specialized competitors"
— electronics is where the spec moat is thinnest and where the company is
spending its M&A dollars.

**Control:** Irish plc, one class of ordinary shares, one vote each; no
controlling holder (insiders 0.32%, institutions ~100%; BlackRock the
disclosed >5% holder per the 2026 proxy). Articles bar business combinations
with a 10% "interested shareholder" for five years, let the board issue
preferred shares, and need 75% to amend; the Irish Takeover Rules
(mandatory-bid, minimum-price) apply. Independent Chair (Dean Peters) since
2024. Nothing forecloses an activist or a bid; the null answer with Irish
friction.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue 2021→2025 +42%,
operating income +61%; operating margin 18.9% → 21.5%, 21.1% TTM after
$14.6M of restructuring and integration expense. Stock compensation is
inside that line at $29.8M (0.7% of revenue), flat for five years.
