## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $299.14.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=7 (2 probable,
4 plausible, 1 possible), refuted=1, unknown=1.

Jabil is a well-run, diversified contract manufacturer that has become one
of the few at-scale integrators of AI data-center racks, power and cooling,
and it is executing: FY26 revenue guided to ~$35B (+17%), AI-related revenue
$13.6B (+50%), core operating margin ~5.8% rising toward "6%+" in FY27, and

the growth is not a story — it is in the segment table. What fails is the
price: $31.3B already needs owner FCF to rise from ~$1.16B to ~$2.0B by FY28

premium). The 30% drawdown from the June 17 high removed froth, not the
requirement. A 5–6% EBIT-margin assembler whose largest customer is 16% of
revenue and whose demand is one capex cycle deep should clear its hurdle

came close because it is the mechanism by which a hyperscaler pause would
turn a 3.9% FCF margin negative for a year, as FY22–23 (FCF $266M/$704M on

## 2. Business

**Created:** Jabil builds other companies' hardware. A hyperscaler gets a
liquid-cooled AI rack — compute trays, storage, InfiniBand/Ethernet
networking, power distribution, in-row heat exchangers — integrated and
tested at a Jabil plant in the US, Mexico or India, without owning the
factory, the 135,000 people, or the component-procurement relationships. An
automaker or a GLP-1 pen maker gets the same service under FDA/IATF quality
systems. The customer surplus is speed and capital avoidance: Jabil's capex
is 1.6% of revenue and the customer's is zero. Three segments (10-Q):
Regulated Industries $3.18B/qtr (auto & transport, healthcare & packaging,
renewables), Intelligent Infrastructure $4.17B/qtr (capital equipment, cloud
& data-center infrastructure, networking), Connected Living & Digital
Commerce $1.40B/qtr.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (a) Cost-plus manufacturing on
"over time" contracts — $6.06B of Q3's $8.75B is recognized over time, i.e.
priced per program with the customer bearing demand risk; segment margins
4.9–6.1%. (b) Component arbitrage: procurement scale across 30 countries
and a "net basis" pass-through on customer-directed components (10-Q
revenue note) — margin on the value added, not the bill of materials.
(c) Since January 2026, Hanley's modular power and *services* revenue —
deployment, maintenance, a recurring stream at double-digit margins, the
only line in the company that is not a manufacturing hour.

**Protected:** thin, and the filing says so first. The first Item 1A risk
factor is customer concentration; the competition factor lists rivals with
"greater direct buying power", "lower cost structures", and willingness "to
make sales at lower margins". The mechanism that does exist is switching
cost inside a live ramp: qualifying a second integrator for a rack program
mid-deployment costs the hyperscaler months, and the "enter with one
capability, expand across the data center" pattern the CEO described for
hyperscalers two and three is real account expansion. But it is a
relationship, not a moat — Apple (Customer B) went from 17% of revenue to
under 10% in two years when the Mobility business was sold, and the same
exit is available to any hyperscaler that moves to an ODM. Diversification
across 87 customers >90% of revenue is the actual defense.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder. Largest holders per the 2025 proxy: Vanguard 15.07M shares (~14%),
BlackRock ~10.3M (~10%); insiders 1.8% (stockanalysis). Nothing forecloses
a takeover or an activist.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue / GAAP operating income
(stockanalysis `/financials/income-statement/`, $M): FY21 29,285 / 1,090
(3.7%) · FY22 33,478 / 1,431 (4.3%) · FY23 34,702 / 1,609 (4.6%) · FY24
28,883 / 1,447 (5.0%, Mobility sold) · FY25 29,802 / 1,446 (4.9%) · TTM
33,590 / 1,700 (5.1%). Revenue is only 15% above FY21; operating income is
56% above it. Margin has expanded every year on a smaller, better-mixed
revenue base — the direction is up, and GAAP already includes $107–149M/yr
of SBC and $82M of intangible amortization.
