## 2. Business

**Created:** Zirconium-based bulk amorphous alloys ("Liquidmetal") that
are die-cast or thermoplastically molded to near-net shape, giving parts
with roughly twice titanium's strength, high elastic limit, tight
tolerances and corrosion resistance without machining. The customer value
is real and specific: a 1–20 g part too complex for CNC and too demanding
for metal-injection molding, at $1–$10 per piece (CEO, LD Micro). The
material was invented at Caltech; the company has commercialized it since
1987 and has never found a volume application it kept — Samsung flip-phone
hinges (2002) and Apple's SIM ejector are the two it cites.

**Captured:** Three mechanisms, and only the first produces money today.
(i) Part sales — custom parts and tooling/prototypes for medical, robotics,
automotive and non-consumer electronics customers, made by the affiliate
contract manufacturer Yihao at cost-plus and resold: $784K in 2025, $452K
in H1 2026 (−21%), 36% gross margin TTM. (ii) Licensing and royalties — a
Swatch watch license paying royalties over the patent life; $3K in H1
2026. (iii) Manufacturing — the Hangzhou JV, which has produced nothing
yet. The Apple license produced a one-time fee in 2010 and nothing since;
by its terms it never will.

**Protected:** No moat this registrant owns. The field that matters for
the hinge story — consumer electronics — is licensed away exclusively to
Apple (worldwide, pre-2016 IP) and the geography that matters — China and
Asia — is licensed away exclusively to Eontec (all IP, royalty-free,
perpetual). What remains exclusive to LQMT is non-consumer-electronics use
in North America and Europe plus non-exclusive rights elsewhere outside
Asia, defended by "about 40 patents" (CEO) whose count and expiry the 10-K
does not table. The CEO's own words: "we actually want competitors to come
in." The manufacturing know-how he calls the real protection sits at
Yihao and Eontec, which the chairman controls.

**Control:** One class, 917.3M shares, 1.1B authorized. Professor Lugee Li
holds 24.8% (227.5M shares via Liquidmetal Technology Limited) after
selling 179.8M shares (44% of his position) to four private buyers on
2024-10-10 (SC 13D/A, 10-Q Note 2016 Purchase Agreement). Directors and
officers hold 26.1% together. A four-person board with one independent
director; no proxy statement has been filed since 2023-05-16. Li is also
founder and chairman of Eontec (Shenzhen-listed), controls Yihao (the
contract manufacturer), and since 2025-05-28 runs Liquidmetal Asia and the
Hangzhou JV. The 10-K's own risk factor: he "may have conflicts of interest
and interests that are not aligned with those of other stockholders." The
structure forecloses nothing formally — no controller above 50% — but every
related-party contract on both sides of the table is his.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue has been flat for five
years while the operating loss widened; the income statement is where the
$4.2M JV contribution and the "factory build out" now land.
