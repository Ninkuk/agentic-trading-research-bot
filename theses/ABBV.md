**p(beat SPY, 63 td): 0.46** · kill-thesis: 0.45 · **Disputed expectation:**
the market is paying $454B for a trailing levered FCF of $18.2B — a 4.0% yield
that solves to a 7.60% implied return on the *most generous* honest inputs (raw

AbbVie is an unusually good business that has already done the hard thing.
It replaced Humira — which fell from $21.2B (2022) to a ~$3.0B run-rate
(Q2 2026, −36.1% operational) — with Skyrizi and Rinvoq, both compounding
above 23%, and it did so without the operating margin permanently breaking

business is bad. It is that the price already pays for the recovery, and that
the reported free cash flow overstates what an owner keeps by enough to turn a
"fairly priced compounder" into a name that clears no hurdle on any input set
I can defend. Underneath that sits a dated structural fact the company states
in its own 10-K: the **US composition-of-matter patents on risankizumab
(Skyrizi) and upadacitinib (Rinvoq) expire in 2033** — a ~$32B franchise, over
half the company, seven years out, which is precisely why AbbVie paid $10.9B
for Apogee for assets that management says accrete in **2032**.

**Closest attack:** that the fully-loaded base FCF of $14.2B double-counts.

gap I deduct is not the payments but the ~$4.2B/yr by which the accrued charge
exceeds them, which is the market's own forecast that the payment schedule
rises. But it does mean the honest range is $14.2B–$18.2B, not a point
estimate, and the top of that range still misses the hurdle.

## 2. Business

**Created:** AbbVie sells small-molecule and biologic medicines that suppress
specific immune signalling pathways, plus a neuroscience and an aesthetics
book. The customer surplus is concrete and measurable rather than rhetorical:
in inflammatory bowel disease the payer and the physician are buying
*endoscopic remission* — objectively healed bowel tissue, not symptom relief
— and management's own framing is that Skyrizi doubled the prior standard of
care on that endpoint in the head-to-head SEQUENCE trial against Stelara, with
the α4β7 combination doubling it again at week 24 in a within-trial comparison
(Q2 2026 call; Wells Fargo conference, 2026-09-09; both primary-transcribed
via stockanalysis). Convenience is the second axis: quarterly Skyrizi dosing,
and for the Apogee assets a quarterly-or-twice-yearly target. The aesthetics
book (Botox Cosmetic, Juvéderm) sells a different good entirely — appearance,
paid cash, no payer — and is the part of the company currently not growing
(−0.9% operational in Q2).

**Captured:** four mechanisms, not one.
(i) *Branded-price capture under patent* — the core engine. Skyrizi at $21.7B
guided FY2026 revenue is a patent monopoly rent, and it ends in 2033 in the
US. (ii) *Line-of-therapy expansion* — the same molecule re-sold into new
indications at incremental cost: Rinvoq now spans rheumatology, IBD,
dermatology (vitiligo and alopecia areata approved in Europe, US decisions
pending), giant cell arteritis. Each indication is a new market with the
manufacturing and field force already paid for. (iii) *Co-positioning two
assets in one call* — the sales force sells Skyrizi and Rinvoq into the same
prescriber for different patient segments, which is why the incremental
indication carries near-100% contribution margin. (iv) *Cash aesthetics* —
Botox Cosmetic and fillers, priced outside the reimbursement system, currently
flat.
What AbbVie does *not* fully capture: the contingent-consideration liability
of $27.5B (June 30, 2026) is a royalty stream running to third parties on
these same products, and it is growing — up from $21.7B eighteen months
earlier — because the projected payments keep being revised up as Skyrizi
scales. The marginal Skyrizi dollar is not entirely AbbVie's.

**Protected:** patents, and the answer has an expiry date printed in the
company's own risk factors. Beyond patents there are three weaker but real
mechanisms: the head-to-head trial library (five different mechanisms beaten
in direct comparison, five-year PsA durability data), which is expensive and
slow for a competitor to replicate; the combination platform, where owning
both the backbone (Skyrizi) and the partner molecules internally lets AbbVie
run within-trial comparisons a licensor cannot; and the biologic manufacturing
and Botox trade-secret position. None of these survives a composition-of-
matter expiry on its own — Humira had all of them and lost 86% of its revenue
in three and a half years.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder. Insiders own 0.070% of shares outstanding; institutions 76.65%; float
is 99.8% of shares out. Nothing in the charter forecloses an unsolicited
takeover or an activist path — at a $454B market cap the practical barrier is
size, not structure. This is the null answer and it is written down.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive** on the current leg, **flat** across
the full window.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The contingent-consideration liability (the thread that mattered).** The
10-Q's fair-value note carries a $27,495M Level-3 contingent-consideration
liability at June 30, 2026, up from $25,374M at December 31, 2025 and $21,666M
at January 1, 2025. The rollforward for H1 2026: beginning $25,374M, change in
fair value recognised in net earnings **+$3,905M**, payments **−$1,784M**,
ending $27,495M. Projected payments run 2026–2044 with a weighted-average year
of 2030; the probability of payment for royalties by indication is a 93%
weighted average, and the discount rate 4.5%. Both halves of the pair sit in
operating cash flow, so `fcf` is *not* overstated for the cash actually paid
this year — but the charge exceeds the payment by ~$4.2B annualized, which is
the company's own marked-to-model statement that the future schedule is rising
as Skyrizi scales. This is the single largest reason trailing FCF ($18.21B)
exceeds even amortization-adjusted cash earnings ($13.18B), and it is why §4's
`growth without reinvestment` warning cannot be made to disappear by adding
back intangible amortization alone.

**Acquired IPR&D sits outside `fcf`.** The H1 2026 cash-flow statement adds
back $1,035M of "Acquired IPR&D and milestones" as a non-cash reconciling item
and shows $1,090M of "Other acquisitions and investments, net of cash
acquired" in *investing*. So roughly $2.2B/yr of pipeline-buying cash — the
tuck-in and milestone spend that keeps the 2030s pipeline alive — never
touches `fcf = NCFO − capex`. That is the serial-acquirer check landing:
AbbVie's growth is partly bought, and `fcf` gets the growth without paying for
it. Episodic deals are larger still: $20.5B of acquisition cash in 2024
(ImmunoGen, Cerevel), $10.9B for Apogee in 2026.

**Apogee, closed after the last call.** The 8-K of 2026-09-03 (Ex-99.1)
records completion: $135.11/share cash, **$10.9B total equity value**, with
guided EPS dilution of $0.14 in 2026 and **~$0.46 in 2027, accretion beginning
in 2032**. Funded by a $10.0B nine-tranche note offering priced 2026-08-04/06
(424B5) at coupons of 4.50%–6.10% plus a floating tranche — a ~5.16%
face-weighted coupon and ~$516M/yr of incremental pre-tax interest, against
$68.4B of senior notes already outstanding at June 30, 2026 and a $2.0B term
loan. The CFO's stated target is a 2.0× net leverage ratio within two to three
years of close. A company buying a Phase-2 asset for $10.9B with a stated
six-year wait for accretion is telling you what it thinks about 2033.

**Capital-allocation track record, both directions.** Management's own count
(Wells Fargo, 2026-09-09): ~30 transactions over the last couple of years for
about $20B, half of it Apogee. R&D has risen ~$3.5B since 2022 to ~$9.8B. The
wins are real (Skyrizi itself was in-licensed). The most recent large failure
is also in the record and management raised it unprompted: emraclidine, the
core of the $8.7B Cerevel purchase, is being re-dosed from an original 30mg to
100mg and now 150mg with Phase II restarting in Q4 2026 — four years and
$8.7B in, still dose-finding. Read against Apogee's price, this is the thread
that should worry an owner most.

**The pricing-regime deal, and its three-year fuse.** The 10-K's Recent
Events section discloses a voluntary agreement with the US government: low
Medicaid prices, expanded direct-to-patient offerings, and a **pledge of $100
billion in US-based R&D and capital investments, including manufacturing, over
the next decade**, in exchange for a **three-year exemption from tariffs and
future price mandates**. Two readings, both material. The $100B/decade is
$10B/yr against a current R&D-plus-capex run-rate of ~$11.1B/yr — so it may be
a restatement of existing spend rather than an increment, but it is a
commitment that constrains where the money goes and pushes it toward
higher-cost US manufacturing. The exemption is the sharper item: it is dated,
it is three years, and it expires while Skyrizi and Rinvoq are still the
company. Neither the Q2 call nor the September conference mentioned it once.

**The asset-light check.** Capex is $1,297M TTM on $64.4B of revenue — 2.0%,
genuinely low. The 10-K's commitments language is generic ("AbbVie enters into
certain unconditional purchase obligations and other commitments in the normal
course of business") with no quantified multi-year take-or-pay disclosed in
the text I retrieved. So the low-capex base is not contradicted by a hidden
obligation — but the $100B pledge above is the same question wearing different
clothes, and it *is* quantified.

**Pension: overfunded, no haircut.** The 10-K's post-employment note shows a
funded status of **+$2,546M** on defined benefit plans (2025) against
**−$822M** on other post-employment plans — net +$1.72B. This is the one item
on the haircut list that runs the other way, and it does not enter §4.

**Integrity checks.**
- *Reinvestment / `growth without reinvestment` warning — answered, not
  dismissed.* The warning fires on every 2.36%-terminal row because base FCF
  exceeds base earnings. The skill's first response (cut terminal growth to
  the breakeven) is already applied — 2.36% *is* the breakeven. The second
  (show the earnings base is understated by acquired-intangible amortization)
  is applied and is not sufficient: even with cash earnings of $13.18B, FCF of
  $17–18B still exceeds it. The residual gap is the contingent-consideration
  accrual-over-cash item from §3 (~$4.2B/yr), and naming it is the answer —
  it argues that the *base* is too high, not that the terminal growth is too
  low. The ROIC-consistent row makes the arithmetic explicit: funding 2.36%
  terminal growth at AbbVie's own 17.7% ROIC requires reinvesting
  g/ROIC = 13.3% of earnings, leaving $11.43B of distributable FCF and an
  **`implied_terminal_roe` of 17.74%** — internally consistent with the actual
  ROIC and comfortably above the hurdle, so the terminal *return* assumption
  is defensible even though the terminal *base* is not.
- *Terminal-ROE two-sided read.* 17.74% is ~950bp above the 8.26% hurdle —
  more than the ~5-point "tough to do in perpetuity" line. It is defensible
  only while the patents hold, which is the next bullet.
- *Terminal growth vs the disclosed terminal risk (Item 1A).* The dominant
  structural risk in the 10-K's own words: "The United States composition of
  matter patents covering risankizumab and upadacitinib are expected to expire
  in **2033**." A 2.36% terminal growth rate applied from year 6 (2032) claims
  that nominal cash flows grow at inflation forever starting the year before
  more than half the revenue base loses US exclusivity. Humira's own precedent
  under this management, this sales force and this pipeline: −49% in 2025
  alone, ~−86% from the 2022 peak in three and a half years. I do **not**
  believe 2.36% survives that risk, which is why the two cliff rows (terminal
  0%) are in the table rather than in a footnote — and they read 5.39% and
  4.48%.
- *Base-year cash tax rate vs marginal.* Cash taxes paid $3,626M TTM against
  pretax income of $8,699M is 41.7% — *above*, not below, the marginal rate,
  because GAAP pretax is depressed by the same non-cash items that inflate
  FCF. The base is not flattered by NOLs or deferrals; if anything the cash
  tax burden is running heavy. No haircut for this.
- *Serial-acquirer charge.* Applied explicitly as the −$2,180M recurring
  acquired-IPR&D/milestone line, and the episodic deals are excluded from the
  growth path (the 8% path is treated as organic; the FY2026 guide of $67.6B
  is +10.5% with Apogee contributing zero revenue).
- *Market-share sentence.* Compounding the FY2026 guide of $67.6B at 8%/yr
  for five years puts 2031 revenue at **$99.3B**, up 47%. The share question
  here is really a *duration* question: reaching it requires the Skyrizi and
  Rinvoq franchise to roughly double from its ~$32B FY2026 run-rate, and both
  molecules lose US composition-of-matter protection two years after the
  forecast ends. A sourced global immunology TAM is an UNKNOWN (§6.2); what
  the arithmetic does show is that the path needs the two drugs to keep
  gaining share right up to the cliff.
- *Distribution clamp.* The most generous row (7.60%) sits below the US
  median cost of capital of **7.79%**; the fully-loaded and cliff rows (4.48%
  –6.48%) sit at or below the **5.26%** 10th percentile of the US
  cost-of-capital distribution — a strong pass by the clamp rule regardless of
  story. Damodaran cost-of-capital distribution and excess-return base rate,
  Data Update 5, 2026 vintage.
- *Policy stance as a scenario input (moved out of §1's condition list).*
