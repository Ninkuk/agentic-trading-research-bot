## 2. Business

Unchanged from `SEZL-2026-07-30.md` §2 (created / captured / protected) — the
print extends it. Structural additions this quarter:

- **SezzleCash** (June, phased): cash advances (~$165 average) for Sezzle
  Anywhere subscribers, repaid Pay-in-4/5, WebBank-originated. ~10% of new
  eligible subscribers take an advance as their **first** transaction [call].
  Management reads that as product-market fit; a credit reader notes it is
  also textbook adverse selection — liquidity-seeking users self-selecting at
  the door. Too new for loss data; guidance assumes little from it.
- **Sezzle Send** (August): P2P transfers by phone number, funded in full or
  Pay-in-5 (recipient gets 100% upfront; ~$3 fee per $100 for non-subscribers,
  free for Anywhere) — each send is a customer-acquisition channel; ~100k
  waitlist pre-launch; excluded from guidance [PR; call].
- **The moat evidence moved against exclusivity, from the company's own
  mouth**: President Paradis — merchants that once committed to one BNPL now
  add "a second or third," which "accelerated the enterprise sales funnel"
  [call, Q&A]. Good for Sezzle's distribution today (Poshmark, Gymshark,
  Debenhams, Brookshire's, RockAuto signed in Q2); direct confirmation that
  multi-tenanting is the equilibrium and switching costs are ~zero.
- **Charter pivot**: the ILC plan is now an OCC **national bank charter**
  application (this quarter; 12–18 months through OCC + FDIC + Fed) [call] —
  management's second consecutive restructuring of its answer to the
  WebBank-dependency problem, which is both mitigation and an admission the
  problem is real.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0, income-statement route): strongly positive and
intact through Q2'26** even with marketing 2.2x YoY (the /statistics/ 57.2%
"operating margin" excludes provision-type costs and is not used). Note the
Q/Q wobble inside the trend: Q1'26 $69.0M → Q2'26 $55.0M on the seasonal
provision step and the marketing test.
