## 2. Business

**Created:** refineries, chemical plants, LNG trains, pipelines, power
stations (fossil and nuclear), mines, water and pharma sites buy engineered
and pre-configured pumps, mechanical seals, isolation and control valves and
actuators, then keep them running for decades. The customer gets equipment
specified into a process that cannot stop — an unplanned outage costs more
than the pump — and a global network of 26 quick-response centres that
repairs, re-rates and re-parts the installed base fast. "Speed wins" is the
aftermarket pitch; nuclear customers additionally get supplier
qualifications most rivals do not hold (assets in 115 operating reactors via
Trillium, ~$100–120M of content per new large reactor).

**Captured:** (a) original-equipment project sales — engineered pumps (FPD)
and valves (FCD) on 12–18-month LNG/nuclear/refining projects at lower
margin, bid selectively for the aftermarket tail; (b) aftermarket parts,
repairs, upgrades and services at a premium margin — 58% of H1 2026 sales,
$2.6B of 2025 bookings, nine straight quarters above $600M; (c) run-rate
"book and ship" MRO valves and parts through distributors; (d) services and
solutions (Red Raven digital, life extensions). Two segments: FPD (Q2 sales
$814M, adjusted OI margin 21.3%) and FCD ($357M, adjusted 12.6%, GAAP 3.1%).
Geography (2025 sales by facility location): US 41.6%, EMA 34.1%, Asia
15.3%, other 9.0%. Energy was 33% of 2025 bookings, down from 37%.

**Protected:** the installed base is the mechanism — a spec'd-in engineered
pump or nuclear-qualified valve is replaced by the OEM's parts and service
far more often than by a competitor's, and the QRC network makes the OEM the
fastest responder. Nuclear adds regulatory qualification. The protection is
partial by the 10-K's own admission: "the aftermarket for standard products
is competitive, as the existence of common standards allows for easier
replacement" — the run-rate valve business FCD depends on is the exposed
edge, and Starboard's "500bp below peer median margins" says the moat has
not been converted into peer-level pricing.

**Control:** one class of common (127,112,780 shares), one vote per share,
no controlling holder; BlackRock 8.6% and D1 Capital Partners 5.7% are the
only 5% holders (proxy, March 2026); insiders 0.6%. Starboard Value has an
undisclosed stake (no 13D on file) and sent a public letter 2026-05-28.
Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist path — the null answer, and an
activist is already at the door.
