## 1. Verdict and thesis

**BUY at $80.66.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=5 (2 probable,
3 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

Globus is a founder-controlled, engineering-led spine-implant company that
the 2023 NuVasive merger made one of the two or three largest spine players,
and it is still taking share the old-fashioned way: an exclusive rep force
that stands in the operating room, a broad bag of implants launched at a

## 2. Business

**Created:** a surgeon operating on a spine, a fractured limb, or a chronic
pain patient gets a complete, sterile kit — implants, instruments, biologics,
navigation — in the room when the case starts, delivered by a rep who knows
the surgeon's technique, backed by a product cadence (over 25 launches in 36
months, 60+ projects in development) that keeps solving specific clinical
problems (single-step screw placement, patient-specific spacers, expandable
interbody devices). The surplus is time and confidence at the table, not
price: hospitals pay list-minus for spine implants and the differentiation
is procedural.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (i) Implant and disposable sales per
procedure — Musculoskeletal Solutions, $2,797.9M in FY25, $763.5M in Q2 26,
priced per component and consigned to the field. (ii) Capital sales,
leases, and rentals of ExcelsiusGPS/Hub/3D/XR — Enabling Technologies,
$141.0M in FY25 and $26.1M in Q2 26 (−26% YoY as the mix shifts from cash
sales to leases, which defers revenue but keeps the robot in the hospital
where it pulls implants through). (iii) Services — intraoperative
neuromonitoring (IONM, from NuVasive) and robot service, both growing 30%+.
(iv) Spinal cord stimulation devices and trials — Nevro, ~$325M annualised,
a separate reimbursement world (CMS's WISER prior-authorisation pilot
touches it). International is 19.4% of FY25 sales, growing 18% as reported
in Q2.

**Protected:** a mechanism, not a label — the exclusive rep in the OR with
a consigned set, plus the robot on the floor. A surgeon who has been trained
on ExcelsiusGPS and has her cases' screw plans in Script Studio does not
switch implant vendors for a 5% price cut; the switching cost sits with the
surgeon, not the purchasing department. In-house manufacturing (the source
of a 75% pre-merger gross margin) funds the rep force that competitors using
independent distributors cannot match on density. The limit is symmetrical:
reps can be poached — recruiting competitors' reps is exactly Globus's own
growth lever — and the 10-K names Medtronic, DePuy Synthes, Stryker, Zimmer
Biomet, Smith + Nephew and VB Spine as the peers doing the same thing.

**Control:** two classes. 111.8M Class A (one vote) and 22.4M Class B (ten
votes). Founder and Executive Chairman David C. Paul holds 22,258,997 Class
B shares (99.2% of the class) for **66.2% of the combined voting power**;
Globus is a "controlled company" under NYSE Rule 303A and relies on the
exemptions (DEF 14A, 2026-04-24). Class B converts to Class A on transfer
except permitted transfers, so control ends when Paul's estate sells. This
forecloses an unsolicited takeover and any activist path; the board is
classified with three-year terms. The same fact aligns the largest
shareholder with per-share value and makes succession a single-person risk
(Paul is 59).
