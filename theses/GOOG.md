## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $337.71.** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=5 (2 probable,
3 plausible), refuted=1, unknown=1.

The operating business is as good as any in the world and it is getting
better, not worse, under AI: Search & other grew 17% in Q2 2026, Cloud grew
82% at a 35.6% operating margin on a $514B backlog, and consolidated
operating margin expanded to 34%. None of that is the problem. The problem
is that Alphabet has stopped being a cash-return business for the
foreseeable future: FY2026 capex is guided to $195–205B (~42% of revenue),
2027 capex is guided to "increase significantly," purchase commitments went
from $149.1B at year-end to **$811.0B** at June 30, Q2 free cash flow was
**−$5.9B**, and the company raised $49.6B of common and preferred equity,
opened a $40B ATM, and added ~$56B of debt in six months to pay for it. At
$4.15T the price already assumes the capex earns its keep and then
normalizes; the reverse DCF says even the optimistic path (capex back to
10.5% of revenue, 14% growth, 4% terminal) implies 8.4% against a 9.9%
hurdle, and the reported-FCF path implies ~5%. A superb business at a price
that needs everything to go right is a pass.

**Closest attack:** the commitments footnote. The bull case needs owner FCF
to normalize to ~$150B within the forecast window; $811B of purchase
commitments ($200.7B short-term) plus $85.2B of unrecorded data-center
leases say the capex intensity is contracted, not discretionary, through at
least 2028. That refutes the normalization condition outright.

## 2. Business

**Created:** For users, the fastest route from a question to an answer or an
action, at zero price — Search, YouTube, Maps, Android, Chrome, Gmail, and
now Gemini (950M MAU app, AI Mode >1B MAU). For advertisers, intent: a
person typing "insurance quote" is worth more than any audience segment,
and Gemini-assisted matching extends that to long, conversational queries
that were previously unmonetizable (Q2 call: AI Max "unlocks billions of
net new searches"). For enterprises, a vertically integrated AI stack —
TPU silicon, Gemini models, Vertex, Wiz security, Workspace — sold as
consumption and subscription.

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms, not "advertising":
(a) Search & other auction ads, $63.3B/qtr, paid per click, TAC paid to
distribution partners ($16.2B/qtr total TAC);
(b) YouTube ads, $11.1B/qtr, brand + direct response, revenue-shared with
creators;
(c) Network ads, $7.3B/qtr, shrinking, and the piece exposed to the ad-tech
remedy;
(d) subscriptions, platforms and devices, $12.9B/qtr — YouTube Premium/Music,
Google One AI plans, Play's 30%/15% take, Pixel;
(e) Google Cloud, $24.8B/qtr — GCP consumption, Workspace seats, and from Q2
2026 outright **sales of TPU systems** delivered into customer data centers,
which is hardware revenue with hardware margins, and the reason inventory
went from $2.4B to $10.0B in six months (10-Q).

**Protected:** In Search, the mechanism is a three-sided flywheel — the
largest query stream trains the ranking and ad models, the largest advertiser
base makes every query more monetizable, and distribution deals (now
restricted by the Dec 2025 judgment, but not exclusive-only) keep the query
stream. The Dec 2025 remedy attacks exactly this by forcing data sharing and
syndication to rivals — it is the one moat mechanism now under court order.
In Cloud/AI the protection is cost: owning TPUs and the model stack gives a
lower cost per token than a GPU-renting rival, and the $514B backlog is
contracted demand. In YouTube it is the creator library and the living-room
share. There is no protection against a frontier-model rival being simply
better; management's own answer to "what is the moat" was "the full stack"
and "the model is just an ingredient" (Q2 call) — a reasonable answer, not a
mechanism that forecloses a rival.

**Control:** Three classes. Class B (10 votes, 835M sh) is 89.3% owned by
Larry Page and Sergey Brin, giving them 52.7% of the vote (10-K FY2025 Item
1A); Class A (GOOGL, 1 vote, 5,868M sh); Class C (GOOG, no vote, 5,527M sh).
Plus 19M shares of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred (GOOGM/GOOGN, $19.0B
liquidation preference) issued June 2026. The founders foreclose any
unsolicited takeover or activist path; a Class C holder has no vote at all.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue 2021→TTM $257.6B →
$445.9B (+73%); operating income $78.7B → $147.6B (+88%); op margin 30.6% →
33.1%. FY2025: rev $402.8B / OI $129.0B; FY2024: $350.0B / $112.4B; FY2023:
$307.4B / $84.3B; FY2022: $282.8B / $74.8B. The only wrinkle is 2022, where
revenue grew 10% and OI fell 5% — the last time opex outran revenue.
**Net income is not the number to read:** TTM net income $244.2B includes
$135.9B of H1 2026 equity-security gains (SpaceX marked to its June IPO,
$94.0B fair value; non-marketable securities remeasured to $124.3B, $87.9B
of it in Q2). Q2 GAAP EPS $9.11 vs ~$3.05 operating. Robinhood's "actual"
EPS matches SEC diluted EPS each quarter (9.11 / 5.11 / 2.82 / 2.87), so its
beat pattern for 2026 is the marks, not execution.
