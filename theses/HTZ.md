## 2. Business

**Created:** a car, at an airport, when you land. The customer buys
availability and time — the surplus is the trip that happens instead of not
happening. Nothing about the Hertz car differs from the Avis car; the product
is the counter being staffed and a vehicle being there.

**Captured:** three mechanisms, not one. (i) Rental revenue per day —
$61.98 in Q2, +9% YoY, the entire source of this quarter's growth since
transaction days were **flat** (38,646k vs 38,695k). (ii) Fleet arbitrage:
buy at OEM fleet pricing, depreciate at $302/unit/month, sell into wholesale
or retail — Hertz is one of the largest used-car dealers in the country, and
the disposal channel mix (currently 70–80% wholesale, which they intend to
invert) is a real profit lever. (iii) Ancillary — insurance, fuel, upgrades,
inside "value-added products."

**Protected:** there is no good answer, and that silence is the answer.
Airport concessions and a three-player structure are labels. Asked as a
mechanism — what stops Avis or Enterprise tomorrow? — nothing does. Same
concessions, same OEM channel, same brands, same customers. Hertz's one
genuine differentiator is *negative*: the weakest balance sheet in the set,
which makes it the forced fleet seller and the operator that cannot add
capacity into strength. Absent a moat, the thesis reduces to leverage plus
execution.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive** — and stated explicitly. Three
sourced points; FY2021–FY2024 GAAP operating income was not retrievable this
session (see §6).

| period | revenue | GAAP operating income |
|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | $5,258M | −$1,453M |
| TTM Jun-2025 | $8,614M | −$587M |
| TTM Jun-2026 | $8,906M | **+$127M** |

Revenue +3.4% YoY carried a **+$714M** swing in operating income. Operating
leverage runs **positive**. It is still not enough: that $127M sits against
$941M of TTM total interest expense — the operating line covers **13.5%** of
the interest bill.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The "GAAP net income $64M" headline is manufactured.** Q2 pre-tax income
  of $71M contains a **$98M** Public Warrant fair-value gain and a **$64M**
  gain on sale of non-vehicle capital assets. Strip both: pre-tax **−$91M**.
  Management's own Adjusted Net Loss was **−$47M**, adjusted diluted EPS
  **−$0.11**. The warrant gain exists *because the stock collapsed* (liability
  $222M → $90M), so a share-price recovery mechanically produces GAAP losses —
  Q2'25 booked a $115M warrant *expense* when the stock rose.
- **The June "37,037,037 share offering" was not a capital raise.** Hertz
  *loaned* the shares to J.P. Morgan Securities, which sold them and kept the
  proceeds; Hertz took a nominal fee. Hence $3M of stock proceeds for all of
  1H26 against 44.2M new shares. The purpose was to let exchangeable-note
  buyers establish a hedge short — so a large slice of reported short interest
  is structural convertible arbitrage, not directional bearishness. It also
  means those hedgers buy stock into strength, which is a mechanical
  contributor to tonight's move.
- **Residual erosion is Hertz-specific, not macro — and this is the sharpest
  finding.** Net DPU rose **18% YoY to $302** while the Manheim index ran
  **+2% YoY** (211.5 mid-July; 212.9 June; Cox guides ~+2% for the year). The
  external market improved and per-unit depreciation still worsened.
  Management concedes the mechanism ("opportunistic rotation of older vehicles
  prioritizing fleet youth over proceeds") and calls it quarter-specific — but
  guides FY2026 net DPU to "at or below $300," i.e. no reversion in their own
  numbers.
- **The 2027 target has no published bridge.** FY2026 guide $225–275M →
  FY2027 target $1,000M is +$750M in one year. Sizing the two levers against
  Q2 actuals: DPU is worth ~$78M/yr per $12/month across 539,118 average
  vehicles, so $750M from DPU alone needs DPU at ~$186 — below anything
  disclosed. RPD is worth ~$67M of EBITDA per 1%, so $750M needs ~11% more RPD
  *on top of* the 9% just delivered. Neither reaches it alone; no bridge
  exists.
- **FY2026 is back-loaded into the one unguided quarter.** 1H26 was −$80M, so
  H2 must produce $305–355M. Q3 is guided $275–325M, leaving **Q4 at ~+$5M to
  +$55M**. Deriving the comparison (FY25 −$339M less 1H25 −$284M less Q4'25
  −$205M) gives Q3'25 = **+$150M**. So Q3 needs a $125–175M YoY improvement
  and Q4 needs **$210–260M** — the heavier lift, in the seasonally weakest
  quarter, unguided.
- **Credit is priced as distressed.** Existing first-lien notes carry
  **12.625%**. The June 2026 money needed first-lien security *and* PIK *and*
  equity conversion to clear at 6.75% (exchange price ~$3.58, 279.5248
  sh/$1,000). Vehicle debt $12,710M against net revenue-earning vehicles of
  $13,680M is **92.9% LTV** — a $970M cushion on a fleet depreciating 18%
  faster per unit than last year. The CFO declined, twice, to give a
  refinancing timeline for the ~$2.0B of term loans maturing June 2028.
- **Options read (mandatory):** ran, refutes nothing, reading UNRELIABLE —
  path 2 only (Robinhood stopgap); table in §4.
- **Dead ends:** *Insider filings.* No open-market purchase by any officer or
  director during a 69% drawdown; the only 2026 open-market trade found was a
  sale (COO, 40,919 sh at $6.07, May 11). **This proves less than it looks:**
  June 24–Aug 6 spans a guidance 8-K, an offering, quarter-close and earnings,
  so a blackout plausibly explains the silence, and the May 11 sale sits in a
  routine post-Q1 window. Recorded as checked, not as evidence.
  (Low-confidence tier — aggregator, not EDGAR; see §6.) *The used-car market
  — a dead end that inverted usefully.* I went looking for evidence that a
  collapsing wholesale market caused the June guidance cut. It did not:
  Manheim was up 2% YoY and Cox expects the year to finish ~+2%. That
  *disconfirmed my own bear framing* and, in doing so, made the DPU finding
  above much stronger — if the market was fine, the miss was Hertz's.

## 4. Valuation

**The catalog FCF input is a category error, and Phase 4 caught it.** For a
fleet business, vehicle purchases and disposals both run gross through
investing: TTM capex **−$11,614M** against proceeds of **+$9,115M** on NCFO of
+$1,429M. So `fcf = ncfo + capex` prints **−$10,185M**, and the solver refused:

```
$ uv run python -m tools.valuation.reverse_dcf --market-cap 718954451 \
    --base-fcf -10185000000 --growth 0.02 0.02 0.02 --terminal-growth 0.02
refused: base_fcf must be positive, got -10185000000.0      # exit 2
```

**The clean statement needs no DCF.** At a **14.41%** hurdle (rf **4.74%** +
beta **2.26** × ERP **4.28%**; Damodaran, ERP as of **2026-08-01**) with 2%
terminal growth, a $719M market cap prices roughly **$89M** of sustainable
annual free cash flow to equity — **$120M** at the overnight $2.71. On FY2026
guidance the actual figure is about **−$544M** ($250M adjusted corporate EBITDA
less ~$600M corporate interest, ~$114M non-vehicle capex, ~$80M SBC). Almost
none of today's price is paid for today's business.

**Granting management's 2027 target in full** — base FCF $206M ($1,000M EBITDA
less the same three items), 3% growth, 2% terminal:

| price | market cap | implied return | spread vs 14.41% hurdle |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2.02 | $719M | **~32%/yr** | +1750 bp |
| $2.71 | $965M | **~24%/yr** | +991 bp |

Cutting terminal growth to 0% moves $2.02 only from 32% to **30.7%** — at these
discount rates the terminal value barely matters, which is itself the finding.

**Quoted to whole percents deliberately.** ATM IV is 141%, far past the skill's
50% precision trigger; "31.91%" would be arithmetic, not knowledge. The band is
wide.

**Assumptions, stated.** Shares **355,918,045** from the Q2 balance sheet — not
stockanalysis's 315.76M, which is stale and would understate market cap by 11%.
Levered FCF paired with **market cap**, never the ~$18.8B EV. Corporate
interest ~$600M/yr annualised from the 1H26 add-back of $285M, grown for the
PIK accretion. SBC deducted (~$80M/yr) per the high-SBC check — adjusted
corporate EBITDA adds it back, but it is compensation, not cash the owner keeps.

**The `growth without reinvestment` warning fired and is answered here.** With
base FCF $206M ≥ base earnings $176M the terminal reinvestment rate prints
−0.1705, so 2% terminal growth is unfunded. The honest response is the 0%
terminal run above, which changes nothing material.

**Terminal risk vs terminal growth.** The dominant structural risk in the
company's own forward-looking factors is residual-value exposure on non-program
vehicles compounded by refinancing access. A 2% terminal growth rate does not
survive that on its own merits — it survives only because, at a 30%+ discount
rate, the terminal period is nearly worthless. Said plainly: the terminal value
is not doing the work here; the next 24 months are.

**The step-change owes a magnitude sentence.** $1,000M on ~$9.2B of revenue is
an ~11% adjusted corporate EBITDA margin — well below the 2021–22 used-car
bubble, but the best non-bubble margin in the company's modern record, and it
must be reached from −$339M two years earlier.
