## 2. Business

**Created:** Gulfstream sells range, speed and cabin (the G650/G700/G800
family: longest range and fastest speed in class per the 10-K product table)
to corporations and governments who buy a business tool — management's own
framing on the Q2 call, rejecting the "luxury" analogy. Marine sells the US
Navy the only two yards in the country that can build nuclear submarines
(Electric Boat with HII's Newport News), plus DDG-51 destroyers (Bath) and
auxiliaries (NASSCO). Combat sells vehicles and munitions to the US Army and,
increasingly, allied governments (Canada ACSV, European Land Systems).
Technologies sells IT and mission systems to federal agencies.

**Captured:** Four distinct mechanisms. (a) Aerospace: aircraft sales at a
14.7% guided margin plus service revenue (Jet Aviation, Gulfstream service)
— commercial revenue was 15% of consolidated revenue in 2025 (10-K). (b)
Marine: multi-year Navy contracts at a thin 7.4% margin, with 44% of US
government revenue on cost-reimbursement terms and 51% fixed-price (10-K).
(c) Combat: fixed-price production at a 13.8% margin, with international
customers paying advance deposits (the ELS advances now unwinding). (d)
Technologies: services at 9.4%, with a $120B+ qualified pipeline (Q2 call).
Non-US revenue was 17% of the total in 2025 (10-K).

**Protected:** Marine's moat is a mechanism — a nuclear-certified industrial
base that the Navy itself is funding to expand; no third yard exists and the
customer's stated goal is 2 Virginia + 1 Columbia per year in the early 2030s
(Q2 call). Gulfstream's is a product-cycle and certification lead: an all-new
G400–G800 family already certified, against which a rival must fund a decade
of development. Combat and Technologies have no comparable mechanism —
incumbency on programs and security clearances, which competitors (HII,
Leidos, Booz Allen, Rheinmetall abroad) also hold. Written down as such.

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controller. Longview Asset Management
(the Crown family's manager) holds 10.0% (27.06M shares, proxy 2026-03-27),
Vanguard 8.9%, BlackRock 5.4%, Newport Trust (401(k) fund) 5.0%. A 10%
long-standing holder with a board history forecloses nothing formally but
makes an unsolicited approach improbable. Danny Deep (President) was elected
to the board 2026-08-05 (8-K) — a succession signal, not a control change.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $38.5B (FY21) → $52.6B
(FY25) → $54.9B TTM; operating income $4.38B → $5.42B → $5.69B. Operating
margin dipped to 8.8% in FY23 (Gulfstream G700 certification delays) and
has recovered to 10.4% TTM; the FY26 guide is 10.5% on $55.7B. Net income
$3.26B → $4.21B → $4.49B TTM. Source: stockanalysis.com financials probe.
