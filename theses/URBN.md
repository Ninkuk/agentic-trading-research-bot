## 2. Business

**Created:** URBN sells a point of view, not a category. Each brand curates
an assortment and a store environment for one demographic slice —
Urban Outfitters for 18–28, Free People/FP Movement for 25–30, Anthropologie
for 28–45 — and the customer pays for the edit: internally designed or
exclusive product, in a store built to be lingered in, plus a digital
channel that carries the same brand voice. Nuuly extends the same thing as a
rental subscription: for a monthly fee a subscriber picks six pieces from
URBN's own brands, third-party brands and vintage, wears them, swaps them.
Wholesale sells Free People and FP Movement to department and specialty
stores. Customer surplus shows up as 22 consecutive quarters of positive
Anthropologie comps and regular-price-driven growth ("it is regular price
that is driving it … not a promotional cadence", COO, Barclays 2026-09-10).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, disclosed as segments (10-K Note
17, FY26): (a) Retail — merchandise margin on owned/exclusive product sold
through 784 company-owned stores and the brand sites; $5.28B sales, $578M
segment income, 10.9% margin; (b) Subscription — a monthly fee ($568M sales,
$34.9M income, 6.1% margin, rising to 8.4% in H1 FY27) plus a second margin
when subscribers buy the rented item, plus intelligence: Nuuly's fit and
preference data flows back to the sister brands, which supply 45–50% of its
assortment; (c) Wholesale — $314M sales at a 19.7% margin ($61.8M), mostly
FP Group into specialty and department stores. Brand mix by sales (H1 FY27):
Anthropologie 39%, FP Group 28%, Urban Outfitters 21%, Nuuly 11%.

**Protected:** design capability and brand identity are the moat, and they
are a mechanism only to the extent the creative teams keep hitting. There is
no switching cost, no network effect in Retail, and the 10-K says so in its
own words: "we rely heavily on our ability to identify changes in fashion."
What defends the cash flow is (i) a portfolio — when Anthropologie is "off
the bullseye" (COO's phrase), FP Group and Urban carry the comp, and the
company has still printed eight record quarters; (ii) owned logistics —
seven owned fulfillment/distribution centers (Gap PA ×2, Indiana PA, Kansas
City, Reno, Peterborough UK, Raymore MO since March 2026) that the capex
program is expanding; (iii) Nuuly's scale (500k+ subscribers, a purpose-
built platform) in a rental market with no US competitor of that size named
in the filing. None of that stops a competitor from hiring the designers.
Written down: the moat is execution, not structure.

**Control:** one class of common shares, one vote each, no controlling
holder. Richard A. Hayne (co-founder, Chairman and CEO, $1 salary) holds
20.0%; director Harry Cherken 9.9% almost entirely as co-trustee of Hayne
family trusts; Margaret Hayne (Co-President, spouse) 6.5%; David Hayne
(CTO, Nuuly president, son) 5.11% via a Schedule 13D filed 2026-02-18 —
the family and its trusts control roughly 30% after de-duplicating trust
overlaps (proxy 2026-04-01, as of 2026-03-11). That forecloses an
unsolicited takeover in practice and makes activism a negotiation with one
family; it does not foreclose the ordinary market discipline of a 35%
insider stake. Chairman and CEO roles are combined. On 2026-03-02 both
Richard and Margaret Hayne terminated 10b5-1 sale plans (800k and 700k
shares) that had been adopted in July 2025 (10-K Item 9B).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue up 42% since FY22
while operating income rose 54%; the FY23 trough (4.9%) was a markdown year,
and margin has expanded every year since:
