## 2. Business

**Created:** a tenant gets a leasable box in a shopping centre positioned so
that a defined local catchment drives past it, anchored by a grocer or a
value retailer that generates recurring, non-discretionary foot traffic.
The customer here is the *tenant*, not the shopper: a nail salon, an urgent
care, a quick-service restaurant buys proximity to a stream of people who
already come weekly for groceries. What they are actually paying for is the
anchor's traffic, which is why grocery-anchored centres command higher small-
shop rents than an unanchored strip. The consumer surplus shows up as the
tenant's willingness to renew at +7.9% and sign new leases at +18.7%.

**Captured:** in four distinct streams, not one.
(i) *Anchor base rent* — long-dated, low-escalator leases to credit tenants
(Kroger 3.3% of ABR, Publix 3.0%, TJX 2.6%, Amazon/Whole Foods 2.1%,
Albertsons 1.8%; top 25 = 31.3%). These are priced cheaply and bought as
traffic generation, not as income.
(ii) *Small-shop base rent* — 60.6% of ABR from sub-10,000 sq ft tenants at
materially higher rent per square foot, with 3–4% contractual escalators
that management says are replacing the historical ~2% rate. This is where
the money is made.
(iii) *Expense recoveries* — CAM, taxes and insurance passed through, which
is what makes the NOI margin (~73%: $230.2M NOI on $317.3M revenue)
structurally high and inflation-resistant.
(iv) *Spread investing* — buying centres at "low sixes" initial yields
against a blended cost of capital nearer 5.6%, funded with debt, retained
cash and equity. $290M closed year-to-date against a stated ~$2B pipeline.

**Protected:** weakly, and this matters. There is no mechanism that stops a
competitor buying the centre across the street tomorrow — open-air retail is
a commodity asset class and capital is the only barrier. The honest
protection is local and physical: zoning and entitlement friction in
supply-constrained Sun Belt submarkets, plus almost no new open-air
construction since 2008, which means existing well-located centres face
limited new supply. That is a *supply* condition, not a company moat, and it
accrues to every owner equally. IVT is also sub-scale — 78 properties against
Kimco's, Regency's and Brixmor's several hundred — and the evidence shows it:
its +8.5% blended spread trails Regency's ~19.5% GAAP spreads and Federal
Realty's +15% first-year cash rents over the same period, and management
described "pricing compression across all property types" while paying up to
enter Knoxville, Greensboro and Charleston. This is a competent price-taker,
not a franchise. No moat is claimed and none is counted.

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.001 par, NYSE-listed; one share,
one vote; no controlling holder and no dual-class structure. Incorporated in
Maryland; institutions hold 91.98% and insiders 0.89%. The board may issue
blank-check preferred without stockholder approval, which the 10-K itself
names as potentially "delaying, deferring or preventing a change in control."
That is the only meaningful takeover friction identified — a dispersed,
single-class REIT with a 99.1% float is structurally available to an
acquirer, and consolidation among strip-centre REITs is an active pattern.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive, mildly.** Revenue grew 41.0% from
FY2021 to the TTM ending 2026-06-30 while operating income grew ~46.2%;
operating margin expanded 59bp.

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs and pairing.** Market cap $2,402M (77,978,431 shares × $30.81).

independent construction. **No SBC haircut is applied, and that is
deliberate** — the Nareit-FFO-to-Core-FFO bridge does *not* add stock
compensation back, so Core FFO is already net of the $11.036M SBC run-rate

interests, no pension underfunding, no material litigation accrual to
haircut. Cash of $74.9M is 3.1% of market cap and not excess.
