## 1. Verdict and thesis

**BUY at $299.68 (live quote).** kill-thesis: **SOUND (on this pass)** —
conditions=5 (3 probable, 2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0.

Primerica distributes term life insurance and investment products to
middle-income households through 148,612 licensed independent
representatives — a sales force no competitor has replicated at this scale.
The term life block (approaching $1T face in force, ~90% of mortality ceded
to reinsurers) behaves like a fee stream at a ~21% margin; the investment
business (42% of revenue, growing 21%) earns sales and asset-based fees on
$140B of client assets, ~75% of it retirement money. The company earns a 33%
ROE, converts essentially all earnings to cash, and returns ~80% of them
(4.7% buyback yield + 1.6% dividend, 15 straight years of dividend growth).
At 12.1x trailing earnings the price implies 11.8% a year on conservative
assumptions against an 8.5% hurdle — the market is pricing the shrinking
sales force as if it were the whole story, when the cash flows ride the
in-force block and the asset base, not this quarter's recruiting.

**Closest attack:** the structural-decline attack on the sales force. New
life licenses fell 15% YoY in Q2, the force is down 3% to 148,612,
productivity (0.18 policies/rep/month) sits below historical levels, and
management trimmed its full-year sales-force guide for the second time
("came a little slower than we had hoped" — CEO, Q2 call). The recruiting
uptick (+2%) that answers it was partly bought with an April licensing-fee
discount. The attack fails to land only because the valuation clears the
hurdle even on a stress path that assumes no help from the force: a
distributable-cash-only base growing 3% still implies 10.05% (+158bp).

## 2. Business

**Created:** middle-income households ($30k–$130k) get in-person financial
education and simple products — term life ("buy term, invest the
difference"), mutual funds, annuities, managed accounts, mortgage referrals
— from a licensed neighbor. The traditional advisory industry does not
profitably serve accounts this small; the need is real (management cites a
$14T protection gap; the claim is theirs, but the mid-market advice vacuum
is well established). The rep gets a low-cost entrepreneurial income
opportunity; that recruiting proposition is itself part of the product.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (1) Term life underwriting on the
in-force block — but with up to 90% of mortality ceded, economics resemble a
fee on ~$1T of face: $444M revenue, 21.3% margin in Q2, claims largely
fixed. (2) ISP distribution: sales-based commissions on $4.4B/qtr of
product sales plus asset-based and advisory fees on $140B of client assets
($361M revenue, $104M pre-tax in Q2, +31%); Canadian principal-distributor
funds and US managed accounts push the mix toward recurring fees. (3) Net
investment income on the invested portfolio (avg rating A, new money at
4.9%) plus US mortgage brokerage ($150.6M closed volume, +13%).

**Protected:** the moat is the distribution itself — a 148,612-rep licensed
sales force built over 50 years, spanning 50 states and 10 provinces of
licensing and compliance infrastructure, with a recruiting culture that must
be perpetually re-primed (conventions, incentives, recognition). No
competitor fields licensed mid-market distribution at this scale, and
incumbent career-agent forces have been shrinking for decades. It is a real
but maintenance-heavy moat: it does not persist passively — which is exactly
why condition 3 is load-bearing.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue FY2021 $2,772M → TTM
$3,486M (+26%); operating income $834M → $1,105M (+32%); operating margin
30.1% → 31.7% (stockanalysis income-statement route, insurer keys
`revenueIns`/`opincIns`). Net income's 2024 dip ($470.5M) was the
e-TeleQuote senior-health disposal reported in discontinued operations
(10-K MD&A), not core deterioration.

## 5. Falsifiers

- **Break —** regulatory action forcing restructure of the independent-
  contractor distribution model (worker reclassification adopted in a major
  state without a direct-sales carve-out, FTC action, or
  business-opportunity-rule extension reaching licensed insurance
  distribution). The moat is the sales force's legal form; if that goes,
  the story is over.
- **Break —** sales-force decline accelerating through the 2027 convention:
  licensed count falling >5% YoY in the two prints after July 2027, i.e.
  the strongest historical catalyst failing to turn the trend — the
  recruiting engine would be structurally broken, not cyclically soft.
- **Shift —** ADP growth below ~2% or the benefits-and-claims ratio
  sustained above ~60%: the in-force block's fee-like stability (condition
  1) is repricing — revalue, don't exit reflexively.
- **Shift —** a bear market compressing ISP asset-based revenue: expected
  beta, not thesis failure; revalue on the new asset base (the 2022 analog
  bounds the damage).
- **Shift —** capital return ratio cut materially below ~80% of adjusted
  earnings without a named organic-growth use for the cash.
