## 2. Business

**Created:** SAP runs the financial and operational system of record for the
enterprises behind a large share of global commerce. What the customer gets is
process integrity — a single auditable ledger of what was ordered, made,
shipped, owed and paid — plus statutory compliance across 130+ jurisdictions,
and now AI agents grounded in that same master data rather than hallucinating
against it. The surplus is that the alternative (getting this wrong) is
existential, and the price is a low-single-digit percentage of the revenue the
system governs.

**Captured:** three stacked and quite different streams. Cloud ERP Suite
subscriptions (~€5.5B/qtr, +27% cc, ~88% of cloud revenue) are the growth
engine. A declining maintenance annuity (~€2.4B/qtr, −8%) at 20%+ margins is
the legacy on-premise base being deliberately run off. Services (~€1.0B/qtr)
is the low-margin implementation tail. The mechanism that connects them is
RISE: a conversion that reprices an existing on-premise maintenance contract
into a cloud subscription at a multiple of the old fee. That is a revenue
*transfer* with an uplift, not purely new demand — which is why total revenue
grows 6.4% while the cloud line grows 24% cc.

**Protected:** the switching cost is not a label, it is a mechanism with a
price tag — re-implementing every integration and control on a live business,
over years, with the risk of being unable to close the books during the
transition. The revealed preference is decades of maintenance payments in
preference to migrating even to a cheaper competitor. What that protection
does *not* cover is the layer above: if agents mediate the user's contact with
the ledger, SAP keeps the substrate and loses the seat-and-module pricing that
monetizes it. That is condition 4, and it is unresolved.

**Control:** one share class, one vote per share, no controlling holder;
institutions hold ~41% of the register. As a German SE, SAP carries co-
determination (employee representation on the supervisory board), which is a
real constraint on restructuring speed but not on capital allocation. The null
answer applies to takeover defence: nothing structural forecloses an
unsolicited approach, though size (~$248B) does. As a foreign private issuer
SAP files no insider Forms 3/4, so the routine US insider-transaction signal
does not exist for this name.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive — but only since 2024, and the
six years before it ran flat-to-negative.** From the audited 20-F (IFRS, €B):

| FY | revenue | operating income | op margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 27.553 | 4.473 | 16.2% |
| 2020 | 27.338 | 6.623 | 24.2% |
| 2021 | 26.953 | 6.308 | 23.4% |
| 2022 | 29.520 | 5.914 | 20.0% |
| 2023 | 31.207 | 5.799 | 18.6% |
| 2024 | 34.176 | 4.665 | 13.7% |
| 2025 | 36.800 | 9.617 | 26.1% |

Over 2019–2024 revenue rose **+24.0%** while operating income rose **+4.3%** —
the cloud transition consumed essentially all of six years of growth. FY25's
+106% step is real but comes off a 2024 base depressed by that year's
restructuring charge, and its 26.1% margin is only ~2pp above the 24.2% SAP
already printed in 2020 on 35% less revenue. TTM margin is 28.8% and still
rising. So the direction today is genuinely positive; the record says this
company has converted revenue growth into profit growth for two years, not
ten.
