## 2. Business

**Created:** Tesla sells electric vehicles that are cheaper to run and better to
drive software-wise than the alternatives, grid-scale batteries (Megapack) that
let utilities and now AI data centers buy firm power without building
generation, and a driver-assistance product (FSD) that ~55% of North American
buyers now pay for. Musk's framing on the Q2'26 call: buyers "are actually
buying Tesla Full Self-Driving with a car attached" — ~1.5M paid FSD customers
globally, 55% upfront / 45% subscription.

**Captured:** three distinct economics, not one. **Auto** (TTM $75.65B revenue,
$14.11B gross profit, 18.66% margin) is unit-margin on hardware plus a
high-incremental-margin software attach plus, until recently, near-100%-margin
regulatory credits — which fell to **$146M in Q2'26 from $439M a year earlier
(−67%)**. **Energy** (TTM $12.80B / $3.76B / 29.40%) is 12.4% of revenue but
**19.3% of gross profit** — the highest-margin business Tesla has. **Services**
(TTM $15.17B / $1.66B / 10.93%) is Supercharging, insurance, parts and used
cars, and its margin has climbed from 5.58% to 10.93% in five quarters.

**Protected:** the auto mechanism (cost position, charging network, brand) is
visibly eroding — gross margin 25.6% (FY22) → 18.85% (TTM). The claimed
autonomy mechanism is a data flywheel across ~8M customer cars that no rival
has; it is a real mechanism, but ten years in it has not produced a driverless
lead — Waymo got there first with HD maps, lidar and geofencing. The energy
mechanism is the most defensible thing Tesla owns today: manufacturing scale,
Autobidder dispatch software, and a US-domiciled cell supply chain in a tariff
regime. That is the one place where the moat is a mechanism visible in the
numbers rather than a label.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): strongly NEGATIVE.** Revenue $53.82B (FY21) →
$103.62B (TTM), +93%; operating income $6.52B → $4.37B, **−33%**. Against the
FY2022 peak: revenue **+27%**, operating income **−68%** ($13.66B → $4.37B).
Operating margin 16.76% → 4.22% (TTM) → **1.4%** in the Q2'26 quarter alone.
The mechanism is opex, not gross profit: TTM gross profit rose $16.90B → $19.53B
(+15.6%) over five quarters while TTM R&D + SG&A rose $9.83B → $14.76B (+50%).
R&D alone is $7.73B TTM, up 61%.

† E–G are the **steelman**: they normalize away the growth capex and (in G) part
of R&D, i.e. they grant the bull's own premise that current spending is
investment rather than deterioration. The steelman narrows the gap; it does not
close it. Row D is the load-bearing arithmetic: **to earn a market-like 10.6%/yr
from here, free cash flow must compound at 40% a year for a decade — $5.76B to
~$167B, more than Apple's entire current TTM FCF of $129.17B.**

Base-FCF honesty: $5.762B is already stale in the wrong direction. Q2'26 FCF was
**−$1.09B** and 2026 capex is guided above $25B against $18.69B of TTM operating
cash flow, so FY2026 FCF is very likely negative. The CLI refuses a non-positive
base (exit 2), so no negative-base scenario was run — but front-loading zero or
negative years can only *lower* every number in the table, never raise it. Rows
A–D are therefore ceilings, not central cases.

Margin-expansion caveat, applied in reverse: the skill's warning is that a flat
growth rate understates a business whose margin is mid-expansion. Tesla's
operating margin is **contracting** (16.76% → 4.22% → 1.4%), so the flat-path
runs if anything *overstate* the name. Row C front-loads growth anyway, to model
the bull's "the S-curve is ahead of us" claim explicitly.

Terminal-growth check: I could not read Item 1A directly (SEC and ir.tesla.com
both 403'd — §6). The dominant disclosed terminal risk, per secondary reporting
of the 2026 10-K's risk-factor changes, is the newly added Optimus
commercialization risk ("no guarantee this business will be successful") plus
the demand-for-autonomy dependency. A 3% terminal rate survives that only
because it is barely above long-run nominal GDP and is not itself carrying the
thesis — every row above is dominated by the explicit decade, not the tail. Row
D's 4% is the bull's rate and is *not* defended here; it exists to make row D
maximally generous.

## 5. Falsifiers

What would flip this pass to a buy, or make an owner sell:

1. **Quarterly** paid robotaxi miles — not the cumulative chart — resuming
   growth and reaching a disclosed weekly ride figure within an order of
   magnitude of Waymo's.
2. Capex guidance peaking and rolling over with FCF turning positive again, or
   the $30B facility going undrawn.
3. Operating margin re-crossing ~8% on a TTM basis (it is 4.22%).
4. Optimus shipping with a disclosed price, unit cost, or named external
   customer — any one of the three.
5. Energy gross margin holding ≥25% for two consecutive quarters at ≥15 GWh.
6. Regulatory credit revenue stabilizing rather than continuing to decay from
   $146M/quarter.
7. Sell-side inverse: a further 45–50% de-rating (toward ~$160–170) would make
   row B — 25%/yr FCF growth for a decade — pay roughly 10%/yr, at which point
   this is a different conversation.

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **Optimus unit economics.** No price, no unit cost, no named customer, no
   units shipped disclosed anywhere. Musk's own words: "There is no supply
   chain." Unverifiable by construction — no disclosure exists to attack. Its
   absence does not kill the *pass* (the pass does not need Optimus to fail) but
   it makes any bull thesis resting on it UNPROVEN rather than wrong.
2. **Whether $25B+/yr of capex converts to productive assets.** The assets are
   not built. Nothing observable today settles it. Note that FY2025 capex came in
   *below* the ">$9B" guide, so the ">$25B" figure is itself imprecise in both
   directions.
3. **The barbell objection to my own method.** A single-path DCF cannot express
   an option-like payoff. I have no way to bound the probability that autonomy
   works, and I do not pretend to. This is the strongest argument against the
   pass and it is unresolved.
4. **`data/*.db` not read this run.** Sandbox permissions denied `sqlite3`,
   `curl` and arbitrary Python execution. Consequently: no `composite.db` flag
   check (TSLA's composite ticker-grain signals are microstructure — short

   actuals against `v_screener.eps_diluted`; no `stocks.db` point-in-time
   capture; no `earnings.db` `event_time` confirmation (the 2026-10-21 date and

5. **SEC EDGAR and ir.tesla.com returned 403 to every fetch.** The FY2025 10-K
   Item 1A, the commitments-and-contingencies footnote, and the Q2'26 8-K
