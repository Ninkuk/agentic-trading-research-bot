## 2. Business

**Created:** Intel sells the general-purpose compute that every data centre
and most PCs still schedule work on. The customer gets an x86 CPU that runs
decades of software unchanged, now with core counts and memory bandwidth
sized for AI orchestration and inference; in PCs, a platform (Core Ultra
Series 3 on 18A) with manageability and security features the enterprise
buyer pays for. The surplus today is scarcity: Xeon is allocated, not sold,
and customers prepay for capacity (Q2 call).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (a) Intel Products sells CPUs at an
ASP per core — Q3 DCAI $7.3B at a 41.5% operating margin, CCPG $8.1B at
28.2% — and the per-core pricing rises mechanically with core counts (CFO,
Q2 call). (b) Intel Foundry charges Intel Products an internal transfer
price for wafers and packaging ($6.7B Q3 revenue, 94% of it intersegment,
at a −27.7% margin) and sells advanced packaging (EMIB-T) and, so far, very
little leading-edge wafer volume to outsiders. (c) Purpose-built silicon and
ASICs, a ~$2B run-rate the CFO expects to double (Q2 call), plus Mobileye
(consolidated, ~80% owned) and IMS. The US government paid $8.9B in CHIPS
cash and accelerated grants for 275M shares plus escrowed and warrant
shares; Nvidia paid $5.0B for 215M shares at $23.28; SoftBank $2.0B for 87M
at $23.00 (10-K).

**Protected:** the x86 installed base and software compatibility are a real
switching cost, but a shared one — AMD sells the same ISA from TSMC wafers
at better process nodes, and Arm-based hyperscaler CPUs now take share at
the top customers (10-K Item 1A names TSMC-fabbed competitors' process lead
as the reason Intel lost ground). The fab network is a capability almost no
one else has (the only US-owned leading-edge logic manufacturer), which is
why the US government, Nvidia and SoftBank own it — but a capability is not
a moat until it earns above its cost of capital, and ROIC is 3.4%
(stockanalysis). The honest answer: the Products business has a partial,
shrinking moat; the Foundry has none yet — its protection is political.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder. The US Department of Commerce holds ~275M shares outright plus the
unreleased Secure Enclave escrow (143M at Q2, released at $20 as $3.2B of
disbursements arrive), ~5.5% rising toward ~8%, and has agreed to vote with
the board's recommendation (10-K Item 1A). It also holds a warrant for 241M
shares at $20 that becomes exercisable if Intel ceases to own at least 51%
of Intel Foundry — at $74 that is a ~$13B penalty on any foundry separation,
so the structure forecloses the spin-off or sale-of-control path an activist
would push. Nvidia ~4.0%, SoftBank ~1.6%. The CEO bought 105,263 shares at
$95.00 in the August offering (Form 4, 2026-08-14).

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal ROE.* The model charges growth at the industry
  sales-to-capital ratio and lets the terminal earn 10% on reinvested
  capital — generous against a 3.4% current ROIC. `reverse_dcf` on the raw
  base was not run with `--base-earnings`: GAAP net income is −$11.3B on the
  escrow mark and non-GAAP earnings (~$6.9B TTM) exceed FCF, so the growth-
  without-reinvestment warning would not fire; the explicit model answers
  it instead.
- *Market share.* The hurdle-clearing path needs $167B of 2031 revenue, 2.1×
  Intel's 2021 all-time peak of $79B, at a 40% operating margin Intel last
  approached in 2020–21 as a monopolist on a node lead it no longer has.
- *Terminal risk (Item 1A).* The dominant structural risk is the one the
  10-K leads with: multi-year manufacturing and R&D bets "may not realize a
  favorable return", with TSMC-fabbed competitors benefiting from Intel's
  node delays. A 2.38% terminal rate survives only if Intel is still a
  leading-edge manufacturer at scale in 2036; the 18A ramp and 14A PDK are
  evidence it may be, and the 2013–2025 foundry-customer record is evidence
  the external leg may never arrive. The rate is kept because it is
  inflation, not real growth; the risk is carried in the margin path.
- *Distribution clamp.* Every path except the hurdle-clearing one sits
  below the 10th percentile of Damodaran's US cost-of-capital distribution
  (5.26%) or inside its lower half; the bull path at 8% is near the US
  median (7.79%) — a median return for a top-decile-risk execution story.
- *Cash tax.* GAAP tax 11.6% in Q3 with a $2.4B valuation allowance on US
  deferred tax assets (10-K); the 11% non-GAAP rate used is already a
  low-tax assumption, not a haircut.
