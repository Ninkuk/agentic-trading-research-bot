## 2. Business

**Created:** MGM sells occupancy of a physical place and the experiences
inside it — rooms, gaming floor, restaurants, shows, convention space —
across ~30 properties anchored by the Las Vegas Strip (Bellagio, MGM Grand,
Mandalay Bay, Luxor, Excalibur, Park MGM, New York-New York, Circus Circus),
regional US casinos (Borgata, MGM National Harbor, MGM Grand Detroit, Beau
Rivage, MGM Springfield), a ~56% controlling interest in MGM China (MGM Macau,
MGM Cotai), and MGM Digital (LeoVegas, BetMGM JV). The customer buys
proximity and occasion: the reason to book Bellagio rather than a hotel a
mile off-Strip is that the building is where the event, the restaurant and
the casino floor already are. For conventions, the product is a block of
thousands of rooms plus contiguous exhibit space that few places on earth can
supply at once — which is why group business is MGM's most defensible
segment and hit a record 20% room mix in Q2 2026.

**Captured:** not one business but at least five distinct take mechanisms.
(1) Gaming win — a statistical hold on wagered volume, the most profitable
and most luck-exposed line. (2) Room revenue at a nightly rate that flexes
with the city's event calendar; Q2 2026 Strip ADR was $242 at 93% occupancy.
(3) Food, beverage, retail and entertainment attach, sold to a guest already
inside the building. (4) Convention and banquet fees, contracted years ahead
at negotiated rates. (5) Digital — online casino and sports betting rake
through LeoVegas and the BetMGM joint venture, still lossmaking at −$31M
adjusted EBITDAR in Q2 2026. Macau is a sixth, structurally separate
business: a concession-licensed operator paid on GGR share, consolidated in
full while ~44% of its economics belong to minority holders.

**Protected:** the honest answer is narrower than the brand suggests. The
genuine mechanism is **licensure** — Nevada and state gaming licences and the
Macau concession are hard regulatory barriers that no competitor can simply
buy into, and MGM Rewards ties ~30 properties into one switching-cost
network. What is *not* a moat any more is the real estate. MGM sold its
Strip property to VICI and the Bellagio REIT venture and leases it back
triple-net; the irreplaceability of the Bellagio site now accrues to the
landlord, not to MGM's shareholders, who hold an operating margin on top of
a fixed, escalating rent. Meanwhile Las Vegas room supply is contested and
growing — the 10-K names competitors that "have either recently expanded
their hotel room capacity and convention space offerings, or have plans to
expand." Strip ADR fell 4% year over year in Q2 2026 at flat occupancy,
which is what a contested pricing environment looks like.

**Control:** one share, one vote; no controlling holder and no dual-class
structure. But the null answer is misleading here: People Incorporated
(formerly IAC) holds ~26.1%, Barry Diller sits on MGM's board, and People
Inc. has stated in a Schedule 13D/A that it has "no intention to sell our
existing ownership stake in MGM, or to pursue or vote in favor of any merger
or other similar extraordinary transaction that would result in a change in
control to another party." That is not legal control, but it forecloses an
auction and any unsolicited third-party takeover as a practical matter.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The takeover bid, and why the stock is below its pre-bid price.** On
  2026-06-01 People Inc. proposed $48.30/share in cash for the MGM it does
  not own, ~$18B including debt, filed as an exhibit to a Schedule 13D/A.
  The letter is explicit that it is "a non-binding expression of interest
  only," that "no legal obligation…will arise unless and until we have
  executed definitive transaction documentation," and that People Inc. may
  "withdraw or modify the proposal at any time…in our sole discretion." It
  carries no financing condition. MGM formed a special committee in
  mid-July. **As of this run there is no merger agreement, no SC 14D9, no
  proxy and no 8-K on EDGAR concerning the proposal** — the only MGM filings

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment warning, answered.* The tool fired `growth without
  reinvestment` (terminal reinvestment rate −1.8788, since FCF $1,226M far
  exceeds earnings $426M). The answer is the prescribed first response:
  terminal growth is set **at** the 10-year breakeven, i.e. nominal
  repricing of existing assets with zero real growth, which requires no
  reinvestment. No real-growth claim is made anywhere in this document.
- *Market-share sentence.* Scenario B compounds FY2025 revenue of $17.54B to
  ~$19.37B by year five. US commercial gaming GGR alone runs far larger, and
  that is before rooms, F&B, conventions, Macau and digital. There is no
  "bigger than the market" problem in this forecast.
- *Terminal risk vs terminal growth.* The 10-K's dominant structural risk is
  the triple-net structure itself: MGM must "pay a significant portion of our
  cash flows as rent," which "could…limit our ability to react to competitive
  and economic changes," alongside Macau concession termination or
  non-renewal (terminable "without compensating MGM Grand Paradise") and
  Strip concentration against expanding competitor room supply. A 2.33%
  terminal rate survives this only because it is pure inflation — roughly the
  rent escalator itself, so real margins are held flat, not expanding. Any
  rate above the breakeven would not survive it.
- *Excess-return base rate.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of
  capital (Damodaran EVA dataset), so fading excess returns is the default;
  nothing in this valuation assumes persistence.
- *Distribution clamp.* The US median cost of capital is ~7.79% with 80% of
  firms between 5.26% and 9.88%. Every scenario here implies **10.1%–16.4%**,
  i.e. *above* the top of the band. That is not a cheapness signal — it is the
  market demanding an unusually high return, which is the appropriate reading
  for a lease-levered, cyclically-exposed operator with EBITDA declining.
- *Cash-heavy bias, not taken.* Cash of $2.55B is 26.8% of market cap.
  Netting excess cash would *raise* the implied return; it was not done,
  because casino cage/operating float and MGM China cash are not clearly
  excess. The conservative direction, stated.
- *Serial-acquirer check, inverted.* MGM is a net divestor, so no
  acquisition-funded growth is being had for free. The reverse problem
  applies and is handled in scenarios C/D: TTM FCF still contains ~3 quarters
  of the divested Northfield Park.
- *Leverage gate — equity-option lens NOT run, with the reason.* Net
  financial debt is ~$3.52B ($6.07B long-term debt less $2.55B cash;
  principal $6,103.2M) against EV ~$13.03B = **27%**, well under the
  "roughly half" trigger; book equity is positive at $2.51B; there is no
  going-concern language. Including the $23.88B operating lease liability
  would put the ratio at ~74%, but that would be double-counting: the full
  cash rent is already deducted *inside* the levered FCF being discounted,
  so the leases are expensed in the flow rather than treated as a claim on
  it. **§1's ownership call is governed by the DCF frame.**
