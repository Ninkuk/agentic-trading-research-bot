## 2. Business

**Created:** mission-critical services and increasingly hardware for US
government
agencies — intelligence/digital modernization, VA disability exams at national
scale,
air-traffic and energy infrastructure engineering, and defense tech
(counter-UAS,
munitions, space sensor payloads). The customer gets cleared,
past-performance-proven
capacity it cannot staff itself: QTC's exam network alone is 15,000+ providers,
90
clinics, 2.8M exams/yr plus mobile units (call, primary-transcribed).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms by segment (Q2'26): Intelligence &
Digital
$1,499M rev / 9.5% OI margin (cost-plus and fixed-price services); Health
$1,086M /
23.4% (volume-based exam fees plus — until suspended — incentive fees); Homeland
$1,018M / 9.0% GAAP, 12.1% non-GAAP (air traffic + commercial energy
engineering,
ENTRUST); Defense $955M / 8.8% (services plus growing fixed-price hardware
production). Health is 24% of revenue but $254M of $572M segment OI — **44% of
segment operating income**. That concentration is the whole story of the stock.

**Protected:** for the exam franchise, physical capacity — a national provider
network a challenger must replicate at VA quality standards; for services,
clearances, incumbency, and past performance; for defense tech,
program-of-record
positions (IFPC, AGM-190A small cruise missile, LCCM framework >$1B, Golden Dome
sensor payloads). These are mechanisms, not labels — but none prevents the
*customer itself* from repricing or insourcing, which is the risk that halved
the
stock.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** FY21–TTM trajectory: revenue
$13.7B→$17.6B, op income $1.15B→$2.03B — positive leverage, FY23 impairment
dip recovered (stockanalysis income-statement history).
