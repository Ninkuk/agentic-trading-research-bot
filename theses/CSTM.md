## 2. Business

**Created:** an aircraft maker, a can maker or an automotive OEM gets
certified aluminum in the alloy, temper and gauge its platform was qualified
on, delivered from a regional mill on a multi-year contract, with the metal
price passed through so the customer carries the LME and the premium, not the
converter. Qualification is slow (NADCAP and OEM platform approvals;
"substantial time and investment and creates high switching costs", 10-K
Item 1) and the product is engineered — Airware Al-Li for airframes and
space, high-recycled-content can body stock, 6xxx body sheet. In the US the
customer also buys local supply inside a 50% Section 232 tariff wall; can
sheet imports hit 350kt by August 2026 only because Novelis's Oswego outage
left the market short (call, 2026-10-28).

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, and the second is the whole story
of 2026. (1) A conversion margin per ton — multi-year contracted in packaging
and automotive, repriced annually or on spot in aerospace and TID ("we are
going to reprice some of our aerospace contracts next year"). (2) A metal
management / recycling spread — the mill buys scrap (UBCs, process scrap) at
a discount to LME plus regional premium and sells product priced at LME plus
premium; the wider the premium and the cheaper the scrap, the bigger the
spread. Recycling is described by the CFO as the mechanism behind P&ARP's
"favorable metal costs" and the thing the company is "focused on making the
best out of" while conditions last. (3) Metal price lag — a non-cash timing
effect of inventory valuation that is +$288M in TTM Adjusted EBITDA (slides
p.20) and nets to zero over a cycle; it is why GAAP net income ($576M TTM)
and the 5.6x P/E overstate the cash business (ex-lag, after ~29% tax, net
income is roughly $370M and the P/E ~8.3x). Segment weights (FY2025 revenue /
Segment Adj EBITDA): P&ARP 60% / 49%, A&T 23% / 47%, AS&I 19% / 10%.

**Protected:** qualification and switching costs on the A&T and automotive
products (real, mechanism-grade); proprietary alloys and three R&D centres
($51M R&D in 2025); $2.46B of net PP&E in rolling and casting assets
(Ravenswood, Muscle Shoals, Issoire, Neuf-Brisach, Singen) that take years
and billions to replicate; an installed UBC recycling loop with can makers
and brand owners; and, for now, the tariff wall. Not protected: the
recycling spread itself — every US rolled-products competitor is adding
recycling and casting capacity, Novelis's Bay Minette greenfield and ADI were
named on the call as new US supply, and the Midwest premium is set by trade
policy, not by Constellium. Can sheet is a competitive commodity with two
customers at $943M and $852M of 2025 revenue (10-K Note 3.6; top ten = 56%).
The moat is real in A&T and thin in P&ARP, which is where the 2026 profit is.

**Control:** one ordinary share, one vote; no dual class, no controller.
Bpifrance group 9.2%, BlackRock 9.1%, FMR 7.8% (DEF 14A 2026-04-10).
Bpifrance keeps a binding board nomination while it holds ≥4%. French SE,
Paris registered office, US domestic filer (10-K), NYSE-listed. The
articles impose a mandatory bid at 30% of capital or votes (10-K Item 1A),
which forecloses a creeping takeover but not an offer; nothing forecloses an
activist. CEO Ingrid Joerg since 2026-01-01 (ex-COO, ex-A&T president);
Jean-Marc Germain is a paid advisor through 2026-12-31 (8-K 2025-10-29).

2021→2022 — the lever is metal and scrap spreads, not volume (shipments
