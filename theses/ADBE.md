## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $225.11.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=6, refuted=0,
unknown=3 (unverifiable).

The pass is not on valuation and not on business quality — it is on
**monitorability**. In Q1 FY2026 Adobe collapsed Digital Media, Digital
Experience and Publishing & Advertising into a **single reportable segment**,
so the Creative Cloud line that the entire bull/bear argument turns on is no
longer disclosed at any audited level. The thesis would be underwritten on a
condition I cannot observe and falsifiers that cannot fire.

The thesis attacked: Adobe is a 60%-ROIC, 89%-gross-margin, 41%-FCF-margin
franchise at 8.7x trailing FCF because the market prices generative-AI
disruption as near-certain while the company is without a permanent CEO or
CFO. At $225.11 the price implies roughly **11%/yr if FCF never grows
again**, and roughly **9%/yr if stock comp is charged as a full cash cost on
top of zero growth**. That floor is real. What is missing is any way to check
the one condition that decides it.

## 2. Business

**Created:** Two distinct jobs. For creative professionals, an end-to-end
ideation-to-production toolchain (Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere,
Lightroom) whose value is the depth of control and the interoperability of
the file formats a professional workflow is built on. For business
professionals and consumers, document work — Acrobat — where the value is
that the PDF is the format the rest of the world will accept.

**Captured:** Not "subscriptions." Three mechanisms: per-seat recurring
subscription to the professional apps; a freemium funnel that converts habit
into payment at a paywall (historically Acrobat Reader → paid Acrobat, now
being replicated for Express and Firefly); and enterprise contracts for
customer-experience orchestration (AEP, GenStudio, AEM), sold on a different
motion to a different buyer (the CMO, not the designer).

**Protected:** The honest mechanism is switching cost in a trained workforce
and in file-format lock-in, not a network effect: a studio's existing
projects, plugins, and hires are all Adobe-shaped. That mechanism is
weakening by management's own description — the call disclosed **deferring
Creative Cloud "line optimizations"** (price increases) while pushing traffic
to free tiers. Deferring a price increase is a disclosed concession on
pricing power, and it is the single most important sentence on the call.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): flat** — not deteriorating. Revenue +60%
since FY2021 ($15.79B → $25.20B) with operating margin 36.8% → 36.1%. The
FY2024 trough of 31.3% is the anomaly, not the trend.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The segment collapse (the finding).** 10-Q Note 15: three segments became
  one in Q1 FY2026, prior periods recast, CODM reviews consolidated results
  only. The note discloses revenue and expense lines for the whole company
  and nothing below it. Creative Cloud revenue is not disclosed at any level.
  ARR by "customer group" exists only in management's unaudited call
  commentary, and that grouping **bundles Creative Cloud with
  customer-experience orchestration and with Semrush** — so a decline in
  creative-professional seats could be masked by growth in marketing software
  and never appear.
- **Leadership vacuum — real, and larger than one departure.** Narayen
  announced on 2026-03-12 he will step down after 18 years, staying as Chair;
  the board formed a special committee under Frank Calderoni and is searching
  internally and externally for a CEO with AI expertise. CFO Dan Durn is
  departing, Steve Day interim. The 8-K filed 2026-07-17 approves a
  **retention letter** for the Chief Legal Officer whose protections "sunset
  12 months following the date the new Chief Executive Officer commences
  employment" — confirming no CEO had commenced as of that date, and that the
  board is paying to prevent attrition.
- **Noted, not chased.** A "loss contingency" of $30M in Q2 and $92M for six
  months appears in the segment note without characterization here, and a
  $70M goodwill impairment was taken on the publishing and advertising unit —
  an impairment on an acquired business while acquiring another (Semrush,
  $480M ARR).
- **The strongest short case.** Generative AI drives the marginal cost of
  content creation toward zero. Willingness to pay per seat erodes from the
  edges inward — prosumer, then SMB, then agency seat counts. Adobe's
  freemium pivot and deferred price increases are the concession that it can
  no longer convert traffic at the old price. It then removed the disclosure
  that would let outsiders watch this happen, is buying growth after
  impairing an earlier acquisition, and has no permanent CEO or CFO to own
  the outcome. On that reading 8.7x is not cheap — it is a correct price for
  an uncertain terminal value.
- **Options read (mandatory):** path 2 only (Robinhood stopgap) — path 1
  unreachable (not in the CBOE catalog; `options.db` below the depth gate).
  See §4's table.
- **Dead ends:**
  - **AI compute destroying gross margin.** Attacked and failed to land.
    Gross margin is flat at 88–89% across FY2021–TTM *including* the Firefly
    buildout; R&D/revenue rose only 16.1% → 17.8%; FCF margin 44% → 41%. The
    freemium push to 90M creative MAU accelerates in Q3 FY26, so the
    inference cost of serving free generative users is prospective and
    unmeasured — this is unresolved, not disproved.
  - **Buyback unsustainable.** Attacked, mostly failed. Repurchases ran at
    121% (FY24) and 115% (FY25) of FCF, funded by moving from net cash to
    **$1.45B net debt**. But TTM is back to 89% of FCF, which is
    sustainable, and $27B (~30% of market cap) remains authorized. The claim
    "continues near current pace" survives; "continues at the FY24–25 pace"
    would not.

**Terminal growth justification.** Adobe's own 10-K Item 1A names the
terminal risk: generative and agentic AI "enables users of all skill levels
to create," with "no assurance that our new or enhanced solutions and AI
innovations will be successful, adopted or monetizable." A terminal rate
above ~2% is not defensible against the company's own disclosed endgame risk,
which is why the **zero-growth row is the honest headline** rather than the
6% row.

## 5. Falsifiers

Stated, then undermined by the segment change — which is the reason for the
PASS:

- Organic ARR growth under 8% for two consecutive quarters *(ARR is a
  management-defined non-GAAP metric, not in the audited statements)*
- Creative & marketing professional subscription revenue declining YoY
  *(blended with experience orchestration and Semrush — a pure-creative
  decline can hide inside it)*
- Buyback pace materially cut below ~85% of FCF *(observable)*
- A new CEO abandoning the freemium strategy or announcing large dilutive M&A
  *(observable)*
- FCF margin below 35% *(observable)*
- Freemium MAU growing without ARR conversion by end FY2027 *(depends on
  management continuing to volunteer both numbers)*

Three of six depend on disclosures management defines and recently
restructured. That is the defect.

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **Creative-professional revenue and its trend.** Would come from the
   pre-FY2026 Digital Media segment disclosure, which was eliminated. Does
   its absence kill the thesis? **It is why the call is PASS** — not because
   the number is bad, but because it is unobservable.
2. **Freemium cohort conversion economics.** No cohort-level conversion,
   retention, or LTV disclosure exists for the new Express/Firefly funnels.
   Management asserts a 2027 payback; there is no way to check it.
3. **Whether paid creative software exists as a category in ten years.**
   Unfalsifiable today; Adobe's own 10-K declines to assure it.
4. **Who the CEO will be, and their capital-allocation policy.** Unknowable
   until named.
5. **Incremental inference cost of serving 90M free creative MAU.** Not
   separately disclosed; gross margin has not yet moved, but the strategy
   inflects in Q3 FY26.
6. **The $92M six-month loss contingency.** Not characterized in this run.
