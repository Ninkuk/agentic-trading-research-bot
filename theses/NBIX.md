## 2. Business

**Created:** Three first-in-class drugs where no prior standard of care
existed. INGREZZA (valbenazine, VMAT2) treats tardive dyskinesia and
Huntington's chorea — movement disorders caused mostly by the
antipsychotics patients cannot stop taking. CRENESSITY (crinecerfont, CRF1)
lets classic-CAH patients control androgens on a physiological
glucocorticoid dose instead of the supraphysiological steroids that were
the only option for 70 years. VYKAT XR (diazoxide choline) reduces
hyperphagia in Prader-Willi syndrome. In each case the customer surplus is
large and obvious.

**Captured:** Specialty pharmacy pricing against near-inelastic demand,
with the payer — not the patient — bearing the cost. Over 90% of CRENESSITY
patients pay ≤$10/mo (Q2 call, Benevich); the list price lands on Medicare
and commercial plans. That is the entire capture mechanism, and it is also
the vulnerability: a business whose customer does not see the price is a
business whose price is set by policy.

**Protected:** Composition-of-matter patents plus a first-mover data moat.
Gano's framing on the Q2 call is the honest version: CRENESSITY has "a
multiple-year head start," 2-year CATALYST data, 35,000+ patient-weeks of
exposure, and a label spanning age ranges a challenger must re-earn.
Management claims VYKAT's IP runs to the mid-2040s. **INGREZZA's patent
expiry date is not disclosed in any source I read — see §6.** The moat is
real but it is a clock, not a wall: every one of these protections expires
on a date, and for the 75% asset the price cut arrives before the patent
does.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive over five years, negative in the
last quarter.** Revenue $1,133.5M (2021) → $3,373.9M (TTM), 3.0x; operating
income $102.5M → $794.8M, 7.8x. Operating margin 9.0% → 23.6%. The Q2
reversal is deal-cost driven and should be transitory, but it has not yet
been shown to be.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The $2.9B VYKAT acquisition is the thread that matters — and it is not
  going well yet.** Q2 pro forma VYKAT sales were $94M, "roughly flat
  sequentially" (Ahmad's question, unchallenged by management). That is
  $376M annualized, so Neurocrine paid **7.7x run-rate sales for an asset
  that is not currently growing**, using ~85% of its cash. Management
  guides Q3 flat again and sequential growth only "exiting 2026." $2.9B is
  17% of market cap and 3.3 years of TTM FCF.
- **"Immediately accretive to non-GAAP earnings" is a near-empty claim
  here.** Non-GAAP excludes the $130M of acquisition costs recognized in Q2
  and the ~$20M of purchase accounting amortization — i.e. it excludes the
  cost of the acquisition. GAAP tells the other story: Q2 operating income
  fell to $151.5M on $959M revenue (15.8% margin) from $193.4M on $814.5M
  (23.7%) in Q1. GAAP EPS $1.39 vs non-GAAP $2.85.
- **Management refused to disclose the VYKAT discontinuation drivers when
  asked directly.** Amsellem asked what share of discontinuations are
  edema-driven; Gano: "I don't think we're going to get into the nature of
  the discontinuations." The forward claim (settling at 25–30%) is
  therefore unfalsifiable from disclosure.
- **The 2029 IRA price control on INGREZZA is confirmed, its magnitude is
  not.** Gano on the Q2 call: "our MFP to IRA outcome... those discussions
  will start next year... the MFP that will be applied to INGREZZA in
  2029." Asked what maintaining access costs, he declined: "too early to
  make a call on that." Abernethy tied EPS variability explicitly to "the
  impact of the IRA implementation in 2029."
- **The -10.1% single-day drop on 2026-07-31 has no disclosed cause.** The
  stock fell $185.50 → $166.80 on 4.58M shares (3–4x normal) the day after

  SPY rose 0.7% — so it is idiosyncratic, not sector. Every sell-side note

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **INGREZZA MFP magnitude (2029).** Does not exist yet — CMS negotiation
   begins 2027. Management explicitly declined to size it. This is the
   single largest driver of terminal value and it is unknowable today. Does
   not kill the thesis; it caps how confident anyone can be, including the
   bulls.
2. **VYKAT XR's actual discontinuation rate and its drivers.** Management
   refused the question on the record. Would come from a 10-Q/10-K
   disclosure or a future call. Load-bearing for whether $2.9B was well
   spent.
3. **CRENESSITY's terminal penetration.** The "15% penetrated" figure and
   the "30–50% peak" band are both management estimates against an
   undisclosed denominator (estimated diagnosed classic-CAH population). No
   independent source.
4. **Both phase III outcomes (osavampator, direclidine).** Domain science I
   cannot evaluate — AMPA potentiation in treatment-resistant MDD and
   selective M4 agonism in schizophrenia. Phase II results are encouraging
   per management; I cannot assess whether they translate. Bounded only by
   base rate: phase III failure in psychiatry is common. Marked UNKNOWN,
   counted as load-bearing.
5. **INGREZZA's patent expiry date.** Not found in any source read here;
   would come from the 10-K Item 1A / patent table or the FDA Orange Book.
   Material to terminal value.
6. **Management compensation structure.** Not read (proxy is a
   Quartr-hosted PDF). Relevant because the metric management led with —
   non-GAAP EPS — excludes acquisition costs by construction, which would
   reward deal-making if it is the comp metric. Unverified; flagged, not
   asserted.
7. **Balance-sheet reconciliation.** Management said "approximately $500
   million in cash and no debt"; stockanalysis shows cash $389.8M and total
   debt $461.6M (net cash −$71.8M). The gap is most likely
   investments/marketable securities on the cash side and capitalized
   leases on the debt side, but I did not confirm against the 10-Q. Not
   thesis-critical at this size — the company is not levered either way.
