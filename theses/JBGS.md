## 2. Business

**Created:** JBG SMITH owns 11.8M sf at share of multifamily, office and retail
concentrated in Metro-served submarkets in and around Washington, D.C., above
all National Landing (Crystal City / Pentagon City, Arlington). What the tenant
gets is location scarcity of a specific kind: walk-to-Metro, walk-to-Amazon-HQ2
space in a submarket where JBG SMITH owns enough contiguous land to control the
streetscape, plus a 3.5M sf development pipeline that lets it deliver
purpose-built space rather than retrofits. For residents the surplus is
proximity; for office tenants it was, until recently, adjacency to a growing
Amazon campus.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not one. (1) Multifamily rent —
annual leases, mark-to-market each renewal, the highest-quality and most
cap-rate-favoured NOI in the book. (2) Commercial rent — long office and
retail leases with fixed escalators, currently 75.4% occupied and 78.0% leased,
where the capture is contractual rather than market-linked until each rollover.
(3) Third-party real estate services — fee income for managing and developing
other owners' assets, a capital-light annuity that is small relative to the
other two. A fourth, unlabelled mechanism does most of the recent work:
**capital recycling** — selling stabilised assets and converting obsolete
office into multifamily (2200 Crystal Drive, 195 units, started May 2026), so
that the reported NOI mix migrates toward the higher-multiple leg.

**Protected:** the honest answer is that the protection is against the wrong
threat. The land assemblage in National Landing is a genuine mechanism — you
cannot replicate a contiguous multi-block position next to two Metro stations
without buying it from JBG SMITH — and it is a real barrier to *new supply*.
But the constraint on this business is not competitive entry, it is demand
absence: National Landing office vacancy is 25.7–28.7% across roughly 10.5M sf.
A moat against supply is worth very little in a submarket with a quarter of its
space empty. The multifamily book has no moat at all beyond location; apartments
are a commodity and the 86.6% occupancy says so.
