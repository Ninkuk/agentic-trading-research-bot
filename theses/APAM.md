## 2. Business

**Created:** the customer buys a specific investment team's judgment. Artisan
does not sell an index, a solution, or a brand — it sells access to 12
autonomous teams running 25 strategies, several of which are capacity-limited.
The claimed surplus is alpha: the 12 strategies with 10-year-plus records have
beaten their benchmarks by an average 189bp annually since inception, net of
fees, and 99% of AUM is ahead over 10 years (Q2 2026 call; the 3- and 5-year
figures — 86% and 77% — are stated *gross* of fees, which at a 68bp blended rate
is doing real work). What the client actually gets, then, is a well-run
concentrated active manager with a genuinely good long record. What the client
increasingly chooses instead is an index fund, and the 10-K says so in its own
words: "the trend in favor of low-fee passive products such as index and certain
exchange-traded funds favors those of our competitors who provide passive
investment strategies. That trend has presented, and likely will continue to
present, a headwind to our business."

**Captured:** a management fee on AUM, and essentially nothing else — only ~3%
of AUM carries a performance-fee arrangement (10-K). The capture mechanism
unpacks into three wrappers at three different rates, and the mix is the whole
story of the fee line: Artisan Funds and Artisan Global Funds at a 0.883%
weighted average (49% of AUM), traditional separate accounts at 0.482%,
Artisan-branded collective investment trusts at 0.699%, Artisan Private Funds at
0.773% (all FY2025, 10-K). The blended rate was 68bp in Q2 2026. There is no
second business here — no custody, no administration, no data, no distribution
fee income. Revenue is one exposure, sold four ways.

**Protected:** at the firm level, very little, and the filings admit it.
"We derive substantially all of our revenues from investment management
agreements, all of which are terminable by clients upon short or no notice…
all of our separate accounts and some of the mutual funds that we sub-advise
have the ability to re-allocate all or any portion of the assets that we manage
away from us at any time with little or no notice" (10-K, Item 1A). In 2026 that
happened twice at scale: two sub-advisory mandates ended the U.S. Value team
outright, and a third client is pulling ~$6B from Global Value in September.
Switching costs are near zero. What protection exists sits one level down, at
the team: a portfolio manager's record cannot be copied, and Artisan's actual
mechanism is retention of that person — autonomy over the process plus direct
equity in the firm through Artisan Partners Holdings LP units. That mechanism is
real and it works (the value teams have been in place for decades), but it
defends against *talent* leaving, not against *clients* leaving. The clients are
leaving. Call it: **no firm-level moat, a narrow and genuine talent-retention
mechanism.**

**Control:** three share classes, no controlling holder. Class A 70,996,433;
Class B 1,158,760; Class C 8,917,476 (10-Q cover, 2026-07-27) — Class B and C
are held by partners who own the economically equivalent limited-partnership
units in Artisan Partners Holdings LP, exchangeable into Class A, so 81.07M is
the fully-exchanged economic share count. A stockholders committee of Gottlieb
(CEO), Colson (Executive Chair) and Daley (CFO) holds an irrevocable voting
proxy over employee shares amounting to ~9% of aggregate voting power as of
2026-02-14 (10-K, Item 1A). Nine percent forecloses nothing — neither a takeover
nor an activist. The real anti-takeover device is that the assets walk out the
door if the investment teams do.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** across the cycle, positive only off
the 2023 trough. Revenue has round-tripped to a new high while operating income
sits 24% below its 2021 peak:

in our business model" (Q2 call). Both readings are true; the window you pick

back.

  that is itself shrinking.
- *Terminal growth vs the Item 1A terminal risk.* The 10-K names the endgame
  risk explicitly: "a continued shift away from actively managed equity
  strategies towards alternative, passive and smart beta strategies," with
  "approximately 88% of our AUM" in equity strategies at 2025-12-31. A 2.36%
  nominal terminal rate survives that only if the fee rate holds while the asset
  base is flat — two things the same disclosed trend argues against. Scenario C
  cuts terminal growth to 0% and the implied return falls to 5.96%; that is the
  honest bound, and the base-case use of 2.36% should be read as generous, not
  conservative.
- *Base-year cash taxes.* The GAAP effective rate of 43.2% on the statistics
  page is an artifact of the noncontrolling-interest deduction, not a real tax
  rate; the 10-Q shows a $44.7M provision on $213.1M of pretax income for H1
  2026, a 21.0% corporate rate on APAM Inc.'s share, with the LP unit holders
  bearing their own tax. No NOL or deferral flatters the base; no haircut.
- *Serial-acquirer check.* One acquisition ever (Grandview, January 2026, $22.5M
  cash net plus a $26.1M contingent earnout). The growth path here is organic
  and none of it is bought, so `fcf = NCFO − capex` is not smuggling in free
  M&A growth.
- *The company's own revenue sensitivity, as a cross-check on the growth
  paths.* Item 7A of the 10-K: a 10% move in AUM, proportionately distributed,
  moves annualized revenue by ~$124.7M at a 69bp weighted average fee rate; the

  scenario table corresponds to roughly a ±2–3% AUM path, and the −15.1%
  organic decay currently running would need essentially all of a normal
  market's drift to offset it. The 2% base-case growth is not conservative; it
  is the assumption that the leak stops getting worse.
- *Distribution clamp.* The implied returns of 5.96%–11.09% straddle the US
  median cost of capital of ~7.8% and sit inside the 5–10% band that holds ~80%
  of firms. So the pass is not the "below the bottom of the band, strong pass
  regardless of story" case — it rests entirely on the spread against APAM's own
  hurdle, and that spread is negative in every scenario at the beta the business
  actually has.

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy)**

- **Shift —** The Q3 print (2026-10-27) shows net client cash flows better than
  −$4.0B *excluding* the disclosed ~$6B Global Value rebalancing — an ongoing
  decay rate under ~8%/yr — with the blended fee rate at or above 68bp. That
  would make 2026's losses discrete rather than a trend and put condition 1 on
  probable evidence.
- **Shift —** Credit plus alternatives reaches ≥15% of firm AUM (from 11.1%)
  with organic growth still ≥15%, changing the mix fast enough to matter within
  the DCF's explicit horizon.
- **Shift —** Price at or below **$27**. At $26.50 (market cap ~$2.15B) the
  conservative owner-cash base (scenario B, $200.5M, +2%, 2.36% terminal) clears
  the 11.83% hurdle; $27 is the round number just above it.
- **Shift —** A defensible case that the equity beta belongs in the 0.8–1.2
  band — a sustained fall in the realized beta, or a mix shift into credit and
  alternatives large enough to decouple revenue from index levels. At the
  clamped 9.92% hurdle the unadjusted-TTM-FCF path clears by +95bp and this
  becomes a marginal hold rather than a pass. This is the one input on which
  the ownership call, rather than the business case, actually turns.
- **Break —** A strategic sale or merger of the firm at a premium — that removes
  the flow question rather than answering it, and the thesis would no longer be

**For an owner (sell)**

## Kill-thesis record

**Ledger:** conditions=5 refuted=0 unknown=0 pending=2 not_obtained=0 —
verdict **SOUND**.

The object of the attack is the bull case — own APAM at $37.92 and beat SPY over
63 trading days — not the pass. What the pass rests on is §4's price test, which
is not a condition; the five conditions below are necessary for the bull case
and none of them is sufficient against an 11.83% cost of equity, which is why a
SOUND label and a PASS call sit together here without contradiction.

Per-condition adjudication:

1. **Ex-one-off organic decay ≤ ~8%/yr** — **PENDING 2026-10-27**. Two attacks.
   First, the condition is stated at exactly the level the current data prints
   (−8.0% annualized ex-U.S.-Value for H1 2026): a threshold calibrated to be
   met rather than tested. Second and harder, the "ex-one-off" carve-out is
   internally inconsistent with condition 4 — the bull case cannot name
   53.5%-in-two-strategies as a live concentration risk and simultaneously
   exclude every realization of that risk from the run rate. Refuse the
   carve-out and the number is −15.1% annualized on a four-year deterioration
   (−3.2%, −2.5%, −7.9%, −15.1%), with a further −3.3% of AUM already committed
   to leave inside the very quarter being tested. It stops short of REFUTED
   because the carve-out dispute is definitional rather than factual and the
   decisive figure — a clean quarter with the U.S. Value book fully wound down —
   is dated. Genuinely pending, and this is the closest attack in the file.
2. **Blended fee rate ≥ ~68bp** — **SURVIVED** at *probable*. Attack: the rate
   rises only because the base is shrinking, so a "surviving" fee rate is a

   grazes: the 10-K puts the weighted average at 69bp as of 2025-12-31 against
   68bp in Q2 2026, and the CFO guided to the YTD rate (~67bp) as the run-rate,

   language — the $6B's revenue impact "expected to be less than the

3. **Credit + alternatives compound ≥15% and become material** —
