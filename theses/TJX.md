## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $131.31.** kill-thesis: **UNPROVEN** — conditions=6 (4 probable,
2 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

TJX is the largest off-price retailer in the world: 5,285 stores in ten
countries selling branded apparel and home goods at 20–60% below full-price
retail, bought opportunistically from ~21,000 vendors by 1,400 buyers. The
model has compounded revenue at 5.6% and operating income at 10.8% a year
for four years, earns a 30% ROIC on a lease-heavy balance sheet with net
financial cash, and returns ~$4.7B a year through buybacks and dividends.
The stock is down 22% from its June high because the core Marmaxx division
comped +1% in Q2 (HomeGoods +7%, Canada +6%, International +7%) and Q3 was
guided to a 2–3% comp with adjusted EPS below the Street. At $131.31 the
price still implies only a ~7.3% annual return on a base-case path (FCF
+7% fading to +6%, 2.5% terminal) against an 8.3% cost of equity — 102bp
short even after the drawdown, and the optimistic path (+9%/3% terminal)
only reaches −18bp. A superb business at a price that pays the owner less
than the cost of equity is a pass; the flip is ~$110 (base case at the
hurdle) or evidence that the Marmaxx wobble was the execution slip
management describes rather than the share loss Ross's +10% comp suggests.

**Closest attack:** the Marmaxx-is-self-inflicted claim. Management's only
evidence that Marmaxx (+1%) did not lose share to Ross (+10%, traffic-led,
same quarter) is an internal study that stores near and far from off-price
competitors comped identically — an unverifiable number. If Marmaxx's

60% of revenue, and condition 3 fails.

## 2. Business

**Created:** a treasure hunt. The customer gets branded and designer
apparel and home goods at 20–60% below full-price retail (10-K Item 1), in
an assortment that turns over so fast (inventory turns 5.6×) that the same
store is a different store next week. The surplus is real and visible —
the customer can price-check the brand elsewhere — which is why traffic is
word-of-mouth and TJX runs no item-and-price advertising (CEO, Q2 call).
The value is delivered in physical stores; e-commerce is a small add-on for
three US banners and TK Maxx.

**Captured:** one mechanism, merchandise margin, run through four segments.
Buyers acquire close-outs, overruns, cancelled orders and packaway goods at
prices that leave a ~31% gross margin (FY26 31.0% adjusted) after buying
and occupancy costs, against 19.5% SG&A, for an ~12% pretax margin. Segment
profit FY26 (10-K Note G): Marmaxx $5.53B on $36.6B sales (15.1%), HomeGoods
$1.25B on $10.2B (12.2%), Canada $0.76B on $5.6B (13.4%), International
$0.56B on $8.0B (7.0%). Product mix: apparel 44%, accessories/beauty 20%,
home 36%. A small credit-card program is under 1% of revenue.

**Protected:** scale on the buy side. TJX is the largest single buyer of
off-price goods, pays promptly, takes partial assortments in any quantity,
asks for no markdown or advertising allowances and never returns goods
(10-K Item 1) — which makes it the vendor's preferred outlet for excess
inventory and the reason 21,000 vendors keep showing up. The second layer
is the planning-and-allocation system that routes 1,400 buyers' purchases
across 5,285 stores, which a new entrant cannot buy and which competitors
(Ross, Burlington) have spent decades approximating. What it does not
protect against: the same vendors sell to Ross and Burlington, the goods
are commodity brands, and the Q2 Marmaxx miss shows the allocation engine
is a practice, not a guarantee.

**Control:** one class of common stock, par $1.00, NYSE (10-K cover). No
controlling holder: BlackRock 8.9%, Vanguard 8.44% (DEF 14A, 30 Apr 2026);
insiders 0.12% (stockanalysis). Delaware charter; nothing forecloses a
takeover or an activist, though at $144B nothing invites one either.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $48.55B → $60.37B
(FY22→FY26, +5.6%/yr) while operating income went $4.76B → $7.18B
(+10.8%/yr); operating margin 9.8% → 11.9%. TTM: revenue $62.36B, operating
income $7.58B (12.2%), net income $6.07B, of which ~$165M after tax is the
Q2 IEEPA tariff refund. Quarterly operating margin ex-refund is flat to
down (Q2 FY27 adjusted pretax 11.9% vs 11.4%; Q3/Q4 guided −20 to −40bp).
