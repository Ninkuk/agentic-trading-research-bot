## 2. Business

**Created:** Energys retrofits existing buildings — LED lighting and
fixtures, lighting controls and IoT (IntelliMesh, IntelliDim), boiler
optimisation, low-carbon heating, CHP, indoor air quality, energy monitoring
— so public and private UK organisations cut energy bills and CO2 (F-3,
2026-07-28). The customer surplus is the energy saving; "Save it Easy®"
packages the financing. Why customers pick Energys over any other
electrical contractor is not stated in anything read: the F-3 itself warns
of dependence on a limited number of customers and "no major recurring
revenue sources."

**Captured:** Project revenue — product supply plus installation and
programme management on a per-job basis. Procurement runs through a Hong
Kong subsidiary (New Vision Lighting), so part of the margin is a
sourcing spread on Chinese-made fixtures. Gross margin is 16–29% (25.4% H1
FY26, 29.7% H1 FY25) — contractor economics. Maintenance and monitoring are
listed but their revenue share is UNKNOWN. Since 2026-08-20 the group also
owns its delivery partners Cube Lighting and Design and Cube Solar
Installations (solar PV, canopies, battery storage), i.e. it insourced
installation labour.

**Protected:** No moat found. LED retrofit is a commoditised, tendered
installation service; the products are sourced, the brands are small, and
lumpy revenue (£10.3M → £5.0M → £6.0M → £9.6M → £6.9M) is what having no
recurring lock-in looks like. Silence on this question is the answer.

**Control:** Cayman Islands company. Since the April 2026 reclassification,
Class B and Series A Preferred carry 50 votes each; Michael Lau (co-founder,
executive director, Hong Kong) holds 9.65M Class B and ~1.16M Series A,
≈91.4% of votes (F-3) / ~95% per the June 2026 proxy, against ~19.5%
economic ownership. This forecloses any takeover or activist path and lets
the controller approve share authorisations unilaterally — he stated he
would vote for the 4.465B-share increase (EGM notice, 6-K 2026-08-18).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue is falling and losses
widen faster than revenue falls; H1 opex rose 66% (£1.27M → £2.11M) on a
28% revenue decline.
