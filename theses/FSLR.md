## 2. Business

**Created:** utility-scale thin-film CdTe modules, US-made. Customers get
the ITC domestic-content bonus (management: "$0.15–0.20/W or more" of
project value), insulation from Chinese-supply-chain risk
(FEOC/UFLPA/tariffs), hot-climate energy yield (temp coefficient,
degradation; CuRe adds up to 8% lifetime energy vs TOPCon), and fixed-price
multi-year supply certainty.

**Captured:** contracted backlog — 47.9 GW / $14.4B (~$0.30/W) through 2030,
substantially committed through 2028; new US bookings at $0.34–0.35/W; CuRe
technology adders (up to $0.6B potential, mostly 2027–28); Section 45X at
~17¢/W monetized via credit sales at ~95¢/$ ($1.34B sold in 2025); India
book-and-bill ~$0.20/W (highest ex-45X GM%, per CEO); nascent IP licensing
(Talon).

**Protected:** the only scaled non-Chinese, non-silicon module maker in the
US; 25 years of CdTe process knowledge (not purchasable turnkey, unlike
TOPCon lines); TOPCon patents asserted at the ITC against most large
importers. But the moat's strongest walls are **legislated** — 45X, FEOC
bars on Chinese-linked rivals, tariffs, domestic-content bonus — and what
Congress builds it can repeal. Ex-45X margin of ~7% is the honest measure of
the intrinsic (non-policy) moat's width today.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): strongly positive** — (FY, SEC
companyfacts): FY22 $2.62B rev / −$27M op income → FY23 $3.32B / $857M →
FY24 $4.21B / $1,394M → FY25 $5.22B / $1,597M. But FY25's $1.6B of 45X
credits sit inside cost of sales, so the entire swing is approximately the
subsidy ramp.
