## 1. Verdict and thesis

> **PASS at $205.76 (live).** kill-thesis: **FLAWED** — conditions=6
> (1 probable, 3 plausible, 1 possible, 1 unknown), refuted=1, unknown=1.

Bloom sells solid-oxide fuel-cell "Energy Servers" — natural-gas-fed on-site
power that permits faster and sits closer to load than a turbine — and in nine
months it went from its first direct hyperscaler order (Oracle) to being
approved by every major US hyperscaler. Revenue is doubling in 2026 to a guided

that Bloom keeps ~all of the fuel-cell share of a 30–40 GW/yr AI build-out and
grows into a ~$25B-revenue, 20%-FCF-margin company by 2031 — and that path only
just reaches the cost of equity. On base assumptions the price implies ~6%/yr.

## 2. Business

**Created:** the customer gets firm on-site power in months instead of the
years a grid interconnect or a gas-turbine order now takes, with a permit
that clears faster (no combustion: negligible NOx, negligible water, quieter
than HVAC), 800 V DC output, and modular "copy-exact" blocks that redeploy
between sites. For an AI operator whose 1 GW site earns $12–24B/yr, a month of
earlier power is worth $1–2B of revenue (CEO, Q2 call) — the surplus is
enormous relative to a fuel cell's premium over a turbine, which is why
customers who had ordered turbines and reciprocating engines cancelled and
switched (Nebius, Q2 2026). In C&I (hospitals, telecom, retail, campuses) the
value is resilience and a predictable power bill.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, from the 10-Q revenue table (Q2 2026):
(a) **product** — outright equipment sale, $935M, 88% of revenue, 36.5% GAAP
gross margin; the buyer is usually a financier (Brookfield vehicle, IDF,
AEP, formerly SK ecoplant) that owns the servers and sells power to the end
customer under a PPA, capacity agreement, or lease Bloom originated;
(b) **installation** — $51M, run at roughly zero margin; (c) **service** —
$69M, recognized ratably, 18.7% GAAP / 22% non-GAAP margin, 100% attached to
product, with ~$14B of contracted backlog (Q4 call) — this is the annuity;
(d) **electricity** — $10M, legacy Bloom-owned PPA fleet, shrinking. Financing
partners take title, so Bloom's revenue is largely cash-on-delivery from
institutional buyers (deferred revenue and customer deposits rose from $144M
to $445M in H1 2026 — customers are prepaying for slots).

**Protected:** the mechanism is a 20-year, 62-PhD materials-science lead in
solid-oxide cells (10-K) plus a manufacturing "copy-exact" model whose
factory ROI is "a few months" (CEO), giving Bloom the only fuel-cell product
at gigawatt scale today; the permit advantage over combustion is a regulatory
moat that holds as long as air-quality rules do. Two honest weaknesses: the
moat is against *other fuel cells*, not against turbines — GE Vernova, Siemens
Energy, Caterpillar and Wärtsilä are adding capacity and a hyperscaler with a
four-year turbine backlog will still take the turbine when it lands; and the
service annuity depends on stack life, which the company only recently got
to a 20% service margin after seven years of losses. Molten-carbonate and
other SOFC entrants exist; none is at scale (Bernstein question, Q2 call).

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controller. Class B ten-vote stock
converted to Class A on 2023-07-27 (10-K). Largest holders: Ameriprise/
Columbia 10.7%, BlackRock 7.9%, Vanguard 6.9%; founder-CEO KR Sridhar 2.0%;
all directors and officers 3.0% (DEF 14A, 2026-04-08). Nothing forecloses a
takeover or an activist; the only structural friction is the 0% convertible
make-whole and the Oracle warrant.
