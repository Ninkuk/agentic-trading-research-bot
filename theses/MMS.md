## 2. Business

**Created:** governments get the citizen-facing operations they are
legally required to run but cannot staff, scale, or modernize themselves:
a contact center that answers 1-800-MEDICARE and the federal marketplace,
eligibility and enrollment processing for Medicaid and SNAP, independent
clinical assessments and appeals, and the medical disability examinations
that turn a veteran's claim into a rated benefit. The agency buys
accountability — per-transaction or milestone pricing with measured
timeliness and accuracy — and surge capacity (open enrollment, disaster
response, Medicaid redeterminations) it would otherwise have to hire and
lay off (10-K Item 1; Q3 call).

**Captured:** (i) per-transaction and milestone fees on performance-based
contracts, 54% of revenue — an exam performed, a participant placed, a
case processed — with incentive/disincentive overlays for quality (10-K
Item 1; the VA incentives added ~$0.35/quarter of EPS in FY26);
(ii) fixed-price contracts, 13%; (iii) cost-plus and T&M for the balance,
mostly technology work. Mix: U.S. Federal Services 56% of FY25 revenue at
a 16.5–17% segment margin, U.S. Services (states) 32% at 9.5–10%, Outside
the U.S. 11% at breakeven (10-K; Q3 release). Gross margin 25.6%,
operating margin 11%, ROIC 13.3% (statistics probe).

**Protected:** the mechanism is incumbency in programs where switching
costs are political as much as operational — a contact center serving
tens of millions of beneficiaries cannot be re-bid without transition
risk the agency owns, which is why option periods cover "more than half"
of most contracts' potential duration (10-K) and why CMS withdrew its
2024 attempt to re-procure CCO early. Scale in clinician networks (the
VES provider network is a booked intangible) and a two-decade record of
protest-surviving rebids are the rest. It is not a moat against the
customer itself: the VA paused incentives unilaterally, agencies can
insource, and 75–80% of new bids now carry AI evaluation criteria that a
better-tooled rival could win on (Q3 call). No mechanism stops a
capable competitor at recompete; the protection is the cost of switching
mid-program, which is real and finite.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, 54.5M shares
entitled to vote at the March 2026 meeting; largest holders BlackRock and
Vanguard (13G/As); insiders 1.4% (DEF 14A 2026-01-21; statistics probe).
Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist. Pay is aligned with the
thesis: PSU metrics are organic revenue growth from FY24, adjusted EBITDA
margin and free cash flow (DEF 14A).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive on a lower base.** Revenue $4.25B
(FY21) → $4.63B → $4.90B → $5.31B → $5.43B (FY25) → $5.25B TTM; operating
income $418M → $315M → $295M → $488M → $528M → $579M TTM. Margin 9.8% →
11.0% while revenue slipped 3% — technology-led efficiency plus the FY26
incentive tailwind (income-statement route, fiscal years to 9/30).
