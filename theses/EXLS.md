## 2. Business

**Created:** EXL takes over the running of a regulated, data-heavy business
process — an insurer's claims intake and adjudication, a health plan's
payment-integrity review of claims before and after payment, a bank's
onboarding or collections — and returns it cheaper, faster and with fewer
errors than the client's own team, then sells the client analytics, data
modernisation and AI built on the workflow and data it already touches. The
customer's surplus is the labour arbitrage plus the outcomes EXL can
guarantee because it operates the process: the payment-integrity client at
Investor Day gets "more than $600M of annual saves" from claims EXL's
algorithms catch. In Q2 a national health plan benchmarked EXL's agentic
customer-facing module against a hyperscaler's and, per management, EXL won
on every measure — the mechanism claimed is that domain context and the
client's own data ontology matter more than the model (Q2 call,
transcribed). The 10-K says 25% of revenue rides on EXL's own platforms
(LifePRO policy administration, the population-health platform, the
payment-integrity algorithm library).

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms, not one. (i) Full-time-equivalent
and time-and-materials fees for operations staff — the legacy BPO stream,
39% of revenue as "digital operations", declining 1.5% reported in Q2 as
work migrates to (ii). (ii) Transaction- and outcome-based fees: a base
payment plus a share of the productivity or recovery gain — the
payment-integrity contingency model, and the form management says new AI
operations deals take ("we share in those gains"). (iii) Analytics and AI
services project fees, growing fastest on the smallest base. (iv) Platform
and subscription fees where clients run on EXL-owned software (LifePRO,
population health). (v) From August 2026, data-labelling, model-evaluation
and reinforcement-learning services to foundation-model labs via iMerit
(~$59M trailing revenue; guided $28–32M for the five months of 2026).
Gross margin 38.0% in Q2 (Healthcare 46.9%, the three others 34.6–35.1%);
adjusted operating margin 19.7%, GAAP 14.7%, the gap being SBC ($24.6M in
the quarter, up 50% YoY) and $3.3M of acquired-intangible amortization.

**Protected:** switching cost and accumulated context, not lock-in. The
contracts are terminable without cause and carry no volume commitment
(10-K, Item 1A). What stops a competitor tomorrow is that EXL already runs
the process, holds the client's data ontology, and has a 20-year record in
a regulated workflow the client cannot hand to an untested vendor without
compliance risk; the payment-integrity library (8,000 algorithms, applied
to a new client's claims from day one) is the closest thing to a product
moat. The 10-K's own competitor list is honest about the threat from every
side: Cognizant, Genpact, Infosys, TCS, NTT DATA; Cotiviti and Optum in
payment integrity; Guidewire, Salesforce and other software owners of the
data; Accenture and Deloitte; and the client's own captive centre. The
Item 1A structural risk is that clients "develop their own internal AI
technology, or acquire such technology from third parties", which reduces
demand for what EXL sells. Management's rebuttal is a 94% AI-deployment
success rate against an industry ~30% (Citi, transcribed; unaudited,
undefined) — a claim, not a mechanism. Verdict: a real but ordinary
services moat, worth the 24% ROIC it earns today and not obviously more.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder (10-K note 19). Chairman and CEO Rohit Kapoor, a co-founder, owns
2.56% (2026 proxy, as of 2026-03-25); all insiders 3.36% (stockanalysis).
Lead independent director Vikram Pandit hands to Sarah Williamson by
year-end 2026. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist; the
$1B senior secured facility (2026-08-18; $400M term loan, $600M revolver,
5-year, 3.25× net-leverage incremental test) is the only structural
constraint on a buyer.
