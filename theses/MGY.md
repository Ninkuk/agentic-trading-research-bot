## 2. Business

**Created:** barrels of oil, natural gas and NGLs (Q2'26 revenue 78% oil, 14%
NGL, 8% gas) delivered to Gulf Coast markets at Magellan East Houston pricing
less a ~$3 differential. The customer buys a commodity; nothing about a
Magnolia barrel is preferred. The value created is in the *cost* of finding
and lifting it: Magnolia took the Giddings field — a 1970s–80s Austin Chalk
play written off as depleted — and applied modern horizontal drilling and
completion to cheap, contiguous acreage, producing at $5.01/boe LOE, $11.55/boe
all-in cash cost, $12.07/boe DD&A and under $1,100 per lateral foot, with a
53-net-well, zero-dry-hole 2025 program. Q2'26 pre-tax adjusted operating
margin was $25.15/boe, 51% of revenue, at a $98.13 realized oil price.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. (i) Oil sales at MEH − ~$3
(Q2'26: $373.8M of $478.8M revenue), unhedged on the legacy book — every dollar
of price flows through, both ways; the 10-Q sensitivity is $14.9M of annual
revenue per $1/bbl on the standalone book, ~$27M pro forma. (ii) Gas and NGL
sales (Q2'26 $105M), gas at $2.18/Mcf — a weak leg that the 50/50 oil mix
after WildFire shrinks. (iii) The capital-allocation rule that turns the
margin into owner cash: D&C capped at 55% of adjusted EBITDAX (34% in Q2'26),
the remainder paid out as a $0.72 dividend and at least 1% of shares
repurchased per quarter (85.5M shares retired since 2019; $2B returned,
~40% of the pre-deal market value). WildFire adds an owned sand mine
supplying ~80% of the combined sand demand and 500 miles of gas gathering —
cost capture, plus third-party sand sales.

**Protected:** there is no moat in the product. The protection, such as it
is, is three mechanisms: (i) a cost position rooted in acreage bought before
the field was re-rated (pre-2018 EnerVest legacy, then $1B+ of bolt-ons),
shallower Eagle Ford wells on the acquired acreage, and owned sand and
gathering; (ii) contiguity at a scale no competitor can now assemble — ~1.25M
net acres, ~80% larger than the second-largest Eagle Ford/Austin Chalk
position, with ~70% acreage adjacency enabling 10,000–15,000 ft laterals;
(iii) eight years of subsurface knowledge of a naturally fractured, variable
Chalk reservoir that skeptical research shops have only recently credited.
The honest limit: (i) and (ii) protect the cost of the *next* well, not the
price of the barrel, and "capital discipline" is a policy, not a moat.

**Control:** one class. Class A common, one vote per share, no cumulative
voting; the Class B/Magnolia LLC unit structure ended 2026-02-12 when the
EnerVest members redeemed their last 4.8M units and sold (10-Q, proxy). No
controlling holder: BlackRock, Vanguard and American Century are the largest
holders; WildFire Energy I LLC (Warburg Pincus / Kayne Anderson) received
32,203,000 shares (~12%) at closing, filed a passive 13G and Form 3 on
2026-09-16, and holds resale registration rights under a 30-day lock-up from
2026-09-10. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist. Chairman and CEO
roles were combined in July 2025 (Stavros); Steve Chazen, the founding CEO,
is deceased.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative on the 2021→2025 and 2022→2025
spans, positive on the trailing rebound.** Fiscal-year revenue / operating
income ($M, stockanalysis income statement): 2021 1,078 / 599 (55.6%) ·
2022 1,694 / 1,074 (63.4%) · 2023 1,227 / 534 (43.6%) · 2024 1,316 / 512
(38.9%) · 2025 1,312 / 439 (33.5%) · TTM to Q2'26 1,480 / 566 (38.2%).
Revenue rose 22% from 2021 to 2025 while operating income fell 27%; the
mechanism is price (WTI $94 in 2022 to $66 in 2025) plus DD&A per boe
climbing from ~$8 to ~$12 as Giddings growth wells replaced the legacy Karnes
PDP, not cash-cost inflation (cash costs per boe flat near $11.5). This is
what a commodity producer's income statement looks like; free cash flow held
in a $409–552M band through the whole span.
