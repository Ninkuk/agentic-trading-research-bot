## 2. Business

**Created.** FactSet collects, cleans, concords, and delivers financial data
(fundamentals, estimates, ownership, deals, private markets, real-time
pricing) into the daily workflows of ~247,000 investment professionals at
9,100+ institutions — 95 of the top 100 asset managers, >85% of the top 50
banks (Q3 FY26 press release). The customer gets audited, entitled,
compliance-ready data with a service layer, cheaper than building it. The CGS
subsidiary (acquired 2022, $1.9B) *issues CUSIP identifiers* — infrastructure
the industry cannot transact without.

**Captured.** Not "subscriptions" generically — three mechanisms: (1)
seat-linked workstation subscriptions, historically the core; (2) enterprise
agreements with **contractual minimum commitments plus consumption tiers**
(the majority of Q3 renewals; terms extended ~30% with, management asserts, no
price compression) covering feeds, APIs, Snowflake/Databricks shares, and now
MCP access; (3) CGS licensing fees on CUSIP usage. ASV $2.486B, +7.1%
organic — the fifth consecutive quarter of acceleration, fastest since Q1
FY24 (Q3 call).

**Protected.** The mechanisms, not labels: decades of point-in-time history
and entity concordance that a rival must rebuild year by year; workflows
embedded in clients' compliance/audit chains (switching is a project, not a
purchase); the CGS franchise, which is a de facto monopoly on US security
identifiers; and — the AI-era version — *licensing rights*: an LLM answering a
portfolio question still needs entitled, auditable market data, and FactSet is
positioning MCP as that toll booth (450+ clients engaged, API volume 13×
quarter-over-quarter, >20% of top-100 clients paying — Q3 call). The honest
weakness: much underlying data is commodity at the source, Bloomberg/LSEG/SPGI
are larger, and FDS ultimately sells to *people* — if AI shrinks analyst
headcount, seat demand shrinks with it. Management's counter is the
seat-to-enterprise contract shift; §5 tracks it as a condition, not a fact.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative (TTM), resolved as deliberate.**
Revenue $2,438M > FY25's $2,322M while operating income $721M < FY25's
$748M; FY21→FY25 margin had climbed 29.8%→32.2%. Q3 adj. margin 34% vs ~37%
a year ago — AI/infrastructure investment, token costs, ASV-linked incentive
comp, and a 60bp FX-hedge swing (CFO, Q3 call). FY26 guide holds 34.0–35.5%
adjusted. The GAAP picture is worse (Q3 26.7% vs 33.2% prior year; FY26
GAAP guide 29.5–31.0%) — the gap is CUSIP-deal amortization plus
restructuring/severance from the ~10% technology-workforce reduction.
