## 2. Business

**Created:** MSA sells the equipment that stands between a worker and a
fatality — self-contained breathing apparatus and turnout gear for firefighters,
fixed and portable gas detection for refineries, chemical plants, mines and
utilities, and head, fall and ballistic protection for industrial and defence
users. It protects "over 40 million workers annually" (CEO, Jefferies
2026-09-10). The customer's preference being satisfied is not price but
*certainty*: an SCBA that fails once ends a career and a lawsuit, so the buyer
pays for a brand whose failure rate is known, whose service network is local,
and whose product is certified to the standard the buyer's insurer and regulator
demand. The surplus is large and asymmetric — the cost of the equipment is
trivial against the cost of the event it prevents, which is why a Type 2 helmet
sells at "3x+" the price of a Type 1 and departments still switch (CEO, Jefferies
2026-09-10).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one.
*One* — durable-equipment sale at premium price points into fleet-standardised
buyers (SCBA to fire departments, helmets to industrial sites); 2025 Fire
Service $647.5M (34% of revenue), Industrial PPE $463.9M (25%).
*Two* — a replacement stream on hardware with a defined service life: SCBA on a
13–14 year cycle, fixed gas detection sensors on ~12 years, which converts an
installed base into revenue without a new sale (CEO, Jefferies 2026-09-10).
*Three* — a subscription: MSA+ connected portable detection, now 14% of portable
sales versus 10% a year ago, sold on a four-to-five year term at a "significantly
higher" price point than the discrete device and margin-accretive (CEO, Jefferies
2026-09-10).
*Four* — project revenue on new-build capacity, 15–20% of fixed monitoring,
where Autronica's fire-detection line now lets MSA specify earlier in the EPC
design cycle.
2025 Detection was $763.4M (41%) and is the largest category post-Autronica.

**Protected:** the mechanism is certification plus fleet standardisation, and it
has a visible crack. To sell an SCBA in the US a competitor must clear the NFPA
standard per SKU, and to sell gas detection into a hazardous area it must clear
ATEX/IECEx — capital and calendar, not merely capability. A fire department then
standardises its whole fleet, and often its mutual-aid partners', on one brand,
so switching is a fleet-wide decision taken once every 13–14 years rather than a
purchase. MSA+ adds contractual lock-in on the connected portables. The crack:
certification is a one-time toll and the competition has now paid it — "all
competitors have approval for the NFPA standard update, that is something that's
in the rearview mirror" (CEO, Q2 call), and Dräger has a new US product "at a
price point much closer to MSA" with a new MES distribution relationship (put to
management by the Jefferies analyst, 2026-09-10; management did not dispute the
premise). Management's own answer to the competitive question was "customer
intimacy" and "the best talent in the industry" — those are labels, not
mechanisms, and they are what a company reaches for when the structural barrier
has been cleared.

**Control:** one class of common stock, no par value, 62,081,391 shares issued
and 38,912,629 outstanding at 2025-12-31, one share one vote; plus a small legacy
4.5% cumulative preferred ($50 par, $3,569k carrying value) paying $41k a year.
No controlling holder — insiders hold 6.13%, institutions 93.35%, and the float
is 93.8% of shares out. The structure forecloses nothing: an unsolicited
takeover or an activist path is open, subject only to Pennsylvania's statutory
anti-takeover regime, which is among the more defendant-friendly in the US.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The legacy mass tort is genuinely gone, and that is the most important fact
about this company's balance sheet.** On 2023-01-05 MSA divested Mine Safety
Appliances Company, LLC — the subsidiary holding every legacy coal-dust,
asbestos and silica cumulative-trauma claim — to a joint venture of R&Q
Insurance Holdings and Obra Capital, contributing $341.2M of cash against the
Purchaser's $35.0M and taking a $199.6M tax-effected loss including $5.6M of
transaction costs (10-K Note 21). All legacy cumulative-trauma reserves,
insurance assets and associated deferred tax assets were derecognised; the
Purchaser and MSA LLC indemnify MSA "not subject to any cap or time limitation,"
and the board took a solvency opinion. This is why 2023 FCF was $50.1M against
$242.2M the next year — the anomaly is a one-time cash exit from a liability,
not a business failure. **The residual thread is counterparty risk**, which the
10-K names explicitly: "the ability of MSA LLC and the Purchaser to honor their
indemnity obligations is subject to commercial risk." R&Q's own financial
condition is not disclosed in any MSA filing and was not verified this run.

**The tort MSA still owns is Globe's, and it carries no reserve.** Globe
(turnout gear) is a defendant in 1,222 lawsuits comprising 21,372 claims as of
2026-07-27 (10-Q), alleging harm from PFAS in the technical fabrics Globe buys
from "a small pool of specialty textile manufacturers." The count is flat-to-down
from 1,288 lawsuits / 22,090 claims at 2026-02-04 (10-K) — mildly reassuring on
trajectory. But MSA books **no loss reserve**: defence costs are expensed as
incurred and the matters are "at a very early stage." Under ASC 450 that means
management judges a loss neither probable nor reasonably estimable; it is not
evidence the expected value is small. This company paid $341M to divest its last
mass tort.
