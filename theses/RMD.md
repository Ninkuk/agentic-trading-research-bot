## 2. Business

**Created:** a patient with obstructive sleep apnea stops choking awake dozens
of times an hour. ResMed sells the flow generator (AirSense 11, AirCurve 11)
that holds the airway open, the mask that connects it to the face, and the
software that proves to a payor that the patient actually used it. What the
customer gets that they cannot get elsewhere is a device that is quiet and
small enough to be tolerated overnight — adherence, not efficacy, is the
binding constraint in this disease, and ResMed's own data has the AirTouch
N30i mask delivering 6% higher 90-day compliance than its silicone
equivalent. There are three customers, not one: the patient wears it, the
prescribing physician chooses it, and the home medical equipment (HME/DME)
provider buys it and bills the payor.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one. (i) A one-time device sale
into the HME channel, $2,892.2M TTM. (ii) A recurring mask-and-consumables
annuity resupplied every few months against the same prescription,
$2,085.6M TTM and growing faster than devices. (iii) Residential Care
Software — Brightree (the HME's ERP and billing system) and MEDIFOX DAN
(German home-care software) — $675.8M in FY26, of which the ~$220M MatrixCare
line was divested on 2026-09-01, leaving ~$456M. (iv) Newly, adjacent
device categories bought in: VirtuOx (home sleep-apnea testing) and Noctrix
(FDA De Novo neurostimulation for restless legs syndrome, closed 2026-06-01).
Geographically, revenue splits Americas and rest-of-world across 140
countries; FY26 group revenue was $5,653.4M.

**Protected:** the mechanism is real and it operates on the provider, not the
patient. Medicare will not pay an HME for a CPAP unless the patient
demonstrates 90-day adherence, and AirView is the compliance-reporting system
of record that generates that proof. Brightree is the same provider's billing
ERP. So ResMed sells the device to a customer whose revenue-cycle software and
whose payment-qualifying evidence it also owns — switching devices means
re-plumbing the workflow that gets the provider paid. On the patient side the
mask is prescription-fitted and patient-specific, so a switch means a refit,
which makes the consumable annuity stickier than the hardware. The honest
limit: this mechanism defends ResMed's *share of the treated OSA population*
and defends nothing whatever against that population shrinking. The moat and
the dominant risk are orthogonal, which is why a 26% ROIC and a 9.19%
short interest coexist without either party being irrational.

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.004 par, 143.06M shares
outstanding; 2,000,000 preferred shares authorized and none issued (a
blank-check tranche the board could use defensively). Insiders hold 0.522%,
institutions 78.84%, and the float is 99.4% of shares out — there is no
controlling holder and no dual-class structure. Founder Peter Farrell is
chairman emeritus; his son Mick Farrell is Chairman and CEO and held 466,256
shares directly after a 2026-09-08 Form 4 option exercise-and-sell. The 10-K
carries its own risk factor that "Delaware law and provisions in our charter
could make it difficult for another company to acquire us," so an unsolicited
takeover is impeded by charter and DGCL §203 rather than by a voting block;
an activist path is open in principle given the dispersed register.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The Astral field safety action, and whether it is one year or more.** In Q4
FY26 ResMed took a $42M provision for a field safety notice on Astral
life-support ventilators and suspended all Astral sales for the whole of FY27
— a $75M revenue and $0.15 EPS headwind, 130bp of the FY27 growth rate. The
damage is already visible: Q4 life-support device revenue fell 45% in the
Americas and 38% in rest-of-world. Two things make this bigger than the
headline. First, the CFO's own characterisation — "a higher cost than what
we've seen on a prior Astral accrual, primarily just because it's impacting a
higher population of devices out in the field based off of the failure mode
that we have." A component failure mode across a large installed base of
life-support devices is the category where scope expands. Second, ResMed is
routing all PCBA and electronic-component supply to remediation rather than
new units, prioritising "the sickest and most severe patient first" — which
means the constraint is a component shortage with no dated resolution.
Management declined to commit for FY28: "We haven't made any decisions for
fiscal 2028 or beyond as yet." The comparison that matters is Philips's own
Respironics recall, which began as a foam-remediation estimate and ended in a
consent decree with revenue disgorgement.

**Whether the GLP-1 evidence answers the question it is offered for.** It does
not, and this is the central thread. ResMed's headline evidence is a claims
cohort of more than 2.5 million de-identified patients showing that patients
with scripts for *both* PAP and a GLP-1 are ~11% more likely to start PAP, 3%
more likely to resupply at one year and 6% at three years. That comparison is
drawn entirely *within the already-diagnosed, already-prescribed population*.
A patient who loses 20% of body weight and never develops symptomatic OSA
never enters either arm — so the study is selected on the very outcome at
risk. It is also unadjusted for health-engagement confounding: a patient
motivated enough to be on a GLP-1 is more adherent to everything, and effect
sizes of 3–11% are squarely inside the range a confound produces. The second
datapoint offered — that at 90 days after a positive OSA diagnosis more than
40% of patients have started PAP versus under 3% starting a GLP-1 — measures
*sequencing*, not substitution; a positive OSA diagnosis is a PAP indication,
while a GLP-1 for OSA needs a separate indication and prior authorisation.
Neither speaks to the state of the funnel in five years, which is what the
terminal value rests on. And ResMed's own 10-K says the opposite of the call:
Item 1A states that GLP-1 drugs "may lower the occurrence of obesity,
eventually reducing the severity of OSA, if significant weight loss is
maintained" and that "oral versions of these drugs are also under development
and may be approved for the treatment of OSA." The CEO's framing and the
filing's framing are not reconcilable as statements of expectation.

**Looking for a disclosed metric that would separate new-patient formation
from resupply — there is none.** If the funnel were narrowing, the first
signature would be a mix shift: fewer new device setups, a fatter resupply
book. ResMed does not disclose new-patient starts, patients on therapy, or
cloud-connected device counts. The `/stocks/RMD/metrics/` route carries only
revenue splits. The best available proxy is device versus mask revenue, and
it is ambiguous by construction:

## Kill-thesis record

**Internal-consistency check (run first, costs nothing).** The bull case
asserts 6–8% growth, a 26% ROIC moat, and — implicitly, by using unadjusted
FCF — near-zero reinvestment. Those three cannot all hold: g = reinvestment
rate × return on capital. ResMed does in fact reinvest, but through
acquisitions ($350.7M in FY26), which `fcf = NCFO − capex` does not charge.
The inconsistency is therefore real and quantified rather than merely noted:
scenario J charges it and the implied return falls from 8.41% to 7.61%. This
attack does not refute any single condition; it is the foundation of the PASS.

**Per-condition adjudication.**

1. *Core cc growth sustains ~6–8%* — **PENDING 2027-01-28.** Attacked with
   what bears on it today: the FY27 core guide is 5–7%, whose midpoint sits at
   the bottom of the claimed range; the 19-analyst three-year consensus is
   5.30%; the candidate-screen record carries FY consensus revenue growth of
   2.78% and FY2 of 6.52%. FY26's 8% cc was earned in a market Philips was
   absent from, and management's own framing ("with our share, we basically
   create the market growth") means ResMed's growth *is* roughly the market's
   mid-to-high-single-digit rate, so 8% requires taking share from the smaller
   entrants the 10-K says the Philips ban helped. Today's evidence stands
   against the *upper* half of the range but not against the condition as
   stated, so this is pending, not refuted. The Q2 FY27 print is the earliest
   filing at which the annual guide is revised or affirmed.
2. *Margin expansion plus capital return outpaces revenue* — **PENDING
   2027-08-05.** This is where the attack came closest. FY26 FCF fell 0.7%
   ($1,661.7M → $1,649.5M) while revenue rose 10% and operating income rose
   13.8%; per-share FCF grew ~0.4% against 10% revenue growth. Capex rose 74%
   ($89.9M → $156.3M) and is guided higher again ($160–180M); working capital
   consumed $74.2M; the $42M Astral accrual's cash follows in FY27. A third of
   the FY27 buyback ($450M of $1.5B) is funded by non-repeating divestiture
   proceeds, and total FY27 capital return of $1.85B already exceeds FY26 FCF,
   so the lever is at its ceiling. And the gross-margin engine is publicly
   spent: 290bp in FY26 and ~600bp over two years falling to "low double-digit"
   basis points guided, with the CFO stating "we can no longer offset inflation
   with productivity alone" and Q1 FY27 guided to year-over-year gross-margin
   contraction. What saves it from REFUTED: the FY24→FY26 two-year CAGR is FCF
   +12.6%/yr against revenue +9.8%/yr, FY25's capex was abnormally low at 1.7%

   operating margin percentage." A one-year miss inside a two-year beat is a
   dispute, not a refutation, and under the uncertainty rule it is pending.

**Standing checks.** *Base rate* — ~29% of firms earn above their cost of
capital (Damodaran EVA), so the persistence of ResMed's 26.2% ROIC through the
terminal period, which every scenario assumes, is a 3-in-10 proposition before
evidence; separately, mean reversion in growth rates says the modal path after
a step-down from 8% cc to a 5–7% guide is further deceleration, not
re-acceleration. *Short case* — ResMed is a single-indication device company
whose entire installed base and resupply annuity rests on OSA remaining a
chronic, device-treated condition; Zepbound is already FDA-approved for
moderate-to-severe OSA in obese adults and orals are in development for the
same indication. The short does not need CPAP to disappear, only for
incremental patient formation to slow, because a 200bp reduction in new
formation compounds into the terminal value far more than into the next two
years — which is exactly how a stock falls 20% in a year while every quarterly
print beats. Meanwhile the two engines of FY24–FY26 (Philips's absence, the
post-COVID refill and margin-recovery cycle) are both non-repeating. At
$218.27 you are paid your cost of equity to hold that, and 9.19% of float is
short with 8.34 days to cover. I could not dismantle this. *Management
incentives* — Mick Farrell is heavily aligned by ownership (466,256 shares),
which cuts the other way; but he has framed GLP-1s as a tailwind at every call
for three years, and the move to annual-only guidance with an explicit refusal
to guide quarterly reduces mark-to-market frequency in precisely the year the
growth rate steps down, alongside a new CFO and a new auditor. Disclosed and
individually benign; collectively it makes FY27 the least comparable year in a
decade. *Disconfirming search* — searched for the bear case and for Philips's
re-entry status, not for support; the 10-K's own Item 1A turned out to be the
strongest disconfirming document, which is why it is quoted against the call.
*Moat mechanism, not checkbox* — AirView is the Medicare 90-day adherence
system of record and Brightree is the HME's billing ERP, so the switching cost
lands on the provider who has to keep getting paid; masks are prescription-
fitted, so a switch means a refit. That is a mechanism. Its limit is that it
defends share of the treated population and nothing against the population
shrinking.

**Statistical checks** (the thesis rests on RMD's claims study). Base rate is
not 0.5 and is not even tested — the comparison is co-prescribed versus
PAP-only, both already prescribed, so the relevant null (who enters the funnel
at all) is never examined. Effective n is not 2.5 million patients: it is one
unreplicated, non-peer-reviewed analysis of one vendor's database over one
window, so effective n for the inference is 1. Selection is on the outcome
variable. No confound adjustment is reported. The mechanism claim
("co-prescribed patients resupply more") is verifiable; the inference claim
("GLP-1s are a net tailwind to the TAM") does not follow from it and has no
null.

**Options timing check** — applicable in principle (dated claims exist and a

disclosed in §4: path 2 stopgap only, a 125-DTE expiry as the sole bracket for
a print 47 days out, and a failed liquidity gate on both legs. No condition
