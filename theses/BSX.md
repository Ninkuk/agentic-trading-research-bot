## 2. Business

**Created:** single-use devices for minimally invasive procedures across
cardiology (coronary stents, drug-coated balloons, imaging, PFA ablation,
LAAC closure, pacemakers/ICDs, embolisation), endoscopy, urology and
neuromodulation. The customer — a hospital cath lab, EP lab or endoscopy
suite — gets a procedure that is faster, safer or reimbursable where the
alternative is a drug, open surgery, or a worse device: FARAPULSE turned a
~2-hour thermal ablation into a shorter, safer pulsed-field one; WATCHMAN
is the only RCT-proven alternative to lifelong anticoagulation for patients
who cannot or will not take it (10-K Item 1; Q2 call, Dr. Stein).

**Captured:** (i) per-procedure disposables at ~69% gross margin — every
catheter, stent, balloon and closure device is single-use (TTM gross margin
69.2%, statistics probe); (ii) capital placements that pull disposables
through — OPAL mapping consoles seed FARAPULSE catheter utilisation, and
management counts console placements as the moat input (Bernstein
2026-05-27); (iii) implantables with device-specific reimbursement (WATCHMAN
under CMS NCD 20.34; CRM). Geography: US 64% of 2025 sales ($12.9B of
$20.1B), EMEA 17%, APAC 15% (10-K Note N). Cardiovascular is 66% of sales;
within it EP $3.3B (+75% in 2025), WATCHMAN $2.0B (+29%), ICVT $4.6B,
CRM $2.3B, IO&E $1.0B.

**Protected:** the mechanism differs by franchise and is weaker than the
labels. WATCHMAN: fifteen years of randomised evidence, a device-specific
CMS coverage decision, and an installed base of trained implanters — 91%
share (Q2 call) — but the same evidence base is now the problem (OCEAN-
CLOSURE and CHAMPION readouts confused referrers), and a competitor's
next-generation device is expected in 2027. FARAPULSE: first-mover PFA
with the OPAL mapping installed base and a "waveform + catheter + recipe"
know-how claim (Bernstein), but PFA is already 80–85% of the US AF market,
Medtronic, J&J and Abbott have launched, and US EP growth fell from +145%
(Q1 2025) to +3% (Q2 2026) — the protection is a refresh cadence, not a
lock. The base business is protected the way all large medtech is: FDA
clearance timelines, sales-force reach into every US lab, and switching
friction for trained physicians. No franchise has a mechanism that stops a
funded rival from launching the next catheter.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder — non-affiliate market value $158.7B against a total of ~$159B at
the 6/30/2025 price (10-K cover); insiders hold 0.18% (statistics probe).
Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist. Board chair and CEO are the
same person (Mahoney).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $11.89B (2021) → $12.68B
→ $14.24B → $16.75B → $20.07B (2025) → $21.00B TTM; operating income $1.82B
→ $2.03B → $2.42B → $3.00B → $3.97B → $4.30B TTM. Operating margin 15.3% →
20.5%. GAAP figures from `/financials/income-statement/` (the statistics
block can exclude restructuring; both routes agree on revenue).
