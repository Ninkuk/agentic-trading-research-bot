## 2. Business

**Created:** On paper, an integrated forestry chain — concession management and
harvesting, then downstream processing into logs, decking, flooring, sawn
timber, charcoal and essential oils, sold to importers, retailers and
processors across Europe, Africa, Asia and South America. Two segments: Direct
Purchase / ODM (sourcing and design for third-party brands) and Manufacturing.
The customer buys certified tropical hardwood supply with documented chain of
custody — real value when EU deforestation rules tightened, which is what the
company's own March 2024 guidance leaned on.

What survives of that today is the question. Forty employees. PP&E of
$530,000. Capex of $52,541 for the year. A business that harvests and
manufactures does not run on half a million dollars of fixed assets; a
business that *brokers* does. Gross margin fell from 33.29% to 9.60% in a
single year, which is what happens when the manufacturing margin leaves and
only the trading spread remains.

**Captured:** Three mechanisms, in declining order of what is left. (1) The
processing spread — buy logs, saw and finish them, sell the product; this is
where a 33–40% gross margin came from and it is the part that appears to have
gone. (2) The trading spread on direct purchase and ODM — source and resell,
a low-single-digit-to-low-teens margin, and the plausible source of today's
9.6%. (3) Ancillary lines the description still names — IT and business
consultancy services, and carbon-credit trading — neither of which is
separately sized in anything I could reach, and both of which read as
appended rather than operating.

**Protected:** Nothing. There is no mechanism here. Tropical hardwood sourcing
and resale has no switching cost, no scale economy at $14.58M of revenue, no
network, no proprietary process, and no brand a buyer of sawn timber pays up
for. The concession rights that could have been a moat are consistent with the
assets that left the balance sheet. The honest sentence is that a competitor
could do this tomorrow, and at 9.6% gross margin several already are.

**Control:** Two classes since the December 16, 2025 recapitalization. Class A
ordinary — 137,892,471 shares, one vote each, and the only class underlying
the traded ADSs (8 ordinary per ADS, so 17,236,557 ADSs). Class B ordinary —
92,932,850 shares, **fifty votes each**, held in their entirety by TUTU
Business Services Limited, which is Ms. Liying Wang, who is also Chairman and
CEO. That is 4,646,642,500 of 4,784,534,971 total votes, or 97.12%. The
sequence matters: TUTU bought ~86.82% of a single-class company on
2026-10-22 [see UNKNOWN 5 on this date], the entire prior senior management
resigned on 2025-11-03, and the vote-widening recapitalization was approved
on 2025-12-16 — after control, not with it. The structure forecloses every
minority remedy at once: no unsolicited takeover, no activist slate, no proxy
contest, no blocking vote on a share issuance or a related-party merger.

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs and pairing.** TTM (= FY2025) levered free cash flow is

**The solver refuses, and the refusal is the finding:**


Per Phase 4, exit 2 is a category error, not a tool failure: there is no
implied return to solve for because there is no cash flow to discount. The
honest substitute is the inversion — **what would FCF have to become** to
justify $17.71M? At a 9.88% hurdle with 2% terminal growth, the perpetuity
requires roughly $1.40M of steady-state free cash flow. The company must
therefore travel from −$0.96M to +$1.40M, a $2.36M swing, on a $14.58M
revenue base still contracting at 10.75% a year — a required FCF margin of
9.6% for a business whose entire *gross* margin is 9.60%. Every operating
dollar would have to come from nowhere. There is no path; the arithmetic is
not close.

**9.88%**, and at the raw beta it is **15.05%**. Both are stated for form
only — with no positive flow there is no implied return to spread against
either. Note 9.88% sits exactly at the 90th percentile of the US
cost-of-capital distribution (median 7.79%, 80% of firms between 5.26% and
