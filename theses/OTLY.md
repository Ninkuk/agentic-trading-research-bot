## 2. Business

**Created:** An oat drink engineered to behave like milk in coffee — the
Barista line foams and steams without splitting — plus a brand that reads as
a lifestyle choice rather than a dietary compromise. The customer gets a
dairy substitute that works in a flat white, a story about oats and climate,
and (per the COO on the Q2 call) increasingly a "beverage" — flavoured and
cold-foam formats sold on taste to consumers who are neither lactose-
intolerant nor motivated by sustainability. The barista pays for reliability
in the cup; the shopper pays a premium over private label for the brand and
the formulation. Oatly's route to market starts in cafés (trial in a trusted
setting) and moves the winning drinks into retail (the 20-F's stated
"foodservice-led market entry strategy").

**Captured:** Three mechanisms, unequal in quality (Q2 2026 release):
(1) branded retail sales through grocers and clubs at a premium price — 77%
of Europe & International and 63% of North America revenue; (2) foodservice
sales to coffee chains, independents and distributors — the rest of those two
segments and 54% of Greater China; (3) intersegment supply of Swedish
production to China and North America (eliminated). Margin capture is where
the mechanism shows: E&I earns $25.0M of adjusted EBITDA on $143.1M (17.5%),
North America $0.7M on $66.9M (1.1%), Greater China −$1.5M on $30.1M, and a
corporate line of −$23.8M a quarter (−$95M/yr, 10% of sales) absorbs most of
the segment profit. Group gross margin 33.9%; group adjusted EBITDA $0.4M in
Q2 and $5.4M in H1. Price/mix contributed +1.5% to Q2 growth; volume +11.2%.

**Protected:** Brand and formulation incumbency in the coffee channel, and
nothing else. The mechanism is that cafés standardise on a milk that behaves
in the machine and on the drink recipes Oatly seeds (63 "lookbook" drinks,
per the call), and that retail shelf share follows café trial: US oat-milk
share at a record ~31%, TDP share 22% from 17%, ACV ~50% from 32% in 2022
(management figures on the Q2 call, not corroborated by a filing). Against
that: the 20-F names private label sold "at lower prices," retailer
consolidation pushing "emphasis on private label," and a co-manufacturing
model with take-or-pay shortfall charges ($13.9M in FY2025, $4.8M in H1 2026)
— there is no cost advantage, no patent that matters (the original enzymatic
process patents are decades old and not cited as a barrier in the 20-F), and
the largest foodservice customer (~6% of 2025 revenue) cut its Oatly spending
in 2025. The moat is a marketing asset that must be fed: SG&A rose $10.6M YoY
in Q2 on branding and distribution. Honest label: a real brand, a thin moat.

**Control:** One share, one vote (ordinary shares; the ADS is 1:20), no dual
class — but four holders control 71.0% of the vote as of 2026-03-11 (20-F
Item 7.A): Nativus Company Limited 44.6% (a 50/50 JV of Verlinvest S.A. and
Blossom Key, a subsidiary of state-owned China Resources), Platin S.à r.l.
10.1% (Olivier Goudet), Blackstone Funds 9.3%, Hillhouse 7.0%. The same
parties bought $200.1M of the Swedish convertible notes and the HH notes.
Float is 12.15M of 31.91M ADSs (38%). The structure forecloses an unsolicited
takeover and any activist path, and — the point that matters here — it puts
the controllers on both sides of the 2028 refinancing. Section 16 filings
(Forms 3/4) began in March 2026 although the company still reports as a
foreign private issuer; the June 30, 2026 FPI test's outcome is not yet
disclosed.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue up 44% from FY2021 to
