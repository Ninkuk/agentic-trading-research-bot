**Disputed expectation:** the price implies a ~7.3% equity return on a 5%
FCF-growth path (6.5% on 3%, 8.1% on 7%), all below an 8.7% cost of equity;
what revises it upward is US comparable guest counts turning positive at an

This is one of the most defensible businesses on the exchange: a landlord and
brand licensor that takes ~12.8% of $130B of franchisee sales as rent and
royalty, owns or controls the dirt under 22,570 conventional franchises for
20-year terms, and converts 46% of revenue to operating income. None of that
is in dispute. What is in dispute is the price. After a 19% one-year drawdown
to a two-year low, the levered FCF yield is 4.2% after stock compensation, and
the reverse DCF says the market is still paying for roughly 5% FCF growth
forever to earn ~7%. That is below the cost of equity on every growth path
short of a heroic one, while the US business, 42% of restaurant margin
dollars, is losing traffic to Burger King and to its own franchisees' pricing
choices. A good business at a below-hurdle return is a pass, not a bargain,
and the drawdown has compensated for the traffic risk without creating a
margin of safety.

**Closest attack:** the beta floor. MCD's regression beta is 0.41; the
anchors rule floors it to 0.8 (hurdle 8.7%). At the raw beta the hurdle is
6.8% and the base path clears by +53bp. The pass survives because the
mature-company absolute companion (risk-free + 4.5% = 9.25%) sits above every
scenario regardless of beta, and because 0.41 is a price-behavior fact about
the past decade of rates, not a claim about the next one.

## 2. Business

**Created:** a predictable, fast, cheap meal in a location the customer
already passes, with the same product in 45,356 restaurants across 100+
countries. The customer surplus is consistency and convenience at a price
anchored below rivals' base menus (management: US beef, chicken, and beverage
base pricing now below nearest competitors, Q2 2026 call). For franchisees the
product is a turnkey box with the industry's highest unit volumes and a
landlord who has already picked the corner.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one "royalty".
(a) *Rent* — conventional franchisees pay rent on real estate McDonald's owns
or leases (net PP&E under franchise arrangements $22.8B incl. $7.1B land);
future minimum rents $31.5B, ~$3.0B a year for the next five. (b) *Royalty* —
a percent of sales from conventional franchisees and from developmental
licensees/affiliates (who fund their own real estate). Together (a)+(b) were
$16.5B in 2025 on $129.7B of franchised sales, a 12.8% take. (c) *Company-
operated sales* — 2,039 restaurants, $9.7B revenue, a 14.7% margin business
that is being shrunk (US company-op margin −14% in 2025; incremental
divestitures announced). (d) *Other* — technology cost-recovery fees charged
to franchisees and brand licensing, $647M and growing 53%. Franchised margins
are ~90% of restaurant-margin dollars; the consolidated operating margin is
45.7% TTM because the income statement is mostly rent and royalty.

**Protected:** the mechanism is the real estate plus the term. At the end of
a 20-year franchise the company keeps the site and can re-let it; a
franchisee cannot take the location to a rival. On top of that: a procurement
system that lets 22,570 independent operators price below Burger King while
paying rent; 220M 90-day-active loyalty users whose data now pools in one
global lake; and delivery at ~90% of restaurants with an "industry-leading
cost structure" (management's phrase, unverified). The honest gap: none of
this stops a competitor from *out-valuing* McDonald's in a given quarter —
Burger King just did — and the real-estate moat protects the rent stream,
not the guest count.

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.01 par, 710.4M shares outstanding
at 2026-01-31; no controlling holder. Vanguard 10.03%, BlackRock 7.3%, State
Street 5.1% (DEF 14A, 2026-04-07); insiders 0.03%. Chairman and CEO are
combined (Kempczinski). The structure forecloses nothing: an activist path and
an unsolicited bid are both open, which is the null answer written out.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive, modest.** Revenue +19% and operating
income +25% from 2021 to TTM; operating margin 43.7% → 45.7%.

- **Break —** two consecutive quarters of negative US comparable sales with
  negative guest counts while Burger King posts positive comps: the value

- **Break —** franchisee non-compliance rising above the disclosed one-third,
  or an NOA-organized refusal of the value architecture; the growth-eligibility
  lever has stopped working.
- **Shift —** IOM or IDL comps turning negative for a quarter (France and
  China are the disclosed soft spots) — the international engine that funds
  58% of restaurant margins has stopped offsetting the US.
- **Shift —** debt/EBITDA above 4× or a downgrade below BBB+/Baa1; buybacks
  would have to stop and the per-share arithmetic changes.
