Capricor is a pre-revenue cell-therapy company whose equity is a claim on
one FDA action. The July pass said the binary was unpriceable and the cash
underneath it was a countdown, not a floor. Since then FDA has agreed to
review a narrower indication on an amendment that adds no new controlled
data, the stock has risen 44% from the July price and 124% from the
post-vote settle, quarterly opex has stepped up 16% QoQ with G&A running
at 2.5× a year ago, the capture mechanism (the NS Pharma distribution
agreement) has moved from a dated court hearing into an undated
arbitration, and an activist has asked for the cash-preservation plan the
prior write-up said was missing. None of that lets me price the approval.
Everything that would make this a buy — an approval with a voucher — is
the one thing no public document can bound.

**Closest attack:** the market has *already* priced this at a modest
probability, so a pass at $9.40 is a bet that P(approval) is below ~25–50%,
and I cannot show that either. FDA accepting a major amendment "citing
the significant unmet medical need" — if that phrase is FDA's and not
Capricor's — is a stated regulatory posture that has produced approvals
over reviewer objections before (Elevidys, 2023, under a CBER director who
has since left). The attack does not land because it is symmetric: it
turns an UNKNOWN into a reason to abstain from *both* sides, and this
write-up recommends neither a long nor a short. It would land on anyone
holding the pass as a bearish view.

Load-bearing conditions of the pass (5):

1. *probable* — **The refined-indication amendment rests on the same
   controlled result FDA's review team found fragile.** The 8-K describes
   the amendment as 24-month OLE data plus "robustness analyses" of the

   showed reads p=0.21 without the imputation of two placebo subjects and
   p=0.24 on the pre-specified absolute-change analysis. Open-label
   extension data is uncontrolled by construction.
2. *probable* — **The CRL-state value is approximately cash, and cash is a
   countdown.** Ten post-vote sessions settled at $3.85–4.21 (~$225–245M

3. *probable* — **The capture mechanism is disputed even on approval.**
   Capricor's own pleading calls the US distribution agreement's pricing
   "fundamentally flawed" and seeks rescission; it withdrew the injunction
   motion and now expects arbitration "this fall". On approval the product
   ships through a partner Capricor is trying to fire, at a transfer price

4. *plausible* — **The market-implied probability (~25–50%) is not
   demonstrably too high or too low.** This is the load-bearing UNKNOWN:
   no public document bounds P(approval) after a 9–3 negative vote and a

   not a view against it.
5. *plausible* — **The approval-state value is itself bounded by things
