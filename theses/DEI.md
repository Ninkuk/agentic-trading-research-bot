## 2. Business

**Created:** Class-A office space in supply-constrained, high-income LA
submarkets (Beverly Hills, Brentwood, Century City, Santa Monica, Sherman
Oaks/Encino, Burbank, Warner Center) plus Honolulu. The customer is mostly a
small-to-mid professional tenant: legal, financial services, real estate,
entertainment (Q2 call). They get a short commute to where executives live and
a landlord with building-level services. The apartments are 99.4% leased, so
demand is not the constraint there.

**Captured:** (a) Office base rent under multi-year leases; Q2 straight-line
spreads were +3.2% on expiring rents, and tenant improvements and leasing
costs ran $5.35/sf/yr (call). (b) Tenant recoveries and parking income on top
of base rent (UNKNOWN split; the 10-K revenue note was not read). (c)
Multifamily rent, with same-property cash NOI +2% YoY in Q2 (call). (d) Fee
and promote economics on consolidated JVs where DEI owns a minority stake,
e.g. Bedford Collection, a $260M purchase in which DEI holds 13.3% (call).
Consolidated JV debt is $1.91B of the $5.77B total (10-Q).

**Protected:** Mostly geography plus scale. Westside entitlement and
construction costs make new office supply close to zero, and DEI's
submarket share lets it move tenants between its own buildings as they grow
or shrink. That protects **rent levels relative to new supply**. It does not
protect against **demand**: remote work and tenants leaving LA cut vacancy
the other way, and no moat covers the cost of debt. Honest read: there is a
real local moat on supply, and nothing that defends the equity against the
capital structure.

**Control:** One class of common stock (167.5M shares). OP units are 39.3M
(19% of fully diluted units; Q2 8-K) and held outside the REIT. stocks.db
shows insiders at 2.64% of shares. The proxy and charter were not read this
run, so the Maryland REIT ownership-limit terms and any takeover defenses are
