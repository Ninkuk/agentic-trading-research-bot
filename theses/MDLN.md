## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $34.68.** kill-thesis: **SOUND (on this pass)** — conditions=5
(4 probable, 1 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0.

Medline is the only US med-surg company that both manufactures and
distributes at scale, and its prime-vendor contracts plus brand conversion
are a real, mechanical growth engine (13% organic growth in H1 2026). But the
equity at $34.68 already prices a return of roughly 6–7% a year against a 9%
cost of equity, and it prices that on a business whose margins have fallen
for two years, whose manufacturing quality the FDA has called out twice in
two months, and whose pre-IPO owners still hold 67% of the vote and are
selling into the market. A good franchise at a price that needs the recovery
to be complete is a pass; it becomes interesting somewhere near $22, or when
the Medline Brand margin and the FDA file both turn.

**Closest attack:** "The base FCF is depressed by one-offs — $336M Tracy
loss, ~$350M of 2026 tariff drag, first-year rebates on $2.4B of new
signings — normalize them away and the stock is cheap." Adding back an
after-tax ~$350M lifts base FCF to $1.8B and the implied return to 7.43%,
still 159bp below the hurdle. The attack moves the number; it does not
change the answer.

## 2. Business

**Created:** A hospital, surgery center, physician office, or nursing home
hands its entire med-surg supply problem — 190,000+ Medline Brand SKUs plus
third-party products — to one logistics partner that owns the trucks
(MedTrans), the distribution centers, and about a third of its own
manufacturing (30 plants, 24 in North America), and sources the rest from
600+ partners in ~40 countries, none over 5% of spend. The customer gets
consolidated ordering, lower landed cost, and a supplier that absorbs cost
shocks rather than reprice ("we don't act in times of uncertainty and
chaos" — CEO, Q2 call, on tariffs and the Strait of Hormuz). Medline is the
only supplier serving every point of care (10-K, company claim based on
public net-sales comparison).

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms. (a) *Supply Chain Solutions*
(FY25 $14.7B, 5.5% adjusted EBITDA margin; Q2 4.9%, guided 4.9–5.0%): a
prime-vendor distribution spread on third-party products, low margin by
design and *lower* in year one because of first-year rebates. (b) *Medline
Brand* (FY25 $13.7B, 24.3% margin): the manufacturer's margin on its own
products, earned twice over when a prime-vendor account converts commodity
lines (gloves, tongue depressors) in year one and clinical lines over the
following years. (c) *International* (~$2B, 10% growth) and *retail*
(<2% of sales, all Medline Brand, volatile). The profit is almost entirely
(b): Medline Brand produced $3.33B of the $3.5B FY25 adjusted EBITDA.

**Protected:** The mechanism is the prime-vendor contract (typically five
years, "vast majority" of the customer's med-surg needs) plus the switching
cost of unwinding a converted formulary across a health system — a
competitor (McKesson, Cardinal, Owens & Minor, Henry Schein) must first win
the distribution contract, then persuade clinicians off Medline-branded
kits. None of those rivals manufactures at Medline's breadth, so none can
match the blended landed cost. What the moat does not stop: GPO contract
loss (69% of sales, three GPOs), a health system consolidating into a
non-Medline customer, or a quality failure that makes the brand itself the
liability — and the last is the live risk.

**Control:** Up-C. Class A (877M shares, one vote, economic) and Class B
(437M shares, one vote, no economics, paired to Common Units in Medline
Holdings LP). "Designating Stockholders" (Blackstone, Carlyle, Hellman &
Friedman, Mills family) control ~67% of the vote and nominate directors
under nomination agreements; the 10-K says they "may be able to prevent a
change of control … and could preclude any unsolicited acquisition." The TRA
accelerates on a change of control at >90% of hypothetical benefits, a
second poison pill. Forecloses: an unsolicited bid, an activist path.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative in 2026; flat in 2025; positive
2022–2024.** GAAP operating income vs revenue ($M, stockanalysis
`/financials/income-statement/`, corroborated against the 10-K/Q2 8-K):

| FY | revenue | op income | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 21,448 | 861 | 4.0% |
| 2023 | 23,231 | 1,411 | 6.1% |
| 2024 | 25,507 | 2,148 | 8.4% |
| 2025 | 28,432 | 2,179 | 7.7% |
| TTM Jun-26 | 29,939 | 1,826 | 6.1% |

H1 2026 vs H1 2025: revenue +11.1%, operating income −32.6% ($817M vs
$1,213M). Excluding the $336M Tracy loss, operating income still fell
~5% on 11% more revenue. On the company's adjusted EBITDA the same
direction holds once the $243M tariff refund is stripped: H1 $1,593M vs
$1,803M (−12%). Revenue is compounding; profit is not, and the reason is
the income statement (SG&A +20.7% in Q2 on +11.6% sales), not the cash
flow.
