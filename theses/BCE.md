## 2. Business

**Created:** BCE sells connectivity that households and businesses cannot do
without: mobile service on a national network, fibre-to-the-home internet
across Ontario, Quebec and Atlantic Canada, enterprise networking and
security, and media (CTV, Crave). Customers stay for network quality and
bundled convenience, plus the switching friction of device financing and
installed service. The surplus is real but thin. Canadian mobile pricing
fell sharply through the first half of 2026 ($25 flash plans, TD's "race to
the bottom" downgrade, April 2026).

**Captured:** six distinct mechanisms.
(a) Monthly postpaid/prepaid wireless plans plus device-financing margin and
roaming, with mobile phone ARPU of C$56.30 in Q2.
(b) Recurring wireline subscriptions: internet, which grew 14.2% in Q2 on
fibre, plus declining TV and home phone.
(c) Enterprise and wholesale: business connectivity and Ateko/Bell Cyber
(+29% in Q2), plus regulated wholesale rents that competitors pay for access
to Bell fibre at CRTC-set final rates effective 2026-04-24.
(d) Ziply Fiber (US): residential fibre subscriptions, with Q2 revenue of
C$234M at a 40.6% EBITDA margin.
(e) Bell Media: advertising (digital video +39%), Crave subscriptions (5.1M)
and carriage fees, with Q2 revenue of C$918M.
(f) AI Fabric: setup fees and prepayments, then capacity rent from
data-centre tenants (335 MW contracted, a 20% return target).

**Protected:** the mechanism is real but eroding. Spectrum licences plus
rights-of-way and a built network take a decade and tens of billions to
replicate. Canadian foreign-ownership limits keep US carriers out. The market
is a three-national-player structure plus Quebecor/Freedom. The erosion is
regulatory. The CRTC mandated wholesale access to Bell's fibre in 2024, with
final rates from 2026-04-24, and facilities-based MVNO access to Bell's
wireless network (Decision 2026-84). Both let rivals sell on Bell's assets
without building. Bell's own response was to cut Canadian fibre capex and
redeploy capital to the US (Ziply), where it is an overbuilder with no
regulatory moat. Protection covers volume better than it covers price.

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controlling holder (insiders 0.06%,
institutions 51.2%, stocks.db). Canadian telecom and broadcasting
foreign-ownership rules foreclose an acquisition by a non-Canadian buyer.
Nothing forecloses an activist path.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue rose 5.7% from 2021 to
TTM while operating income rose 2.3%. Since 2023, operating income has
fallen 4.0% on flat-to-up revenue, and that revenue includes Ziply from
August 2025 (≈C$0.9B a year), which masks core decline.
