## 2. Business

**Created:** airlines get a large jet that carries passengers at the lowest
cost per seat-mile their route structure allows, delivered with a global
support network and a residual value that lessors will finance. There are
two vendors in the world for this above ~150 seats, and the airline's
alternative to Boeing is a multi-year wait at Airbus. Governments get
tankers, fighters, rotorcraft, satellites and launch from one of a handful
of primes cleared to build them.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms. BCA sells airframes on multi-year
contracts with pre-delivery payments starting about two years before
delivery and the balance at handover (Malave, Laguna) — so cash arrives
ahead of revenue when rates rise and behind it when they fall; program
accounting spreads cost over an accounting quantity, so the reported margin
lags cash margin (737 deferred production costs $13.1B, 787 $14.4B; 10-Q
Note 7). BDS sells on fixed-price development and cost-type production
contracts to the US government and allies ($85B backlog, 27% non-US). BGS
sells parts, modifications and training on the installed base at 18% margins
($33B backlog), the only segment that earns its cost of capital today.
Consolidated: revenue $94.0B TTM, gross margin 4.7%, operating margin −5.4%.

**Protected:** the moat is a mechanism, not a label — certification. A new
large-jet entrant needs a type certificate from the FAA or EASA, which takes
a decade and tens of billions, and then a global maintenance, training and
spares network airlines will accept. COMAC's C919 has neither outside China.
The same mechanism now cuts the other way: the FAA "ultimately determines
the timing of certification" (10-K Item 1A), and since 2019 it has used that
authority against Boeing repeatedly — the MAX grounding, the 38/mo cap, the
airworthiness-certificate delegation only restored in July 2026, and today's
MAX 10 delay. The duopoly protects revenue; it does not protect the calendar.

**Control:** one class of common, one vote per share (2026 proxy); insiders
own 0.05% (stockanalysis), institutions 73%; the >5% holders in the proxy's
principal-shareholders table are Schedule 13G filers, so no controlling
holder. The $5.75B 6.00% Series A mandatory convertible preferred is senior
to the common for dividends and liquidation and converts on or about
2027-10-15 into 33.5M–40.2M common shares (10-K Item 1A), 4.2–5.1% of the
count. Nothing in the charter forecloses a takeover or an activist; the
size, the national-security posture and the antitrust facts do.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue has risen 51% from 2021
to TTM while operating income never turned positive for a full year except
2023.
