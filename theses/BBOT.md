## 2. Business

**Created:** nothing yet, for anyone. BBOT sells no product and books no
revenue. What it is building is a set of small molecules aimed at the RAS
pathway — the most commonly mutated oncogene family in human cancer and, for
three decades, the canonical undruggable target. Three clinical programs:
BBO-8520, a dual ON/OFF-state KRAS-G12C inhibitor (the approved drugs,
sotorasib and adagrasib, bind only the OFF state); BBO-11818, a pan-KRAS
ON/OFF inhibitor claiming >500× selectivity over N- and H-RAS; and BBO-10203,
a RAS–PI3Kα disruptor designed to block the interaction without the
hyperglycaemia that limits wild-type PI3K inhibitors, in tumours that are
>90% PI3K-wild-type. The value to a patient, if any of it works, is a line of
therapy that does not currently exist.

**Captured:** not at all today, and only ever through one of two mechanisms —
a US-priced branded oncology drug behind composition-of-matter patents and
regulatory exclusivity, or an upfront-plus-milestones licence to a partner
with a commercial organisation. There is no third path. The $196k of
"income from transition services agreements" in Q2 is a BridgeBio Pharma
administrative recharge, not a business.

**Protected:** today, nothing that qualifies as a moat. A clinical-stage
biotech's protection is a patent estate plus a development head start, and
neither is enforceable revenue until approval. The company's own defensibility
claim is chemical — dual ON/OFF binding and >500× RAS-isoform selectivity —
and that is a claim about molecules I cannot adjudicate (§6.1). What can be
said plainly: five other G12C inhibitors are in or through the clinic
(sotorasib, adagrasib approved; divarasib, glecirasib, olomorasib, calderasib
in development), Roche's divarasib posted Phase 3 data in July 2026 described
as edging Amgen and BMS, and BBOT has just conceded the 1L setting to them.
A company that withdraws from the largest segment of its own market citing
"competitive crowding" is telling you where its moat is not.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
stockholder, no warrants, no earnouts, no preferred outstanding. The board is
classified into three staggered classes with three-year terms and there is no
cumulative voting — the proxy itself says the structure "may delay or prevent
stockholder efforts to effect a change." Two holders dominate the register:
the Cormorant complex at 22.32% (17,878,594 shares; Bihua Chen sits on the
board) and BridgeBio Pharma at 18.2% as of 6/30/26. Neil Kumar, BridgeBio
Pharma's CEO, and Frank McCormick — among the most cited RAS biologists alive
— are directors. The practical effect of the staggered board plus a 40.5%
concentrated register is that an activist or an unsolicited acquirer has no
fast path; a friendly sale negotiated by those two holders does.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** There is no revenue line to
lever, and costs are compounding against it.

## 4. Valuation

**`reverse_dcf` refuses this input, and the refusal is the honest starting
point:**

```

    --base-fcf -160556000 --growth 0.0 0.0 0.0 --terminal-growth 0.024
refused: base_fcf must be positive, got -160556000.0        (exit 2)
```

There is no free cash flow to discount and no revenue to grow. What follows
is the substitute arithmetic: not "what return is priced in," but "what would
have to be true at the far end for today's price to clear the hurdle."

**Inputs.** Market cap $442,561,954 (80,174,267 shares × $5.52). Cash,
equivalents and marketable securities at 6/30/26: $344,130k ($54,063k cash,
$171,648k short-term, $118,419k long-term). The only balance-sheet obligation
is $2,514k of operating leases — no notes, no converts, no warrants. Net cash
$341,616k gives EV **$100.9M** on the 6/30 balance. Adjusting for burn since
(≈$16M/month × 2.34 months = $37.4M) gives estimated net cash ~$304.2M and EV
**~$138.4M** today. The levered-FCF ↔ market-cap pairing rule does not apply
here because there is no positive flow to pair; the enterprise value is used
directly as the price of the pipeline, with the cash netted out.

**Hurdle.** rf 4.83% (DGS10, `fred.db`, 2026-09-09) + beta 0.8 × ERP 4.14%
(Damodaran implied ERP, as of 2026-09-01, against his T-bond rate of 4.75%)
= **8.14%**. The printed beta is 0.385 and is floored to the 0.8 band
minimum on the thin-float trigger — float 35,812,033 of 80,174,267 shares out
= 44.7%, far under four-fifths — and because the regression spans only the
13 months since the 2025-08-12 de-SPAC. Damodaran's US cost-of-capital
distribution (median 7.79%, 80% of firms within 5.26–9.88%) **does not
contain a pre-revenue Phase 1b biotech at all**; 8.14% is a floor, not an
estimate, and the table below therefore also runs at a 12% practical cost of
equity. Terminal growth is capped at the 10-year breakeven **T10YIE 2.40%**
(`fred.db`, 2026-09-10).

**Required-revenue inversion.** Read down: each row is the prior row plus one
honest adjustment.

| step | basis | result |
|---|---|---|
| perpetual FCF to justify EV at the mechanical hurdle | $138.4M × (8.14% − 2.40%) | **$7.94M/yr** |
| same, at a 12% biotech cost of equity | $138.4M × (12.0% − 2.40%) | **$13.29M/yr** |
| deferred to 2031 (Phase 1b → mid-2027 data → registrational → earliest approval) | × 1.762 (5 yr @ 12%) | **$23.41M/yr from 2031** |
| grossed up for dilution: $600M raised 2027-2030 at ~$8 avg ⇒ today's holders own 51.7% | ÷ 0.517 | **$45.3M/yr company FCF** |
| at a 25% FCF margin on revenue | ÷ 0.25 | **~$181M/yr revenue, risk-unadjusted** |
| at 20% probability of reaching approval from Phase 1b | ÷ 0.20 | **~$906M/yr revenue in the success case** |

Spread against the hurdle: the mechanical 8.14% hurdle is cleared by
$7.94M/yr of perpetual free cash flow, which sounds trivial and is the
wrong reading — the whole distance is in the last four rows, where the
deferral, the dilution and the probability compound to a 114× multiple on
that number.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Market-share sentence.* The company's own figure is ~21,000 US NSCLC
  KRAS-G12C diagnoses in 2026. At a realized $100k per patient-course,
  $906M/yr is ~9,060 courses = **43% of every KRAS-G12C NSCLC patient
  diagnosed in the US each year**; at a more generous $150k (sotorasib lists
  near $18k/month), ~6,040 courses = **29%**. Both understate the required
  share, because the denominator that matters is not all 21,000 — it is the
  subset who receive a 1L G12C inhibitor and progress. On the company's own
  2027-2029 framing that subset is perhaps 60% of diagnoses, which roughly
  doubles the required share of the *treated* population to ~50-70%. This is
  Damodaran's "bigger than the market" failure, and it is why the prioritized
  indication alone does not defend $5.52. The price is only defensible if
  BBO-11818 reaches the KRAS-G12D PDAC/CRC population, which is several times
  larger — and that asset's first human monotherapy data has not been seen.
- *Reinvestment / terminal-ROE warning.* Does not arise: there are no
  earnings and no retained earnings to reinvest. The terminal growth of 2.40%
  is the 10-year breakeven and claims zero real growth, which is the correct
  conservative posture for a single-product franchise facing generic entry at
  patent expiry.
- *Item 1A terminal risk.* The dominant structural risk is that RAS-pathway
  competition compresses the window: five G12C inhibitors are in or through
  the clinic and the company has already ceded 1L. A terminal rate at or
  below expected inflation survives that; anything above it would not.
- *Base rate.* Phase 1 oncology assets reach approval roughly 5% of the time
  (BIO/Informa clinical development success rates). The 20% used above is
  already generous — it credits the disclosed ORR heavily — and it is applied
  to a single asset, ignoring that all three programs share one pathway and
  therefore fail together under a class-level mechanism problem.
- *Distribution clamp.* Not applicable in the usual direction: no implied
  return was solved for. The relevant clamp is on the hurdle itself, and
  8.14% sits below Damodaran's US median of 7.79% only by virtue of the
  beta floor — a genuine cost of equity for this risk profile is nearer
  15-20%, which makes every row above optimistic.
- *SBC.* $11,098k for H1 2026 (~$22M annualized, ~9.7% of H1 operating
  expenses). It flatters the operating-cash-use figure relative to the net
  loss and is a real claim on future share count, on top of the dilution row
  above.

**Options-implied move: no listed options.** `get_option_chains` returns an

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy):**

- **Break —** BBO-11818 Q4 2026 monotherapy data shows confirmed RECIST
  responses in KRAS-mutant (especially G12D) solid tumours at tolerable
  doses. This is the single observation that changes the company's identity
  from "a salvage-setting G12C asset" to "a pan-KRAS platform," and it is the
  only thing that makes $138M of enterprise value look small.
- **Shift —** a partnership or licensing deal that funds the registrational
  program with upfront cash, removing the dilution row from §4 entirely.
- **Shift —** the stock at or below ~$3.00, where the market capitalization
  (~$240M) falls below projected year-end net cash, making it a different
  question from this one.

**For an owner (sell):**

- **Break —** BBO-11818 Q4 2026 data shows no RECIST responses, or
  dose-limiting toxicity across the dose range. Two of three programs then
  rest on a conceded competitive setting.
- **Break —** an equity issuance above ~25% of shares outstanding at or below
  ~$6, whether through the ATM or a marketed deal. The first evidence of ATM
  usage will be the Q3 10-Q share count.
- **Shift —** the expanded BBO-8520 + pembrolizumab dataset (mid-2027) comes
  in below ~40% ORR, versus the 53% all-dose base already disclosed.
- **Shift —** a competitor's 1L G12C-inhibitor combination Phase 3 materially
  extends 1L PFS, delaying or shrinking the 2L+ experienced pool this thesis
  is now entirely dependent on.
