## 2. Business

**Created:** TELUS sells connectivity households and businesses cannot do
without. That means mobile service on a national network (5G covers 34.2M
Canadians, ~92%) and fibre internet across BC, Alberta and eastern Quebec,
plus security, TV, and two non-telecom arms. TELUS Health runs benefits
administration, virtual care, EAP; 158.9M lives covered. TELUS Digital runs
outsourced customer experience, trust & safety, and AI data services.
Customers stay for network quality, bundles, device-financing tie-in and
installation friction. TTech counts 17.9M connections (+6% YoY).

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms.
(a) Monthly postpaid/prepaid wireless plans (phone ARPU C$56.36) plus
device-financing margin and roaming.
(b) Monthly fixed fees for fibre internet, TV and security, bundled to cut
churn.
(c) Wholesale: CRTC-mandated aggregated FTTP access lets resellers ride
TELUS fibre at regulated rates, and TELUS resells Bell's fibre in Ontario
and Quebec.
(d) Per-member and per-contract fees in Health (EAP, benefits, retirement
administration).
(e) Per-hour/per-seat outsourcing fees in Digital, a business AI is
automating out from under it.
Q2 2026 revenue split: TTech C$3,746M, Digital C$774M, Health C$536M (6-K
ex. 99.1).

**Protected:** the core mechanism is real but rented from regulation.
Spectrum licences, a sunk fibre and tower network, and three national
facilities-based carriers keep entry expensive. But the CRTC has mandated
wholesale fibre access (final rates effective 2026-04-24), Quebecor/Freedom
is expanding west (management: "no material effect yet"), and slower
immigration shrinks the pool of new subscribers. Digital has no moat: its
legacy trust-and-safety and ad-relevancy work for hyperscalers is "being
automated faster than we anticipated" (CFO, Q2 call), hence the C$2.1B
impairment. Health's moat is contract stickiness, which is UNKNOWN in depth.

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controlling holder (insiders 0.08%,
institutions 47.8%, stocks.db). Canadian ownership-and-control rules for
facilities-based carriers (Telecommunications Act) foreclose a foreign
takeover; a domestic bid by a rival would face Competition Bureau review.
An activist path is open in principle; none is visible.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue grew 20.8% over four
years while operating income grew 13.3%, so margin compressed ~1pt. Since
2023, flat revenue with cost cuts lifted op income ~6%: mildly positive
lately, negative over the window. Below the operating line it is worse:
interest expense doubled.
