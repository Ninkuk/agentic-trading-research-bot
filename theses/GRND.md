## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $15.585.** kill-thesis: **SOUND** — conditions=5 (4 probable,
1 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0.

Grindr is a genuinely excellent business: 75% gross margin, 31% operating
margin, 32% ROIC, $462k of capex on $510M of revenue, 178 employees, and
seventeen years of unbroken category leadership in a market where local
user density *is* the product. The problem is entirely price and cash. The
headline free cash flow of $154.0M is 41% stock compensation added back;
charge the compensation and the owner's base is $91.3M, a 3.4% yield. At
$15.585 the market already implies a 7.28%/yr return on a 12% five-year
growth path — 66bp *below* a hurdle that has already been made generous by
flooring a nonsense 0.21 beta up to 0.8. The bull path needs 18%/yr for
five years to reach +109bp, which is above the growth rate management's own
second-half guidance implies. Meanwhile the controlling holder, who offered
$18 to take the company private in October 2025 and then told the market he
would "continue to purchase additional shares," filed a plan on 2026-06-17
to sell up to 6,000,000 shares — 13.5% of the free float — beginning
2026-09-16. A very good business at a price with no margin of safety, held
by a majority owner who has turned seller, is a pass. It becomes
interesting near $11.50–12.00, where the company itself was buying in the
first half of 2026.

**Closest attack:** "You are charging the wrong compensation number. The
accounting SBC expense is $62.7M, but the shares actually issued for
compensation in H1 2026 were 1.31M net — about $40.8M a year at today's
price. Use the realized figure and the base FCF is $113.2M, the implied
return is 8.49%, and the spread flips to **+55bp over the hurdle** — a buy,
not a pass." This is the only attack that moved the answer across the line,
and it is refuted by the award rollforward in the same filing: Grindr
granted 4,084,748 shares in H1 2026 against 1,507,081 vested, the unvested
pool grew from 11,221,343 to 13,216,606 shares (+17.8% in six months, 7.6%
of shares outstanding), and stockholders approved 11,600,000 *additional*
plan shares in June 2026. Net issuance is low because the grants have not
vested yet, not because the cost is low. The $62.7M accounting charge is a
floor on the forward cost, not a ceiling.

## 2. Business

**Created:** Grindr is a location-sorted grid of nearby profiles for gay,
bi, trans and queer people. What the user gets is not matching software; it
is *proximity liquidity* — the knowledge that opening the app anywhere in
190 countries will show real people within walking distance, right now.
Speed and density are the preference being satisfied, which is why the
company describes the core use case as "hookup" and why Right Now (an
explicit "available soon" state, free to all users) is the flagship free
feature. The free tier is deliberately generous: the CEO said on the Q2
2026 call that "free users are the lifeblood of Grindr" and that "we expect
most of our users to not be payers."

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, not one.
(i) *Subscriptions* — XTRA and Unlimited, sold on how many nearby profiles
you can see; a free user sees a capped grid, XTRA more, Unlimited all.
(ii) *Consumables* — one-off add-ons (Boosts, and the like) bought à la
carte, which is why the company's own definition of a Paying User counts
subscribers and add-on purchasers together. Together these are "direct" or
"app-based" revenue: $113M in Q2 2026, +30% YoY, driven by 1.4M average
paying users (+16%) at $26.51 ARPPU (+12%).
(iii) *Advertising* — mostly programmatic third-party inventory plus a
small direct-brand business: $25M in Q2 2026, +44%, flattered by one
year-long brand campaign that management expects not to repeat (guided back
to ~15% of revenue in 2027).
A fourth line is starting: Woodwork, a telehealth subscription launched May
2025 that facilitates access to compounded medications through a clinical
partner. Grindr also holds an equity-method stake in a start-up to which it
has extended a $4.0M revolver at SOFR+5.5% and a $4.0M member loan maturing
2030; it took $1.9M of investee losses and credit loss in Q2 2026.

The gross margin is the tell on who else gets paid. Cost of revenue "consists
primarily of the distribution fees we pay to Apple and Google Play" (10-K).
Gross margin has been 74.4%, 73.7%, 74.0%, 74.6%, 74.4% and 74.9% in
2021–TTM: five years of price increases, tier redesigns and a US regulatory
change that opened external payment links, and the app-store share has not
moved a point.

**Protected:** the mechanism is simultaneous local density. A competitor
does not need a better app; it needs enough gay men *in your neighbourhood*
on the same night, and it needs that in ten thousand neighbourhoods at once.
That is a cold-start problem per micro-market, not a global one, and it has
defeated every well-funded challenger since 2009. The second, softer layer
is identity default: the CEO's framing is that when someone turns 18 and is
working out whether they are gay, Grindr is where they go — a category-noun
position rather than a product position. What the moat does *not* protect
against is the two firms that own distribution. Apple and Google can
delist, restrict, or reprice at discretion, and governments can pressure
them to remove the app in hostile jurisdictions; the 10-K says so in its own
summary risk factors. That is a gate Grindr can never build around.

**Control:** one class of common stock, 1,000,000,000 authorized, no
preferred outstanding — no dual-class structure. But G. Raymond Zage III
beneficially owns 95,439,583 shares, **54.9%**, as of 2026-08-03 (Schedule
13D/A No. 11, 2026-08-11), and insiders hold 74.5% in total against a float
of 44,288,165 shares (25.5%). Grindr is a "controlled company" under NYSE
rules (10-Q Note 13). Zage's majority was not bought: Amendment No. 11 was
filed *solely* to report that his percentage rose because the company
retired shares. That creeping control is being litigated — in *Fellows v.
G. Raymond Zage, III, et al.*, C.A. No. 2025-0477-PAF (Del. Ch.), Zage
submitted an affidavit on 2025-05-15 undertaking not to vote shares in
excess of ~44.9% of voting power without 30 days' written notice to the
court. The structure forecloses any unsolicited takeover and any activist
path outright; a minority holder's only lever is the Special Committee,
which — to its credit — is the body that killed the $18 buyout.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The user base has stopped growing; only monetization intensity has not.**
This is the thread that reframes everything. Average MAU: 13,268k (2023) →
14,248k (+7.4%) → 14,985k (+5.2%) → "15 million" in the Q2 2026 boilerplate.
Against that, average paying users 937k → 1,076k → 1,258k → 1.4M, paying
penetration 7.1% → 7.6% → 8.4% → ~9.3%, and ARPPU $20.05 → $22.53 → $24.25
→ $26.51. Every dollar of the last three years' growth came from converting
and repricing a static audience, not from finding new ones. Management's own
framing is that removing spammers and bad actors suppresses MAU ("bad actors
will appear in your MAU one month, then you remove them," Q2 2026 call) —
plausible, and unfalsifiable from outside. The 10-K says the quiet part
plainly: "To the extent our ARPPU growth slows, our revenue growth will
become increasingly dependent on our ability to increase our Average Paying
Users."

**The short report aimed at exactly that metric — and one of its claims is
checkable.** Ningi Research published a short thesis on 2025-09-09 alleging
an undisclosed SEC investigation into the paying-user metric, double-counting,
and a "quietly redefined" APU that counts daily purchases rather than unique
monthly users (low-confidence tier). I checked the redefinition claim against
the filings themselves. The definition is verbatim identical in the FY2023,
FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks — "A Paying User is a user that has purchased or
renewed a Grindr subscription and/or purchased a premium add-on… We calculate
Average Paying Users by adding up the number of Paying Users in each day and
then dividing that number by the number of days in the relevant measurement
period… Duplicate Paying Users may exist if the same individual holds more
than one Grindr subscription during the same period." The only edits between
2023 and 2025 are "in the same day" → "on the same day" and "one Average
Paying User" → "one Paying User." **The disclosed definition did not change**,
and it has always been a daily count that dedupes within a day only. What
*did* change: the FY2025 10-K adds a risk factor — absent from both prior
10-Ks — stating "From time to time, we receive inquiries, subpoenas, and
demands for documents or information from various governmental and regulatory
authorities in connection with investigations or reviews of our business
practices, **financial reporting, and public disclosures**," and warning that
"public reporting about investigations… including **reports by short sellers**
… may adversely affect the trading price of our securities." That is not an
admission; it is the company adding language covering precisely this scenario
after the report. Whether an SEC inquiry exists is not disclosed anywhere
(§6 #1). Revenue is audited and unaffected either way — if APU is overstated,
ARPPU is understated by the same factor and the $138.1M top line does not
move.

**The buyback is the whole per-share story, and it was funded with debt at
prices above today's.** Plan-to-date through 2026-06-30: $597.8M deployed,
37,647,633 shares retired, average $15.88 — versus $15.585 today. The split
matters: $450.5M of open-market repurchases at **$17.93**, then $147.2M
through prepaid written puts, ASRs and forward repurchases at $11.49–$12.23.
The 2025 tranche destroyed value; the 2026 tranche created it. Funding: the
term loan was upsized to $400M and fully drawn 2025-12-16 (maturity extended
to 2031-01-01, Term SOFR + 2.75–3.25%), the revolver raised to $200M and
undrawn. Consequences visible in the numbers: total debt $389.6M, cash
$6.5M, book equity **−$11.7M**, and Q2 interest expense of $6.5M against
$3.6M a year ago (+83%). This is why net income grew 6.6% on 32.5% revenue
growth. About $32M of ASR/FRT forwards remain to settle in Q3 2026, which
retires a further ~2M shares for cash already paid — worth roughly 8bp on
the implied return, immaterial.

**The controlling holder went from buyer to seller in seven months.** The
sequence is entirely primary. October 2025: Zage and James Lu propose $18.00
per share. 2025-11-26: the Special Committee terminates engagement citing
financing uncertainty; the proposers withdraw and Zage writes that his
intention "in lieu of a bid to privatize the Issuer, is to continue to
purchase additional shares of the Issuer in the market," and that he will
"strongly recommend… a large and incremental repurchase." 2026-06-17: he
adopts a Rule 10b5-1 plan with Morgan Stanley for the sale of up to
**6,000,000 shares**, commencing 2026-09-16 after the cooling-off period and
expiring 2027-03-15. Six million shares is 3.5% of shares outstanding and
13.5% of the 44.3M float; against 1.73M shares of average daily volume it is
mechanically absorbable over six months, so the signal matters more than the
supply. Note the bid was never a valuation: it died for want of committed
financing, and the independent directors declined to bless it.

**Insider activity since the 2026-08-06 print is one-directional.** Form 4s:
CLO Zachary Katz sold 10,172 shares at $15.83 on 2026-08-17; director Daniel
Baer sold 3,500 at $15.65 on 2026-08-19; Form 144 filed 2026-08-19. All
10b5-1, all small, no purchases. The only post-call 13D/A (2026-08-11) was
filed to report that buybacks lifted Zage from ~52% to 54.9%.

**Short interest is large and rising.** 8,694,709 shares short, up from
6,901,404 the prior month — 5.0% of shares outstanding but **19.6% of the
float**, 5.0 days to cover. A float that small with that much short interest
cuts both ways and I am not going to pretend to know which.

**Litigation is real but unquantified.** UK group action in the High Court:
10,041 claimants in the first claim plus 1,964 in a second, alleging unlawful
processing of personal data 2009–2020. The Israeli class action settlement,
approved July 2025, drew objections from the Israeli Attorney General in

~$6.5M) was paid February 2026; Grindr disputes the interest. Litigation-
related costs ran $2.9M in Q2 2026 against $0.8M a year earlier. No provision
quantum is disclosed for the UK matter, so it is an equity claim of unknown
size that I have *not* haircut from the base (§6 #2).

**The asset-light claim survives the commitments footnote.** Checked
deliberately, because the whole valuation leans on a near-zero capex base.
10-Q Note 14 discloses litigation only — no purchase obligations, no
take-or-pay, no capacity reservations. Total operating lease liabilities are
$3.275M with a 2.4-year weighted average term, plus one uncommenced 5-year
lease with $3.130M of minimum payments. Capex was $462k on $510M of revenue.
The business genuinely is capital-free.

**The AI operating-leverage claim is management's own unaudited estimate.**
The CEO says engineering output rose "approximately 2.5x from July 2025 to
April 2026 with minimal growth in the technical team," worth ~200 avoided
engineers and ~$60M of annual cost. Asked for methodology he answered: "the
numbers actually came out to a 3.5x more, but then we reduced that number to
2.5x because we just thought it was unreasonable to expect things to have
changed that much." That is a self-graded metric with no external anchor. It
is corroborated indirectly — 178 employees generating $2.86M of revenue each,
H1 headcount below plan — but I do not credit the 2.5x itself.

## 4. Valuation

**Inputs.** Levered TTM free cash flow of $154,017,000 (`ncfo` $154,479,000
+ `capex` −$462,000) against **market cap** $2,708,125,369; net debt is zero
by the pairing rule, because this flow is post-interest and the $389.6M of
term debt has already been served out of it. Pairing it against the $3.09B
enterprise value would re-count that debt and print ~6.4% instead. From that
flow I deduct the TTM stock-compensation run-rate of $62,677,000, giving a
base of **$91,340,000** — compensation is not cash the owner keeps, and this
name adds back an unusually large slug of it (40.7% of reported FCF, 12.3%
of revenue). No minority-interest haircut (none consolidated), no pension,
no excess-cash adjustment ($6.5M of cash is 0.24% of the cap), no acquisition
charge (no acquisitions). The UK group action is an unquantified equity claim
left un-haircut, which biases the implied return *upward*.

**Hurdle.** rf 4.52% + beta 0.8 × ERP 4.28% = **7.94%** (Damodaran, implied
ERP as of 2026-08-01, trailing-12-month with adjusted payout; risk-free is
the 4.52% he pairs with it). The regression beta on the statistics page is
**0.21** — far below the 0.8–1.2 stable band and exactly the artifact the
band exists to catch: a 25.5% float, 19.6% short interest and a 49.9% ATM
implied vol are not a low-risk security. Floored to 0.8, and stated. For
reference, the raw beta would give a 5.41% hurdle (which 7.28% clears by
186bp) and beta 1.2 gives 9.66% (which even the bull path fails); Damodaran's
absolute companion — a mature company's cost of equity ≈ rf + 4.5% = 9.02% —
sits above the floored figure, so 7.94% is generous, not harsh.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment and terminal ROE.* At the base, reinvestment rate = 1 −
  91.3/95.6 = 4.44% and implied terminal ROE = 2.0%/4.44% = **45.0%**, some
  37 points above the hurdle and far beyond Damodaran's "more than ~5 points
  above is tough to do." The defence is genuine but narrow: this business
  needs essentially no capital ($462k of capex on $510M of revenue), so the
  arithmetic marginal return on the trivial amount reinvested really is very
  high — which is exactly why the company returns cash instead. The honest
  reading is that terminal growth here is repricing, not real expansion,
  which is why I hold terminal growth at 2.0% (inflation) rather than
  reaching for more. Note the two rows that use the $113.2M attack base
  trip the tool's *growth without reinvestment* warning (FCF ≥ earnings);
  restating base earnings upward by the same $21.9M leaves the answer at
  8.49% but pushes implied terminal ROE to 54.7%, worse on this check.
- *Market share.* Compounding 12% off the $540M FY26 guide puts revenue at
  **$952M in FY2031**. Holding advertising at the guided ~15%, that is $809M
  of direct revenue — on a 15M MAU base and an ARPPU of $35/month (+32% from
  today) it needs 1.93M average paying users, i.e. **12.9% paying
  penetration**, up from ~9.3% now and 7.1% in 2023. That is roughly the
  historical rate of 0.7pp a year, sustained for five more years with no
  price-elasticity break and no MAU decline. The 18% path implies $1,235M and
  ~16.7% penetration, or MAU growing to ~20M against a three-year trend
  decelerating toward zero. The bull case needs both levers, and management
  has told us only one of them is working.
- *Terminal risk from Item 1A.* The dominant structural risk that bears on
  year-10 cash flows is not competition — it is that Apple and Google are the
  sole distribution channel *and* the sole payment rail, take roughly a
  quarter of gross revenue (gross margin pinned at ~74% for five years
  through every pricing change), can restrict or delist at discretion, and
  can be pressured by governments to remove the app in hostile jurisdictions.
  A 2.0% terminal rate survives that only as "keeps pace with prices, grows
  no real share of a channel it does not own." Anything higher would be
  asserting durability over a gate Grindr cannot control, so 2.0% stands and
  is not raised. It is also below the 4.52% risk-free cap the tool enforces.
- *Distribution clamp.* Damodaran's 2026 update puts the US median cost of
  capital at 7.79% with 80% of firms between 5.26% and 9.88%. The base
  case's 7.28% sits below the median, inside the band. Not a strong-pass
  signal on its own; combined with a below-hurdle spread, it says the price
  offers no compensation for this risk stack.
- *Precision.* ATM IV is 49.9% — a hair under the 50% line at which whole
  percents become mandatory. Treat every figure above as ±100bp at best; the
  spread, not the level, is the finding.
