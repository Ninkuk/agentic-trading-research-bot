## 2. Business

**Created:** A general contractor or owner building a data center, hospital,
casino, fab or substation needs a licensed electrical/mechanical
subcontractor who can put 500 union tradespeople on a site on schedule and
finish on budget — 60–70% of a data center's construction cost is E&M work.
Everus sells that execution certainty through ~30 locally branded operating
companies (Wagner-Smith, International Line Builders, Bombard, etc.) with
IBEW relationships that let it scale labor up and down; 4,000 customers
across 44,000 projects in 2025. Customers come back because the local
company delivered last time — the 10-K's own framing is "repeat business,
negotiated or semi-negotiated work."

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms. (1) Fixed-price contracts — 52% of
2025 revenue (53% of E&M) — where the spread between bid and actual cost is
the profit; this is where the 2025–26 margin expansion came from (E&M gross
margin 14.9% Q2-26 vs 13.0% Q2-25, part of it "project timing" and 7.5% of
revenue from favorable estimate changes). (2) Cost-reimbursable work — 43%
(46% of E&M) — a fee on labor and materials, lower margin but no overrun
risk. (3) T&D master service agreements with utilities, unit-priced,
plus equipment rental and manufacturing of line-construction tooling
(Wagner-Smith) — a 14% EBITDA-margin segment with $388M backlog. Off-site
prefabrication (and now Epsilon) is a cost lever inside (1), not a separate
revenue line.

**Protected:** Thin. The protections that exist are (a) local union
relationships and training pipelines that a new entrant cannot buy quickly
(85% of employees unionized; ~10,000 at peak), (b) bonding capacity and a
track record that lets it win negotiated rather than bid work, and (c)
customer switching friction on multi-year campus programs. None stops
EMCOR, Comfort Systems, Quanta, MYR or a well-capitalized private from
bidding the next building; the 10-K calls the market "highly fragmented."
Returns (ROIC 28% TTM) are cycle-high returns on a business with 1.5%
capex/revenue, not evidence of a moat — the excess return is exactly what
draws capacity. Honest answer: no moat beyond execution reputation.

**Control:** One class of common, one share one vote, no controlling holder.
BlackRock 11.9% and Vanguard 11.0% (proxy, as of 2026-03-20); insiders
0.3%. Majority voting for directors, no rights plan (proxy). The MDU tax
matters agreement restricted stock issuances/acquisitions for two years
post-separation (to 2026-10-31) and carries an uncapped Section 355(e)
indemnity to MDU — a takeover or large equity issuance before then risks
that indemnity. Nothing forecloses an activist.
