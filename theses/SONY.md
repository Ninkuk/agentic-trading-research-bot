## 2. Business

**Created:** PlayStation gives 125 million monthly active accounts (June
2026, record for the month) a curated, stable, subsidised-hardware game
environment and the network that sells them software, add-ons and
subscriptions; Sony Music gives artists and songwriters global
distribution, marketing and a catalog machine (45,000 songs added in July
with Recognition Music Group) and gives streaming platforms the catalog
they cannot license around; Sony Pictures and Crunchyroll (21M+ paid
subscribers) supply franchises and anime; the ET&S arm sells cameras,
headphones and TVs on the brand; and Sony Semiconductor sells the image
sensors in most premium smartphones, cameras and an increasing share of
cars, where its stacked-sensor process is the incumbent. The customer
surplus is clearest in games (a $549 console that loses or barely earns
money at the hardware line) and in music (the artist's alternative is a
smaller label with worse distribution).

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms. (1) G&NS: a platform take on
third-party digital software and add-on content (¥485B in Q1), PlayStation
Plus and other network subscriptions (¥209B, +21%), first-party software,
and hardware sold near cost — FY2025 segment operating income ¥463B (¥602B
ex one-time charges). (2) Music: recorded-music streaming royalties (¥237B
in Q1, +10% in dollars), physical/live/merchandising, music-publishing
licensing (¥116B, +8% in dollars), and anime/game applications (Visual
Media and Platform) — ¥447B. (3) Pictures: theatrical and TV licensing plus
Crunchyroll subscriptions — ¥105B, structurally the weakest. (4) ET&S:
device margin on cameras and audio, with the display business being moved
into a TCL partnership at ¥20B of FY2026 implementation cost — ¥159B.
(5) I&SS: sensor unit sales where price is set by mix and process
leadership — ¥357B on ¥2,152B of sales. Sony's FY2025 sales were 32.6%
United States, 22.7% Europe, 13.6% Asia-Pacific, 11.4% China, 10.7% Japan,
9.1% other (20-F FY2025 geographic note).

**Protected:** two real mechanisms and three thin ones. PlayStation's
protection is the installed base itself — 95.3M PS5s shipped through June
2026 — plus the exclusive first-party studios and the switching cost of a
digital library; it does not protect against a platform transition, which
is exactly the FY2027–28 question. Music's protection is the catalog: a
song's rights are perpetual, the three majors control most of the
streaming catalog, and Sony keeps buying (EMI, Queen, Pink Floyd, the
Michael Jackson estate stake, Recognition). I&SS's protection is process
know-how and fab scale in stacked CMOS sensors — real, but it is a
single-product supplier to a single dominant customer, and the TSMC JV is
an admission that the next node is too expensive to carry alone. Pictures
and ET&S have brands, not moats. Written down: the games moat is a
franchise-and-installed-base moat that resets once a generation; the
sensor moat is a capital moat whose capital Sony no longer wants to fund
alone.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per 100-share unit, no
controlling holder. Largest reported holders: BlackRock Japan 8.53%
(December 2024 report), Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Asset Management 5.88%
(September 2025), Nomura Asset Management 5.01% (2020); insiders 0.03%;
institutions 60.8%; each ADS is one common share and 523M shares (8.5%)
sit in ADR form. The Articles contain no anti-takeover provisions (20-F
Item 10.B). Formally nothing is foreclosed; practically an unsolicited
foreign bid for a ¥21T Japanese national champion runs into the Foreign
Exchange and Foreign Trade Act review, so the activist path is board
engagement, not a takeover.
