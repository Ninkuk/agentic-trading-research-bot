## 2. Business

**Created:** The P&C industry's shared actuarial infrastructure. Carriers contribute claims and
policy data and get back what none could build alone: the industry-wide contributory database
(claims db: 1.9B records, ~193k new claims/day per the 2025 10-K), standardized forms/rules/
loss costs filed with state regulators, catastrophe models (re-engineered US Tropical Cyclone
model + Synergy Studio cloud platform launched June 2026), Xactware claims estimating, and
anti-fraud analytics. The customer's alternative is building a decades-deep dataset that
requires every *other* carrier's cooperation — that surplus is the product.

**Captured:** 83% subscriptions (+8% OCC in Q2'26, on +9.3% prior year), typically multi-year,
with price realization through added value (Core Lines Reimagine: 7 new modules in Q2, 25
planned for 2026); 17% transactional usage fees (−4.2% OCC in Q2). Adjusted EBITDA margin
57.5% in Q2 (TTM 56.3%); GAAP operating margin 43.9% TTM; FCF margin 39.4%. (Q2'26 call +
10-Q + stockanalysis statistics.)

**Protected:** A mechanism, not a checkbox: contribution is the price of access, so the moat
compounds with every submitted claim; regulatory embedding (Verisk loss costs sit inside state
rate filings); Xactware is the claims-workflow standard. Onboarding is still running — 8 new
Core Lines contributors and 10 excess-&-surplus contributors in H1 2026 (Q2 call). The 10-K's
own top structural risk is the mirror image: data-supplier agreements are short-term, some
suppliers are competitors, and exclusivity or withdrawal would be the moat failing.

## 5. Falsifiers

Sell (or don't reopen) on any of:
- A contributor bloc withdrawal, consortium bypass, or rival exclusive with a major data
  supplier (10-K risk language turning into an 8-K/news fact).
- Subscription OCC growth below 7% for two consecutive quarters.
- Transactional revenue still declining through a *normal*-weather half (that would make the
  decline structural, refuting condition 3).
- Nationwide class certification in the telematics MDL, or any settlement/judgment
  disclosure suggesting >$500M exposure or a structural restriction on insurer data use.
- Leverage above 3× to fund buybacks, or a large levered acquisition.

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **Premium-linkage sensitivity of subscriptions** — how much soft-market premium
   deceleration flows into renewal escalators is not quantified in any filing. Would come
   from contract-structure disclosure Verisk doesn't give. Doesn't kill; it is why growth
   ≥7% can't be credited, only watched (Q3 print 2026-10-28).
2. **Cyclical vs structural split of the transactional decline** — not disclosed. Bounded:
   transactional is 17% of revenue, so even a permanent −4% drift there costs ~0.7pp of
   total growth; degrades the return, doesn't kill the thesis.
3. **AI's net effect on the moat** — unquantifiable today by the CEO's own statement. The
   valuation doesn't price AI upside, so the thesis needs only "not net-negative"; still
   unverifiable.
4. **Litigation magnitude** — the company itself cannot estimate it (10-Q). Tail risk, not
   priced, not boundable. The absence doesn't kill the thesis; the *coupling* (see kill
   attack below) is what would.

**Kill-thesis: UNPROVEN (conditions=6, refuted=0, unknown=4).** The attack that came closest:
the litigation–flywheel coupling — telematics-MDL discovery surfacing data-sharing practices
that simultaneously cost damages *and* make carriers skittish about contributing data, hitting
conditions 1 and 5 at once. No current evidence it is happening; it is the scenario that turns
two independent unknowns into one correlated failure. Flip evidence: two more quarters of
subscription OCC ≥8% with transactional stabilized in normal weather, plus MDL resolution
without structural data-use restriction.
