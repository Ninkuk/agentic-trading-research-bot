## 2. Business

**Created:** a curated general-merchandise store for "busy families" —
1,995 US stores (250.5M retail sq ft) that combine a full grocery line in
the large formats with owned-brand apparel, home, beauty and toys at a
"expect more, pay less" price point (10-K Item 1). The customer surplus is
one-trip convenience plus style at mass prices: owned and exclusive brands
are ~30% of merchandise sales and carry higher margins than national-brand
equivalents (10-K Item 1A). Stores fulfil 97.6% of all merchandise sales,
including two-thirds of digital via same-day options (10-K MD&A), which is
what makes $35 same-day delivery economic.

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms, from the 10-K/10-Q revenue note:
(1) merchandise sales, $102.7B FY2025 — six categories, food & beverage
the largest at $24.1B, apparel $15.7B and home $15.6B the two that have
shrunk two years running; (2) Roundel retail-media advertising, $915M
FY2025 (+41% YoY), Q2 FY2026 $279M (+29%), gross billings +~20% — vendor
money spent to reach Target's traffic, near-100% incremental margin;
(3) Target Circle 360 membership revenue (+40% YoY in Q2) and Target+
marketplace commissions (GMV +40%); (4) TD Bank credit-card profit sharing,
$522M FY2025 and falling ($667M FY2023); (5) shop-in-shop and amenity rent
(Starbucks, CVS pharmacies, Apple). The three growth lines together were
$2.06B of FY2025 net sales, ~2%, but a much larger share of profit.

**Protected:** partially, and the mechanism is specific rather than a label.
The 30% owned-brand mix (Cat & Jack, Good & Gather, Threshold, Heyday) is
the one thing Walmart and Amazon cannot stock, and the limited-time design
collaborations (LoveShackFancy in Q2 was the largest in company history)
are a traffic engine competitors have not replicated. Against that: the
other 70% of the basket is national brands sold cheaper at Walmart and
faster at Amazon, food is a traffic driver Target runs at a structural
price disadvantage to Walmart/Costco/Aldi, and the 2025 boycott showed the
brand can be damaged by a corporate-policy decision with no product change
at all. The Item 1A text itself concedes the point: digital comparison
shopping means "decisions based solely on price or convenience, which could
limit our ability to differentiate". Verdict on protection: a real but
narrow moat around ~30% of sales; no moat around the rest.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share, no controlling
holder (DEF 14A 2026-04-27: "Target common stock is the only class of voting
shares outstanding"). State Street 7.9% and BlackRock 7.2% are the only >5%
holders. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist; Item 1A lists
"shareholder activism" as a live risk. Brian Cornell moved from CEO to
Executive Chair on 2026-02-01; Michael Fiddelke (ex-CFO/COO) is CEO.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative over five years, recovering in
the trailing year.** Fiscal years end late Jan/early Feb; TTM = Q3 FY2025
through Q2 FY2026 and includes the $994M refund.
