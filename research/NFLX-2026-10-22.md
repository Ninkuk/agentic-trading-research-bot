## 2. Business

**Created:** a single subscription that answers "what's on tonight" for a
household in ~190 countries — a weekly cadence of exclusive originals
(series, films, now live NFL/boxing), licensed second-run libraries, and the
best discovery UI in the category. The member surplus is real: management
says US members pay the least per hour viewed of any SVOD, in some cases half
(Q3 call, Peters); churn is "industry leading" and improved y/y in Q3.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (a) Tiered monthly subscription
(ad tier, standard, premium) with a price ladder raised every ~1–2 years per
market — the dominant revenue line; ARM rises with each round. (b) Paid
sharing (extra-member fees) — folded into subscription. (c) Advertising on
the ad tier: CPM sales through its own ad-tech stack (Netflix Ads Suite) plus
programmatic partners (Q3: CTV marketplace deals, MercadoLibre, AdForm);
~$2.7B in 2026, ~5% of revenue. (d) Small lines: licensing-out, theatrical
(≈30 films a year, now reporting grosses), consumer products/live
experiences — "not material" per the 10-K. A newer fifth, TF1-style
aggregation of third-party networks inside the app, is early.

**Protected:** scale economics on a fixed cost. Content is a fixed cost
spread over ~325M+ paid memberships; Netflix can outspend any single rival
per title while spending less per member, and its audience makes it the best
buyer *and* the best licensor-of-choice for rivals' libraries (Disney, Sony,
Universal, Globo deals in 2026). Retention data and the recommender compound
on top. What it does **not** protect against: attention substitution by free
video whose content cost is borne by creators (YouTube) — the scale advantage
is over other paid streamers, not over free platforms.

**Control:** one class of common stock, one vote per share (10-K charter
exhibit); insiders own 0.58% (stockanalysis). No controlling holder. Delaware
§203, no written consent, advance-notice bylaws and blank-check preferred
(10-K Item 1A) deter but do not foreclose an unsolicited bid; an activist path
is open in principle.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $29.7B (2021) → $31.6B
(2022) → $33.7B (2023) → $39.0B (2024) → $45.2B (2025) → $49.7B (TTM, +67% vs
2021); operating income $6.19B → $5.63B → $6.95B → $10.42B → $13.33B →
$15.56B (+151%). Margin 20.9% → 31.3%. Operating income has grown roughly 2.3×
as fast as revenue since 2021 (stockanalysis income statement).
