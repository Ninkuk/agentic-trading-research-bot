12% anyway.** Both halves are the finding.

(6.8–7.7% growth) against the June guide of $13.40–13.55B, and adjusted EBITDA
margin 6.7–6.8% against 6.6–6.8% — the low end lifted 10bp, implying $912M of
adjusted EBITDA at the midpoint and "over 100 basis points" of y/y expansion
(Q2 call, 2026-09-09). Net adds were 208,000 (165,000 excluding the 43,000

The market disagreed: $23.27 (Sep 8 close) → $20.75 (Sep 9, −10.8% on 21.6M
shares) → $20.44 (Sep 11). Five facts the trigger did not test explain it, and
they are why the pass survives:

1. **The raise is bought, not earned.** The total-guide midpoint moved
   $13.475B → $13.515B, +$40M or +0.3%, and the CFO attributed it to SmartPak
   and Modern Animal "performing ahead of initial expectations." The *organic*
   range was narrowed by deleting the severe downside case, not raised: FY
   organic growth is 5.5–6.3%, Q3 organic 5.3–6.2%.
2. **The beat had no operating content.** Adjusted EBITDA of $226.7M beat a
   6.3–6.4% margin guide, but ~$10M was timing (tariff refunds received early,
   rebates shifted out of H2) and >$5M discrete (gift-card breakage, inventory
   adjustments, vendor-funded merchandising). The CFO's own words: these
   "accounted for essentially all of the adjusted EBITDA outperformance." Two of
   the three items are borrowed from the second half.
3. **The seven-quarter beat streak ended.** Adjusted diluted EPS $0.36 against
   a $0.36 estimate — the first non-beat in the eight quarters Robinhood
   carries (the prior seven beat by $0.08, $0.01, $0.08, $0.09, $0.07, $0.04).
   The prior thesis argued the beat headline "carries no information" because
   guidance is managed. That was right, and it cuts the other way: the
   informative event was the streak breaking, and ex the ~$15M of timing and
   discrete items (≈$0.03/share after tax on 410.1M diluted shares) the quarter
   was a miss.
4. **EBITDA stopped converting to earnings.** Adjusted EBITDA rose $43.4M y/y;
   adjusted net income rose $7.7M (8-K Ex-99.1). The wedge is term-loan
   interest, acquired-intangible amortization, and tax. Adjusted EPS grew $0.03
   and the buyback did much of that.
5. **The capex base moved.** FY capex is now guided at 1.5–2% of net sales
   ($202–271M) against TTM capex of $149.1M (1.14% of sales). The prior thesis's
   ~1%-of-sales capex assumption is stale by 50–100%.

Net: the thesis is *better* on everything the trigger tested (share gains,
Autoship, buyback, guide) and *worse* on two things it did not (earnings
conversion, the capex base). Price fell 9.6% since the last run; the honest
owner-earnings math landed in roughly the same place. Still a pass.

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $20.445.** kill-thesis: **SOUND** — conditions=7 (4 probable,
3 plausible), refuted=0, unknown=0, pending=3, not_obtained=0.

**p(beat SPY, 63 td): 0.42** · kill-thesis: 0.40. **Disputed expectation:** the
market is paying roughly 8–10%/yr on trailing levered FCF with mid-single-digit
growth — it has accepted the share-gain story and refused to pay for the 10%
long-term EBITDA-margin target. What revises it: a Q3 print where organic
growth holds ≥5.5% *and* adjusted net income grows in line with adjusted EBITDA
(the Q2 wedge closes), or a price near $18.

Chewy is a good-not-great business at a price that is now nearly fair and still
not cheap. The share-gain engine is real and re-verified this quarter — organic
+5.7% against a broadly flat consumables market, Autoship compounding faster
than the company, 70bp of SG&A leverage, a genuine automation-and-AI cost
program. What the quarter also showed is that adjusted EBITDA growth is no
longer reaching the owner: interest on a new $600M term loan, amortization on
$953M of acquisitions, and a capex base stepping from ~1% to 1.5–2% of sales
all sit between the two. On the base that nets out $297.9M of stock
compensation — the base the buyback is spending real cash to neutralize — the
price implies about 7%/yr against a 10.85% hurdle. That is a 379bp shortfall on
a mild-moat retailer with 11.7% of its shares floating, 44% of that float sold
short, and 52% implied vol. It needs a margin of safety, not a rounding error.

**Closest attack:** *the second-half free-cash-flow arithmetic.* H1 FCF was
$160.3M on $479.8M of H1 adjusted EBITDA — 33.4% conversion, down from 41.1%
a year ago. Holding the ~80% full-year guide against the $912M EBITDA midpoint
requires $570M of H2 FCF on $432M of H2 adjusted EBITDA, or **132% conversion**,
while H2 capex roughly doubles-to-triples y/y under the 1.5–2%-of-sales guide.
Last year's H2 delivered 119% on holiday payables. The bull case needs
condition 4, because scenario B is the only run that clears the hurdle and it
cannot clear it on a shrinking cash base. It does not quite land: 119% is a
real seasonal precedent and 132% is a stretch, not a contradiction — which is
why condition 4 is PENDING the Q3 10-Q rather than refuted.

Load-bearing conditions of the bull case:

1. *probable* — **Share gains continue at 2–3× the category.** Organic +5.7%
   against a broadly flat consumables market; Autoship +9.3% at 84.6% of sales;
   208k net adds. Evidence: 8-K Ex-99.1 and Q2 call, 2026-09-09.
2. *probable* — **Structural SG&A leverage keeps compounding.** 70bp of y/y
   leverage in Q2, >50% of volume through automated facilities, AI savings in
   the low tens of millions in FY26 scaling to ~$50M annualized in FY27.
   Evidence: Q2 call.
3. *plausible* — **The 10%+ long-term adjusted EBITDA margin arrives without
   eating the growth.** Management asserts confidence "stronger at this point"
   but explicitly declined to quantify 2027 reinvestment and warned against
   "mechanically layer[ing]" AI savings onto the margin path. Settled by the
   FY27 guide at the Q4 print (~2027-03-24). PENDING.
4. *plausible* — **FCF conversion holds near 80% despite the capex step-up.**
   Management restated "roughly 80%" for the full year while guiding capex to
   1.5–2% of sales; H1 FCF was $160.3M, +3.7% y/y. Settled directionally by the
   Q3 10-Q (2026-12-09) and fully by the FY26 10-K (~2027-03). PENDING.
5. *plausible* — **The $953M of acquisitions does not destroy value.** Stated
   as a floor, not a return: nothing dated will ever disclose what SmartPak and
   Modern Animal earned on their cost — the 10-Q's business-combination note
   declines ASC 805 pro-forma because the deals are "not material to the
   Company's consolidated results," and Chewy reports one segment, so there is
   no acquired-segment margin either. What is dated is the impairment test, and
   it matters: goodwill plus intangibles went $39.7M → $483.8M against $370.5M

6. *probable* — **The buyback keeps absorbing the SBC wedge.** $400M in H1
   against $159.3M of SBC-and-related taxes; diluted count 410.1M vs 428.4M
   y/y. Evidence: 10-Q, 2026-09-09.
7. *plausible* — **Cash flows accrue to public shareholders on ordinary
   terms.** Not boilerplate here: BC Partners holds 43.9% of shares and a
   litigated history of proposing to push PetSmart-sale tax liabilities into
   Chewy (*Gilbert v. BC Partners*, Del. Ch. 2024-1165-KSJM, settled 2026-04-06
   for $29.5M cash **to** Chewy), plus ongoing related-party purchases from
   BC-Partners-controlled PetLab ($8,199,604 FY2025-to-record-date). The
   evidence today is that the board's special litigation committee pursued the
   claim and recovered, and that the related-party flow is 0.06% of revenue.
   Re-testable at each proxy (next ~2027-05).

**Dominant shared risk factor:** US discretionary consumer spend at the

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The raise is acquisition-funded (the crux).** Total guide +$40M at the
  midpoint, all of it attributed to SmartPak and Modern Animal beating plan;
  the organic range narrowed only because the severe downside scenario was
  removed. Organic 5.5–6.3% FY, 5.3–6.2% Q3 — the core is decelerating from the
  7.7% total Q1 printed, and the composition of "7.3% growth" is now
  materially inorganic. This is the single largest gap between the headline the
  trigger tested and what actually happened.
- **The beat that wasn't.** ~$10M timing (early tariff refunds, rebates pulled
  from H2) + >$5M discrete (gift-card breakage, inventory adjustments, vendor
  funding) = essentially all of the EBITDA upside, per the CFO. Q3 gross margin
  is guided down sequentially as those normalise, and fuel is a mid-single-digit
  million headwind through year end.
- **EBITDA-to-earnings wedge.** Adj EBITDA +$43.4M y/y; adjusted net income
  +$7.7M. Term-loan interest (weighted-average rate 5.41%, effective 5.66%),
  acquired-intangible amortization on $483.8M of goodwill-plus-intangibles, and
  a guided 24–26% tax rate against a 22.8% TTM effective rate are all now
  standing between the operating line and the owner. This is the mechanism the
  September selloff priced, and it is structural, not timing.
- **Capex step-up.** FY guide 1.5–2% of net sales versus $149.1M TTM (1.14%).
  Against the FY26 revenue midpoint that is $202–271M — a $53–122M annual

  and vet clinics (the 10-Q names all three). Feeds scenario D in §4.

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal-ROE warning, answered.* A, B and D all print
  "growth without reinvestment" because levered FCF ($568.1M) exceeds net
  income ($273.7M). The add-back doing that work is SBC, which is compensation
  and not cash the owner keeps — so the answer here is not a cash-earnings
  rerun (there is no large acquired-intangible amortization behind the gap yet)
  but scenario C, where the base is FCF net of SBC and the warning disappears.
  C's `implied_terminal_roe` of 184.6% is an artifact of the identity — base FCF
  ≈ base earnings leaves a 1.3% reinvestment rate — not a claim about returns on
  new capital. Terminal growth sits exactly at the breakeven, so the terminal
  value claims nominal repricing and zero real growth, which needs no
  reinvestment.
- *Market-share sentence.* Scenario B's 10% is FCF growth, margin-led; the
  revenue path underneath it is the company's own organic guide. At 5.5% for
  five years revenue goes $13.07B → $17.1B. The 10-K, citing Packaged Facts,
  puts the US pet market at $157B in 2025 growing ~4% through 2029 — about
  $199B by 2031. Chewy would be ~8.6% of US pet spend versus ~8.0% today:
  roughly 0.6 points of share over five years, not a bigger-than-the-market
  forecast.
- *Terminal growth vs the disclosed terminal risk.* The 10-K's dominant
  structural risk is competition in pet e-commerce and pet retail, Amazon and
  Walmart specifically. A 2.36% terminal rate survives it by asserting nothing
  beyond expected inflation. The disputable part of this valuation is B's
  five-year 10%, not the terminal.
- *Base-year cash tax.* TTM tax expense $80.9M, effective rate 22.81%, against

  base year is flattered by roughly 1.5–3 points of rate; the effect on the

  carried $552.8M of it. Scenario B's 10% FCF growth would be partly *bought* if
  the SmartPak/Modern Animal pattern repeats; A and D are the runs that pay for
  their own growth, and they are the ones that miss the hurdle.
- *Distribution clamp.* The US median cost of capital is ~7.8% with roughly 80%
