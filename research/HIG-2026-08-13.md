## 2. Business

**Created:** Three franchises. (1) *Business Insurance* (~$695M of $945M Q2'26
core earnings): P&C for small and mid-size US businesses. In Small Business the
customer gets fast, accurate, largely automated quoting (Spectrum BOP, E&S
binding) — agents consolidate flow onto the carrier that is easiest to transact
with, and HIG's underlying combined ratio there is 86.5 with 7% premium growth
(Q2'26 call). (2) *Personal Insurance*: auto/home for the 50+ market through an
**exclusive AARP license in place since 1984, running through Dec 31, 2032** —
$3.4B of 2025 earned premium, ~38M AARP members (10-K Item 1). (3) *Employee
Benefits*: group disability/life plus absence-and-leave administration, where
integrated leave management is the differentiator at the large-employer end
(7.4% core margin vs a 6–7% long-term target).

**Captured:** Underwriting profit (TTM P&C underlying combined ratios: BI 89.3,
PI 86.3) + float income: $73B+ investment portfolio yielding 4.7% ex-LPs, NII
$800M in Q2 alone (+22% YoY). Fee income from Hartford Funds is being monetized
— sold to Wellington, announced 6/3/2026, expected transaction NPV **$1.9B**
(8-K EX-99.1).

**Protected:** Small-commercial: workflow/data advantage — automation and
digital service that brokers consolidate onto, plus decades of small-business
loss data feeding risk selection; competitors can copy the ambition but not
quickly the loss history or the agent integration. Personal: the AARP
exclusivity is contractual through 2032. Benefits: absence management is
sticky, integrated into employer HR systems, persistency low-90s. None of these
stop Travelers or Chubb; they do stop *new entrants tomorrow*, and
small-commercial pricing (renewal +7%, flat q/q) held while national-account
property collapsed — evidence the moat lives where claimed.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** FY2021→FY2025: revenue
$22.4B→$28.4B (+27%); operating income $3.14B→$4.96B (+58%); net income
$2.35B→$3.82B (+62%); diluted EPS $6.64→$13.32 (+101%, buybacks compounding).
Combined-ratio improvement, not accounting mix.

## 5. Falsifiers

  strengthening > $200M, or strengthening that spreads into accident years
  2024–25, or total P&C prior-year development turning net *unfavorable* for
  two consecutive quarters. The pricing-above-trend claim failed; sell.
- **Break —** underwriting discipline: BI underlying combined ratio > 95 for
  two consecutive quarters absent an identified one-off. The franchise claim
  failed.
- **Shift —** pricing cycle: BI renewal written pricing ex-comp below ~4%
  while premium growth stays positive (growth bought with margin); revalue on
  a lower base.
- **Shift —** Wellington deal: deal breaks or proceeds materially below the
  $1.9B NPV; re-size the capital-return math.
- **Shift —** Employee Benefits: core margin below 6% (out of the stated band)
  for a full year; revalue.
