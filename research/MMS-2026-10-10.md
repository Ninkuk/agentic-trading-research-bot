## 2. Business

Unchanged from `MMS-2026-09-08.md` §2; restated briefly.

**Created:** governments get citizen-facing operations they must run but
cannot staff or scale — the 1-800-MEDICARE and marketplace contact centers,
Medicaid/SNAP eligibility processing, clinical assessments and appeals, and
the VA's compensation-claim medical exams — bought as measured,
surge-capable accountability (10-K Item 1).

**Captured:** (i) per-transaction/milestone fees on performance-based
contracts, 54% of revenue, with incentive/disincentive overlays (the VA
incentives were worth ~$0.35/quarter of EPS in FY26); (ii) fixed-price, 13%;
(iii) cost-plus and T&M for the rest, mostly technology work. Segments: U.S.
Federal 56% of FY25 revenue at a 16.5–17% margin, U.S. Services (states) 32%
at 9.5–10%, Outside the U.S. 11% at breakeven (10-K; Q3 release).

**Protected:** incumbency where switching costs are political as well as
operational — a re-bid contact center or exam network carries transition
risk the agency owns, which is why CMS withdrew its 2024 early rebid; plus a
booked clinician network (VES). It is not a moat against the customer: the
VA paused incentives unilaterally, a federal customer stretched payments, and
75–80% of new bids carry AI criteria a better-tooled rival could win on. The
protection is the finite cost of switching mid-program.

**Control:** one class of common, one vote per share, no controlling holder;
BlackRock and Vanguard largest (13G/As; a further 13G filed 2026-10-08,
filer not read); insiders 1.44% (stocks.db). Nothing forecloses a takeover
or an activist. PSUs pay on organic growth, adjusted EBITDA margin and FCF
(DEF 14A 2026-01-21).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive on a lower base.** Revenue $4.25B
(FY21) → $4.63B → $4.90B → $5.31B → $5.43B (FY25) → $5.25B TTM; operating
income $418M → $315M → $295M → $488M → $528M → $579M TTM (income-statement
route, carried from the 2026-09-08 read — no new quarter has been filed).
