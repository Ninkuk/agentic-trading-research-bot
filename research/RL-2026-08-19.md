## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS at $380.60.** kill-thesis: **SOUND** — conditions=5 (2 probable,
2 plausible, 1 possible), refuted=0, unknown=1.

Ralph Lauren is a durably excellent business — a 60-year brand still
compounding, with strongly positive operating leverage, 28% ROIC, net cash
excluding leases, a shrinking share count, and a nine-year elevation strategy
that is visibly working (AUR +15% in Q1 FY27 *with* unit growth, China +40%).
But at $380.60 the price already pays for all of it: across
conservative-to-generous assumptions the market implies a 6.0–8.5% annual
equity return against a 10.5–11.3% cost-of-equity hurdle. The quality is
real; the return at this price is not. Seven consecutive EPS beats and a
raised guide mean the market discovered this story before this run did.

**Closest attack:** the bull-side hurdle attack. The 5Y regression beta
(1.37) looks inflated — the 1Y and 2Y betas are 0.95 and 1.09 — and at
beta 1.0 with the US-only ERP, the optimistic run (raw TTM FCF, 8%×5)
narrows to −54bp. Only the everything-at-once configuration — raw TTM base
AND 12%×5 growth AND beta 1.0 AND the lower ERP — reaches +49bp, and that
run trips the solver's growth-without-reinvestment warning while doing it.
A pass that survives every configuration but the maximal one stands.

Load-bearing conditions (for owning it at this price, not for the pass):

1. *Probable* — brand elevation continues: mid-to-high single-digit AUR
   growth without global volume decline. Nine consecutive years of AUR
   growth (Q1 FY27 call); Q1 FY27 AUR +15% with unit growth; gross margin
   66.5% (FY22) → 70.3% (TTM).
2. *Probable* — operating margin expansion continues per the Drive plan.
   GAAP operating margin 10.4% → 15.9% FY22→FY26; FY27 guide raised to
   +60–80bp cc (from +40–60bp) on Aug 6.
3. *Plausible* — Asia/China sustains low-double-digit growth through the
   plan period. China +40% in Q1 but management itself guides moderation to
   mid-teens FY27 and low-double-digit over the 3-year plan; China is 10% of
   revenue (3% pre-COVID). No RL-specific counter-evidence, but the China
   luxury cycle base rate is poor and Item 1A names anti-American-sentiment
   and UFLPA risks.
4. *Plausible* — the tariff regime stays absorbable. Company assumes ~10%
   through H1 FY27 and high-teens reciprocal rates in H2, and raised margin
   guidance anyway; FY26 margins expanded through the actual tariffs.
   Post-SCOTUS IEEPA churn (refund status excluded from guidance) keeps

5. *Possible* — the brand survives the founder transition. Ralph Lauren is
   86 (10-K), Executive Chairman & CCO, and the 10-K's first
   business-and-operations risk factor is his loss. Louvet has run the
   company since 2017 with Ralph as CCO only — evidence the operating
   machine runs without him as CEO, but no disclosure can evidence the brand
   after him. Not load-bearing for the base case at a pass; it is the
   terminal UNKNOWN an owner would carry.

## 2. Business

**Created:** aspirational American-heritage style at accessible-luxury
prices — "inclusive luxury" spanning price points from Polo to Purple Label
across apparel, handbags, footwear, fragrance, home, and hospitality. The
customer buys entry to a 60-year coherent world (Wimbledon, polo, the
Hamptons) that no fast-fashion competitor can counterfeit as an identity;
1.5M new DTC customers added in Q1 FY27 alone, 70M+ social followers.

**Captured:** not one mechanism. (a) DTC retail and digital (~70% of
revenue, mix rising) at full price — the elevation strategy is literally a
capture upgrade: nine years of AUR growth, fewer promotions, off-price
deliberately shrunk; (b) quality wholesale (~9,500 doors) used consciously
as brand-discovery and recruitment; (c) licensing (other non-reportable
segments: $142.5M revenue at an 87% operating margin — nearly pure profit on
the trademark). Segment operating margins FY26: NA 21.8%, Europe 27.8%, Asia
27.4%.

**Protected:** the moat is the brand as an accumulated, non-replicable
asset: six decades of consistent iconography plus current, measured
desirability (rising NPS and luxury-perception scores, younger cohorts —
management-reported, so directionally credible but self-graded). The
mechanism protecting it is capital: ~8% of sales in marketing (up from 3.5%
a decade ago) spent on activations competitors cannot credibly copy
(Wimbledon, a 74M-livestream Beijing polo match), plus control of
distribution (door exits, full-price discipline) that keeps the price
architecture from eroding. What it does not protect against: fashion-cycle
irrelevance and founder mortality — both named in Item 1A, both real.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** FY22→FY26 revenue $6,218.5M →
$8,114.5M (+30.5%); operating income $647.2M → $1,287.3M (+98.9%); operating
margin 10.4% → 15.9%, TTM 16.2%. Income statement, stockanalysis
income-statement route (TTM column checked against `datekey`).

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **Post-founder brand trajectory.** No disclosure can evidence it; the
   closest observables are ten years of Louvet-era execution with Ralph as
   CCO only. Absence does not kill a pass; it caps how aggressive any
   future buy case's terminal can be.
2. **True steady-state capex.** TTM 3.3% of revenue vs the 4–5% guide vs
   FY26's 5.0%. Handled by normalizing the base; resolved by FY27 actuals
   (10-K, ~May 2027). Absence does not kill the thesis — both bounds were
   run.
3. **Tariff end-state and IEEPA refund size.** Policy-dependent,
   undisclosed by nature; guidance excludes refunds (conservative). A
   refund would be a one-time upside the valuation ignores; a worse regime
   is condition 4's shift falsifier. Not thesis-killing either way.
4. **Brand-equity metrics are management-reported.** NPS, luxury
   perception, new-customer quality have no independent source; treated as
   directional only. Does not kill the thesis — AUR and gross margin are
   the audited shadow of the same claim.
