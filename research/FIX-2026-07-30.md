# FIX — Comfort Systems USA — 2026-07-30

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**Pass at today's price ($1,697.80, market cap $59.6B).** An exceptionally
well-run mechanical/electrical contractor riding the AI data-center buildout —
but even on the sell-side dream path (three years of ~37% growth, margins never
reverting) the price implies only ~9–10%/yr, and on merely optimistic
assumptions ~7%; heroic assumptions should buy an excess return, not a
market-rate one. **kill-thesis verdict: SOUND** (for the pass) — 5 load-bearing
conditions, 0 refuted, 1 UNKNOWN. The attack that came closest: the
consensus-path reverse DCF (~9.5%), which makes the price defensible — just
uncompensated for 58% single-sector concentration at 69% implied vol.

## 2. Business

**Created** — designs, installs, and services mechanical (HVAC, piping,
plumbing, controls) and electrical systems: the most labor-intensive,
schedule-critical trades in nonresidential construction. For a data center,
cooling and power *are* the building. Customers get scarce skilled labor at
scale (25,000+ employees), schedule certainty, and — increasingly — modular
off-site prefabrication (17% of revenue, 3.5M sq ft capacity → 4M by year-end
→ ~5M by late summer 2027) that compresses build time. Customers pay ahead to
lock capacity: contract liabilities of $3.23B against contract assets of $145M
(10-Q) is the surplus made visible.

**Captured** — ~90% construction revenue on fixed-price/GMP contracts, priced
today with unusual power ("extraordinarily good payment terms because we have
extraordinarily good negotiating power" — CFO, Q2'26 call); ~10% service at
high margin. Q2'26: gross margin 25.9%, operating margin 17.1%. TTM revenue
$11.23B, operating income $1.85B, net income $1.43B.

**Protected** — the moat mechanism is skilled-labor scarcity plus decades-long
local reputations across ~45 operating companies, and modular capacity
co-designed with (and volume-committed by) two hyperscalers. Management on
modular competition: "our customers are not inducing other people to build this
to replace us. They're inducing other people to build this because they want
more than we'll build." The protection is real but **cyclical** — it exists
because demand exceeds industry capacity; in a downturn it inverts into bid
competition (10-K: "a large portion of our work is awarded through a bid
process. Consequently, price is…").

**Operating leverage (Phase 0 print): strongly positive.** Revenue FY2021→TTM:
$3.07B → $11.23B (3.7×); operating income $188M → $1.85B (9.8×); op margin
6.1% → 16.5%; diluted shares shrank 36.45M → 35.30M.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **FCF vs net income (the big one).** TTM FCF $2.16B vs net income $1.43B.
  Decomposition (cash-flow statement): +$1.59B TTM from growth in unearned
  revenue/billings in excess (Q2 alone +$836M), −$988M receivables growth.
  CFO on the call, unprompted honesty: "Over time, our cash flow will match
  our net income, plus a little… there is definitely an element of advanced
  cash" (his guess: ~⅓ of the surge). Contract liabilities $2.12B → $3.23B in
  six months. **Conclusion: headline FCF is float-inflated; owner FCF ≈ net
  income.** The float is a growth dividend — it reverses only if revenue
  shrinks, but it is not recurring owner earnings.
- **Margin quality.** Q2 gross margin includes net favorable estimate
  revisions (analyst on the call citing the 10-Q: ~7.7% of revenue impact,
  roughly double a year ago); Q1'26 had $43M of gains management itself called
  "outside the scope of the ordinary." Management: "We believe gross profit
  margins are likely to continue in the strong ranges that we have averaged in
  recent quarters" — i.e., even they guide flat, not up.
- **Concentration.** Technology = 58% of revenue (40% a year ago); industrial
  75%; one customer ≈ 12.8% of consolidated 2025 revenue (10-K Item 1A).
  Modular expansion is committed against volume guarantees from **two**
  hyperscalers; expansion to 5M sq ft is "overwhelmingly for existing
  customers." Frontier-lab/co-location pilots are small.
- **The tape proves the exposure.** After a 20% EPS beat (Jul 23), the stock
  fell 17% in four sessions during the Alphabet-report hyperscaler sell-off,
  then rebounded ~12% on Jul 30 as Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to
  $125–145B and Microsoft printed capex +84% YoY. FIX trades as a derivative
  of hyperscaler capex sentiment, not of its own prints.
- **Growth deceleration is guided.** Full-year 2026 same-store growth
  "mid-to-high 30s" implies 2H slowdown from 1H's 47%; CFO flagged "really
  heavy comparables… in particular in the fourth quarter."
- **Capex step-up.** Guided ~5% of revenue (~$560M/yr) vs $155M in FY2025 —
  buying buildings, robots, paint booths. Trailing capex understates forward.
  Claimed paybacks "within a year or two" (CFO). No take-or-pay/purchase
  obligations found in the 10-Q commitments note beyond leases and the
  (undrawn-heavy) credit facility.
- **Insider activity.** Form 144 sales May 2026 (~$1,900–2,000 range prices);
  a routine Form 4 grant cluster May 19; a Form 3 (new officer — COO Craig
  Sasser, effective Jul 1) on Jul 9. **No insider filings since the Jul 23
  print.** Management "likes our stock better than ever" but repurchases
  slowed as the price rose — actions are price-disciplined.
- **Dead ends.** No `data/composite.db` flag ever (FIX is far outside the
  microcap dislocation universe); `data/earnings.db` carries no FIX row;
  monitors had nothing. `docs` catalog's Pro-gated segments route not needed —
  10-Q segment tables sufficed. "Data center" appears zero times in the
  FY2025 10-K risk factors — the company's own risk list lags its actual risk.

## 5. Falsifiers (what would flip the pass to a buy)

1. **Price**: ≤ ~$1,100 (market cap ≈ $38B) restores a ~10% implied return on
   normalized FCF with the optimistic path — re-run the DCF then.
2. **Proof the margin is structural**: a fiscal year of ≤10% revenue growth
   with operating margin held ≥15% (margin surviving decelerating volume would
   refute the cyclical-peak condition).
3. **Concentration broken**: programmatic modular revenue from a third/fourth
   hyperscaler or frontier labs (watch the pilot contracts named on the Q2'26
   call), or technology share of revenue falling while totals grow.

## 6. UNKNOWNs

- **Structural vs cyclical split of the margin gain.** No disclosure separates
  modular margins from stick-built (segments are Mechanical/Electrical; the
  financials/segments route is Pro-gated and the 10-K doesn't split it).
  Absence does not kill the pass — the pass needs only that today's price
  already assumes no reversion — but it caps conviction in both directions.
- **Which customer is the 12.8%.** Not named in the 10-K (standard practice).
  Triangulation (Texas electrical + Texas modular driving backlog; "we sell
  directly to the hyperscalers") points at one of the two committed
  hyperscalers, but that is inference, not disclosure.
- **Advance-cash reversal timing.** If bookings flatten, unearned-revenue
  build stops and reported FCF converges down to (or below) net income; the
  magnitude per quarter is not forecastable from outside.
