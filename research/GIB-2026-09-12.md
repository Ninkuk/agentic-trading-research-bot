## 2. Business

**Created:** CGI takes over IT work that large enterprises and governments
cannot afford to break and will not staff themselves. Two thirds of the
value is boring and that is the point: application maintenance, managed
infrastructure, benefits and claims processing, core banking and utility
billing platforms — run under a multi-year contract with a service level,
with CGI absorbing the hiring, attrition and location risk. The remaining
third is project work: modernization, integration, consulting. What the
client buys that they cannot get elsewhere is a vendor already inside their
procurement, already cleared, already holding the institutional memory of a
system nobody documented. CGI's stated differentiator is a "local relationship
model" — client-facing leaders who live in the client's metro and carry the
P&L — paired with global delivery centres for cost. It is a real preference
being satisfied (proximity and accountability), and it is why CGI wins
government work: 94,000 people, and the government vertical is its largest.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, plus a fourth that is really the
return engine.

- *Managed IT and business process services (outsourcing)* — C$8,820.9M in
  FY2025, 55.4% of revenue. Multi-year contracted, backlog C$31.79B = 1.9×
  annual revenue. CGI capitalizes the transition cost of winning these
  (C$91.4M of "additions to contract costs" in nine months) and amortizes it
  across the deal, which is why the contract, not the quarter, is the unit
  of economics. Repriced at renewal.
- *Systems integration and consulting* — C$7,091.8M in FY2025, 44.6%. Time
  and materials or fixed price. Discretionary, cyclical, the first line cut.
- *Intellectual property solutions* — configurable "business platforms as a
  service" embedded inside service deals, named repeatedly as a margin and
  growth driver in the government vertical. Not separately sized in any
  disclosure; see §6.
- *Share repurchase* — C$1,976M in the trailing twelve months, against
  C$1,966M of owner free cash flow. Diluted share count fell from 224.4M to
  208.9M year over year (−6.9%). Adjusted diluted EPS grew 9.0% in Q3 on
  adjusted net earnings growth of 1.7%. Almost all of the per-share growth
  is the buyback.

**Protected:** honestly, thinly, and the honest answer matters more than a
label. What does defend it: incumbency in mission-critical managed services,
where the switching cost is a migration project the client's CIO does not
want; government accreditation, clearances and past-performance
qualification in the US federal, UK and Canadian markets; and the local
relationship model, which makes a procurement-led swap personal as well as
expensive. What does **not** defend it: price. There is no evidence anywhere
in the record of pricing power. The margin has sat at 16.1–16.5% for six
years, and the MD&A itself explains how that is achieved — by moving work
into Asia Pacific delivery centres, which it names as a *reason revenue
fell* in U.S. Commercial and State Government and in Canada while Asia
Pacific grew 9.7% in constant currency. CGI holds its margin by deflating
its own revenue. That is a cost moat, not a price moat, and it is exactly
the muscle the AI question tests.

**Control:** two classes. Class A subordinate voting, one vote, 182,952,649
shares as at 2026-07-24; Class B multiple voting, **ten votes**, 24,122,758
shares — a count that has not changed. As at 2025-12-09 the classes carried
44.40% and 55.60% of votes respectively, and the FY2025 Annual Information
Form states plainly that "the Company and its related subsidiaries are
controlled by Mr. Serge Godin, the Founder and Co-Chair of the Board of
Directors." Julie Godin is Executive Chair; Michael Godin is President,
Canada. Because the buyback retires only Class A, founder voting control
ratchets upward mechanically every quarter — roughly 57% today. This
forecloses an unsolicited takeover, a proxy contest and any activist path
entirely. There is no takeout-premium leg to this thesis, and no external
forcing function on capital allocation. (Note: `stocks.db` reports
`sharesInsiders` of 0.27% for GIB. That is wrong — it misses the Class B
block, 11.8% of shares outstanding. The screen cannot see this structure.)

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The AI thread — the whole reason this is cheap.** On 2026-06-18
  Accenture cut FY2026 revenue growth guidance to 3–4% from 4–5% and fell
  about 18%, its worst single-day drop on record; the IT-services complex
  derated on the claim that agentic AI compresses integration work by an
  order of magnitude. GIB fell 7.3% that day (66.07 → 61.28) and printed its
  52-week low of 59.63 intraday. What CGI's own numbers say: Q3-FY2026
  constant-currency growth +1.3% (from +7.0% a year earlier), nine months
  +2.1%, U.S. Commercial and State Government −3.9% cc, Germany −6.3% cc,
  Scandinavia/NW/Central-East Europe −1.1% cc. The deceleration is real and
  already in the print. What cuts the other way: TTM book-to-bill 108.1%, up
  7 points year over year, backlog up C$1.21B to C$31.79B, and the highest
  trailing book-to-bill sits in the two US segments (124.4% and 118.6%)
  where revenue is weakest. Bookings and revenue have now diverged for three
  quarters. That gap is either a conversion lag on long-dated government
  work or bookings dollars buying fewer hours of work — the disclosure
  cannot separate them (§6.1), and which one it is decides the thesis.
- **Offshoring cannibalization — the milder version of the same
  mechanism, already survived.** The MD&A names "increased use of our Asia
  Pacific global delivery centers" as a cause of revenue *decline* in U.S.
  Commercial and State Government and in Canada, and as the cause of Asia
  Pacific's +9.7% cc growth. CGI is deliberately moving delivery to lower
  price points and keeping the margin. This is a self-inflicted revenue
  deflation running at roughly the same magnitude as the AI fear, and
  adjusted EBIT margin has not moved a basis point through it. It is the
  strongest available evidence that CGI's margin structure absorbs price
  deflation, which is condition 3.
- **Free cash flow is overstated by every headline that quotes it.** The
  `stockanalysis` `fcf` line (US$1,788M, a 12.46% yield; the `candidates`
  screen's `fcfy` of 12.7%) is NCFO less PP&E capex of C$111.8M only. CGI
  also spends C$264.7M a year on `salePurchaseIntangibles` — the MD&A's
  "additions to contract costs" (C$91.4M in nine months) plus "additions to
  intangible assets" (C$120.5M) — which are recurring, have grown every year
  from C$179M in FY2021, and are genuinely capital spending: contract
  transition costs and internally developed software. Under IFRS 16 the
  lease principal (C$185.7M) sits in financing, not operating, so NCFO
  flatters an office-and-delivery-centre business; CGI publishes its own
  `fcfAfterLeases` for exactly this reason. Deduct those, plus SBC of
  C$62.3M, and owner FCF is C$1,966M, not C$2,479M — a 21% haircut, taking
  the owner yield from 12.5% to 9.9%. This is the single most consequential
  correction in the file. It generalises: every IFRS-16 filer with
  capitalized contract costs in the `candidates` universe has the same
  overstated `fcfy`.
- **Capital allocation is running slightly ahead of cash.** Trailing twelve
  months: buybacks C$1,976M, acquisitions C$401M, dividends C$142M —
  C$2,519M of outflow against C$1,966M of owner FCF. The gap was funded with
  debt: net debt rose C$568M year over year to C$3.68B, net debt to
  capitalization from 23.4% to 26.6%, and on 2026-09-10 CGI priced C$500M of
  senior unsecured notes (C$250M at 4.195% for 3.25 years, C$250M at 4.484%
  for 4.75 years) to repay debt and for general purposes. At 1.46× EBITDA
  and 20.9× interest cover this is comfortable, but the current
  shrink-the-float pace is not self-funding, which is condition 5.
- **Working capital released cash this year.** TTM `changeWorkingCapital` is
  +C$124.9M against a five-year average near +C$13M (FY2021–FY2025: +214.9,

**Integrity checks.**

- *Reinvestment / terminal warning, answered.* The tool prints "growth
  without reinvestment" because owner FCF (US$1,417M) exceeds net income
  (US$1,249M), giving a negative terminal reinvestment rate of −13.4%. Two
  responses, both taken. First, terminal growth is set exactly at the
  10-year breakeven — 2.36% is pure nominal inflation pass-through with zero
  real growth, which is repricing existing contracts and needs no
  reinvestment. Second, the earnings base *is* understated: CGI amortizes
  C$229.3M a year of acquired and contract intangibles (`otherAmortization`),
  and adding that back gives cash earnings of C$1,962.6M = US$1,414.7M, at
  which the reinvestment rate is −0.18% — essentially zero, and the warning
  clears. The implied return is unchanged at 12.53% either way.
- *Market-share sentence.* The base path compounds revenue to C$18.6B in
  five years from C$16.4B. Global IT services spend is well above US$1.5T,
  so CGI would hold roughly 0.9% of it. There is no "bigger than the market"
  problem here; the constraint on this name is competitive and technological,
  not addressable-market size.
- *Terminal growth against the disclosed terminal risk.* CGI's own risk
  section names it: "changes and developments in artificial intelligence
  (including agentic AI, generative AI…)… If we do not keep pace with meeting
  the evolving needs of clients, including in the emerging field of AI, our
  ability to retain existing clients and gain new business may be adversely
  affected… These factors may result in pressure on our revenue, net
  earnings and resulting cash flow from operations." A 2.36% terminal is
  inflation-only, zero real growth, zero reinvestment — the weakest terminal
  claim available short of decline, and it is the reason the base case is
  not run at a higher rate. It does **not** survive the strong form of the
  AI risk, in which nominal revenue falls; that case is the top two rows of
  the table and is priced at −168bp to +26bp. The terminal is where the
  entire disagreement lives, and the write-up does not pretend to resolve it.
- *Base-year cash tax rate.* Cash taxes ~22% versus a 27% book rate, about
  C$115M a year. Charged in full, the base case falls to 11.93% (+367bp).
- *Serial-acquirer check.* CGI spends C$401M of TTM cash on acquisitions and
  those acquisitions are inside reported growth (nine-month revenue +4.4%
  reported versus +2.1% constant-currency organic). `fcf` excludes
  acquisition spend, so crediting reported growth would be growth for free.
  Every scenario above therefore grows **the organic rate only** — 2.5% is
  the nine-month constant-currency figure, not the 4.4% reported one, and
  the acquisition spend is left uncharged as the offsetting simplification.
  Said explicitly per the rule.
- *Asset-light check.* Not claimed and not true. Trailing PP&E capex is 0.7%
  of revenue, but intangibles and contract costs take real capital intensity
  to 2.3%, which is the correction already made in the base. The business is
  people-heavy, not asset-light.
- *Distribution clamp.* The US median cost of capital is ~7.8% and roughly
  80% of firms fall in 5–10%. The base case's implied 12.53% sits well above
  that band, which is the sanity check passing in the interesting direction —
  the price is not asking for a heroic return, it is offering one, provided
  the flows do not shrink.
- *Currency.* Cash flows are Canadian dollars, the quote is US dollars, and
  the conversion is at a single spot rate with no forward path. A persistent
  CAD depreciation reduces the USD return one-for-one; that is an exogenous
  price, a scenario input, not a condition (§6.5).

## 5. Falsifiers

- **Break —** adjusted EBIT margin prints below 15.0% in two consecutive
  quarters. The margin is the entire evidence base for condition 3 and for
  the claim that CGI absorbs price deflation. Losing it means AI deflation
  is reaching profit, not just revenue, and the −2% terminal stops being
  pessimistic.
- **Break —** constant-currency revenue turns negative for two consecutive
  quarters *while* TTM book-to-bill also falls below 100%. Either alone is
  survivable; together they remove the conversion-lag explanation for
  condition 4 and say demand, not timing, is the problem.
- **Break —** the buyback is suspended or cut materially while net debt to
  EBITDA passes 2.5×. The per-share compounding is the return; without it a
  ~2% grower at a 9.9% yield is a bond with equity risk.
- **Shift —** TTM book-to-bill falls below 100% while revenue holds. The
  backlog is being drawn down rather than replenished; revalue on a lower
  terminal.
- **Shift —** the cash tax rate normalizes toward the 27% book rate. Worth
  about C$115M a year and roughly 60bp of implied return.
- **Shift —** US federal civilian retrenchment deepens. The U.S. Federal
  segment is C$1,580M of nine-month revenue (12.7% of the company), of which
  C$1,367M is federal civilian, and it already fell 5.7% cc over nine months
  on efficiency initiatives and the shutdown.
- **Shift —** the Class B block moves — conversion, sale, or a change in the
  Godin family's holding. It cuts both ways: it would open a takeout path
  that today does not exist, and it would remove the long-horizon owner who
  has defended the margin discipline.

## Kill-thesis record

Ledger line: `2026-09-12 GIB SOUND conditions=5 refuted=0 unknown=0
pending=3 not_obtained=0 reopen=2026-11-11:...` — five load-bearing
conditions, none refuted, two SURVIVED, three PENDING the same dated
disclosure. Five conditions is a lot of surface area, and the count is
stated deliberately: this is not a one-legged "good business at a fair
price" thesis. Three of the five legs, however, are the *same* leg wearing
different clothes — growth, conversion and buyback capacity all resolve at
the FY2026 print — which concentrates the risk rather than diversifying it.

**Per-condition adjudication.**

1. *Owner FCF of ~C$1,966M is the right base* — **SURVIVED** at *probable*,
   with a refinement. Three attacks were run. (a) *Double-counting*: could
   deducting the full C$185.7M of lease payments from NCFO double-charge an
   interest portion already in NCFO? Possibly, by perhaps C$40M — which
   makes the base too *low*, so the error runs against the thesis and is
   left in. (b) *A missing fourth cost*: the Q3-FY2026 MD&A prints a
   complete investing table (business acquisitions, loan receivable, PP&E,
   proceeds, contract costs, intangibles, investments) whose lines reconcile
   to the reported C$159.9M and C$474.5M totals, so the deduction set is
   closed. (c) *Working-capital flattery*: this one lands. TTM
   `changeWorkingCapital` is +C$124.9M versus a ~+C$13M five-year average;
   about C$112M of the base is non-repeatable. Stressed together with the
   cash-tax gap the base falls to US$1,253.5M and the implied return to
   11.36% (+309bp over the hurdle), and the flat-nominal-forever floor still
   clears at +47bp. The condition survives at its tier; the *number* was
   wrong by 11.5% and is now carried as a scenario.
2. *Nominal owner FCF does not decline more than ~2% a year in perpetuity* —
   **PENDING 2026-11-11**. Attacked with what bears on it today: nine-month
   constant-currency growth is **+2.1%** and the quarter **+1.3%** — positive,
   decelerating, not negative; backlog is up C$1.21B year over year. Nothing
   in the current record stands against the condition, and nothing in it
   settles a perpetuity either. Routed to PENDING, not UNKNOWN: the FY2026
   full-year print is a dated checkpoint on the trajectory, even though no
   filing will ever settle the perpetuity itself.
3. *Adjusted EBIT margin holds near 16.3%* — **SURVIVED** at *probable*. The
   real attack is that "adjusted" launders recurring costs: CGI adjusts out
   C$98M of restructuring (9M FY2025) and C$84.4M of acquisition and
   integration costs (9M FY2026), and a serial acquirer books those every
   year. So the check was rerun *unadjusted*: EBIT before adjustments is
   (1,851.4 + 92.6) / 12,427.5 = **15.6%** for nine months of FY2026 versus
   (1,725.9 + 54.1) / 11,898.8 = **15.0%** a year earlier. The unadjusted
   margin rose 60bp while revenue decelerated 570bp in constant currency.
   The condition survives on the harsher measure, which is stronger evidence
   than the one the thesis quoted. Note also that the base FCF is a cash
   number and already absorbs those "one-time" costs, so there is no double
   jeopardy between this condition and condition 1.
4. *Bookings convert into positive organic growth within four to six
   quarters* — **PENDING 2026-11-11**. Attacked hard. Bookings and backlog
   are company-defined KPIs and the MD&A concedes backlog "incorporates
   estimates from management that are subject to change"; a book-to-bill
   above 100% is fully compatible with permanent revenue decline if bookings
   dollars buy fewer hours, in which case the metric measures nothing. And
   the segment table already shows two of eight running below 100% trailing
   (U.K. and Australia 96.1%, Finland/Poland/Baltics 82.0%). Against that:
   the company total is 108.1% and up 7 points, backlog is up C$1.21B, and
   the highest trailing book-to-bill sits in the two US segments (124.4%,
   118.6%) where revenue is weakest — the signature of a lag, not a
   collapse. Mixed, net supportive, not refuted, dated.
5. *The buyback keeps shrinking the count without leverage past ~2× EBITDA*
   — **PENDING 2026-11-11**. TTM repurchases of C$1,976M already exceed owner
   FCF, and acquisitions plus dividends add C$543M of genuinely debt-funded
   outflow. Headroom: net debt C$3.68B against TTM EBITDA of ~C$2.85B is
   1.29×; C$543M a year of incremental net debt reaches 2.0× in roughly
   three to four years if EBITDA is flat — inside the five-year explicit
   window, which is the honest statement. The NCIB authorization is not the
   constraint (12,764,360 shares still available to 2027-02-05). The FY2026
   annual cash-flow statement settles the run rate.

**Standing checks.**

- *Base rate.* Only ~29% of firms earn above their cost of capital
  (Damodaran's EVA dataset), so "the excess return persists" is a
  three-in-ten proposition before evidence. CGI is in that 29% today —
  ROIC 14.3% on the stockanalysis basis against an ~8.3% cost of equity —
  but its **own** disclosed ROIC fell from 14.6% to 13.4% year over year, a
  120bp compression. The base rate is visibly biting. The mitigant is that
  the thesis does not require the excess return to persist: the base case
  needs 2.5% nominal growth and the break-even needs no decline. That is a
  materially weaker claim than the one the 29% figure prices.
- *The short case, strongest form.* CGI sells billable hours priced by
  headcount. Agentic AI compresses the hours integration and application
  maintenance require; clients do not pay for hours they do not consume; and
  the 1.9×-revenue backlog is the very mechanism by which that repricing
  arrives, since roughly half of it renews inside two years. CGI has no
  product to sell instead — IP solutions are never sized (§6.7) and are
  probably small. It has held revenue flat by offshoring, a one-time lever
  with a floor, and it has borrowed to retire 6% of the float a year, which
  levers the equity into a structurally declining cash flow. Adjusted EPS
  +9.0% on adjusted net earnings +1.7% is arithmetic on a shrinking asset.
  The right multiple for a melting ice cube is not 8× FCF. This case refutes
  no condition today, but it is why condition 2 is the thesis and everything
  else is scaffolding.
- *Management incentives.* **Not run to completion.** The compensation
  design sits in the Management Proxy Circular dated 2025-12-01, which is
  incorporated by reference into the AIF and was not read this run. What is
  known: a founder-controlled board (Serge Godin, 57% of votes, ~11.8% of
  economics via Class B) is structurally aligned to long-horizon per-share
  value. What is not known is whether the LTIP keys on adjusted EPS, which
  would reward debt-funded buybacks independent of value. The thesis does
  **not** assume management acts against incentive — it assumes they keep
  buying stock and managing to margin, which any plausible plan rewards. So
  the gap does not cut against the thesis and no load-bearing condition is
  tagged NOT OBTAINED on its account; it is recorded here as an incomplete
  check.
- *Disconfirming search.* One dated web search was run, aimed at the bear
  case rather than the bull: it returned the 2026-06-18 Accenture guidance
  cut and 18% drop, Citi's warning on AI disruption and growth, a Microsoft
  executive's "Accenture doesn't need 750,000 employees", and founder claims
  of 10–15× compression in integration work. That is the strongest available
  disconfirming material and it is in §3 and §7 as low-confidence colour. It
  did not refute a condition because none of it is a measurement of CGI.
- *Moat: checkbox or mechanism?* Interrogated and downgraded. Incumbency in
  mission-critical managed services and government accreditation are real
  mechanisms with named switching costs. Pricing power is not: the six-year
  flat margin is achieved by CGI deflating its own revenue through Asia
  Pacific delivery, which the MD&A states in as many words. The thesis says
  this out loud rather than labelling a moat, which is why it survives the
  check — a cost moat that absorbs deflation is exactly the mechanism
  condition 3 needs, and it is not oversold as a price moat.

**Statistical checks.** The thesis rests on no backtest, hit rate, or repo
signal, so the overlapping-window, multiple-comparison and effective-n
checks have no object. One check did bite, on the *screen* rather than the
thesis: the `candidates` row's `fcfy` of 12.7% for GIB is computed from a
free-cash-flow definition that deducts PP&E capex only, overstating the
owner yield by roughly 280bp. The correction is applied throughout §4, and
the same defect will affect every IFRS-16 filer with capitalized contract
costs in that universe.

**Options timing check: NOT RUN, and the reason matters.** The thesis makes
no dated required-move claim — it asserts a valuation gap, not a move by a
date — so there is nothing for the 2-sigma test to refute, and substituting
the next earnings date would test a claim the thesis never made.
Independently, the check could not have been trusted had it applied:
coverage is **path 2 only** (GIB is outside the 24-symbol CBOE catalog, so
