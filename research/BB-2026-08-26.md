## 2. Business

**Created:** QNX sells a safety-certified (ISO 26262 ASIL D) real-time
OS and hypervisor that lets an automaker or device maker run mixed-
criticality workloads — instrument cluster, ADAS, infotainment — on one
SoC without re-certifying the foundation each program; the customer buys
a certification and reliability shortcut measured in engineering-years.
Secure Communications sells governments and regulated enterprises
certified secure voice/messaging (SecuSUITE — NSA CSfC, NATO NCIA), mass
notification (AtHoc — the only FedRAMP High critical-events platform,
>75% of US government personnel), and device management (UEM — DoD APL,
BSI-certified). The customer buys the certification and the sovereign
on-premise deployment, not features.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (a) QNX per-unit royalties on
shipped vehicles/devices, sometimes with contractual minimum volumes —
the $950M backlog is management's estimate of these over design
lifetimes; (b) QNX development seats, tools and professional services,
paid up front when a program starts (Q1 development-license revenue was
an eight-quarter high — the lead indicator); (c) Secure Communications
term and perpetual licenses plus maintenance (~80% of the segment is
ARR per the CFO) with large sovereign deals recognized largely in-quarter
on top; (d) Licensing — residual patent royalties, mostly the Malikie
earn-out, ~$29M guided for FY27 and running off. Q1 FY27 segment adjusted
EBITDA margins: QNX 27%, Secure Communications 27%.

**Protected:** QNX's protection is a mechanism — design-in switching
cost. Once an OEM certifies a platform on QNX the foundation persists for
the vehicle program's life (7–10 years of royalties), and the Tier-1 and
silicon ecosystem (every major Tier 1, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm) ships
reference stacks on it. It is narrow, not wide: the 10-K names in-house
OEM development and "open source software that is perceived to be free"
(embedded Linux) as the competitive threat, and per-unit royalty is small
enough that OEMs tolerate it rather than depend on it. Secure
Communications' protection is the certification gate (a competitor needs
years to earn NSA/NATO/FedRAMP/BSI listings) — real for SecuSUITE and
AtHoc, evidently not for UEM, which is losing revenue to Microsoft/VMware
class suites despite the same certifications. Business quality: ROIC
10.6% (stockanalysis) sits below the cost of equity; the excess return a
moat implies is not yet in the numbers.

**Control:** one class of voting common shares outstanding (unlimited
authorized; no Class A or preferred issued). No controller: Fairfax
Financial 4.4% (25.8M shares, 13D/A 2026-05-06, down from 35.4M in the
2025 proxy's ownership table) plus the full $200M notes (52M shares if
converted → ~12% as-converted); Legal & General 5.7%, BlackRock 5.1%.
Insiders own 0.25%. Nothing forecloses a takeover or an activist; Fairfax
is a seller, not a defender.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive, on a base that is restated.**
Figures are continuing operations (Cylance, sold to Arctic Wolf Feb 2025,
is in discontinued ops), USD millions, fiscal years ending February:

is cost-out (opex $681M → $371M), not top-line, and that lever is now
mostly pulled. From here the operating-income trajectory has to come
from revenue growth, which is the whole question.
