## 2. Business

**Created:** multiplayer game worlds Chinese players return to for years —
the Fantasy Westward Journey franchise is in its third decade — plus a
growing slate of self-developed PC/console titles sold globally (Where Winds
Meet, Marvel Rivals, Naraka, Once Human). The player gets a persistent social
world with continual content; the surplus is time well spent with a group,
priced in small discretionary purchases. Youdao (AI learning tools and
devices), Cloud Music (subscription streaming, 9899.HK) and Yanxuan
(e-commerce) are side businesses, together ~17% of revenue.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms.
- In-game sales of virtual items and time in self-developed titles: 97.7% of
  the games segment (Q2 6-K), the segment itself 83% of revenue. A PC title
  keeps the whole payment; a mobile title pays the app store 30% and Android
  stores more — the Q2 games gross margin rose to 76.1% from 70.2% on "lower
  platform revenue-sharing costs" (CFO on the call, transcribed), i.e. mix
  toward self-developed PC.
- Licensed-game operation: Blizzard's titles (World of Warcraft, Hearthstone,
  Overwatch, Diablo) re-licensed in 2024 and co-developed Diablo Immortal;
  the 20-F says these earn "relatively lower profits" than owned titles.
- Interest on the cash pile: RMB3.7B TTM (falling from RMB5.9B in 2024 as
  rates drop and cash moves into investments) — 9% of pretax income.
- Youdao learning services, Cloud Music memberships and advertising, Yanxuan
  product margin.

**Protected:** the mechanism is accumulated player time. A 20-year
franchise's players hold characters, items, guilds and friendships inside
it; a competitor cannot copy that stock of sunk social capital, only start
a new one. In new genres NetEase has no such moat — Sea of Remnants
(July 2026) under-delivered against a crowded field, and Ananta launches
into a genre miHoYo already occupies. The honest statement: a durable moat
around the installed franchises, none around the pipeline, and the state as
referee of both (license approvals, minors' playtime limits — 20-F Item 3.D).

**Control:** one class of ordinary shares, one vote each (20-F Item 10 and
Note 17). William Lei Ding (founder, CEO) beneficially owns 1,450,300,000
shares = 45.5% through a trust (Shining Globe International, 20-F Item 6.E,
as of 2026-02-28). Officers and directors hold 45.5% in total. That blocks
an unsolicited takeover or an activist path and ties the dividend policy to
his own 45% receipt of it. The VIEs (Guangzhou NetEase 99% Ding-owned,
Hangzhou Leihuo, Youdao Computer 71.1% Ding) hold the operating licenses;
the ADR owns contractual claims, untested in a PRC court (20-F).
