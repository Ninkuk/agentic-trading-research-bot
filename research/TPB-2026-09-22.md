## 2. Business

**Created:** adult nicotine users get three things: value-priced moist snuff
and chew (Stoker's, #1 value player, near 10% of moist snuff), premium
rolling papers (Zig-Zag, #1 premium), and a nicotine pouch positioned against
Zyn. FRE is "moisture-forward" with 20-count cans and strengths up to 15mg.
Low-confidence colour prices it ~30–40% cheaper per pouch than Zyn or Velo's
high-strength products. ALP is an "irreverent", D2C-first brand. The consumer
surplus in pouches is mostly price and format. Zyn owns trust and
availability at roughly 75% US share (low-confidence).

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms.
- Wholesale sales to distributors and chains, net of a large gross-to-net
  spread. Q2 modern oral: gross $87.0M, net $68.4M, a 21% spread covering
  promotions, slotting and chain terms.
- Slotting payments that buy shelf placement. These are recurring, not
  one-off: management expects "similar terms in Q4".
- D2C e-commerce (ALP especially). TPB sells product to ALP and provides
  warehousing, but ALP's own profit is split 50/50 with the Tucker Carlson
  Network: Q2 NCI $6.7M of $10.3M consolidated net income.
- Distribution of Zig-Zag papers bought from a supplier (RTI) under a
  purchase agreement.

Stoker's gross margin fell 600bp to 57% as chain penetration rose. Chains buy
volume with margin.

**Protected:** in legacy, scale and brand plus a shrinking, regulated
category that entrants avoid. In pouches, the real barrier is the PMTA
itself: few firms can afford it (10-K: "very few have the capability or
resources"). Today that barrier protects the authorized incumbents (Zyn,
on!), not TPB, whose products remain pending. FRE has no mechanism a better-
capitalized competitor cannot copy (moisture, can count, price). The honest
answer is that the pouch moat is **conditional on authorization** and absent
without it.

**Control:** the listed voting common stock (NYSE: TPB) is one class, plus a
registered but unlisted non-voting common. Insiders hold 3.77% and
institutions 94.4% (stocks.db). No controlling holder was identified. The
incoming CEO, David Glazek, is Executive Chair and a former Standard General
partner. Standard General is TPB's 51% partner in the GWO JV (TPB holds 49%
after contributing its CDS segment in January 2025), a related-party link.
Standard General's current stake in TPB was NOT OBTAINED (proxy not read).
Nothing in the filings read here forecloses a takeover or activist path.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue is up 56% since FY2023;
operating income is down 21%. The drop is deliberate (pouch investment) but
it is the direction that matters.
