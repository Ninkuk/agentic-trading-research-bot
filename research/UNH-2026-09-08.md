## 2. Business

**Created:** For employers and consumers, UnitedHealthcare pools medical
risk and buys care at negotiated unit prices that no individual or small
employer could obtain; 48.5M medical members (Q2 2026) get network rates,
claims administration, and benefit design. For the federal government and
states, it takes a fixed per-capita payment and assumes the medical risk of
7.6M Medicare Advantage and 6.8M Medicaid members, delivering extra
benefits (dental, vision, OTC, $0 premiums) that fee-for-service Medicare
does not fund. Optum Rx buys drugs at scale for ~387M adjusted scripts a
quarter and manages formularies; Optum Health delivers primary, specialist,
ambulatory-surgery and home care to ~20M patients, much of it under
capitation where it is paid to keep people out of hospital; Optum Insight
sells revenue-cycle, payment-integrity and analytics software to payers
and providers. (10-K 2025; Q2 2026 release.)

**Captured:** Not one margin but six:
(1) the medical-care-ratio spread on $352B of premiums — 88.1% MCR guided
for 2026 leaves ~12 points, of which operating costs consume ~12.7 points
across the consolidated base, so the benefits business earns a 4–6%
operating margin (UnitedHealthcare 4.6% in Q2 2026, 2.4% a year earlier);
(2) Medicare Advantage capitation, where CMS sets the rate and the risk
model and UnitedHealth keeps the difference between capitation and cost —
the regulator, not the company, sets the ceiling on this one;
(3) administrative fees on 22.3M fee-based commercial lives, capital-light
and margin-stable;
(4) Optum Rx: historically spread and rebate retention, now converting to
per-member fees with 100% rebate pass-through by 2028, plus specialty and
home-delivery pharmacy product margin — 3.9% on $38.3B a quarter;
(5) Optum Health: capitated premium from payers, ~$40B a quarter of it
from UnitedHealthcare itself (corporate eliminations $39.6B in Q2), plus
fee-for-service and ASC fees — 5.1% margin in Q2;
(6) Optum Insight: software and services at a 25% margin on $5.4B; and,
across all of it, investment income on ~$89B of cash and investments
($1.2B in Q2). (Q2 2026 release supplemental tables.)

**Protected:** The mechanisms are scale in provider contracting (unit-cost
discounts scale with local share), claims and clinical data across 48.5M
medical lives and 120M Optum consumers, vertical integration (employed
physicians whose coding feeds the capitation the parent collects), and
regulatory capital and licensing that make a de novo national payer
impractical. The AARP endorsement is a specific, named moat in Medicare
Supplement and Medicare Advantage distribution. The honest limit: the moat
protects volume, not margin. Roughly 80% of revenue is premium on risk
business (10-K Item 1A) whose price is set annually by CMS, states, or a
competitive commercial bid, and the 2024–25 episode showed the regulator
compressing the industry's Medicare margin for two consecutive years while
UnitedHealth's scale did nothing to stop it. Excess returns here are at the
regulator's discretion.

**Control:** One class of common stock, $0.01 par, 905M shares outstanding
(June 30, 2026); no preferred issued; no controlling holder — insiders
0.17%, institutions 89.2% (stockanalysis); 8,734 holders of record (10-K).
Nothing in the charter forecloses a takeover or an activist; size ($360B)
and insurance-regulatory change-of-control approvals do. The board added a
Public Responsibility Committee and a new Lead Independent Director in the
2025–26 refresh (Q2 release).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative.** Revenue rose 56% from 2021 to
2025 while earnings from operations fell.

| year | revenue | earnings from operations (10-K) | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $287.6B | $24.0B | 8.3% |
| 2022 | $324.2B | $28.4B | 8.8% |
| 2023 | $371.6B | $32.4B | 8.7% |
| 2024 | $400.3B | $32.3B | 8.1% |
| 2025 | $447.6B | $19.0B (after $2.4B restructuring) | 4.2% |

Quarterly the direction has turned: $5.2B (Q2 2025), $4.3B, $2.8B, then
$9.0B (Q1 2026) and $8.0B (Q2 2026, 7.1% margin). The trajectory is
negative over five years and positive over four quarters; the thesis is a
bet on which one continues. Stock compensation is charged in operating
costs and runs ~$1.0B a year, 0.2% of revenue.

  reinvestment" on every run where FCF ≥ earnings. FCF exceeds net income
