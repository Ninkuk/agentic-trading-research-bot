## 2. Business

**Creates:** vertical transportation plus the certainty it keeps running —
uptime, safety-code compliance, response time on ~2.4M serviced units
(10-K: 1.1M connected on Otis ONE). Customers buy an elevator once and buy its
availability for 30+ years.

**Captures:** three distinct mechanisms, not one. New equipment at ~3% operating
margin (Q2'26: $40M profit on $1.3B sales — essentially a customer-acquisition
channel); maintenance contracts repriced on multi-year cycles (4-year typical in
NA, per Q2 call); repair and modernization billed against an aging installed
base (FY25: NE $4,989M / M&R $7,584M / Mod $1,858M — service 65.4% of revenue
and, at 23–25% segment margin vs 3–4% NE, roughly 90% of segment profit).

**Protects:** route density (profit per mechanic-mile — the denser the
territory, the cheaper each callback), OEM parts/diagnostics access on Otis
units, and safety-code inspection regimes that favor OEMs. Management claims
ISPs are not taking share ("It's not ISPs gaining share" — Q2 call); the
retention dip is attributed to residual UpLift churn. That claim is theirs, not
independently verified — but the retention decline being "a small number of
basis points" (same call) bounds the current leak.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Is the service-margin decline temporary or structural?** Both. Temporary:
  mechanic onboarding lag, ramp-rate premiums ($30M of the $50M incremental
  headwind, per CFO). Structural-ish: mix shift toward mod (24% growth,
  dilutive) and materials-heavy comprehensive contracts on aging non-Otis
  equipment with parts inflation running against 4-year fixed prices — that one
  only reprices at renewal. H2 bridge to mid-24%: 80bp repair price (in
  backlog), 50bp volume (in backlog), 30bp SG&A (restructuring already
  executed), and it assumes Q2 productivity headwinds persist. Checkable in one
  quarter.
- **Retention:** down small bps in H1, Americas-centered; management explicitly
  "taking longer than expected." Root cause: multi-year contract churn tail from
  UpLift disruption. Absolute retention level not disclosed → UNKNOWN.
- **China:** NE revenue $2,444M→$1,919M→$1,650M (FY23→FY25, −32%); Q2'26 still
  down high-teens. 10-K: China is ~1/5 of NE sales but >half of NE *unit*
  volume, and a growing service book (portfolio up high-single-digits, mod
  orders +100% on bond stimulus + Tianjin 117). Blast radius bounded: total NE
  op profit is $40M/quarter (~6% of company profit).
- **Kone–TKE ($34.4B, announced ~April 2026, close ≥Q2'27, Schindler
  opposing):** 4→3 consolidation with likely overlap divestitures; more risk to
  NE bidding discipline upside than to service pricing. Not a 2026 event.
- **Insider activity since the call:** one Form 4 (2026-08-04), a director's
  routine DSU accrual. No selling wave. Dead end.
- **Earnings-estimate pattern (8 quarters, broker tier):** meets and penny-level
  beats/misses; two 1–3¢ misses (Q4'24, Q1'26). Tightly managed guidance; the
  damage shows in segment margins and revisions, not the EPS line. GAAP Q2'26

  adjusted EPS; definitions differ as previously observed on other names.
- **Commitments footnote (asset-light check):** purchase obligations $1,427M
  total, mostly 2026–28 supplier/IT — no hidden capacity capex. Capex $159M on
  $14.9B revenue (1.1%) is genuinely asset-light; long-lived assets $743M.

## 5. Falsifiers

- **Break:** Q3 (Oct 28) service margin misses mid-24% → the recovery bridge
  failed with its components already in backlog; the margin problem is
  structural mix, not ramp cost. Sell/stay-away signal.
- **Break:** FY-end retention down again → the moat is leaking through the
  renewal cycle; the annuity premise degrades.
- **Shift:** mod backlog growth decelerating below ~10% cc → revalue the
  growth path down toward the conservative case.
- **Shift:** China stimulus reversal or NE re-acceleration → revalue China
  service pivot in either direction.
