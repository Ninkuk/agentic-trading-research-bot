## 2. Business

**Created:** Brazilian SMB merchants get card + Pix acceptance, fast
settlement/receivables prepayment (working capital — the binding constraint
for small Brazilian merchants), banking, and credit from one local partner
with physical hub distribution the digital-only rivals lack.

**Captured:** (four distinct engines, not one): (i) MDR on card TPV (FY25
card TPV R$469.7B); (ii) financial income from receivables prepayment —
funded increasingly by ~0%-cost client deposits (R$10.1B retail, avg daily
+26% YoY; total funding cost down from 100% to 87% of CDI — Q1'26 call);
(iii) POS subscription/rental; (iv) credit interest — R$3.2B book yielding
3.3%/mo (Q1'26). Blended monetization: ARPAC R$247/mo.

**Protected:** hub distribution density + bundle switching costs (payments
+ deposits + credit in one place) + proprietary transaction data for
underwriting. The honest read: the moat is in distribution and the bundle,
not the rail — and the churn spike in the 2025-onboarded cohort is evidence
it is thin at the entry margin. The rail itself is being nationalized by
Pix.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive over the decade, mildly negative
right now.** Revenue and income, BRL, IFRS 20-F (2016–2024) and
continuing-ops recast (2024–25): 2016 R$0.44B → 2018 R$1.58B → 2020 R$3.32B
→ 2022 R$9.59B → 2024 R$12.05B → 2025 R$14.15B. Pretax: −0.15 → +0.44 →
+1.13 → −0.39 → adj EBT 2024 R$2.58B → 2025 R$2.90B. Adj EBT margin
21.4%→20.5% (FY25), adj GP margin 46.2%→44.6% (FY25) →41.6% (Q1'26), eaten
by credit provisions and Selic-linked funding costs. Loss years (2021,
2022, 2024 IFRS) were one-offs: the 2021 credit-product blowup, investment
losses, and the R$3.6B-class software goodwill impairment.
