## 2. Business

**Created:** a non-endoscopic way to find Barrett's esophagus (BE) and
dysplasia, the precursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma. A nurse or PA does a
balloon swipe (EsoCheck) in the office in a few minutes. The cells go to
Lucid's lab for a methylation panel (EsoGuard). The alternative is a sedated
upper endoscopy that guideline-eligible patients (chronic GERD plus risk
factors) mostly never get. The customer surplus is real if the test
performs: screening that would otherwise not happen. Its clinical
performance in a screening population is domain science this run cannot
grade (§6).

**Captured:** a per-test lab fee, three ways:

- Medicare at the gapfill-derived CLFS rate of $1,938.01 (PLA 0114U). It is
  paid only for the Texas lab, under a MAC outside MolDX (Novitas), since
  October. The California lab waits on the MolDX LCD (8-Ks 2026-09-22,
  2026-10-22).
- Commercial and contracted payers, VA, and the firefighter-event channel.
  These are just under 40% of tests together with Medicare. In Q2, 28% of
  adjudicated claims were allowed, at an average of $1,424. Denials were
  experimental/investigational (18%), prior authorization (22%) and missing
  records (5%) (Q2 call). One LBM policy (Concert, ~10M lives) has been
  adopted by three client plans.
- Nothing else. There is no device revenue line to speak of. Revenue is
  recognised on expected collections, not billings (Q2 10-Q).

**Protected:** patents on the methylation panel and the EsoCheck device
(licensed from Case Western, per company history; not re-read this run), the
PLA code and its established rate, and a head start in coverage. The real
gate is payer coverage, and it protects only once won. Castle Biosciences'
TissueCypher works downstream (BE risk stratification), not in screening.
Sponge-based rivals exist abroad (Cytosponge/Cyted in the UK; low-confidence,
from memory). On the mechanism question: what stops a competitor is the
years of coverage work, which Lucid has not finished either.

**Control:** one class of common plus Series C preferred voting on an
as-converted basis under a 4.99% blocker (8-K 2026-10-22). PAVmed (PAVM)
holds ~15% of the common and ~25% of the vote (Q2 call). Lucid is PAVmed's
"non-consolidated subsidiary" over which PAVmed "has the ability to exercise
significant influence" (Q2 10-Q Note 3). A Management Services Agreement
with no termination date charges $3.15M a quarter, 22% of Q2 opex; $7.97M
was paid in cash in H1. While 25% of the Series C is outstanding, its
holders can designate one board nominee. No single controller blocks a bid.
But PAVmed's influence, plus a creditor-holder base that also owns the
preferred, makes an outside activist path narrow.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): flat.** Revenue rose 11× since 2021 and
2.2× since 2023, while the operating loss stayed in a $46–56M band:

| period | revenue | operating income |
|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | $0.50M | −$27.42M |
| FY2022 | $0.38M | −$56.25M |
| FY2023 | $2.43M | −$48.48M |
| FY2024 | $4.35M | −$46.05M |
| FY2025 | $4.71M | −$49.64M |
| TTM 6/30/26 | $5.45M | −$50.68M |

Persistently loss-making, and the Phase 0 kill applies. Book equity was
positive at 6/30 ($10.9M), but it was negative at 3/31/25 (−$5.4M), and the
Q2 10-Q carries going-concern language.
