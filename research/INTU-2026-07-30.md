## 2. Business

**Created:** removes the two highest-anxiety financial chores for consumers and
small businesses — tax filing (TurboTax: DIY and now human-assisted "Live") and
bookkeeping/payroll/payments (QuickBooks ecosystem) — plus consumer credit
(Credit Karma). The customer buys *confidence in a compliance outcome with liability
attached*, not software; management's own framing ("customers buy confidence, not
code") is also the best available explanation of why 88% of the $42B US tax-prep TAM
is paid human assistance rather than $0 software, a share that has *grown* as software
got better (Q3 FY26 call).

**Captured:** not one mechanism — per-return pricing tiered by complexity (DIY),
per-return + expert-hours pricing (Live, now 53% of TurboTax revenue), subscriptions
(QBO, ~$90B mid-market TAM claim), payments take-rate (online payment volume +30% Y/Y),
payroll, lending referral fees (Credit Karma: personal loans, auto insurance, cards),
and refund-timing products ($25B of "fast money" refunds delivered FY26). Announced
next: consumption-based pricing for AI-agent services and high-end price increases
(Aug 2026 lineup).

**Protected:** switching costs on the QuickBooks side (books + payroll + payments
+ accountant lock-in: ~10M business customers, ~1M accountants who *recommend* it — a
real distribution network effect); brand + data + audit-liability shield on the tax
side. The honest reading: the DIY tax moat at the price-sensitive low end is **thin and
was just breached on price** — management said "we lost on price" — while the
assisted and business moats held. The moat claim this thesis leans on is the
assisted/business side, not low-end DIY.

(pre-template) — no direction label was printed (the FY21–TTM

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Why is it down 61%?** Three legs, reconstructed from the tape and news flow:
  (a) Jan–May: AI-disruption narrative built (embedding of tax/finance agents in LLMs);
  (b) **May 20 AMC**: Q3 FY26 print — revenue +10%, EPS beat, guidance *raised*, but
  TurboTax FY26 growth cut to ~7% on the worst industry filing contraction since
  post-COVID (total IRS filers ~-30bps, a ~2M-unit gap) plus a loss of price-sensitive
  sub-$50K DIY filers, and a same-day **17% workforce reduction** — stock -20% next day;
  (c) early June: **Goldman downgrade to Sell** modeling 20% of US filers moving to
  fully-AI tax prep by 2030 (TurboTax revenue -18% vs FY25 by then); trough $252.84 on
  Jun 22 (~$73B cap), then a ~30% July recovery. Fundamentals and price moved in
  opposite directions all year: every one of the last 6 quarters beat consensus EPS
  (Robinhood earnings history, cross-checked pattern vs press releases).
- **Is the DIY wound AI?** Management says no — "none of this has anything to do with
  AI... they lost on price" (CEO, Q3 call), and the Consumer GM was more specific in
  June: AI tax startups are "**not at scale right now**"; churn went to *cheaper
  traditional* DIY and to assisted; the emerging model that worries them is free-tax +
  monetize-elsewhere (Cash-App-style), which Intuit now plans to copy at the low end
  (free/cheap tax, monetize via Credit Karma). The Goldman bear case is a forecast about
  *next* seasons, not evidence about this one. Both can be true.
- **Is TurboTax itself behind on AI?** No — this thread died. 93% of form ingestion now
  runs through LLM extraction, the 40-year-old interview flow was retired this season,
  and the in-product assistant handled 15M interactions (Mizuho conference, June).
  Intuit scrapped and rebuilt its agent architecture twice in four months (VB Transform,
  July) — that reads as speed, not paralysis. Their ChatGPT/Claude embeds "did not
  provide a lot of outcomes... customers struggled to connect accounts" — early, and a
  funnel bet, with >$100M committed to running OpenAI models (news reports of the
  partnership).
- **Insider tape (Forms 3/4, EDGAR, May–Jul):** one genuine open-market buy — director
  Vasant Prabhu (former Visa CFO), 1,750 sh (~$0.5M) on May 26, post-crash. Small
  director sales (Dalzell, June). The large Jul 27 entries are annual equity *grants*
  (code A), not buys — the CEO's 152,836-sh grant priced at the bottom. **No executive
  open-market buying** despite the corporate "compelling value" buyback framing ($1.6B
  repurchased in Q3 alone, 2× Y/Y; shares out -1.15% Y/Y, so the buyback outruns SBC).
- **Class actions (July filings):** stock-drop suits alleging misrepresentation of

## 5. Falsifiers

What would make me sell:

1. TurboTax Live customer growth decelerating below ~15%/yr (long-term guide floor) or
   Live retention falling — first read: FY27 season metrics, spring 2027.
2. Paying-unit or revenue-share losses spreading **above** the sub-$50K price-sensitive
   segment — i.e., the DIY breach climbing the complexity ladder.
3. QBO online-ecosystem revenue growth below ~12% for two consecutive quarters
   (Q3 FY26: +19%).
4. An AI-native tax filer demonstrably at scale (millions of returns) with assisted-
   grade accuracy — watch next season's e-file share data and app rankings.
5. FY27 guidance (Aug 20) walking away from the "mid-teens+ annual EPS growth"
   commitment, or share count turning *up* (buyback no longer covering SBC).
6. Discovery in the pricing class actions revealing the DIY weakness was known and
   materially larger than disclosed.

   fall commentary. Does not kill the thesis (DIY low end is ~$5B of a $42B TAM and

2. **Whether "free tax at the low end, monetize via Credit Karma" works** — the
   announced fix is unproven; the claimed mechanics (TurboTax+Credit Karma ARPU ~30%
   higher; 54% growth in filers starting in Credit Karma) are management's numbers with
   no external check. First evidence: FY27 season.
3. **Class-action merits** — early stage; no complaint text read for this note.
4. **True AI-startup competitive quality next season** — today's "not at scale" is a
   point-in-time fact from an interested party.
5. **AI-agent consumption pricing uptake** (August lineup) — could be the next leg of
   ARPU or a repricing stumble on already-bruised customers; no data yet.
