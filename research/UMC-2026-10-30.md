## 2. Business

**Created:** UMC manufactures other companies' chip designs on mature and
specialty process nodes — 22/28nm (35% of Q3 wafer revenue; 22nm alone a
record 18.3%), 40nm (16%), 65nm (19%), down to 0.5um — for fabless designers
(86% of revenue) and IDMs (14%): MediaTek, Realtek, Novatek, Texas
Instruments, Intel (20-F). The customer gets a qualified, automotive-grade
(all twelve fabs IATF 16949) process for display drivers, Wi-Fi, RF-SOI,
embedded high-voltage, embedded NVM, BCD power management and automotive
radar, at a node where the design does not need, and could not pay for,
TSMC's leading edge. What they value is yield, capacity reliability and a
long tail of specialty IP; the surplus is a stable supply of parts that are
small in a system's bill of materials but gate its shipment.

**Captured:** one mechanism, priced per wafer: UMC sells 12-inch-equivalent
wafers (1,212K in Q3 against 1,325K of quarterly capacity) at a blended ASP
it reports as "firm" and will raise ~10% in 2027. Revenue = shipments × ASP;
the lever is utilization against a fixed cost base (Q3 depreciation
NT$14.7B of NT$49.0B COGS), which is why operating income rose 67.7% YoY on
25.6% revenue growth. Customers that signed multi-year supply agreements
pre-pay capacity (guarantee deposits "of capacity reservation", 20-F), which
is how the Singapore P3 fab was underwritten. Side income — share of listed
associates, dividends on a portfolio of Taiwan tech stakes — is real but
lumpy (see §3).

**Protected:** moderately, by mechanism rather than brand. (i) Qualification
switching costs: a display-driver or automotive part qualified on UMC's 22nm
embedded-HV or RF-SOI flow takes a re-qualification cycle and new masks to
move, so mature-node share moves slowly; the 20-F's own framing is that
prices "generally decline over the processing technology life cycle" and UMC
has "continuously been migrating" to hold profitability — the moat is a
treadmill. (ii) Scale and footprint outside China: Taiwan ~60% of capacity,
Singapore 15%, Japan 9%, China 15% (Xiamen 12X, Suzhou 8N), plus the Intel
12nm collaboration for US production in 2027 and a Polar Semiconductor MOU —
a "China+1" foundry for Western customers that SMIC and Hua Hong cannot be.
(iii) What does not protect it: there is no pricing power across the cycle.
Gross margin went 45.1% (2022) → 29.0% (2025) as utilization fell to 78%;
the 2027 price increase is being explained to customers as cost pass-through
and tight capacity, not as a franchise. The silicon-photonics/TFLN claims
(propagation loss, yield, "much ahead of anyone else in TFLN") are management
assertions this run cannot assess — UNKNOWN, not absent (§6).

**Control:** one share, one vote, no controller (20-F: "not directly or
indirectly owned or controlled by another corporation, by any foreign
government or by any other natural or legal person"). Nine directors, six
independent, cumulative voting, 1% nomination threshold. Largest holders are
Taiwan high-dividend ETFs (Capital Tip 5.17%, Cathay 4.31%, Yuanta 3.34%),
then Hsun Chieh Investment 3.51% — itself 36.49% owned by UMC — and Silicon
Integrated Systems 2.12% (18% UMC-owned): a cross-holding web that
entrenches management without a formal controller. Directors and officers
as a group 6.62%, mostly through the legal-entity representatives. Nothing
in the charter forecloses a takeover; ROC rules and the cross-holdings make
one impractical. Insiders proper hold 1.39%.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative into the 2023–2025 trough, strongly
positive out of it.** Fixed-cost fab economics, both ways:

| Revenue | 213.0 | 278.7 | 222.5 | 232.3 | 237.6 | 265.8 |

Revenue fell 15% from the 2022 peak to 2025 while operating income fell 58%;
9M 2026 revenue +16.1% carried operating income +41.4% (stockanalysis

recovering (Q3 34.0%) but the CFO says it will not revisit 2022's level
under the new depreciation load.
