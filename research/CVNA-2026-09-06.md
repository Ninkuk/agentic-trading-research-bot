## 2. Business

**Created:** a customer buys a used car entirely online — search tens of
thousands of reconditioned vehicles with 360° imagery, attach financing,
trade-in and ancillary products, sign on a phone, and take home delivery
from Carvana's own fleet, with a return window and no doc or dealer fees.
The surplus is selection, transparency and time; the Q2 letter's regional
data (Midwest/Northeast inventory +57%, sales +54%; West/Southeast
inventory +17%, sales +30%) shows demand tracks inventory, i.e. the
customer is buying selection and speed, not price.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms, not one margin. (i) Retail vehicle
gross profit, $3,547 per unit in Q2 (the spread between acquisition plus
reconditioning cost and sale price). (ii) Wholesale, $801 per retail unit —
trade-ins and customer-sourced cars sold through ADESA, the auction
business bought in 2022. (iii) Other GPU, $2,666 per retail unit: gain on
sale of the loans Carvana originates (sold to Ally under an $8B committed
forward-flow through July 2027, to three loan purchasers under agreements
of up to $12B through Q4 2027, and into its own securitizations where it
retains ≥5%), plus commissions on vehicle service contracts and GAP.
(iv) Below gross profit, SG&A per unit falls with scale — non-GAAP SG&A per
retail unit down $157 YoY in Q2 with overhead per unit down $272.
Mechanism (iii) is the one the 10-K names first among finance risks: "we
depend on the sale of automotive finance receivables for a substantial
portion of our gross profit."

**Protected:** the moat is a physical network, which is a mechanism rather
than a label. Nineteen ADESA sites integrated for retail reconditioning
since mid-2024 on top of ~18 inspection centers, 56 ADESA properties with
real estate for 3M units of annual production, a first-party long-haul and
last-mile logistics fleet, and pricing/credit models trained on ~600k
retail units a year. A competitor tomorrow needs the sites, the permits,
the fleet, and years of operating learning; the 2024–25 reconditioning
stumble (16 new facilities on a base of 18 in 18 months) shows even the
incumbent finds the machine hard to scale. What the moat does not do is
exclude: CarMax has 250 stores and an omnichannel offer, franchised dealers
have the FTC fee rule pushing their headline prices up, and the finance
gain depends on lenders' appetite, not on Carvana.

**Control:** two classes. Class A (719.9M shares, one vote) and Class B
(380.5M shares, ten votes each while the Garcia Parties hold ≥25% on an
as-exchanged basis). Ernest Garcia II and III and their entities hold ~83%
of the vote (10-K, as of 2025-12-31); NYSE "controlled company" (exemptions
not currently used). A Section 203-style charter provision blocks
business combinations with a 15% holder for three years unless approved.
This forecloses an unsolicited takeover and any activist path; it also
means the TRA's $1.6B due to the Garcias and DriveTime's related-party
contracts (VSC administration, loan servicing, leases; $117M of Q2's $526M
other revenue is from related parties) are governed by the same hands.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue and operating income,
$M (stockanalysis income-statement route; TTM = four quarters to
2026-06-30):

| year | revenue | operating income | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 12,814 | −104 | −0.8% |
| 2022 | 13,604 | −1,447 | −10.6% |
| 2023 | 10,771 | −65 | −0.6% |
| 2024 | 13,673 | 1,002 | 7.3% |
| 2025 | 20,322 | 1,884 | 9.3% |

Revenue roughly doubled from 2023 while operating income swung from −$65M
to +$2.24B; the TTM margin dipped 40bp because Q2's revenue line carries
gross-revenue treatment for marketplace-sourced cars (management: lapses
in Q3). Stock compensation is $97M TTM and is charged inside these
figures. The kill in Phase 0 does not fire: the company is profitable on
every line down to net income, and leverage is 1.0x net debt to adjusted
EBITDA.
