## 1. Verdict and thesis

**Ownership call: PASS at $51.16.** Kill-thesis verdict: **SOUND** (§5). This is a
genuinely good, defensible business having the best year in its history — and the price
already pays for it. On an owner-share, SBC-adjusted FCF base of ~$63M (2.3% yield),
every reverse-DCF path lands below the 8.6% hurdle: conservative ≈5%, base ≈6%,
optimistic ≈7% (ranges deliberately rounded; ATM IV is ~49%). Granting the bull his
whole case — a $100M forward base *plus* five more years of 8–15% growth on top of the
record year — still implies only 8.0% (−57bp). **Three load-bearing conditions, two
probable, one plausible.** The average analyst PT is $51.18 — the market cap sits
exactly on consensus.

The sentence that survives the attack: pre-COVID IMAX did $395.7M of revenue in 2019
and $410.2M in 2025 (+3.7% over six years, EDGAR XBRL); the "new era of growth" has
exactly one data point above the old plateau, it is a Nolan-plus-Dune year, and the
price treats it as the new floor.

Reopen: **2027-02-25** (Q4'26 print — FY26 owner FCF actual and FY27 guide), or on
price ≤ ~$35, where the market-case path clears the hurdle (shift-class: revalue,
not sell — there is no position).

## 2. Business

**Created** — the premium theatrical event. The customer gets the biggest, sharpest
picture in a format some directors design for from the first shot; the surplus shows in
behavior no ordinary release produces: 70mm shows selling out 7–8 weeks deep, sellouts
at 2 a.m., tickets scalped near $1,000 (CEO, Q2 call). Exhibitors get a certified
traffic magnet; studios get a marketing platform (the Odyssey campaign made IMAX the
centerpiece "from the very first tweet").

**Captured** — three concrete mechanisms (Q2'26 10-Q segments): (1) *Content
solutions* (~$35M/q, 63% gross margin) — a take-rate on IMAX-ticket box office via DMR
remastering fees; higher take at the 41 film-projection locations, whose prints IMAX
subsidizes and shares in. (2) *Technology products & services* (~$65M/q, 60% GM) —
system sales, joint-revenue-share installs (ongoing box-office share instead of an
upfront sale; 20 of 38 Q2 installs), maintenance. (3) Nascent: merchandising (popcorn
buckets: 10,000+ units, sell-outs in minutes — proof of brand heat, ~$1M-scale today)
and a China in-vehicle-entertainment license where the partner absorbs R&D and capex.
Incrementality: adjusted EBITDA margin kicks up when quarterly GBO clears ~$250M (CFO,
Q2 call — Q2 did $284M, 46.6% margin, +400bp YoY).

**Protected** — mechanisms, not labels: (1) end-to-end proprietary stack (cameras →
DMR → projection → certified screens) that took decades and a Nolan partnership to
assemble — a rival must win filmmakers, studios, *and* exhibitors simultaneously;
(2) filmmaker lock-in at the top of the craft (Nolan, Villeneuve, Coogler shooting
*with IMAX cameras* — the format is embedded in production, not added in post; "knockoffs
and marketing gimmicks won't cut it" was the exhibitor read-through of Odyssey week);
(3) finite 70mm real estate (Rolls-Royce economics — $50k/print logistics, huge booths;
CEO says the count is bounded, which *protects* scarcity value while capping that
segment); (4) the brand as the consumer's one-word shorthand for premium theatrical.
The honest hole: the moat protects IMAX's *share of blockbusters*, not the size or
stability of the blockbuster pool — the pool is the studios' slate, which IMAX does not
control. Write that down as the structural dependency.

## 5. Kill-thesis (verdict: SOUND)

The PASS rests on three conditions: **(a)** the owner-flow construction (NCI haircut +
SBC deduction) [probable — NCI ratio verified against IMAX China's own USD statements;
SBC deduction is the skill's standing rule]; **(b)** a hurdle near 8.6% [probable —
the 0.8 floor turned out to equal the bottom-up sector beta]; **(c)** no unmodeled
optionality worth ≥200bp [plausible — merch is ~$1M-scale, the AV deal is an
unquantified royalty, 70mm is deliberately finite].

**The attack that came closest:** accept the printed 0.39 beta (hurdle 6.61%) *and*
the optimistic path (7.09%) — the pass flips by +48bp. It fails because it needs two
stretched inputs simultaneously, and the bottom-up beta independently rejects the
first. **The attack that failed most informatively:** grant the bull everything —
FY26 windfall base and five more years of double-digit growth — and the implied
return still misses the hurdle (−57bp). The pass does not depend on the slate
mean-reverting; conditions "2027 normalizes" and "TTM base understated" dropped out
as load-bearing because the pass survives their negation. Base rates cited: ~29%
excess-return persistence; IMAX's own 2015→2017 and 2023→2024 post-peak regressions.
