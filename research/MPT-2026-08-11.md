# MPT — Medical Properties Trust (2026-08-11)

Price $4.02 (official close 2026-08-11) · market cap $2.404B · 598.1M shares ·
formerly ticker MPW (`/stocks/MPW/` is now dead; the company files as MPT).

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**PASS.** MPT just refinanced its 2026–2028 maturity wall into $2.4B of 9.25%
senior secured notes due 2032 — roughly 575bp above the ~3.5% weighted coupon
it retired — which adds about **$133M/yr of interest** against trailing free
cash flow of **$167M**. The equity is a thin residual (net debt $9.31B is 3.9×
market cap) whose implied return straddles its own cost-of-equity hurdle only
under assumptions I could not verify.

kill-thesis: **FLAWED** (repairable) · **5 load-bearing conditions, 1 refuted,
3 UNKNOWN.** The refuted condition is my own: "every scenario, including the
optimistic one, sits below the hurdle" does not survive — two defensible input
choices each flip the sign (see §4).

## 2. Business

**Created.** MPT buys hospital real estate and leases it back to the operator,
usually on long triple-net master leases with fixed escalators. The operator
gets capital out of a building it was using anyway; MPT gets a bond-like income
stream at a wider spread than a bond, because it is lending against a
single-purpose asset to a single-purpose credit.

**Captured.** Base rent plus escalators, plus interest on operator loans
(TTM interest income $85.7M against property revenue $974.3M), plus equity
stakes in operators. Three of these are the same bet: the landlord, the lender,
and the shareholder of the tenant are all MPT.

**Protected.** There is no mechanism. Hospital real estate is bought at auction
by anyone with capital; the only barrier is willingness to underwrite the
operator. MPT's contractual protection — master leases, cross-default — is what
Steward was supposed to trip and did not, because a landlord cannot evict a
hospital. What actually protects the rent is the operator's EBITDARM coverage,
and MPT discloses that: general acute 2.8×, post-acute 2.4×, behavioral 1.4×
(Q2 2026 call). The 1.4× segment is the UK behavioral book under NHS funding
pressure. Its own two largest recovery tenants still are not paying full rent
two years after Steward.

## 3. Threads pulled

**The refinancing is the whole story, and it is a cost-of-capital event.**
Retired: $577.8M of 0.993% 2026 notes (cash) plus $1,945.3M exchanged
(5.000% '27 $734.6M · 3.692% '28 $202.7M · 4.625% '29 $318.8M · 3.375% '30
$76.7M · 3.500% '31 $612.5M) = $2,523.1M at a 3.52% weighted coupon = $88.7M/yr.
Issued: $2,400.0M at 9.25% = $222.0M/yr. **Δ = +$133.3M/yr = $0.223/share**,
against TTM normalized FFO of ~$0.60/share — a 37% hit. Independent cross-check:
the reported weighted-average rate of 5.2% moves to ~6.6% on $9.705B of debt,
= +$136M. GoldenTree led. The $123M "debt reduction" headline is the exchange
discount, and it is worth $11M/yr at the new coupon — a twelfth of the step-up.

**Two covenant numbers explain why they paid 9.25%.** Unencumbered-to-unsecured
was running 155–160% against a **150% covenant**, and secured debt goes from
~25% to "near 40%," which management called the covenant limit. This was a
necessary refinancing done from a weak position, not an opportunistic one — and
it structurally subordinates the ~$2.9B of unsecured notes that stayed put.

**Cash is materially below FFO, and that gap is the bull case.** TTM normalized
FFO annualizes to ~$359M; TTM operating cash flow was $226.8M. The $132M gap is
close to the refi step-up, which is why the bull case is "the cash-rent ramp
cancels the coupon." NOR pays 50% of contractual rent, going to 100% by
mid-December; HSA pays 75%, going to 100% in mid-September. **I could not verify
how much of that $132M gap is uncollected rent** — the Q2 supplemental PDF is
unreadable and EDGAR returned 403 (below). Working capital, the $20.9M tax
provision, and a $17M impairment of Steward-successor working-capital loans are
also in it.

**Asset sales are the deleveraging plan, and the notes may block them.**
Management expects ~$1.1B of liquidity: Infracore IPO (~$140M), one sale closing
at $172M net of debt (60% gain, 34% IRR), $200–400M in negotiation — all
inbound, none marketed. But the new notes carry a **make-whole for two years**.
Whether asset-sale proceeds can retire 9.25% paper at par inside that window is
the single most valuable unknown here and it sits in an indenture I could not
fetch. Selection bias also applies: the assets buyers want are the Swiss and
international ones, which leaves a residual of US general-acute hospitals with
recovering operators.

**Operating leverage on a cash basis is flat, not positive.** Revenue
1,545 → 1,543 → 872 → 996 → 972 → 1,019 ($M, FY2021→TTM); operating income
999 → 735 → (296) → (1,439) → 346 → 425; free cash flow 744 → 630 → 391 →
166 → 151 → 167. The 2024→TTM swing in operating income is impairments
reversing out, not leverage. Two years of "recovery" produced **$1M** of
incremental free cash flow. Debt is down only 14% from the 2021 peak and rose
$857M in 2025.

**Dead ends.** (i) No insider open-market buying after an 11.7% one-day drop —
July Form 4s are tax-withholding dispositions only; no conviction signal either
way. (ii) SEC EDGAR returned HTTP 403 to every request this run (browse-edgar
and Archives alike), so the 8-K, the indenture, and the 10-K Item 1A sweep did
not happen; refinancing terms rest on two independent renderings of the same
company press release, which agree. (iii) `sqlite3` was blocked by this
session's sandbox, so `sec_fundamentals.db`, `composite.db`, `stocks.db`,
`options.db` and `earnings.db` were not read — no point-in-time cross-check,
and no record of whether composite ever flagged this name. Live probe figures
reconcile exactly against the rendered financials table on the same source
(revenue $1,019M, operating income $425M, debt $9,705M, FCF $167M), which is
the only cross-check available.

**Analyst distribution** (context, not evidence): 3 buy / 3 hold / 2 sell /
1 strong sell, mean target $5.43. A sell-side split this wide is unusual and is
consistent with a name whose value depends on which lens you use.

## 4. Valuation

Levered `fcf` (= `ncfo` + `capex`, post-interest under US GAAP) paired with
**market cap**, per the pairing rule. Using EV here would be a catastrophic
error — net debt is 3.9× market cap. Minority interest ($1.05M on the balance
sheet) is immaterial; no haircut. Hurdle = 4.74% + 1.46 × 4.28% = **10.99%**
(risk-free and implied ERP as of **2026-08-01**; beta 1.46 from the statistics
page). Base FCF is deliberately generous: acquisitions ($69M TTM) are excluded
from `fcf` and I did not charge them.

| scenario | base FCF | growth / terminal | implied return | spread vs 10.99% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| trailing, unadjusted (pre-refi) | $167.3M | 0% ×5 / 2.0% | **8.50%** | −249bp |
| conservative: less SBC, pre-refi | $154.6M | 0% ×5 / 1.5% | **7.61%** | −338bp |
| refi bites, ramp half-converts | $117.3M | 2% ×5 / 1.5% | **6.56%** | −443bp |
| optimistic: full ramp + accretive sales | $185.0M | 3% ×5 / 2.5% | **10.55%** | −43bp |
| aggressive: sales at a 5.5% exit cap, no make-whole | $195.6M | 3% ×5 / 2.5% | **11.01%** | **+3bp** |

**The last row is why the pass call is FLAWED as originally stated.** At beta
1.0 — a REIT-typical figure — the hurdle falls to 9.02% and the *optimistic*
row clears by +153bp. Two defensible input choices each erase the margin. I
keep beta 1.46 because an equity stub with 3.9× net-debt-to-market-cap should
carry a high levered beta, but the pass does not rest on that 43bp.

**What the pass actually rests on:** the whole plausible range is 6.6%–11.0%,
against a US median cost of capital of 7.79% with 80% of firms in 5.26%–9.88%.
An asset base of $12.9B supports a $2.4B equity: **a 10% write-down removes 61%
of the market cap**, and MPT wrote down $2.4B in FY2024 alone. That asymmetry,
not the hurdle arithmetic, is the argument.

`--base-earnings` on the trailing run prints `implied_terminal_roe: 3.74%`
against a 10.99% hurdle — the terminal value assumes MPT destroys value
forever, using annualized normalized FFO ($359M) as cash earnings because
real-estate depreciation ($273M) overstates economic decay. That is not an
oversight to fix: it is the correct description of a business earning 6.7% on
gross assets while paying 9.25% at the margin. Only ~29% of firms earn above
their cost of capital; MPT's own numbers put it in the other 71%.

**Terminal-risk note.** The dominant structural risk is payer, not tenant:
HSA's Q2 shortfall was caused by delayed Florida state supplemental (DPP)
payments, and the UK behavioral book (1.4× coverage) turns on NHS funding.
Terminal growth of 1.5% in the conservative case is deliberately below
inflation for that reason. **The Item 1A sweep this should have rested on did
not run — EDGAR 403.**

**Market share:** 3%/yr for five years takes rent from ~$1.0B to ~$1.19B, a
rounding error against US hospital real estate. No "bigger than the market"
problem.

## 5. Falsifiers

What would flip this to a buy — the pass is the position, so these run the
other way:

1. **[shift]** Q3 (2026-10-29): cash rent up more than ~$30M/quarter, closing
   the FFO-to-cash gap faster than the coupon compounds. This is the whole bull
   case in one number.
2. **[shift]** Step-two refinancing prices materially below 9.25%, or an
   indenture disclosure showing asset-sale proceeds retire the notes at par
   inside the two-year make-whole.
3. **[shift]** The $1.1B of sales closing with disclosed exit cap rates below
   ~7%, making the sell-assets-to-retire-9.25%-debt loop clearly accretive.
4. **[shift]** Price below ~$3.00: at 0.40× book a 20% portfolio write-down
   still leaves book above price, and the option value starts to dominate.

What would confirm the pass and make it a short-adjacent avoid:

5. **[break]** A third dividend cut, or any tenant in the recovering cohort
   (NOR, HSA) slipping backwards rather than ramping.
6. **[break]** Secured debt hitting the ~40% covenant limit with the UI/UD
   ratio failing to reach the promised ~200%.
