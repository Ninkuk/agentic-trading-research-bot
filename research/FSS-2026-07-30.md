## 2. Business

**Creates value:** municipal and industrial work trucks that are legally or operationally
non-discretionary — street sweeping (Elgin), sewer cleaning (Vactor), safe-digging /
hydro-excavation (TRUVAC, mandated by damage-prevention law), industrial vacuum (Guzzler),
dump bodies (TBEI brands), refuse trucks (New Way), plus SSG's police/emergency lightbars,
sirens, and warning systems. Customers buy uptime and spec compliance, not price alone.

**Captures value:** three distinct mechanisms, not one. (1) New-vehicle sales, ~45% of
sales backlog-driven ($1.0B backlog = forward visibility); (2) an aftermarket ecosystem —
parts, service, rental, rent-to-own, used — now ~25% of ESG revenue, +24% YoY, higher
margin, deliberately expanded (~20 service centers added since 2019, "Build More Parts"
vertical integration); (3) price: 3.5pts of Q2's 6% organic growth was price, taken
without volume loss.

**Protects value:** leading domestic share in niches too small for a diversified truck
OEM to attack economically; dealer/service network density; certification and
spec-relationship barriers (management: "motor barrier to entry around certifications",
SSG police/fire); an install base that feeds the aftermarket flywheel. The mechanism is
evidenced, not asserted: gross margin 29.2% / op margin 16.7% (statistics route, TTM) are
far above specialty-vehicle peers, and ROIC is 16.2% with goodwill at 26% of assets.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Growth mix (the big one).** Q2 sales +19%, but organic only +6%, and price was
  3.5pts of that → real organic volume ~2.5%. $75M of the $106M increase was acquired
  (New Way, Mega). This is a serial-acquirer story; organic volume alone does not
  justify a premium multiple. Fed into valuation as the 5–6% growth assumption.
- **Operating-leverage trajectory (Phase 0, direction: positive).** Revenue 2021→TTM:
  $1,213M → $2,448M (2.0×); operating income $131M → $395M (3.0×); margin 10.8% → 16.2%,
  rising every year. Attacked via SEC XBRL: FY2019 margin was 12.0%, so ~120bps of the
  headline expansion is recovery from the disrupted 2021 base; **~420bps is structural**.
- **Backlog decline.** $1.0B, -8% YoY — but $75M of the decline is the planned
  discontinuation of third-party Labrie refuse production (Q4'25); $44M of that backlog
  remains. Underlying orders +19% ex-Labrie. Dead end as a bear signal, alive as a
  "watch next two quarters" item.
- **Cyclicality history.** SEC XBRL 2009–2016: operating income $35.8M (2009), $12.4M
  (2010), $60.8M (2016 dip). The pre-transformation company was crushed by the muni
  funding crunch. The 2020 test was mild (-11% opinc). Management's diversification
  story (55% public funding, largest single source <15% of sales, only pure-play US muni
  exposure = street sweepers at mid-to-high single digits) is credible but untested at
  this scale through a real tax-revenue recession; the 10-K itself states a 1–2 year lag
  between the economy and government sales.
- **Cash conversion.** Q2 OCF $113M (+89%), H1 +122% YoY, 131% of net income — above
  management's own ~100% through-cycle target, so the TTM FCF of $339M is likely
  WC-flattered. Handled by running the valuation on a $285M normalized base too.
- **Asset-light check (10-K commitments).** Purchase obligations $281.5M ($268.5M <1yr)
  are "primarily commercial chassis" — backlog-serving inventory, not hidden capex.
  Capex guide $45–55M (~2% of sales) stands. Long-term debt $564M at 12/31/25, $97M paid
  down in Q2, net debt now $391M (<1× EBITDA of $486M TTM). Contingent earn-outs $29.6M.

- **Refuse-industry contradiction check.** Competing refuse OEMs gave mixed commentary
