## 2. Business

**Created:** MPT buys hospital real estate and leases it back to the operator
on long triple-net master leases with fixed escalators. The operator converts
a building it was already using into capital; MPT gets a bond-like income
stream at a wider spread than a bond, because it is lending against a
single-purpose asset to a single-purpose credit.

**Captured:** three distinct mechanisms, and they are the same bet three
times. (i) Base rent plus escalators on $12.66B of gross real estate. (ii)
Interest on operator loans — "Other loans" rose from $186.3M to $286.5M in
the six months to 2026-06-30, including a $50M working-capital advance to HSA
"due to ongoing revenue cycle management issues," plus $172.0M of working
capital loans advanced in the re-tenanting cohort. (iii) Equity stakes in the
operators themselves — $313.7M of investments in unconsolidated operating
entities, plus $1,371.7M in real-estate joint ventures. The landlord, the
lender and the shareholder of the tenant are one entity, so a tenant's
distress arrives through three doors at once.

**Protected:** there is no mechanism. Hospital real estate is bought at
auction by anyone with capital; the only barrier is willingness to underwrite
the operator. MPT's contractual protection — master leases, cross-default —
is exactly what Steward was supposed to trip and did not, because a landlord
cannot evict a hospital. What actually protects the rent is operator EBITDARM
coverage: general acute 2.8×, post-acute 2.4×, behavioral 1.4× (Q2 2026
call). Behavioral is 16.2% of assets and the 1.4× figure was "down slightly."

**Control:** one share, one vote; no controlling holder. `stocks.db` shows
insiders at 17.9% and institutions at 62.1% of shares, with float 489.8M of
597.2M shares out (82.0%). Nothing in the structure forecloses a takeover or
an activist — but 4.27× net-debt-to-market-cap does: any acquirer buys the
$9.3B of debt with the equity, and the new 9.25% secured notes sit ahead of
everything.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative,** on a cash basis. Q2 2026 revenue
was $259.3M against $240.4M a year earlier (+7.9%), and 1H26 net income swung
to +$30.8M from −$216.1M as impairments stopped repeating — but 1H26
operating cash flow was **$48.2M against $52.1M** in 1H25, a 7.6% decline,
and non-cash straight-line rent revenue of $70.3M was deducted to get there.
Revenue up, cash down. The full-year series carried forward from the
2026-08-11 run (stockanalysis, FY2021→TTM, $M): revenue 1,545 → 1,543 → 872 →
996 → 972 → 1,019; operating income 999 → 735 → (296) → (1,439) → 346 → 425;
free cash flow 744 → 630 → 391 → 166 → 151 → 167. The 2024→TTM swing in
operating income is impairments reversing out, not leverage. Two and a half
years of "recovery" have produced no incremental free cash flow.

## 5. Falsifiers

**For the pass (flip toward buy):**

1. **Shift —** Q3 2026 (2026-10-29): quarterly operating cash flow at or above
   ~$90M **with the straight-line rent receivable flat or below $927.5M**.
   Both halves are required — cash up while the receivable also grows is the
   accrual getting bigger, not converting. This un-refutes condition 1 and is
   the whole bull case in two numbers.
2. **Shift —** Step Two completes at a coupon materially below 9.25% with no
   equity issuance, disclosed by 8-K or at the Q3 print. That would say the
   capital markets disagree with the 9.25% print, and would cut rows 3–6's
   interest drag directly.
3. **Shift —** further disposals at disclosed exit caps at or below ~7.5%
   covering a materially larger slice of the $12.66B gross book than the 1.9%
   the Idaho sale covered. That would be the portfolio mark no filing
   otherwise supplies.
4. **Shift —** price below ~$2.50 — the option lens's bear-mark equity value
   (book at a ~9% cap). Below it the market is paying less than a distressed
   appraisal, and the asymmetry inverts.

**For an owner (sell):**

5. **Break —** a third dividend cut, or any straight-line rent write-off
   against the $927.5M balance. Either says the accrual was never rent.
6. **Break —** secured debt reaching the ~40% covenant ceiling while the
   unencumbered-to-unsecured ratio fails to hold the "almost 200%" management
   claimed post-Step-One. That forecloses the last financing lever and leaves
   only forced asset sales.
7. **Break —** NOR or HSA slipping from the stated ramp (NOR to 100% in
   mid-December, HSA to 100% in mid-September), or a further working-capital
   advance to either. MPT has already lent HSA $50M; a second advance means
   the rent is being funded by the landlord.
