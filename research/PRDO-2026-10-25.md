## 2. Business

**Created:** working adults (63% over 30) get an accredited degree they can
finish around a job. CTU (69% of enrollments) and AIUS (22%) are
open-enrollment and 91% fully online, on a mobile-first platform with an
adaptive learning engine. USAHS (9%) is the opposite product: a selective,
campus-based graduate health school (DPT, OTD, SLP, nursing) that sells a
path to a professional licence (FY2025 10-K).

**Captured:** (i) tuition and fees, 99.4% of revenue, billed per term and
~76% funded by Title IV grants and loans that the institution draws down;
(ii) employer-paid corporate student programs, non-federal money that also
helps 90/10; (iii) military/VA benefits, which count as federal money under
the 2023 90/10 rule. Gross margin is 79.9%. Growth spending (marketing and
admissions) sits in a G&A line equal to 46% of revenue, not in capex
(capex ~1% of revenue). TTM operating margin is 24.6% (statistics page;
10-K).

**Protected:** no pricing power. The online adult market is fragmented (Grand
Canyon, Strategic, Adtalem, SNHU, WGU). The protection comes from three
frictions. (1) The regulatory licence: Title IV certification, state
authorization and accreditation take years, and any change of control
brings provisional re-certification. (2) The shared-services spine (aid
processing, compliance, marketing, curriculum) took USAHS from $3.2M of
operating income to a ~$43M run-rate in six quarters. (3) Retention near
multi-year highs. The gap: rivals can buy the same search and LLM traffic,
and management's reply that most inquiries come from unaffected channels
is a claim, not a moat.

**Control:** one share class, one vote per share, no controlling holder;
insiders 1.04% (`stocks.db`), institutions 98.0%. The real anti-takeover
device is regulatory: a change of control triggers Department, state and
accreditor re-approval (10-K). The annual bonus is 80% weighted to
adjusted operating income (DEF 2026-04-09).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue $693M (2021) → $695M →
$710M → $681M → $846M (2025) → $859M TTM. Operating income $149M → $133M →
$165M → $179M → $196M → $211M. Revenue +24% vs operating income +41% over
the span. Excluding the USAHS acquisition, 2021–2024 revenue fell 2% while
operating income rose 20%. The leverage comes from cost discipline and mix,
not volume. These are the prior file's figures; TTM is unchanged on today's
statistics page (revenue $858.61M, operating income $210.88M).
