## 2. Business

**Created** — overbuilt durable outdoor gear (coolers, drinkware, bags,
cases) at 2–4× category price points. The customer buys durability and
performance plus status signaling; the product is deliberately
over-engineered, which is where the consumer surplus lives. Brand functions
as a trust marker for "won't fail in the field."

**Captured** — premium pricing across two channels: wholesale ~45% of sales
(Q2: $218M, +10%) and DTC ~55% ($266M, +7% — e-commerce, Amazon marketplace,
2 new owned stores in Q2, corporate/customization sales). Category mix:
Drinkware $241M (+2%), Coolers & Equipment $232M (+16%). Geography: US $391M
(+6%), international $93M (+19%; Europe/Australia/Japan; 11 markets by
end-2026 vs 4 a year ago). Capture is concrete: same product platforms
re-sold across new channels, new geographies, and corporate customization at
premium ASPs.

**Protected** — the protection is the brand and the wholesale shelf position,
not technology. Insulation is commodity engineering: Stanley's 2023–24 surge
and Owala's current one prove entry into drinkware is cheap and
fashion-driven. YETI's answer is two decades of authenticity (ambassadors,
hunting/fishing/BBQ communities), breadth (growth "isn't tethered to a single
product cycle" — CEO, Q2 call), and premium shelf relationships. That moat
defends *price* (gross margin 58–59% held through the fad cycle) better than
it defends *category growth* (US drinkware flat in Q2, ~600bp drag on US
drinkware growth in 2026 from three trend-driven SKUs — management's own
number). The silence worth writing down: nothing structural stops the next
viral drinkware brand; what is protected is the premium-cooler franchise and
the brand's license to extend into bags/cases.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** — the structural flag from triage.
Revenue FY2021→TTM: $1,411M→$1,936M (+37%); operating income $274.9M→$235.8M
(−14%); op margin 19.5%→12.2%. All of it is SG&A: $527.5M→$873.1M (+65%),
37.4%→45.1% of sales, while gross margin *improved* to 59.2%. The SG&A is DTC
mix (stores, fulfillment), international build-out, five design centers, and
brand campaigns. The open question this leaves: is that spend an investment
that scales down as a % of sales (management: H2'26 OM +280bp YoY; Investor
Day 9/17 promises "a clear, credible path to outsized EPS growth"), or a
treadmill required to defend a contested category? Five years of evidence say
it has not yet operated as leverage. — *Unresolved; this is the pivotal
load-bearing condition.*
