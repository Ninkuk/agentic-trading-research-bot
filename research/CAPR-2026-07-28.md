## 2. Business

**Created — contingent, and disputed.** DMD cardiomyopathy is the leading
cause of death in Duchenne; standard of care (ACE inhibitors, beta blockers,
corticosteroids) is borrowed from general cardiology and disease-modifying options
are absent. Deramiocel (allogeneic cardiosphere-derived cells, IV infusion) claims
to slow that decline. Whether it does is precisely what FDA disputes. FDA's
briefing document says the exosome/growth-factor mechanism is "not targeted or
specific enough to make this mechanism biologically plausible as a
disease-modifying therapy in DMD or DMD cardiomyopathy," and notes cardiac
retention below 1% after IV dosing — i.e., the agency questions whether the cells
reach the organ they are meant to treat. Two further nonclinical findings cut the
same way: deramiocel cultures **upregulate IL-6 mRNA**, while the literature FDA
cites (Rufo 2011, Wada 2017) shows IL-6 *inhibition* slows dystrophy progression in
DMD mice — the product moves the marker the wrong way; and the mdx-mouse POC study
"is not adequate to serve as confirmatory evidence" (analogous murine test article,
saline-only control, no dose response). **I cannot adjudicate cell-therapy
paracrine-mechanism claims. Marked UNKNOWN, and Phase 3 counts it among the
load-bearing conditions.**

**Captured — not directly, and the mechanism is in litigation.** Under a
January 2022 Commercialization and Distribution Agreement, Nippon Shinyaku's US
arm NS Pharma distributes deramiocel in the US and Capricor takes a contractual
transfer price. On 2026-05-07 Capricor sued its own partner, alleging a "fatal
flaw" in that pricing formula that makes the therapy "economically impracticable"
under Medicare/Medicaid/commercial reimbursement, and seeking **rescission** of the
agreement. So the company's own pleading says its capture mechanism does not work.
Secondary capture: a one-time, transferable PRV on approval.

**Protected — a real mechanism, but it protects nothing until approval.**
Capricor owns and operates its own San Diego GMP cell-therapy facility (PLI
completed; second-floor expansion targeting H1 2027). Allogeneic CDC manufacture
is genuinely hard to copy and it is the strongest non-label answer here. Layered
on top: orphan designation (7-year exclusivity) and 12-year BLA reference-product
exclusivity. None of it binds on a CRL.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** — and it is not close.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **The July 27 gap is the whole story, and it is not a sentiment event.** FDA's
  briefing document states the pre-specified HOPE-3 primary analysis missed: PUL 2.0
  absolute change **0.66 points (95% CI −0.45, 1.77), p=0.24** — LS means −1.73
  (deramiocel) vs −2.39 (placebo), i.e. both arms declined and the gap is noise. The
  trial was **powered for 1.5 points at 90%** (n=68 assumed, SD 1.71), so it observed
  under half the effect it was built to find. No subgroup rescues it: none reached
  nominal significance, and in some the subgroup and its complement move in opposite
  directions — "consistent with what would be expected under an overall null result."
- **Capricor's headline win comes from a late SAP.** Its headline win (PUL 2.0
  p=0.029; LVEF ranked-change p=0.041 — **the LVEF figure was revised to p=0.09
  by the company on 2026-07-29**, see below) comes from **SAP v3.0, finalized
  2025-11-24** — nine days before the 2025-12-03 topline — which FDA says was
  never submitted to or agreed by the agency before resubmission. The changes
  converted the primary from absolute to *percent* change and switched LVEF to
  a "ranked change" FDA calls clinically uninterpretable.
- **Is that reading fair to Capricor?** Partly. A sponsor is entitled to finalize an
  SAP before unblinding, and Capricor asserts v3.0 was. FDA's counter is not "you
  changed it late" but "you changed it without us, and we assess against what was
  agreed." Both can be true. On the percent-change switch specifically, Capricor's
  stated justification — heterogeneity of functional ability in DMD — was already
  answered by the trial's own design: FDA notes that heterogeneity "was known at the
  time of HOPE-3 study design," which had addressed it through randomization
  stratification and a baseline-PUL covariate.
- **What is *not* rescuable is the sensitivity finding:** significance disappears
  under alternative missing-data assumptions affecting **as few as two placebo
  patients** — FDA's words, "their handling had a large influence on the study
  results and conclusions." Strip the imputation and p goes 0.045 → 0.21. A
  result that fragile is fragile under any SAP.
- **Functional unblinding is the sharper problem.** Hypersensitivity reactions ran
  **41.5% treated vs 15.4% placebo**, and eleven placebo patients developed them on
  crossing to open-label. PUL 2.0 is an effort-dependent performance measure. A
  patient who knows they are dosed, assessed on effort, is the textbook bias channel
  — and it points the same direction as the claimed benefit.
- **But do not read the safety data as the obstacle — it isn't.** Of six SAEs in the
  double-blind period, **five were placebo and one deramiocel**, and the single Grade
  4 anaphylaxis was a *placebo* subject, which FDA attributes to shared excipients
  (placebo carried every excipient except the cells). Hypersensitivity matters here
  as an *unblinding* channel, not a tolerability verdict. FDA's actual bottom line is
  efficacy-driven: the review team "was unable to identify a subpopulation that may
  potentially derive benefit," HOPE-3 with HOPE-2 "do not conclusively provide
  evidence that deramiocel is effective," and benefit-risk "appears unfavorable **in
  the absence of evidence of effectiveness**." That last clause is the whole finding
  — risk unoffset by absent benefit, not an unsafe product.
- **The mechanism finding is regulatory, not just biological — and that closes the
  back door.** A missed primary endpoint can sometimes still reach substantial
  evidence via one adequate trial plus *confirmatory evidence* (FDA guidance, Sept
  2023). FDA shuts both routes: biological plausibility and "the strength of the
  nonclinical evidence" are named factors in its substantial-evidence framework
  (June 2026 draft guidance) and it answers them negatively, while the mdx POC study
  is separately ruled inadequate *as* confirmatory evidence. So the mechanism section
  is not colour commentary — it is the second lock.
- **2026-07-29, hours before the vote — three same-day disclosures, and one of them
  retracts the bull's cardiac number.** All three sit in a single 8-K (Item 8.01,
  acc. 0001104659-26-087891), issued 10:05am ET on the morning of the AdCom:
  - ***The Lancet* published HOPE-3**, on the SAP 3.0 analysis. Independent peer

  - **Capricor revised its own LVEF result downward: p=0.09 (1.8pp difference), from

  - **A BIMO inspection (2026-07-06 → 07-20) closed with a Form FDA 483**, one
