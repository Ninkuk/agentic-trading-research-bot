## 2. Business

**Created:** one commercial asset: XPOVIO (selinexor), a first-in-class oral
XPO1 (exportin-1) nuclear-export inhibitor, approved in relapsed/refractory
multiple myeloma and DLBCL. The customer is a haematologist with a patient
who has exhausted prior lines; what they get is a mechanism nothing else on
the shelf shares, given orally. Real but narrow — US net product revenue
$114.9M in FY2025 vs $112.8M in FY2024 (company). Eleven years post-IPO, the
drug has never covered its own R&D.

**Captured:** almost entirely US branded net product revenue ($29.2M of
Q1'26's $35.1M total), plus a thin licensing tail ($5.9M in Q1'26 — $3.5M
milestones, $1.9M royalties) from ex-US partners. Gross margin is ~96% (TTM
revenue $151.1M less COGS $6.0M), so the economics are entirely about opex,
not unit cost.

**Protected:** composition-of-matter patents plus regulatory exclusivity on a
first-in-class mechanism — a genuine mechanism, not a checkbox label. But the
moat protects the *enterprise*, and in this capital structure the creditors
own the enterprise. A moat around an asset whose equity sits $212M underwater
in net debt protects the wrong party. That distinction is the whole thesis.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0):** flat revenue, persistent
nine-figure-adjacent operating loss — no year in which the product covered
its cost base.

| Period | Revenue | Operating income |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 (US product only) | $112.8M | n/d |
| FY2025 | $146M total / $114.9M US product | n/d |

The loss is narrowing (−$118.9M → −$84.2M), which is real. But revenue has
been flat at ~$113–115M of US product for three years, and management's own
2026 guidance holds the operating loss at $80–115M. On the current plan the
business does not reach breakeven, and that is before ~$47M/yr of interest
expense.
