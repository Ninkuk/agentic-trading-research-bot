# PAYC — Paycom Software — 2026-07-30

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**Pass at $161.43.** A genuinely high-quality, founder-led payroll/HCM business at a
merely fair price whose margin of safety was consumed by a 22% sector-sentiment rip in
the three sessions before this run (Jul 24 $145.35 → Jul 29 $168.92, −4.4% today), with
the Q2 print six days out and an AI-disruption tail the price no longer pays you to
carry. Kill-thesis verdict: **UNPROVEN** (6 load-bearing conditions, 0 refuted, 2
unverifiable); the attack that came closest was the IBM-pattern short case — high-margin
incumbent + secular AI threat + decelerating growth + leveraged buyback is the classic
value-trap shape, and it fails to land today only because retention, client count, and
platform headcount are all still rising.

Thesis (plain): Paycom is a good business, defensible in its installed base, priced at
~17× levered FCF. The reverse DCF implies ~10%/yr on guided growth, ~8%/yr on
AI-erosion assumptions, ~6%/yr at literal zero growth forever. At ~$140 (where it
traded a week ago) the conservative path cleared 9% and this was a buy; at $161 the
conservative path (~8%) barely beats an index fund while retaining a concentrated
left tail. Revisit after the Aug 5 print or on give-back toward ~$140.

## 2. Business

**Creates value:** one system of record for the entire employment lifecycle — payroll,
HR, time, talent — in a single database, so mid-market employers (39,200 clients, ~7.4M
employees on platform, FY25 10-K) escape the integration mess of multi-vendor HCM.
Automation products (Beti: employees run their own payroll; GONE: automated
time-off decisioning; IWant: natural-language interface, usage +33% QoQ per Q1'26 call)
convert that architecture into measured client labor savings (Forrester: 90% payroll-labor
reduction — company-commissioned, treat as directional).

**Captures value:** recurring per-employee-per-period fees across ~33 applications
(recurring + other = 97% of revenue, $544M of $572M in Q1'26), plus interest on ~$3.1B
average client payroll float (~$103M guided for 2026, ~5% of revenue at near-100%
incremental margin). Gross margin 87.6%, GAAP op margin 28.3% TTM, ROIC 30%
(stockanalysis.com TTM).

**Protects value:** no patents ("We do not have any patents or patent applications
pending" — FY25 10-K). The moat is switching costs (payroll conversion is painful,
mid-year tax/W-2 continuity, benefits integrations) plus the single-database
architecture competitors would have to rebuild from scratch to match. Mechanism, not
label — but note its asymmetry: **switching costs defend the installed base, not the
95% of TAM Paycom doesn't have.** New-logo wins run on product and distribution, where
an AI-native entrant is unencumbered.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Why is the stock down 32% in a year?** Growth deceleration (+30% → +23% → +11% →
  +9% FY22–25; FY26 guide +6.5% total) plus the "AI prophecy" — Richison's own words on
  the Q1'26 call: the stock "trades based on the AI prophecy of the day… a sky's
  falling narrative." The 10-K lists AI/disruptive technology first among risk factors.
- **The leveraged buyback (the defining capital-allocation fact).** 8.4M shares — ~15%
  of the company — repurchased in Q1'26 alone for $1.06B (avg ~$126), new $2B
  authorization May 4, funded partly by $675M drawn on a new 5-yr $2.125B facility
  (Q1'26 call). Diluted shares 56.1M FY25 → 46.6M now. Debt $763.6M ≈ 0.98× EBITDA.
  Run-rate interest ~$30M/yr is not yet in TTM FCF (TTM interest expense $6.6M).
- **Is the base eroding? (attacked, survived on primary data)** Revenue retention 90%
  (FY23) → 90% (FY24) → 91% (FY25); clients 37,500 → 39,200; stored employees 7.0M →
  7.4M (FY24 vs FY25 10-Ks). Every observable trend is up. But retention was 93–94%
  "a few years ago" (analyst framing on the Q1 call, uncontested by management).
- **Post-call event sweep (May 6 → today):** no adverse events. Product launch (Asset
  Management tool, Jul 14), board additions incl. former CFO Craig Boelte (8-K Jul 9),
  repeated price-target raises. Insider forms: Richison's Jun 16 Form 4 is a 12,500-share

- **This week's tape:** the Jul 27–29 rip was a sector-wide HCM rally (Paylocity +6.7%,
  ADP +5.0%, Paychex +3.7% same day — Alphastreet, low-confidence tier) — sentiment

- **Rate sensitivity (quantified):** ±100bp = ±$22.1M on float interest (FY25 10-K,

## 4. Valuation

Reverse DCF, levered TTM FCF $445.8M paired with **market cap** $7,531,208,869 (hover
figures, statistics route; net debt 0 per the pairing rule; no minority interests).
ATM IV is 66% > 50%, so implied returns are quoted to the nearest whole percent:

| Assumptions | Implied return |
|---|---|
| FCF +7/7/6/6/5%, terminal 2.5% (≈ guidance) | ~10%/yr |
| +4% flat, terminal 1.5% (AI erosion) | ~8%/yr |
| Base FCF $415M (interest drag), guided path | ~9%/yr |
| Zero growth forever, terminal 0% | ~6%/yr |

The terminal-growth input vs the disclosed terminal risk: the 10-K's first-listed risk
is exactly the AI-disruption scenario, so 2.5% terminal is **not** defended — that is
why the AI-erosion and zero-growth rows are the decision rows. Capex normalization
(Q1'26 ~6% of revenue vs 12.6% TTM) is upside to base FCF; unguided data-center build
is the offsetting downside.

**Options-implied move (path 2 — Robinhood stopgap; path 1 N/A, PAYC not in the CBOE
catalog):** Aug 21 expiry (nearest listed; brackets the Aug 5 AMC print), DTE 22, ATM

## 5. Falsifiers (what would make this a sell if owned / keep it a pass)

- Revenue retention prints < 90% in any 10-K (annual datum; next ~Feb 2027).
- Recurring revenue growth < 5% for two consecutive quarters.
- Stored-employee count falls while client count grows (per-seat shrinkage — the AI
  headcount fear turning observable).
- Demonstrated AI-native competitor wins in Paycom's mid-market segment at scale.
- Capex sustained > 15% of revenue, or debt > 2× EBITDA to fund buybacks.
- What would flip the pass to buy: price near ~$140 (conservative path ≥ 9%), or the
  Aug 5 print showing recurring growth ≥ 8% with a bounded capex guide.
