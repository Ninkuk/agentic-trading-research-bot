# TPR — Tapestry, Inc. — 2026-08-17

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**Kill-thesis verdict: SOUND (6 conditions — 4 probable, 2 plausible — 0 refuted, 0
load-bearing unknowns). Ownership: PASS at $129.02.** Coach is a genuinely healthy brand machine — FY26 revenue
+17% pro forma cc, operating margin 23.4% (+340bp), 11M new customers, ~$1.7B/yr returned
to holders (~6.6% of market cap) — and the −16.5% post-print drop (Aug 12 $153.74 → Aug 13
$128.39) repriced deceleration, not a miss. But even at the new price the market implies
only **9.6% (conservative base) to 10.5% (raw TTM base)** against a **10.4% (β floored
to 1.2) to 11.6% (regression β 1.46) cost-of-equity hurdle** — a spread of −78 to −200bp
on the conservative base. A mono-brand fashion house whose second brand (Kate Spade,
−11% cc, guided to an operating loss) is the live demonstration of what a cold brand does
has to *clear* its hurdle, not brush it. The conservative case clears the friendly hurdle
near **$116**, and the strict one near **$100**. Six load-bearing conditions: four
probable, two plausible.

**Closest attack (kill-thesis):** the hurdle/growth stack — the pass is narrower than it
looks. Bull-max rerun (raw $1.8125B base, 8%×5y, β 1.2, US-only headline ERP 4.28%) flips
the spread to **+166bp** — but that requires counting SBC as owner cash *and* applying a
US-only ERP to a 37%-non-US revenue mix, each a stated-rule violation. The strongest
*honest* bull case — conservative base, growth at 8% (top of credible for the consolidated
company while Kate Spade still shrinks), β floored to 1.2, country-weighted ERP — reaches
~10.35% vs a 10.37% hurdle: zero spread, never positive. The pass therefore hangs on two
defensible choices (the SBC haircut, the country ERP) plus fashion-brand base rates against
five straight years of above-algorithm growth — Coach itself went cold within three years
of its early-2010s peak, and only ~29% of firms sustain excess returns. Options timing
check: no dated claim in the thesis; path-2 stopgap only, liquidity gate failed (§4) — no
2σ refutation applies and none was used.

## 2. Business

**Created.** Tapestry (post the Aug-2025 Stuart Weitzman divestiture to Caleres) is two
brands: **Coach** — FY26 pro forma revenue $6,914.7M, +23% cc, 87% of sales — and
**Kate Spade** — $1,074.9M, −11% cc (Q4 FY26 release, Schedule of pro forma revenue).
The customer buys "expressive luxury": a $200–500 leather handbag with genuine
craft-and-design content at a fifth of European-luxury price points — an accessible entry
into status goods for a customer (deliberately Gen Z at the point of market entry) whom
LVMH/Kering price out. Management's TAM frame: "25 million women turning 18 every year in
the markets we play that can afford our bags" (Q4 call, Todd Kahn). The consumer surplus is
real: handbag AUR rose mid-teens in FY26 *while units grew low double digits* — price and
volume up together is the signature of a brand giving more perceived value than it charges.

**Captured.** Direct-to-consumer retail (~90% of sales through own stores + coach.com and
outlets; DTC +16% cc FY26) at a **76.6% non-GAAP gross margin — above Hermès-class —**
funding 12% of sales (~$1B) in demand-creation marketing, and still leaving a 23.4%
operating margin (Coach ~36%, best-in-class for accessible luxury; Kate Spade ~breakeven;
figures from Q4 release and call). The "One Coach" strategy blurs full-price and outlet:
collection product sells at full price inside outlet doors, raising outlet AUR without
diluting the main line. Capital intensity is trivial: FY26 capex+cloud $217M on $8.0B
revenue (2.7%), guided ~$300M (3–4%) for FY27.

**Protected.** Management's own words are the honest version: "there are no barriers of
entry in our space… there are barriers to scale, and those barriers are getting higher"
(Todd Kahn / Scott Roe, Q4 call). The mechanism, concretely: (1) an 85-year-old brand with
~$1B/yr of marketing behind it — a scale of demand creation a new entrant cannot fund from
a $200–500 price point; (2) a DTC data loop (patented data fabric + first AI patent,
per the call — labelled management-sourced) that shortens the insight-to-product cycle;
(3) 1,000+ own doors giving physical distribution no D2C startup replicates. What the moat
does NOT stop: the consumer walking away when the product goes cold. Coach itself decayed
2013–2016 under promotion-led growth; Kate Spade is decaying now inside this same
corporate structure with full access to the same data fabric and marketing dollars. The
moat is scale-in-execution, not lock-in — it amplifies a hot brand and cannot save a cold
one.

## 3. Threads pulled

- **Why −16.5% on a beat?** Q4 adj EPS $1.32 vs $1.28 consensus; FY27 guide $7.80–7.90 vs
  $7.84 consensus; Q1 guide $1.55 vs $1.48 (TheFly consensus notes, Aug 17). The guide was
  *in line* — the selloff is multiple compression on deceleration (Coach low-teens Q1 →
  high-single-digit FY; NA to low-single-digit; gross margin guided +30bp after +120bp;
  tariffs flip to a headwind in H2 FY27) from an all-time high $164.80 three days before
  the print, +28% over the prior year. Labelled inference — no primary source states the
  market's reason. What it means for the thesis: the price cut is expectations resetting,
  not new negative information about the business.
- **FY25's GAAP collapse was one-offs, not decay.** FY25 net income $183.2M on $216.1M
  pretax carried a $244.1M goodwill impairment, $619.5M asset writedown (Kate Spade), and
  $166.2M restructuring (stockanalysis income-statement route, matching the 10-K). FY26
  GAAP EPS $7.27 / non-GAAP $7.05; GAAP includes ~+$46M of one-time benefits (IEEPA tariff
  refunds, SW divestiture — Q4 release non-GAAP reconciliation). Operating leverage runs
  positive: revenue $6.68B → $8.00B FY22→FY26 while operating income went $1.18B → $1.86B
  and gross margin 69.6% → 76.6%.
- **FCF quality: the FY26 print is flattered.** FY26 levered FCF $1,812.5M (NCFO $1,978.5M
  + capex −$166.0M) includes a +$119.2M working-capital swing and cash taxes of $200.7M
  vs $332.9M book tax (cash tax rate ~10.8% vs ~18.5% guided marginal — IEEPA refunds and
  timing). Management's own FY27 guide: adjusted FCF ~$1.7B *after* the capex step-up to
  ~$300M. Base used for valuation: **$1.60B = FY27 guided $1.7B − SBC run-rate $104.5M**
  (SBC from the cash-flow statement; it is compensation, not owner cash).
- **AUR-vs-units — the pre-2014 Coach failure mode.** Growth led by price with declining
  units is how Coach broke the first time. Checked: FY26 handbag AUR +mid-teens AND units
  +low-double-digits; Q4 units flat *by design* (fewer promo days, sell-through pulled into
  Q3 — CFO). FY27 guide embeds both AUR and unit growth (Scott Roe, explicit). Watch this
  quarterly; it is falsifier #2.
- **Kate Spade: containment, not recovery.** Multi-year decline — FY26 revenue $1,074.9M,
  −10% reported / −11% cc vs FY25's ≈$1,194M (Q4 release pro forma schedule; earlier years
  not re-verified here), FY27 guided to a high-single-digit
  decline and a "modest operating loss." Management's own admission: "unaided brand
  awareness more broadly has not yet improved" (CEO, Q4 call). New creative director
  (Jonathan Saunders) and CMO (Allison Badea) — a real attempt, no evidence yet. This
  thesis treats KS as a contained ~13%-of-revenue drag, not a recovery option; a fix is
  upside not priced in here.
- **The Capri shadow — capital-allocation risk.** This management signed an $8.5B all-cash
  leveraged acquisition of Capri Holdings in Aug 2023; the FTC blocked it and the deal died
  Nov 2024 — shareholders were rescued by the regulator, and the $2.06B FY25 + $1.64B FY26
  buybacks are partly the returned deal capital. The stated gate — no acquisitions "before…
  Coach remains strong and Kate Spade has returned to sustainable top-line growth" (CFO) —
  is prose, not structure; note the irony that fixing KS re-opens the M&A door. Demonstrated
  appetite for large leveraged M&A is a live falsifier.
- **Estimate pattern.** 7/7 beats over the trailing quarters (Robinhood
  `get_earnings_results`): +$0.25, +$0.15, +$0.02, +$0.12, +$0.47, +$0.37, +$0.05. Chronic

## 5. Falsifiers

1. **Coach cc growth below MSD for two consecutive quarters, or NA negative with AUR still
   rising** → BREAK. The engine is 87% of revenue; the deceleration the market fears
   becoming a stall is the thesis-ending event.
2. **Units declining while AUR rises for 2+ quarters** → BREAK. The 2013 failure mode:
   price masking volume exit. (Q4 FY26 flat units were explained and guided to resume.)
3. **Gross margin declining ex-tariff, or promo re-escalation (promo days rising)** →
   SHIFT, revalue — the 76.6% GM is the value-capture core.
4. **Kate Spade loss widening beyond "modest," a new impairment, or KS revenue below
   ~$900M/yr** → SHIFT, haircut the base FCF.
5. **A large leveraged acquisition (the Capri impulse resurfacing) or buyback suspension**
   → BREAK of the capital-return leg that carries ~6.6%/yr of the return.
6. **Greater China growth below MSD** → SHIFT (17.5% of revenue, currently +28% Q4; guided
   mid-teens).

## 6. UNKNOWNs

1. **Gen Z retention/repeat rates** — management asserts "highest retention rates in our
   customer file"; no quantitative disclosure exists. Would come from company data only.
   Absence does not kill: the conditions rest on reported revenue/AUR/units, not on the
   retention claim.
2. **How much one-time IEEPA refund benefit sits inside the FY27 adj-FCF guide** — the
   10-K doesn't decompose it. Bounded: the base already deducts SBC and absorbs the capex
   step-up; a full refund unwind is roughly the $130M cash-vs-book tax gap, ~8% of base.
3. **The market's actual reason for −16.5%** — inference from public consensus figures;
   no primary source. Doesn't bear load: the valuation stands on today's price, whatever
   caused it.
4. **Kate Spade fix probability** — genuinely unassessable until awareness metrics move;
   treated as contained drag (condition #4), never as upside.
