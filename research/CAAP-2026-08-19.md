## 2. Business

**Created:** Passengers and airlines get functioning gateway infrastructure
in markets where the state under-invested for decades — runway, terminal,
security, and the commercial layer (duty-free, VIP lounges, parking, fuel in
Armenia, cargo in Argentina). The customer's alternative is usually nothing:
these are the only international airports in their catchments.

**Captured:** Three distinct mechanisms, not one. (a) Regulated aeronautical
fees per passenger and aircraft movement — dollar-denominated for
international traffic, tariff-indexed by concession contract (increases in
Brazil, Uruguay, Ecuador supported Q2'26). (b) Commercial revenue —
duty-free, VIP lounges (fastest-growing line), space rental, parking,
fuel — which grows faster than traffic (Q2'26 commercial +13% headline,
+26% ex-Argentine-cargo). (c) Construction margin under IFRIC 12 —
pass-through revenue at thin margin; excluded from management's own EBITDA
metric and from this analysis. The state takes its cut off the top: AA2000
allocates 15% of revenue to a state trust; TAGSA pays ~50–55% of regulated
revenue as concession fee.

**Protected:** Exclusive concession contracts with dated terms — a legal
mechanism, not a label: AA2000 to 2038 (85% owned), TA Pisa 2048/Florence
2045 (62.3%), Brasilia 2037 (51%), Carrasco 2053 and Punta del Este 2043
(100%), Guayaquil 2031 (50%), Armenia 2067 (100%). No competitor can enter
during the term. The weakness is the counterparty: the same state that
grants exclusivity resets the economics at every renegotiation, holds a
disclosed buy-out right on AA2000, and conditions extension on investment
commitments.

  the actual terms.
- **Shift —** price: below ~$20.20 (market cap ~$3.3B) the unchanged base

- **Break —** the rebalancing imposes large committed capex without tariff

  value-defining asset reprices down and the story is over.

  no compensating term: cut the base by the difference and revalue.
- **Shift —** Baghdad/Angola definitive agreements arrive with equity
  commitments large enough to consume the dividend capacity (> ~$300M):
  revalue capital allocation.
