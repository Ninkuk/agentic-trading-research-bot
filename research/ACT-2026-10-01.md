## 2. Business

**Created:** a lender can sell a low-down-payment (<20%) conforming loan to
Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac only with credit enhancement — the GSE charter
requires it — and private MI is the cheapest standing form of it. The
borrower gets a home years before saving 20%; the lender gets a saleable
loan; the GSE gets first-loss protection on the riskiest slice. 10-K
2025: risk-weighted average FICO 746, LTV 93%.

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (a) **Monthly premiums on the in-force
book** — base premium rate 39.1bp on $274B primary IIF; net earned
premiums $245M in Q2 (flat YoY). The revenue is an annuity whose length is
persistency (80%): higher mortgage rates lock borrowers in and lengthen
it. (b) **Investment income on the float and capital** — $73M in Q2
(+11% YoY, book yield 4.6%), the source of all recent revenue growth.
(c) **Risk-based pricing spread** — a proprietary engine prices each
loan; the profit is premium minus claims minus ~21% expense ratio, with
CRT (quota share and ILNs, $1.9B PMIERs credit) ceding part of the tail.
Enact Re (Bermuda, $500M contributed 2023) adds GSE credit-risk-transfer
reinsurance.

**Protected:** regulation, not product. Writing MI on GSE loans requires
GSE approval under PMIERs (risk-based capital standards) plus state
licensing; six approved writers exist and no new entrant has been
approved in a decade (NMI was the last, ~2012 — low-confidence colour, not
verified this run). Lenders route volume through rate-card/engine
pricing, so share is contestable among the six — the base premium rate
has drifted down — but entry is not. The threat is substitution, not a
seventh insurer: the 10-K names GSE alternative credit enhancement,
lender self-insurance and capital-markets structures as competition, and
the GSEs' regulator can change coverage levels or cancellation rules by
policy. The moat is exactly as wide as FHFA lets it be.

**Control:** one share class; Genworth Financial owns ~81.6% (10-K 2025,
post-2021 minority IPO of 18.4%) and participates pro rata in buybacks to
hold it (Genworth Form 4s). Controlled company: no unsolicited takeover or
activist path runs through ACT's own minority; any change of control is
Genworth's decision. Genworth needs Enact's cash (it received $202M in
H1 2026), which aligns it with high payouts — and a Genworth activist has
twice proposed "Enact stake strategic alternatives" (Genworth
DEFC14A/PREC14A 2026), excluded both times on bylaw grounds.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): negative** since 2022 (positive vs 2021,
which carried COVID-era losses). Revenue growth is investment income;
premiums are flat and 2022's operating income was inflated by reserve
releases.
