## 2. Business

**Created:** a homeowner pays $500–900 a year (CFO, KeyBanc forum,
2026-08-11) and, when the HVAC, water heater or dishwasher fails, pays a
trade-service fee and Frontdoor dispatches a vetted contractor and
guarantees the repair or replacement. The customer buys budget protection
and convenience — "insurance is in case something happens, a warranty is
for when something happens" — and uses it: almost two claims per member
per year, 3.8M service requests annually (10-K). Contractors get volume
they do not have to market for; 4,200 "preferred" firms do 84% of jobs.
Builders (2-10 HBW, acquired 2024-12-19 for $580M net) transfer ten-year
structural risk and workmanship administration; roughly one in six new US
homes carries a 2-10 warranty.

**Captured:** four distinct mechanisms. (a) Renewal subscriptions: $1,587M
in 2025, 76% of revenue, priced individually by a 60-plus-factor dynamic
pricing model that added ~3% realized price in Q2. (b) First-year plans
sold through real estate closings ($141M in 2025, renewing at 29% after
year one) and direct-to-consumer marketing ($172M, renewing at 74%) —
both currently sold at *lower* realized price than a year ago to buy
units. (c) Non-warranty: the HVAC upgrade program sells a new system at
20–40% below retail to a member whose unit just failed, using the
company's purchasing scale; $13M five years ago, ~$170M expected in 2026,
low-20s margin, near-zero acquisition cost; appliances are the next
trade. (d) 2-10 builder warranties: insurance-backed structural coverage
with $236M of unearned premium held long-term, reinsured, regulated as an
insurer. Trade-service fees and interest on ~$627M of cash ($155M of it
restricted) sit on top.

**Protected:** a scale-and-data mechanism, not a label. Claims volume
gives purchasing power on parts and equipment and lets the company route
84% of jobs to the contractors it has measured as cheapest and best; the
member data feeds a pricing model that prices each renewal to its own risk
and elasticity. A national entrant would need the volume before it could
have the cost, and the renewal history before it could have the pricing.
Evidence it binds: gross margin up 13 points in three years while
retention rose. Evidence of its limits: the category is fragmented and
price-competitive, first-year realized price is falling in both
acquisition channels, and the last share gain of size (2-10) was bought,
not won. Brand (40%+ ad recall) is real but rentable. Honest read: a
durable cost advantage inside a mature category, not a fortress.

**Control:** one class of common, one vote per share, 68.9M shares out;
no controlling holder — BlackRock 14.8%, Vanguard 13.9%, Boston Partners
8.4%, State Street 5.1% (proxy, as of 2026-03-01); insiders 1.56% (CEO
Cobb 657k shares). No classified board, no dual class, no supermajority
requirements; a forum-selection clause is the only anti-takeover feature
the company itself flags. Nothing forecloses an unsolicited bid or an
activist. Written as the null answer it is.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue +34% from 2021 to
TTM; operating income +81%, with a 2022 trough when claims inflation ran
ahead of price (stockanalysis income statement, $M):
