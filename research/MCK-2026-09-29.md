## 2. Business

**Created:** a pharmacy, hospital, or oncology clinic gets every drug it
dispenses — brand, generic, specialty, cold-chain biologic — delivered next
day from a regional distribution centre, on trade credit, with the
manufacturer relationships, DEA compliance, returns, and inventory
financing handled. The customer's alternative is to carry weeks of
inventory and hundreds of supplier relationships itself; nobody does. A
community oncology practice additionally gets a management company (US
Oncology Network, Florida Cancer Specialists via Core Ventures) that runs
its billing, purchasing (Unity/Onmark GPO), and clinical-trial access
(Sarah Cannon Research Institute, 43 of 52 adult oncology approvals in
2025). A manufacturer gets CoverMyMeds: 95% of prior authorizations
answered inside 30 minutes, which is the difference between a filled and an
abandoned prescription.

**Captured:** five distinct mechanisms, not one. (i) Brand distribution:
a fee for service paid by the manufacturer, disclosed as >95% of brand
contracts, so revenue moves with WAC but the fee does not. (ii) Generics:
a sourcing spread — ClarusONE buys at scale and sells at a mark-up, the
single largest profit pool in North American Pharmaceutical (segment OP
$3.66B on $337B revenue in FY26, 1.09%). (iii) Specialty and oncology
distribution plus GPO fees: the higher-margin volume (Oncology &
Multispecialty OP margin 2.29% in Q1 FY27 vs 1.04% for NAP). (iv) Practice
management: a share of the practice's economics through the 70%/80%
controlling stakes (Core Ventures, PRISM Vision) — this is why
noncontrolling-interest income is guided at $295–325M for FY27 and matters
to §4. (v) Prescription Technology Solutions: per-transaction and
per-program fees from manufacturers for access and affordability programs
(OP margin 14.4% in Q1; $1.04B OP in FY26, the highest-margin segment).
Medical-Surgical ($938M OP in FY26) is being separated as "Wellverse" and
is not part of the forward thesis.

**Protected:** scale in a three-player market where the product is a
commodity and the service is not. The mechanism is fixed cost: a national
DC network (a new Oklahoma DC adds 75% throughput), DEA suspicious-order
systems built under a consent judgment, and $24B of inventory financed by
manufacturer payables (working capital −$7.5B) that a new entrant cannot
fund at a 1.6% margin. The customer side is also concentrated — the ten
largest are 73% of revenue — which is why the moat cuts both ways: the
wholesaler is hard to replace, and so is CVS. The oncology platform's
protection is different and stronger: a physician who sold 70% of his
management company to McKesson with a put exercisable every five years
does not move his purchasing to Cencora. CoverMyMeds' protection is a
two-sided network (pharmacies, payers, EHRs, manufacturers) that a
competitor would have to rebuild on all four sides at once.

**Control:** one class of common stock, $0.01 par, one vote per share (10-K
cover lists only the common and two exchange-listed notes); no controlling
holder — insiders 0.05%, institutions 91.9%, float 99.8% of shares
outstanding (statistics probe). Nothing forecloses a takeover or an
activist; the size ($100B) does. The null answer, written.

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): flat over five years, positive over the
last eighteen months.** Revenue and operating income, fiscal years to
March 31 (stockanalysis `financials/income-statement/`, $B):

| FY | revenue | op income | op margin |

| 2022 | 264.0 | 4.15 | 1.57% |
| 2023 | 276.7 | 4.32 | 1.56% |
| 2024 | 309.0 | 4.57 | 1.48% |
| 2025 | 359.1 | 4.94 | 1.38% |
| 2026 | 403.4 | 6.29 | 1.56% |
| TTM (Jun-26) | 411.0 | 6.44 | 1.57% |

Revenue +56% and operating income +55% over the span: the business scales
linearly, which is what a fee-per-unit model should do. FY26 was the
exception (OP +27% on revenue +12%; opex as a share of gross profit fell
~150bp again in Q1 FY27) and it is the mix shift — oncology, RxTS, generics
sourcing — not the core distribution margin, that produced it. FY24–25's
margin dip was GLP-1 revenue at near-zero margin diluting the ratio.
