# G — Genpact Limited, 2026-08-17

## 1. Verdict and thesis

**Own it at $33.70. Kill-thesis verdict: UNPROVEN (5 load-bearing conditions, 0 refuted, 2 unknown).**
Genpact is a good business priced as a dying one: the market's AI-kills-BPO repricing has it at 10.0x
trailing earnings and a 10.1% FCF yield, while the reported numbers run the other way — 13 consecutive
quarters of gross-margin expansion, revenue +7.1% on headcount −3%, and a 5.0% shareholder yield. The
ownership call is **buy** because a reverse DCF shows even a zero-growth-forever bear case clears the
cost-of-equity hurdle (+96bp); the conservative case clears it by +350bp. You are not paying for the
agentic pivot to succeed — only for the core not to collapse. The UNPROVEN hinge is terminal: whether
non-FTE pricing lets Genpact capture (rather than compete away) the AI automation surplus, and no
disclosure can answer that today.

## 2. Business

**Creates value:** runs mission-critical back-office operations (finance & accounting, supply chain,
insurance claims, banking ops) for large enterprises at lower cost and higher reliability than in-house,
from an Indian-centric delivery base of ~141-146k employees; increasingly sells data/AI transformation
and "agentic" automation of those same workflows (Advanced Technology Solutions, 27% of revenue,
+24% YoY). Customer surplus: clients shed non-core cost centers and get process expertise they cannot
hire at scale. (10-K FY2025; Q2'26 call.)

**Captures value:** long-dated MSA + statement-of-work contracts; historically per-FTE pricing, now
>50% of revenue on non-FTE models (fixed-fee, consumption, outcome) — Q2'26 was the first quarter past
50%. Agentic contracts are described as multi-year annuitized recurring revenue with minimum volume
commitments, floor plus usage upside (CEO, Q2'26 call — transcribed primary). Diversified: top-5
clients 15.2% of revenue, no client >10% of AR (10-K).

**Protects value:** the mechanism is embedded switching cost — Genpact operates the client's live
process (the exceptions, controls, handoffs: "process intelligence"), and moving a running F&A or
claims operation is costly and risky mid-contract. Decades of accumulated last-mile knowledge is the
claimed differentiator against both IT-services rivals and model vendors. Honest caveat: AI lowers the
cost of rebuilding that knowledge over time — the moat's decay rate is exactly the terminal question.
Where the answer is "nothing stops a competitor": commoditized contact-center and content work — which
management is explicitly exiting (~2pts of revenue).
