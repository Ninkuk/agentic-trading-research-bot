## 2. Business

**Created:** a national, digital-first lender to small businesses in about
33 named verticals (veterinary, pharmacy, funeral, self-storage, energy and
infrastructure, and others), using SBA 7(a)/USDA guarantees. The borrower
gets a vertical-specialist underwriter and a fast, mostly remote process.
That beats a local bank that sees one vet clinic a year. Depositors get a
competitive online savings rate. Q2: $1.55B originated across 33 industries.
The 10-year NCO ratio is ~40bp vs 120bp+ for the SBA industry (Q2 2026 call).

**Captured:** three mechanisms. (a) **Spread income** on the retained book:
NII $125.3M/qtr, NIM 3.33%, on $12.63B loans HFI. Retained exposure is mainly
the unguaranteed 25%, plus conventional loans held whole. (b) **Gain on sale**
of the government-guaranteed portions into the secondary market: $17.6M in
Q2, premiums 109–111 against a 109–113 target. (c) **Servicing fees** on
$5.99B of sold loans it still services; the servicing asset is $66.7M. A
growing fourth line, business checking and merchant services ($744M of
DDA/checking, ~5% of deposits), lowers funding cost rather than adding fees.

**Protected:** two partial mechanisms and one honest gap. *Mechanism 1:*
vertical underwriting data accumulated since 2008. The loss record (40bp vs
120bp+) is the evidence that it works, and a generalist cannot copy it
quickly. *Mechanism 2:* scale with the SBA. LOB has been the largest 7(a)
lender by dollar volume for years (low-confidence, trade press), and that
scale makes secondary-market execution cheap. *Gap:* the product rests on a
government program whose fees, terms and even day-to-day operation (shutdown
lapses) LOB does not control. Funding is rate-shopped online deposits with
no franchise stickiness beyond the checking push. Excess returns here depend
on underwriting skill. They are not structurally locked in.

**Control:** one class of voting common (46,239,891 shares at the 3/20/2026
record date). The 8.375% Series A preferred ($96.3M, 4,000,000 depositary
shares) is non-voting (2026 proxy, p.3). Founder/CEO James S. Mahan III owns
6,619,682 shares, 14.4% (2026 13G/A); insiders total ~22%
(`stocks.db`). That is no controlling holder, but a 14% founder bloc plus
North Carolina bank-holding-company rules make an unsolicited takeover
impractical without him. Mahan has taken no equity awards since the IPO
(proxy p.31), and his 10b5-1 plan sells up to 400k shares (~6% of his stake)
from November 2026 to November 2027 (8-K 2026-08-28).

**Operating leverage (Phase 0): positive.** Revenue (NII + noninterest

## 4. Valuation

**Why not the default pairing.** stockanalysis `fcf` ($237.4M = NCFO $253.3M
− capex $15.9M) is not owner cash for a bank. A growing bank must retain
equity in step with its loan book to hold its capital ratios. CET1 already
slipped from 10.67% to 10.42% YoY while LOB retained almost all earnings
(dividend $0.12/yr, payout 4%). Owner cash is FCFE = net income − Δrequired
equity = BV × (ROE − book growth). I built the flows that way: common
tangible book $1,216.6M (total equity $1,316.9M less $96.3M preferred, which
also drops the preferred dividend out of common ROE), market cap $1,600.0M,
no net-debt adjustment (deposits are operating liabilities). Year 1 FCFE =
BV0 × (ROE − b). Years 1–4 grow at b. Year 5 steps up by (1+b)(ROE−gT)/(ROE−b)
as book growth fades to the terminal rate, then grows at gT. No SBC
deduction: SBC ($26.9M TTM, 5.1% of revenue) is already expensed in GAAP net
income; dilution runs +1.0%/yr in shares. No minority interest. The $928M
cash is regulatory liquidity, not excess cash. No pension.

**Hurdle:** rf 5.28% (DGS10, 2026-10-07) + beta 1.2 × ERP 4.20% (Damodaran
implied, 2026-10-01, against his 5.29% T-bond) = **10.32%**. Beta: the 5y
regression is 1.94 (it spans the 2021 fintech peak at $99.89 and the crash),
2y 1.05, 1y 0.61. Insiders at 22% trip the thin-float flag, which argues
against trusting the low 1y figure. I used 1.2, the top of the stable band,
because a cyclical small-business lender should not hide in the middle of
it. Sensitivity at the 5y beta of 1.94: hurdle 13.43%, every scenario below.
The mature-company check (rf + 4.5% = 9.78%) sits 54bp under the hurdle.

**Terminal-growth ceiling:** 10-year breakeven 2.35% (T10YIE, 2026-10-08),
used as `--terminal-growth`, which means no real growth in perpetuity.

| scenario | base FCF | growth ×5y | terminal | implied return | vs hurdle |

| Bear: ROE 10%, book +8% | $22.53M (Y1 $24.3M) | 8% ×4, step ×4.13 | 2.35% | 8.42% | −190bp |

Closed-form cross-check (stable growth, P/TBV = (ROE−g)/(k−g)): at 1.32× and
12% ROE, k = 2.35% + 9.65%/1.32 = 9.66%. The five-year growth leg adds ~40bp
because ROE exceeds k during it, which agrees with the base row.
